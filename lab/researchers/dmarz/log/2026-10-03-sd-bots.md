## What surprised me

- The strongest empirical result in this subfield is negative: Gallwitz & Kreil manually checked 230 accounts that
  Botometer studies counted as bots and found no malicious social bot; 116 of 121 'vaccine bots' were ordinary humans.
  Hays et al. show benchmark datasets are separable by one feature that reflects collection (every human in cresci-2017
  tweeted 'earthquake'). Bot-prevalence estimates span under 5% (Twitter), 9-15% (Varol 2017) and about 20% (Ng & Carley),
  and the thresholding critique explains why they cannot be reconciled.
- The only hard in-the-wild base rates for generative-AI accounts come from image tells, not text: 0.021-0.044% (Yang
  et al.) and 0.052% of 15M profile images (Ricker et al.) used GAN faces in 2023. Text-side, La Cava et al. find
  machine-generated text marginal on Reddit but up to 9% in some subreddit-months, concentrated in few users.
- Every in-the-wild LLM botnet found so far (fox8) was found through an operator slip plus coordination structure, and
  LLM-text classifiers failed on it. A 2024 field test ran an LLM bot on 8 major platforms and none stopped it.
- No public labelled dataset of real LLM-driven bot accounts exists; detection papers train on simulated swarms (BotSim-24,
  Masquerade-23 from the all-bot Chirper platform), and Ng & Carley find simulated LLM bots do not look like wild bots or humans.
  Near-perfect scores on these datasets (TRACE-Bot 98%) should be read with Hays et al. in mind.
- Speculation (not measured anywhere I read): detection by coordination and by costly-to-fake account history should hold up
  better against LLM swarms than content detection; Katyal's simulated laundering supports this, but it uses no real LLM.
