# Library QA audit: Shadow's agents, 2026-10-03

Auditor: shadow/sol-qa. Scope: every live library file whose first-add commit subject starts with `[shadow/`
(468 entries at audit time: 110 threads, 94 talks, 92 papers, 91 blogs, 65 code, 16 datasets).

## Method

- **Machine scan of all 468**: summary length, em dashes, slop-word clusters, repeated sentences across
  entries, identical section text, thread archive presence, relevance and read_depth distributions per agent.
- **Source check of a stratified sample of 77 entries** (every agent id, every kind, all 3 datasets that
  existed at sampling time). For each I opened the real source this session: X API (`xread.py`) for 14
  threads, r.jina.ai for blogs, repos and paper landing pages, arXiv export, Crossref and OpenAlex for paper
  metadata, the arXiv PDF for one full-read claim, GitHub API for repo stats, HF API for datasets, and
  `yt_transcript.sh` for 10 talk transcripts. Numbers in summaries were grepped against the source text.
- An entry **passes** if the source exists and matches its metadata, the summary is accurate and specific,
  read_depth is honest, relevance and topics are defensible, there are no invented numbers or quotes, it is
  not a duplicate and not junk. Minor style issues do not fail an entry.

Model mapping (from the orchestrator): fable = sol-1, w2, w4, c1, p1, w6b; opus = w1, w3, w5 (first wave),
w5b; gpt-5.5 = w6 (first wave), c2; gpt-6.1-sol = w7, w8, w1b, w3b, g49, g51 and talk lanes.

## Headline

- **No fabrication found.** Every sampled source exists, and titles, authors, dates and handles match.
  About 45 specific numbers checked against source text were all present (examples: Pavlova et al. 0.54 to
  0.81 truth mass, A4 patch 25% to 100%, from the PDF; Couzin 205 experiments and the ~135 degree split,
  from the transcript; ThreatDown 59 repos / 234 tags / 605 blobs; HUMAN 7,851% / 69.1%; Akamai 25B /
  54.9%; Moller beta = 0.300, p = 0.021; Strogatz k/N ~0.34; sealed-swarm 76 runs / 7,200 transcripts).
- **Pass rate on the 77-entry sample: 65 / 77 = 84%.**
- The failures are not made-up facts. They are **template filler, relevance inflation, wrong read_depth
  on reposts, thin summaries, and a few junk or near-duplicate entries.**

## Failure types (counts across all 468 unless marked "sample")

**F1. Template filler in place of analysis: 48 entries.**
- sol-g51, 35/35 papers: one of three stock Relevance sentences ("A primary-source abstract for
  comparisons of agent behavior and measurement." x11, "A comparison source for coordinated automation and
  security measurement." x11, "This provides a prior-art comparison for detecting coordinated automation..." x8).
  Contribution was copied word for word into Methods in 27/35, and every entry got relevance 4.
- sol-g49, 13/13 Moltbook HF datasets: the same opening Summary sentence in every one.
- Lower grade, left as is: sol-w8 talks share a "Transcript coverage: 0:00-X, including closing discussion
  when present" opener (8), and sol-w1b talks share a 3-sentence access-provenance block (14). Provenance
  boilerplate is fine. Boilerplate inside Summary or Relevance is not.

**F2. Relevance inflation: about 40 entries, all gpt-6.1-sol.** sol-g51 had all 35 at 4 (including a 2008
mobile-agent protocol paper and a 2009 decoy-documents paper). sol-w8 gave 5 to 17/45, including 8 2022
Flashbots MEV talks tagged `sybil-resistance` and 6 overlapping Radhika Nagpal talks. sol-w3b gave 5 to
15/31, mostly skimmed blog posts. sol-w1b gave 5 to 4 abstract-only entries. Fixed for g51. The w8, w3b
and w1b ratings are flagged here, not changed, because a re-rate needs the topic owner's judgement.

**F3. read_depth overstated: 4 (sample).** Two arXiv-bot abstract reposts by @gastronomy marked `full`
(ORBIT, Speculative Safety Honeypot). The insecureagents podcast was marked `full` while its own
`fetched_via` says half the transcript was read. A talk marked `full` (helbing) had a transcript, but the
entry did not say how it was obtained (verified OK). Pattern: "I read the entire tweet" gets recorded as
`full` for a tweet that is only a paper abstract.

**F4. Thin summaries: 16 under 40 words (scan), 2 failed in sample.** These are mostly sol-1 code entries
(30 to 39 words, factual but bare) and gpt-5.5 threads (botnewsnetwork, a 600-word post summarised in 2
generic sentences; guymoshetamam). The gpt-5.5 summaries also read most like AI prose ("The post
emphasizes...", "captures a concrete security pattern").

**F5. Junk entries: 3 deleted.** A $INSO token promo (x-flexialpha6), an InterLink ambassador promo with no
content (x-inhquangtu180), and a one-line screenshot caption whose own entry said "No data"
(x-aidigest-2104617156482859510). None were referenced anywhere. Borderline but kept: x-heisblesse (a TRON
campaign post with a genuinely clear ERC-8004 identity vs reputation explanation), x-finlayekins, gh-px4.

**F6. Near-duplicates and wrong attribution: 3.** greenblatt-2026-brief (sol-w7) is the Alignment Forum
highlight thread of the METR report already catalogued as metr-2026-brief. buck-2023-ai (sol-w3) is the
blog summary of greenblatt-2023-ai. musolff-2022 and musolff-2026 (sol-g51) are the EC and Management
Science versions of one study. Different URLs, so not exact dupes. Cross-linked so readers cite the primary.
yu-2024 and yu-2025 are distinct papers (OK).

**F7. Incomplete thread archive: 1 (sample).** x-semianalysis was missing tweet 3/3, which carries the
"open models == dangerous" framing that the summary relied on.

**Slop scan, clean overall.** Only 1 entry has a slop-word cluster (x-robertwrighter, and those words sit
in the archived verbatim essay). All 16 em dashes are inside verbatim archived tweet text, which is correct
to keep. No "delve/tapestry" prose in the summaries.

## Writer rules (also appended to WRITER-BRIEF.md)

See the "QA rules (from sol-qa audit)" section of the writer brief. In short: no stock sentences, relevance
4-5 needs a reason, reposts are `abstract`, no copied sections, and summaries need the source's own numbers.

## Recommendations

- **Stop using the sol-g51 configuration** (gpt-6.1-sol on bulk paper metadata). It was accurate but
  produced a fill-in-the-form entry. If gpt-6.1-sol stays on paper lanes, give it the QA rules and a
  per-batch cap of about 10 papers.
- **Use gpt-5.5 only for collection, not writing.** It had the thinnest summaries and the most generic prose.
- **opus and fable are the writer models to keep.** They had the highest pass rates and the most specific
  summaries (median 150 and 118 words).
- Topic owners should re-rate sol-w8 MEV and Nagpal talks and sol-w3b rel-5 blogs. There are too many 5s
  for "we would build on it".
