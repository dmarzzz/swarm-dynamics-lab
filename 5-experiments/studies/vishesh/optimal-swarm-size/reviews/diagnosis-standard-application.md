# Applying the shared diagnosis step to Optimal Swarm Size

Status: prospective task-design application; no new native attempt, implementation claim or budget change. [Shared procedure](../../../../../tooling/agent-experiments/DIAGNOSIS.md), [O2 closeout](outage-o2-post.md).

## Diagnosis

O2 repaired a weak comparator: explicit ownership eliminated duplicate work. The remaining eight-service task is fully batchable and reaches a recovery ceiling for single, four, eight and rule-controller policies. This is a useful result on its support, not evidence that a forty-agent roster would improve utility or that large teams cannot help elsewhere.

The practical unresolved question is whether distributing costly incident investigation reduces time to a correct, safe intervention enough to offset coordination and compute. Competing explanations are (1) independent evidence acquisition limits recovery, so parallel investigation helps, and (2) evidence is cheap to batch while integration/coupled remediation limits recovery, so additional agents add overhead. O2 cannot distinguish those explanations because authoritative inspection directly exposes the needed configuration.

## Concrete next development contract

Create authored development incidents with distinct service logs, metrics and a dependency graph. Candidate mechanism families: dependency saturation, deployment/configuration drift and credential/protocol mismatch. These are proposed distinct mechanisms, not already-built independent samples. Each case has a versioned evidence ledger and evaluator-only causal state. Include a plausible misleading symptom, a discriminating observation available through a documented tool, and a clean matched control. Do not require guessing inaccessible facts; an insufficient-evidence case must reward escalation or abstention.

Separate investigation actions from remediation. Charge observation/tool access consistently across policies; retain acquired facts, hypotheses, cited evidence, proposed changes, conflict resolution and measured execution. Make shared-resource or dependent-service repairs interact only where the causal model supports it. Evaluate root-cause diagnosis separately from safe action and verified recovery. Preserve unsafe attempts/commits and successful restraint as distinct outcomes.

Retain a competent batching single agent, explicit-ownership fixed team and relevant rule/tool baseline. Before native collection, verify reference solutions, wrong-root-cause counterfactuals, misleading-symptom removal, answer-preserving renaming, label isolation and repair conflicts offline. A successful rule baseline remains a legitimate winner. Development cases must distinguish the two explanations before any larger comparison is worth running.
