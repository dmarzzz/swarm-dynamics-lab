> **collective-dynamics.md** — What makes a group of agents converge on a wrong option, and the mechanisms by which independently sourced dissent protects the decision.  
> Compiled 2026-10-03. Supporting note for the seo-poisoning proposal; hunch-level, not a survey or a hypothesis.

## 0. The answer in one paragraph

A population converges on a wrong option when three things hold at once: (a) agents act **sequentially or iteratively while observing each other's actions rather than each other's evidence**, (b) each agent's private evidence is **bounded in informativeness** (a short ranked list, a coarse score, a discrete choice), and (c) the **aggregation rule is nonlinear enough to lock in** (quorum, plurality, confidence-weighted averaging with low self-weight). Under (a)+(b)+(c) early noise is amplified into a self-sustaining commitment that carries almost no information, and the wrong-commit probability does not fall with population size. Protection comes from exactly three counter-levers, in descending order of measured effect: **evidence-root independence** (how many *disjoint* sources the population's beliefs descend from, not how many agents hold them), **unbounded or re-openable private signals** (an agent that can run a direct test, not only read), and **cross-inhibition / self-weight** (a mechanism that lets a minority holding a disjoint root suppress an over-advertised option rather than merely abstain). "More agents", "more rounds of debate", and "a mandated contrarian" are **not** on that list, and three independent measurements say each of them is neutral-to-harmful.

## 1. Information cascades and herding

**1.1 The mechanism and its exact conditions.** Bikhchandani, Hirshleifer & Welch (1992) [1] define an informational cascade as the case where "an individual's action does not depend on his private information signal" (Definition, Sec. II). Their model's conditions are: agents act **in sequence**, each observes **only the actions** of predecessors (not their signals), the action space is **discrete** (adopt/reject), and signals are drawn from a common bounded distribution. Once agent *i* is in a cascade its action conveys nothing, so by induction every successor is too — "a cascade once started will last forever, even if it is wrong" (Sec. II). Banerjee (1992) [2] derives the same herd outcome from a different primitive (a continuum of actions with a rational tie-break toward the observed action). *Boundary of evidence:* both are analytical results in stylised Bayesian models with one-shot sequential play and no cost of acquiring extra evidence; neither is an empirical measurement.

**1.2 How often the cascade is wrong.** In BHW's binary example with signal accuracy *p*, eq. (3) gives the limiting probability of the *correct* cascade as p(p+1)/[2(1-p+p^2)], so the wrong-cascade limit is (2-p)(1-p)/[2(1-p+p^2)] — **0.368 at p = 0.6 and 0.143 at p = 0.8** (arithmetic from their eq. (3); the paper itself states only that it "can be close to .5 if the signal is noisy enough", n. 16, p. 1002, and that "even for large p, the probability of ending up in the wrong cascade is considerable", Fig. 1 caption). *Boundary:* the closed-form numbers above are my arithmetic on their published formula, not values printed in the paper. The qualitative claim is the paper's own.

**1.3 Coarse signals are necessary, not incidental.** Smith & Sørensen (2000) [3] Theorem 3: with a single rational type and no noise, (a) a herd almost surely arises in finite time; (b) **with unbounded private beliefs individuals almost surely settle on the optimal action**; (c) **with bounded private beliefs an incorrect herd arises with positive probability**. They state the boundary directly: "barring discrete signals, cascades need not arise: No decision need ever be a foregone conclusion even during a herd" (Sec. 1). They also identify **confounded learning** — with heterogeneous preference types, beliefs can converge to an interior point where history stops being informative, and this "exists even with unbounded beliefs, when an incorrect herd is impossible." *Boundary:* analytical, single-good sequential-choice setting. For the proposal this is the single most load-bearing theoretical result: **it says the poisoning scenario's severity is set by whether an agent's evidence channel is bounded (read a ranked list) or unbounded (run a probe against the provider).**

**1.4 Fragility.** BHW's Result 3: "The release of a small amount of public information can shatter a long-lasting cascade, where a 'small amount' refers to a signal less informative than the private signal of a single individual" (Sec. III.A.1, pp. 1005-1006). Sec. III opens: "The arrival of a little information or the mere possibility of a value change (even if the change does not actually occur) can shatter an informational cascade." They also note a higher-precision agent arriving later can "shatter a cascade because he is more inclined to use his own information than those that precede him" (Sec. II.C), and that socially it "may be desirable to order decision makers inversely with their precisions." *Boundary:* fragility is a property of their Bayesian agents who correctly discount uninformative history. An agent that treats consensus as evidence (which LLM agents measurably do, §6) will be *less* fragile and therefore *more* capturable than BHW predicts. Treat 1.4 as the optimistic bound, not the expectation.

**1.5 Amplification of early error inside biological quorum rules.** Sumpter & Pratt (2009) [4] measure the same phenomenon in a quorum model: steep quorum responses (Hill exponent k = 9) put 83.3% of individuals on the better option vs 75.5% at k = 1 and 66.7% for independent choice, but take longer (307.8 +/- 71.0 vs 253.7 +/- 64.0 steps) and have a **wider accuracy distribution "because early random errors get amplified"** (n = 40, 1000 runs). McCormick et al. (2024) [5] review maladaptive cascades in animal groups and the four mechanisms groups use to limit them. Wang et al. (2012) [6] give cascade-tracing measures for swarms. *Boundary:* [4] is a model plus re-analysis of ant data; [5] is a qualitative review; [6] was read at abstract depth in the team library.

## 2. Wisdom of crowds requires independence

**2.1 Social influence erodes it (the human baseline).** Lorenz et al. (2011, PNAS) [7]: 144 subjects, 12 groups of 12, six factual estimation questions, five rounds, monetary accuracy incentives. Seeing others' estimates collapsed group diversity while **collective error barely changed**, pushed the truth to the edge of the estimate range (wisdom-of-crowd indicator ~1 unit lower than control, and worse under aggregated mean-only information than under full information), and **raised confidence without raising accuracy**. *Boundary:* the "undermining" is about diversity, truth position and confidence — the paper does not measure a large increase in collective error. Groups of 12, all-to-all visibility, no network structure.

**2.2 The sign depends on topology.** Becker, Brackbill & Centola (2017, PNAS) [8]: 1,360 subjects, groups of 40, three rounds. In **equal-degree (decentralised)** networks individual error fell 23%, group-median error 0.76 -> 0.67 SD and group-mean error 0.69 -> 0.62 SD (all P < 0.01). In **centralised (one hub)** networks the group followed the hub — better when the hub was right (n = 12), worse when wrong (n = 13), both P < 0.01. The mechanism is a DeGroot update with heterogeneous self-weight: revision magnitude correlated with initial error (rho = 0.41, 95% CI 0.39-0.43, n = 4,340 estimates). *Boundary:* continuous estimation only, three rounds, one centralisation type, modest effects (10-12%), and the improvement **depends on accurate agents revising less** — an empirical regularity that need not hold for overconfident or adversarially-primed populations. This is the result that makes topology a first-class experimental factor rather than a nuisance.

**2.3 Condorcet and correlated error.** Ladha (1992) [9] is the canonical statement that the Condorcet jury theorem survives *some* correlation but breaks when votes are positively correlated beyond a bound: majority competence rises with group size only while the average pairwise correlation of errors stays below a threshold set by individual competence. List, Elsholtz & Seeley (2009) [10] put it in exactly our terms: "when voting is supplemented by communication, however, the resulting interdependencies between decision makers can strengthen or undermine this effect: they can facilitate information pooling, but also amplify errors." *Boundary:* [9] verified at metadata + title level (AJPS 36(3):617, DOI 10.2307/2111584); I did not read its proofs, so the exact correlation bound is **unverified** here and the synthesis should cite the inequality from the paper if it needs the number. [10]'s abstract was verified via Crossref.

**2.4 Measured correlated failure, three independent datasets.**
- *Humans writing code independently:* Knight & Leveson (1986) [11] — 27 independently written programs from one spec, 10^6 inputs: 1,255 inputs made >=2 versions fail at once, one input made 8 of 27 fail, **z = 100.51 against the independence model**; about half of 45 faults were shared, and every shared fault crossed the two universities. Correlated faults were "far more obscure" and several survived the acceptance test.
- *LLMs writing code:* Nogueira et al. (2026) [12] — 224 problems, 12 models, 5 languages: 3- and 5-version majority ensembles realise only **0.43 and 0.44** of the reliability gain independence would predict, and **below 0.3 when all versions come from one model**.
- *LLMs reading shared evidence (the case this proposal is about):* Bara (2026) [13] — defines an **epistemic Sybil** as an extra report Z with I(Theta; Z | R) = 0, proves no aggregator reading only report *content* can distinguish replication from independent corroboration (Theorem 1), and measures in >20,000 calls on 300 evaluation worlds: multiplying reports from **one** evidence root from n = 1 to 32 drops naive 95% interval coverage **0.940 -> 0.263**, while a provenance-aware aggregator stays 0.850-0.940. At fixed n = 16, raising independent roots from 1 to 16 closes the gap (0.927 both). Extraction errors across agents sharing a base model are correlated at gamma_cal = 0.719; the effective-sample-size discount is m/[1 + rho(m-1)].
- *The killer control:* in Bara's 2x2, an embedding-dedup defence moved its cluster count by **+1.425 when only the rationale wording changed** and by **+0.040 when true ancestry changed fourfold** — i.e. **surface diversity is not evidence diversity, and deduplication tracks the wrong variable.** *Boundary:* [11] is student programmers, one problem, 1986. [12] and [13] are 2026 preprints; [13] is one task family, one small model, synthetic memos, and the author flags heavy-tailed extraction noise and untested chain-retransmission.

**2.5 Hong & Page — the actual scope.** Hong & Page (2004, PNAS) [14] prove that in a model of heuristic search agents (each a function on a finite set X, composed in sequence), a randomly drawn group outperforms a group of the individually best agents, because the best agents become similar as the pool grows. Thompson (2014, Notices of the AMS) [15] shows the theorem "is revealed to be little more than a straightforward restatement of its hypotheses": their Assumption 2 ("when one agent gets stuck, there is another agent who can make progress") directly implies the full collection always finds the optimum, while k copies of the best agent behave identically to one copy; and the theorem's N and N1 "can, and generally will, be substantially larger than k", so the hiring application does not follow. Singer (2019) [16] argues a weaker, correctly-scoped version survives ("Diversity, Not Randomness, Trumps Ability"). *Boundary:* use Hong & Page as the *intuition* for why heuristic diversity matters, never as a proof that diversity beats ability in a swarm. The defensible quantitative claim in our setting is **[12]/[13]/[17]**, not [14]. [16] verified at metadata level only; its argument is **unverified** here.

**2.6 Non-interacting heterogeneous aggregation works.** Schoenegger et al. (2024, Sci. Adv.) [17]: 12 LLMs, 31 binary forecasting questions, no interaction between models. The LLM crowd's median matched a crowd of 925 humans (Brier 0.20 +/- 0.12 vs 0.19 +/- 0.19, p = 0.850; 50% baseline 0.25). Yang et al. (2026) [18] give the information-theoretic version: homogeneous agent scaling saturates early, heterogeneity keeps helping, and **2 diverse agents can match or exceed 16 homogeneous agents**; they define K*, a label-free estimate of effective independent channels. *Boundary:* [17] is 31 questions and reports an acquiescence bias (mean LLM forecast 57.35% with 14/31 resolving positive). [18]'s headline is an abstract-level claim read from the team library, and its bounds are on information only, not on coordination cost.

## 3. Dissent and decision quality

**3.1 Minority influence is qualitatively different from majority influence.** Nemeth (1986, Psych. Review) [19] is the theoretical statement: majority influence induces convergent attention to the majority position, minority influence induces **divergent** thinking and attention to the task, so minority dissent improves the *quality* of reasoning even when the dissenting position is wrong. *Boundary:* a theoretical review paper (886 Crossref citations). Metadata verified; the publisher elides the abstract and no OA copy was reachable, so specific experiment numbers are **unverified** here.

**3.2 Genuine vs contrived dissent — direction confirmed, sizes not.** Schulz-Hardt, Jochims & Frey (2002, OBHDP 88(2):563-586) [20] is the canonical genuine-vs-contrived comparison, testing dissent as a counter to biased (preference-confirming) information seeking. Nemeth, Brown & Rogers (2001, EJSP 31(6):707-720) [21] is the direct devil's-advocate-vs-authentic-dissent comparison. **I could not verify the effect sizes for either.** Both publishers elide the abstract (confirmed via the Semantic Scholar record's own disclaimer) and Unpaywall reports `is_oa: false` for both DOIs; the one OA mirror for the adjacent [23] (TUM repository) returned an access-denied interstitial. **Flagged as unverified — the synthesis must not state a number for 3.2 without a primary read.** What *is* verified, from the same group and directly on point:
- Brodbeck, Kerschreiter, Mojzisch, Frey & Schulz-Hardt (2002, EJSP) [22], abstract verified: in three-person hidden-profile groups, "consideration of unshared information increased with minority dissent", and **"in diversity groups (i.e. each member prefers a different alternative) consideration of unshared information and group decision quality was significantly higher than in simple minority groups"** — decision-quality improvement was only *partially* supported.
- Greitemeyer, Schulz-Hardt & Frey (2008, EJSP) [24], abstract verified: "escalation tendencies over multiple decisions were reduced in **heterogeneous groups that used the devil's advocacy procedure**" — i.e. the contrived device worked *conditional on genuine preference heterogeneity being present*, not on its own.
- Schulz-Hardt, Brodbeck, Mojzisch, Kerschreiter & Frey (2006, JPSP 91(6):1080-1093) [23] is the hidden-profile dissent-as-facilitator study; metadata verified, content **unverified**.
- De Dreu & West (2001, JAppl Psych) [25]: minority dissent predicts team innovation **only when participation in decision making is high** — a moderator, not a main effect. Metadata verified; content unverified. *Direction to carry forward:* dissent helps when it is *structural* (members genuinely hold different alternatives / different information) and is channelled through a procedure that forces the unshared information out; a procedural dissenter layered on a homogeneous group is the weakest version and has no verified standalone effect in this literature.

**3.3 Groupthink — the boundary.** Esser (1998, OBHDP) [26] reviews 25 years of groupthink research; the standard summary is that the full Janis model is not well supported as a causal chain and that the individual antecedents (directive leadership, insulation, lack of procedures) have mixed support. *Boundary:* metadata verified, content unverified here. **Do not build the proposal's argument on groupthink.** Use the three quantitative lines instead: correlated-error (§2.4), conformity-response measurement (§6), and cross-inhibition (§4).

**3.4 The one clean quantitative dose-response for dissent, and it is from LLMs.** Bellina, De Marzo & Garcia (2026) [27], Asch-style, 13 open multimodal models, conformity measured as the token probability of the wrong answer p_wrong(N): **"Breaking unanimity strongly suppresses conformity: even 20% correct confederate answers markedly reduce it, and 50% almost removes it"** (measured on Qwen2.5-VL-32B, colour task). Conformity area correlates with task difficulty at Spearman rho = 0.97 (p < 1e-10); in a multivariate regression difficulty is significant (beta = 0.657, p < 1e-9) and model performance is not (p = 0.46). Public answers raise conformity relative to private ones. *Boundary:* single-shot prompts with *fictitious* confederates, not interacting agents; open models; visual tasks. This is the best available prior for the shape of our dissent dose-response curve (sharply concave, most of the gain in the first break of unanimity) but it is not measured in a live population.

**3.5 Dissent that carries evidence vs dissent that carries only disagreement.** Zhu, Zhang, Chi, Stafford, Collier & Vlachos (2026) "Demystifying Multi-Agent Debate" [28] verified via arXiv: vanilla debate "often underperforms simple majority vote despite higher computational cost" and "under homogeneous agents and uniform belief updates, debate preserves expected correctness and therefore cannot reliably improve outcomes". Their two interventions are exactly the two levers this note cares about: **diversity-aware initialisation** (raise the prior probability that a correct hypothesis is present at the start) and **confidence-modulated updates** (calibrated confidence conditioning, which "enable debate to systematically drift to the correct hypothesis"). Both beat vanilla debate and majority vote across six reasoning QA benchmarks. *Boundary:* reasoning-oriented QA, no adversarial injector, per-benchmark deltas not read here.

## 4. Biological quorum decisions as a robustness template

**4.1 The template.** Honeybee nest-site choice (Seeley, *Honeybee Democracy*, 2010 [29]) is the best-studied natural best-of-N decision: scouts **independently discover and independently evaluate** candidate cavities, advertise by waggle dance with **intensity proportional to assessed quality**, and the swarm commits on a **quorum** at the site, not on a vote count at the swarm.

**4.2 What makes it robust — the independence/interdependence split.** List, Elsholtz & Seeley (2009) [10], abstract verified: "We consider an intriguing non-human case of independent information pooling combined with communication... We develop a new agent-based model of the bees' decision process and show that its remarkable reliability stems from a **particular interplay of independence and interdependence** between the bees." The structural property: a scout's *assessment* is independent (it flies to the site and judges it itself); only *attention allocation* is interdependent (dancing recruits others to go and look). **No bee adopts a site because other bees say it is good; it adopts because it went and checked.** That is the biological analogue of Smith & Sørensen's unbounded-signal condition (§1.3) and of Bara's independent-evidence-roots condition (§2.4). *Boundary:* agent-based model plus field natural history, not a controlled manipulation of independence.

**4.3 Cross-inhibition is the mechanism that defeats an over-advertised option.** Seeley, Visscher, Schlegel, Hogan, Franks & Marshall (2012, Science) [30]: scouts deliver **stop signals** (head-butts) preferentially to dancers advertising *other* sites; an analytic model shows this cross-inhibition between committed populations is what breaks deadlock and makes the decision reliable, mirroring cross-inhibition between neural populations. Marshall et al. (2009) [31] show the two-population competition can implement the optimal sequential probability ratio test. Reina, Marshall, Trianni & Bose (2017, Phys. Rev. E) [32] generalise to N options and give the exact control parameter: with recruitment gamma_i = k v_i, abandonment alpha_i = k/v_i, self-recruitment rho_i = h v_i and cross-inhibition beta_ij = h v_i, **r = h/k** governs deadlock-breaking and best-of-N accuracy; symmetric bifurcations are r1 = 1/v^2 - 2 + N + 2*sqrt(2N-3)/v and r2 = (N-3)N + 2 + 1/v^2 + ((N-1)/v)*sqrt(4 + v^2(N-2)^2). Critically for us: **low r gives a unique attractor for the best option but possibly below quorum; high r creates attractors for inferior options** — and for quality ratio kappa = 0.97 with N = 3 **no r gives a unique best-option attractor**. The r needed to hold >=75% of the population on the best option rises roughly linearly with N. *Boundary:* [32] is deterministic, infinite-population mean-field, inferior options assumed equal, cross-inhibition depends only on the sender's option quality; no stochastic finite-N analysis. [30] was read at abstract depth in the team library. **Why this is the right template for the proposal:** an over-advertised decoy listing is exactly an option with inflated *apparent* quality v. In this model an injector raises v_decoy, which raises both its recruitment *and* the stop signals it emits — but a dissenting sub-population holding a disjoint assessment of the decoy listing emits stop signals *against* it, and that is a mechanism (not a vote) by which a minority with better evidence can suppress a popular option. That is the structural reason to prefer cross-inhibition over plurality voting as the proposal's aggregation rule.

**4.4 Less communication can be more.** Talamali, Saha, Marshall & Reina (2021, Science Robotics) [33]: robot swarms agreeing on the currently-best option in a *changing* environment adapt **better with fewer communication links** (shorter range or lower density), confirmed in mean-field models, simulation and 50-Kilobot experiments. Valentini, Ferrante, Hamann & Dorigo (2016) [34]: with 100 Kilobots, larger neighbourhoods give faster but **less accurate** decisions (90% consensus reached at t ~ 55-69 for G_max = 25 vs 66-80 for G_max = 5, GLMM 95% CI), and majority rule beats the weighted voter model by 1.89x in speed at equal accuracy — but the voter model is *more* accurate when the better option starts below 50% share. Brambilla, Ferrante, Birattari & Dorigo (2013) [35] is the taxonomy these sit in (consensus achievement vs task allocation). *Boundary:* [33] read at abstract depth; [34] is binary discrimination with quality ratio 0.5 and rarely reached 100% consensus; [35] is a 2013 review predating learning-based controllers.

**4.5 Uninformed agents are protective, not exploitable.** Couzin et al. (2011, Science) [36]: a strongly opinionated minority dictates group choice in model and fish experiments, but **adding uninformed individuals spontaneously restores control to the numerical majority**. *Boundary:* abstract-depth read; preference strengths were induced by training; the team library notes the effect reverses under highly nonlinear interactions (Hartnett et al. 2016). Relevant caution for the proposal: "add naive agents" is a *conditional* defence, not a general one.

## 5. Opinion-dynamics models usable as a simulation backbone

| Model | Rule | What it predicts about a stubborn minority / an external field |
|---|---|---|
| **DeGroot (1974)** [37] | x(t+1) = W x(t), W row-stochastic | Consensus = weighted average of initial opinions (weights = stationary distribution of W). A zealot row with w_ii = 1 makes that agent an absorbing state; if the graph is connected, **one zealot drags the whole population to its value**. Becker [8] uses the heterogeneous-self-weight version (alpha_i) as the mechanism by which accuracy can improve. |
| **Hegselmann-Krause (2002)** [38] | synchronous average over all j with \|x_i - x_j\| <= eps | Order is preserved; once two agents split by > eps they stay split forever. n = 625 simulations: eps = 0.01 -> 38 clusters, eps = 0.15 -> two camps, eps = 0.25 -> consensus, abrupt switch near eps = 0.25. **Asymmetric confidence intervals drive the population toward the favoured side** — the cleanest minimal model of an injector as a bias in *who you are willing to listen to*. |
| **Deffuant-Weisbuch (2000)** [39] | pairwise, random encounters, same threshold | "High thresholds yield convergence of opinions towards an average opinion, whereas low thresholds result in several opinion clusters" (abstract, verified). Asynchronous counterpart of HK; survey: Bernardo et al. (2024) [40]. |
| **Voter model with zealots** | copy a random neighbour; zealots never update | Mobilia (2003, PRL) [41]: a **single zealot** changes the magnetisation of an infinite group in d <= 2. Mobilia, Petersen & Redner (2007) [42]: with zealots of both opinions the population reaches a **steady state with a non-trivial opinion distribution instead of consensus** — the system never freezes. |
| **Granovetter thresholds (1978)** [43] | act if the acted fraction >= personal threshold | Outcome is determined by the *distribution* of thresholds, not the mean; a single low-threshold instigator can or cannot start a bandwagon depending on whether the threshold distribution has gaps. The origin of "the equilibrium is set by the shape of the tail." |
| **Watts cascades on networks (2002)** [44] | threshold rule on a sparse random graph | Two regimes of susceptibility to **global cascades**: connectivity-limited (power-law cascade-size distribution) and stability-limited (bimodal, "a more extreme kind" of cascade). A small shock produces a large but **rare** cascade — the key prediction being *variance*, so a swarm experiment must report the full cascade-size distribution, not a mean. |
| **Committed-minority tipping** | zealots in a 2-word naming game | Xie et al. (2011, PRE) [45], verified: beyond **p_c ~ 10%** committed agents the time for the whole population to adopt the committed opinion collapses from T_c ~ exp(alpha(p)N) to T_c ~ ln N; qualitatively similar on Erdos-Renyi and scale-free graphs. Centola, Becker, Brackbill & Baronchelli (2018, Science) [46], verified: in human experiments "when the number of confederates was roughly **25%** of the group, the opinion of the majority could be tipped". |
| **Galam sociophysics** | local discussion groups + tie-breaking prejudice + inflexibles | Galam (2002) [47]: minority opinion spreading in random geometry. Galam & Jacobs (2007) [48]: **inflexible minorities break democratic opinion dynamics** — a small inflexible fraction shifts the deterministic attractor. Galam (2025) [49] maps the (p0, k, x) landscape and finds "in most of the landscape the final outcome is predetermined, with a single-attractor dynamics, independent of the initial support". |

**5.1 The LLM-calibrated versions (this is what makes the backbone defensible).**
- De Marzo, Castellano & Garcia (2024, Sci. Adv.) [50]: LLM binary opinion dynamics are, to good approximation, **Glauber dynamics of the mean-field Ising model**: P(adopt \| m) = [tanh(beta m) + 1]/2, with beta the "majority force". beta decreases with N, giving each model a critical group size N_c (~30 Llama-3-70B, ~80 GPT-4o, ~1000 GPT-4-Turbo, >1000 Claude-3.5-Sonnet), and log N_c is roughly linear in MMLU.
- Okawa (2026, ICML) [51]: debate as an Ising/Potts system, m(t+1) = tanh[beta(J m(t) + h)] with **h the injector's field and J the conformity strength**; transition to biased consensus when beta*J > 1 (mean-field). **Measured: mixing agent types smooths the transition and suppresses biased consensus**; GPT-4.1 variants fit a *negative* conformity lambda_i, DeepSeek V3 the strongest positive.
- El et al. (2026) [52]: ~10,000 communities of N = 32 persona-conditioned agents on signed networks; a three-coupling kinetic-Ising model predicts held-out trajectories at 75-86% one-step / 61-77% rollout balanced accuracy, generalises to unseen graph families (85.0-97.8% one-step), and places every fitted community **below its critical temperature**. Fitted concordant coupling 0.99-3.03 vs discordant <= 0.73, so attraction dominates.
- Flint, Aiello, Pastor-Satorras & Baronchelli (2026, PNAS) [53]: the **cached-policy trick** — extract the model's token-level policy once for every memory state, then simulate N ~ 10^4 agents cheaply. Collective preference for the "strong" option rises monotonically with N until deterministic above a model-specific N_c (N = 2 to ~10^4). Three misalignment types: amplification, induction from neutrality, and **reversal**.
- Tanaka (2026) [54]: Quantized Simplex Gossip — agents learn from each other's *sampled outputs*, so one agent's arbitrary sample becomes the next agent's evidence ("memetic drift"); predicts a **drift-dominated regime where the outcome is a lottery** and a **selection regime where weak biases decide**, with scaling laws in population size, bandwidth, adaptation rate and internal uncertainty.
- Pavlova et al. (2026) "Flag Game" [55]: the closest existing testbed to our scenario — a hidden ground truth, **assigned and replayable private evidence per agent** (some crops decisive, some ambiguous), three protocols (pairwise / broadcast / manager). Measured (GPT-4o pairwise, 40 seeds per N): wrong consensus 26-34% at N = 4 falling to 0% at N >= 32, while **polarisation rises from 13-24% to 57-60% at N = 128**; accuracy peaks at intermediate N = 16. Social-awareness prompting raises terminal truth mass 0.54 -> 0.81 in broadcast but has an **interior optimum at alpha = 0.75 in pairwise (more uptake amplifies wrong speakers)**. Mixed GPT-4o/GPT-5.4 teams beat homogeneous ones *and fail differently*. One model (Claude Haiku 4.5) abandons a uniquely-identifying private crop as social memory accumulates; GPT-4o and GPT-5.4 hold firm. *Boundary:* [50]-[55] are all 2024-2026, mostly N <= 32 for live-LLM runs (only [53] reaches 10^4, via the cached policy), binary or small option sets, and [51]/[52]/[54] fit their own parameters from the same traces they predict. The Ising mapping is a *fitted phenomenology*, not a derivation.

## 6. LLM multi-agent evidence

**6.1 Debate's gains come from aggregation, not from the exchange.** Du, Li, Torralba, Tenenbaum & Mordatch (2023) [56] launched multi-agent debate (MAD). Choi, Zhu & Li (2025, NeurIPS) [57] decompose it: **majority voting accounts for most of the improvement**, and debate induces a **martingale over beliefs**, so unbiased interaction cannot improve expected correctness. The team library's full read records seven-benchmark averages: single agent 72.05%, majority voting 76.91%, **decentralised debate 73.77%, centralised debate 65.51%** (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct); 62.03 / 72.42 / 69.29 / 60.94% (Llama3.1-8B). **Debate on top of voting costs 3.1pp on both**, degradation is monotone in rounds (Arithmetics 0.99 at voting -> 0.67 at five rounds), and the fixes that work pull debate *back toward the majority* (Conformist +2.9pp, Follower +3.0pp, oracle +18.3pp). [28] is the constructive counterpart (diversity-aware init + calibrated confidence beats both). *Boundary:* 7-8B models plus one 32B; QA-style benchmarks; no adversarial injector.

**6.2 Conformity in LLM populations is measured and large.** Weng, Chen & Wang (2025, ICLR) [58] (BenchForm): conformity exists, **grows with majority size and interaction duration**; personas and reflection reduce it. Cho, Guntuku & Ungar (2025) [59]: the **gap between an agent's self-confidence and its perceived confidence in peers predicts conformity**, and the *presentation format* of peer information modulates herding. Han et al. (2026) [60], N = 7, confidence-normalised DeGroot pooling with tunable self-weight alpha: centralised structures are capped by the hub's competence and hubs favour peers whose reasoning resembles their own; distributed structures are more robust, higher connectivity converges faster and raises mean accuracy **but increases high-confidence wrong cascades**; **larger alpha (more self-reliance) generally improves accuracy**; mixed GPT-3.5/GPT-4o groups converge more slowly and final accuracy follows the *majority model class*. Choi et al. (2025, ACL Findings) [61]: 2,500+ debates on five contentious topics — significant conformity toward the numerically dominant group, and agents labelled "more intelligent" exert more influence. YS (2026) [62]: public conformity **64-94%** while privately opposing; a single norm entrepreneur triggers a cascade **< 26% of the time for 7 of 8 models** (GPT-4o 48%, one model 0%); removing the false-consensus framing lowers conformity only to 52-92%. De Marzo, Bellina, Castellano, Priesemann & Garcia (2026) [63]: two measured forces per model (majority-following, intrinsic bias); tipping points at which a small adversarial fraction shifts population alignment **irreversibly, persisting after the adversaries stop**. Magistrali & Shani (2026) [64] report the opposite on a security-triage task: a response function calibrated in **benign** operation forecasts the shift before any attack; reasoning visibility neutralises weak attacks and only delays strong ones; and **capture reverses once the committed agents leave**. *Boundary:* [58]-[62] are mostly subject-vs-confederate or small-N designs, several read at abstract depth. [63] vs [64] **disagree on reversibility** — probably a memory difference (payoff-accumulating vs current-decision-only). The proposal should treat reversibility as an open question and measure it.

**6.3 Persuasion as the attack, measured.** Kraidia, Qaddara, Almutairi, Alzaben & Belhouari (2026, Sci. Reports) [65]: one adversarial agent inside an ordinary debate, **no prompt injection**, four-stage pipeline (three independent reasoning chains for the wrong answer, counterarguments, fusion, confidence polish). Measured accuracy loss vs clean baseline: **TruthfulQA 21.86% +/- 0.19, MMLU 18.70% +/- 0.14, MedMCQA 23.90% +/- 0.14, SCALR 20.40% +/- 0.14**. Susceptibility is model-dependent (GPT-3.5-Turbo loses ~20-30 points, agreement with adversary rises +0.40 to +0.68; GPT-4o ~5 points, agreement near zero). **Damage accumulates over rounds** (~0.5 at round 1 to <0.2 by round 3 vs a flat 55-60% baseline) and **group size does not rescue the system** (up to six agents raises the baseline but not the resistance). Varying retrieved passages (k = 0, 3, 5, 10) keeps degradation in the 18.10-24.30% band, which the authors read as credibility reinforcement rather than retrieval preprocessing. A plain adversary that merely asserts the wrong answer has limited impact — **the structured persuasion is what does the damage.** Prompt-based warnings as a defence are "highly variable". The authors explicitly flag their cooperative agents' assumption of **uniform consumption of peer outputs with no trust weighting or source-aware filtering** as the untested lever. *Boundary:* 8-14B open models plus two OpenAI models, limited repetitions, an academic debate protocol; the abstract's headline "10-40%" range is wider than the per-dataset table (18-24%) — quote the table.

**6.4 Do LLM agents herd more or less than humans?** The honest answer is **differently, and with more variance across models than between species.**
- *More, on the microscopic response:* measured conformity reaches p_wrong near 1 for some models [27]; public conformity 64-94% [62] vs the classic Asch rates of roughly a third of trials.
- *Comparable, on saturation shape:* some models saturate near N = 3-4 confederates "as in human Asch experiments" [27].
- *Wildly model-dependent, on tipping:* critical mass to flip an established convention ranges **2% (Llama-3-70B-Instruct) to 67% (Llama-2-70b-Chat)** [66], against ~25% in human experiments [46] and 10-40% in theory [45].
- *Less, on cascade initiation:* a single dissenting norm entrepreneur succeeds < 26% of the time for 7 of 8 models [62] — LLM populations may *overstate* norm stability, the opposite failure to [66]'s low critical mass.
- *Beyond human scale on coordination:* critical group size N_c up to >1000 vs the human Dunbar-scale ~200 [50].

**6.5 Does diversity of base model or prompt restore independence?** Partially for model diversity, **barely for prompt diversity**, and the difference is measured.
- Model diversity: cross-model code ensembles realise 0.43-0.44 of the independence gain vs < 0.3 same-model [12]; 2 diverse agents >= 16 homogeneous [18]; mixing agent types smooths the bias transition and suppresses biased consensus [51]; mixed-model Flag Game teams beat homogeneous ones and fail differently [55]; the non-interacting 12-model crowd matches a human crowd [17].
- But not unconditionally: mixed GPT-3.5/GPT-4o groups converge *more slowly* and final accuracy **follows the majority model class** [60] — model diversity without weighting can import the weaker model's errors.
- Prompt / persona / rationale diversity: Bara's 2x2 [13] is decisive — rationale-wording change moves a dedup defence's cluster count by **+1.425** while a **fourfold change in true evidence ancestry moves it by +0.040**. Surface variation is cheap and legible; evidence independence is neither. Correlated extraction error across agents sharing a base model is gamma_cal = 0.719.

**6.6 Methodological boundary that constrains every number above.** Zhou et al. (2025) "PIMMUR" [67]: pre-registered audit of 576 studies in 350 papers of LLM social simulation. Frontier LLMs correctly identified the underlying social experiment in **65.2%** of cases, and **50.6%** of prompts imposed constraints that pre-determined the outcome (both upper bounds, from incomplete reporting). Re-running five representative experiments with PIMMUR controls enforced, **reported collective phenomena often vanish or reverse.** Cemri et al. (2025) [68] supply the failure vocabulary (MAST: 14 modes, kappa = 0.88 human agreement; 41-86.7% failure rates across 7 frameworks). Kim et al. (2025/2026) [69] supply the coordination cost: once single-agent accuracy exceeds ~45% adding agents has negative returns (beta = -0.236, p = 0.004); trace-level error amplification 1.0 (single), 4.4 (centralised), 7.8 (decentralised), 17.2 (independent); turns T = 2.72(n + 0.5)^1.724 (R^2 = 0.974). *Boundary:* [67] is the reason our experiment must pre-register an **unawareness check** (ask the agents what experiment they think they are in) and a **minimal-control audit** (no prompt that names the right answer, the attack, or the hypothesis). Without those, a positive dissent result is not evidence.

## Design implications for the proposal

### (a) Which dynamics model(s) as the simulation backbone, and why

**Use a three-layer stack, not one model.** Each layer is there because a single-layer choice would beg the question the proposal is asking.

1. **Evidence layer — bounded-signal sequential/iterated observational learning (BHW [1] + Smith & Sørensen Theorem 3 [3]).** The injector acts here: it changes the *distribution and boundedness* of the private signal each agent draws from the source population. This layer is what makes the proposal's thesis falsifiable rather than definitional: §1.3 says the wrong-commit probability is positive **iff** private beliefs are bounded, so the experiment can switch poisoning on and off by changing only the evidence channel's informativeness (ranked-list-only vs ranked-list-plus-direct-probe) and predict the sign in advance.
2. **Decision layer — value-sensitive cross-inhibition best-of-N (Reina et al. [32], from Seeley et al. [30] and Marshall et al. [31]), with the quorum/Hill parameterisation of Sumpter & Pratt [4] as the simpler alternative arm.** Reasons: it is the only candidate in the literature with (i) a closed-form control parameter (r = h/k) whose bifurcations in N and quality-ratio kappa are published, so our simulation has analytic predictions to be wrong against; (ii) an explicit **per-option apparent-quality v_i**, which is exactly the variable an over-advertising injector manipulates; and (iii) a built-in mechanism — stop signals — by which a minority can **suppress** an over-advertised option instead of merely abstaining from it. Plurality voting and the voter model have no such mechanism and would make hypothesis 2 nearly untestable.
3. **Agent-response layer — an empirically fitted conformity response, not an assumed one.** Fit P(adopt | m) = [tanh(beta m) + 1]/2 per model as in De Marzo et al. [50], with the injector entering as the external field h in Okawa's m(t+1) = tanh[beta(J m + h)] [51]. Extract the policy **once** with Flint et al.'s cached-policy trick [53] so N can reach 10^3-10^4 on a laptop, and validate the cached policy against live generation on a subsample as [53] does. *Add Tanaka's QSG [54] as a second theoretical lens* for the regime question — "is the outcome a lottery (drift) or is the injector's weak bias being amplified (selection)?" is precisely the question the proposal needs to answer, and QSG is the only model that frames it that way.

**Secondary models, with a stated job each.** Hegselmann-Krause [38] with *asymmetric* confidence intervals is the cheapest minimal model of "the injector shifts who you are willing to listen to" and should appear as an analytic appendix, not the main engine. Watts [44] is the reason to report the **full cascade-size distribution** rather than means. Xie et al. [45] and Centola et al. [46] set the prior for the committed-fraction sweep. Becker et al. [8] is the reason topology is a factor and not a fixed choice. Pavlova et al. [55] is the closest existing *testbed* and should be the proposal's named comparison, including its per-agent replayable-evidence design, which our experiment should copy.

**Explicitly not the backbone:** plain multi-agent debate [56]. §6.1 shows it is a martingale and loses to majority voting, so building on it would confound "does dissent help" with "does debate help", and the latter already has a negative answer.

### (b) Minimal set of swarm parameters

| Communication topology | complete / d-regular ring-lattice (d = 2, 4, 8) / star hub / none | 5 levels | [8] shows the *sign* of the influence effect flips between equal-degree and hub; [33]/[34] show fewer links adapt better; [70] predicts a critical degree d_c |

Hold constant and report: total token budget per arm (**token-matched**, because [69] shows matched budgets thin each agent's reasoning and [57]/[28] show cost-equal comparisons reverse conclusions), temperature, and the per-agent private-evidence assignment (replayable, as in [55]).

### (c) How to operationalize "dissent" so it is genuine evidence-bearing disagreement

**Definition.** A dissent act is **admissible** only if it satisfies all three, machine-checkably:
1. **Targeted** — it names the specific claim it contradicts (a claim id, not "I disagree").
2. **Rooted** — it attaches a **provenance token for an evidence root not already in the majority's root set**. Root identity is assigned by the *simulator* (which knows ground-truth ancestry), not inferred from text.
3. **Verifiable** — a cheap deterministic verifier confirms the cited root exists and contains the cited claim. A dissent that fails the verifier is recorded as a failed dissent, not silently dropped.

**Score it by information, not by disagreement.** Bara's criterion [13]: an admissible dissent must have I(Theta; Z | R) > 0 — it must move the posterior given what is already admitted. A dissent that is a rationale-level restatement of an existing root is an **epistemic Sybil** and must be down-weighted to its effective-sample-size contribution m/[1 + rho(m-1)], not counted as a vote. **Do not use embedding deduplication to detect this:** [13] measured that it responds to wording (+1.425 clusters) far more than to true ancestry (+0.040), with a best achievable worst-case false-merge/false-split rate of 0.846.

**The four arms (and why each must exist).**
- **A0 — no critic.** Baseline.
- **A1 — generic critic (contrived dissent).** Always disagrees; **same evidence access as the majority**; no new root. This is the arm that isolates "disagreement as noise". The literature's prediction is ~0 or negative ([57]: debate on top of voting costs 3.1pp; [65]: a plain asserting adversary has limited impact but a confident one costs 18-24pp — so a *confident* contrarian is a liability, not a safeguard).
- **A2 — evidence-constrained critic (genuine dissent, same corpus).** Must satisfy (1)-(3) but draws from the same (poisoned) corpus. This arm separates "procedure" from "independence" — and corresponds to Greitemeyer et al.'s finding [24] that devil's advocacy works *conditional on* genuine heterogeneity.
- **A3 — independent-root agent (genuine dissent, disjoint evidence).** Retrieves via a different query, engine, or a **direct probe of the candidate provider** (the unbounded-signal condition of [3] and the honeybee "go and look yourself" property of [10]). Optionally **A4 — cross-inhibition on**: A3 plus the ability to emit stop signals against the over-advertised option [30]/[32], which is the mechanism arm as opposed to the vote arm.

**Charge and measure honestly.** Give every arm the **same token and retrieval budget** (the dissent brief in the team repo flags this: "Give critics the same evidence access and charge their tokens to the budget"). Report **two** outcomes separately, never pooled: *corrections* (cases where the swarm was heading to the poisoned option and dissent flipped it) and *damage* (cases where the swarm was heading to the correct option and dissent flipped it away) — this split is what [57] and the dissent brief both demand, and it is the difference between a real robustness gain and a wash.

**Pre-register the PIMMUR controls [67]:** an unawareness probe (ask a held-out sample of agents to state what experiment they believe they are in; report the recognition rate against [67]'s 65.2% benchmark) and a minimal-control audit (no prompt in any arm names the attack, the decoy listing, or the hypothesis; report the audit).

### (d) What the literature predicts for hypothesis 2 — stated direction, magnitude, and falsifiers

**Stated expected direction.** Evidence-bearing dissent increases robustness; contrived dissent does not; and the effect is **sharply concave in the dissenting fraction**, with most of the gain in the first break of unanimity.

**Quantitative priors to write into the hypothesis.**
- *Unanimity-breaking dominates:* 20% correct confederates "markedly reduce" conformity and 50% "almost removes it" [27]. Expect the marginal return at d = 1/N to exceed the marginal return from d = 0.3 -> 0.5.
- *A very small informative signal should suffice if agents discount uninformative consensus:* BHW Result 3 — a signal **less informative than one individual's private signal** can shatter a long-lasting cascade [1].
- *But LLM agents do not fully discount it:* measured conformity [27][58][59][62] and [55]'s sycophantic-override probe mean the realised effect should be **smaller than BHW's bound**. Predict ordering A3 > A2 > A0 >= A1, not A3 ~ BHW.
- *Independence buys about 40%, not 100%:* cross-model ensembles realise 0.43-0.44 of the theoretical independence gain [12]; coverage recovers 0.263 -> 0.927 only when independent **roots** go 1 -> 16 at fixed agent count [13]. So predict the A3-vs-A0 gain to track **root count**, not agent count or dissenter count.
- *Rounds and population size will not help:* monotone degradation in rounds [57][65]; group size up to six does not rescue an attacked debate [65]; negative returns to agents above ~45% single-agent accuracy [69]. If the proposal's design makes robustness improve with N or R, suspect a confound.
- *Expect the defence to be mechanism-shaped, not vote-shaped:* [32] shows there exist (kappa, N) for which **no value of the interaction parameter r gives a unique best-option attractor** (kappa = 0.97, N = 3). So predict a regime where no amount of dissent helps — and name it in advance as the boundary rather than treating it as a failed experiment.

**Falsifiers (any one of these falsifies H2 as stated).**
1. **No separation from noise.** If A3 (independent-root dissent) does not reduce the wrong-commit rate relative to A0 by more than A1 (contrived dissent) does, with non-overlapping 95% intervals at d = 0.20, H2 is false: the observed benefit is disagreement-as-noise, not evidence.
2. **Budget confound.** If giving A0 the *same extra retrieval and token budget* that A3's dissenters consume closes the gap, then "dissent" is just "more evidence per decision" and H2 as stated (dissent is the active ingredient) is false. This control is mandatory, not optional.
3. **Net-negative damage.** If dissent's damage term (correct -> poisoned flips) exceeds its correction term (poisoned -> correct flips) at any d in the sweep, robustness has fallen; H2 must then be restated with a bounded d.
4. **Independence is an artefact.** If the A3-vs-A2 difference vanishes when the simulator's assigned evidence roots are shuffled (so "disjoint" is relabelled but the text is unchanged), the mechanism is textual novelty rather than evidence independence — consistent with [13]'s dedup result, and fatal to the stated mechanism.
5. **Awareness.** If the unawareness probe shows agents recognise the experiment above [67]'s 65.2% benchmark, or the minimal-control audit finds any arm's prompt pre-determines the outcome, the result is not evidence either way and must be rerun.
