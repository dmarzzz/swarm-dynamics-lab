# B3: downstream questions after repeated handoffs

Prospective, 2026-10-04, written before implementation. Unfunded candidate responding to B2's copied-answer ceiling. B2 is inspected development evidence; none of its outputs are reused as fresh evaluation.

## Question and design

Does a later teammate recover a different, predeclared operational fact from a repeated handoff? Three fresh handoff actors preserve an operational record and its current GO/HOLD decision. Only then does a fresh reader receive a previously hidden question, without the prior decision field. The question asks about a different fact, so copying the old decision cannot answer it. The source-access arm receives original source plus prior handoff at each step, including the reader; the handoff-only arm receives only its predecessor's text after hop one. The source-access reader is the native source-available competence comparator, but also sees a generated handoff, so it is not a standalone singleton baseline. A deterministic full-copy/lookup baseline uses no model calls; direct-source question answerability is checked offline. A separate native singleton would require a prospective extension and additional calls. Source access intentionally has more information and input cost; no equal-information coordination claim.

Use 24 prospectively frozen generated worlds nested in eight authored mechanism families: absent versus failed restore evidence; initial versus latest version; shared versus independent receipt; required passing versus merely performed check; rollback scope; suggested versus actual consent-scope change; recorded permission with conditional scope; scoped versus universal exception. Vary identities, operational labels, evidence state, corrective updates and record position. Each family includes YES/NO/UNKNOWN question labels. Current GO/HOLD and downstream label are balanced independently where possible. All source facts needed to answer are visible; UNKNOWN is explicitly justified by missing evidence. Preserve old cases as offline regressions, not extra independent units.

These are generated worlds from eight shared templates, not 24 independent semantic families or real incidents. All are inspected development material frozen before native outputs; no natural-data holdout claim. Report root-paired and family-stratified outcomes and the two fresh blocks separately. This packet uses three roots per family (24 total), one per answer class. No expansion, replacement or automatic extension is included.

## Endpoints and scoring

Reader returns JSON with answer YES, NO or UNKNOWN and evidence text. Primary correctness and false certainty are deterministic against explicit source labels; abstention, wrong, invalid and missing remain distinct. Prior answers are never given as reader fields. Record each full request/response and exact parent hash. Deterministic scoring is independently checked against record witnesses and answer-changing controls. Manual audit is bounded to at most 32 reader outputs per stage, selected prospectively by hash, stratified across families and error/correct categories after deterministic classification. Review every execution/schema failure separately. This audit does not claim full semantic coverage.

Source-available reader competence is reported per family/block; fewer than 80% correct in a family blocks a downstream-policy interpretation for that family, but never removes its assigned outcomes. This is descriptive competence screening, not a confidence guarantee. No errors are corrected in-chain. Report paired contrasts, all-case denominators, per-family uncertainty and block disagreement. Twenty-four generated roots give 4.17-point resolution per block for the finite selected-world mean; eight shared families remain the broader diversity limit.
