## What the native decisions teach us

Internal reports were not uniformly reliable: one rounded 97.6 to 97, four reports overstated a vendor total by USD1,000, and four treated a 50-day migration as 40 days. In the evidence-gap case, nine deployment initial reports and eight revisions classified unknown location evidence as failure; one even asserted outside-EU location. The lead and final decision preserved UNKNOWN in this case. This contrasts with the D1 scope case, where the lead and large Decider retained the stronger failure claim. The observed paths show both correction and persistence, not proof that peers caused either.

Across the eight new roots, 26 accepted reports had incorrect fields. Primitive values were 4,245/4,288 correct, checks 4,250/4,288, source-ID coverage 4,288/4,288 and cost components 4,226/4,230. Source coverage is not proof of entailment. Every mismatched field and all 23 observed final rationales were inspected. Several 160-character rationales end mid-sentence or with stray punctuation; explanation quality remains limited despite acceptable actions.

The authored cases exercise procurement issues including pricing units, superseding documents, weighted pilots, migration limits and evidence gaps. Most have a single eligible vendor, so observed decision accuracy can hide intermediate mistakes. These nine related development roots do not establish deployment reliability, population vulnerability or a pure effect of agent count. There is no new adverse vendor condition: outside persuasion remains untested in R41. Earlier E1 unsafe purchases occurred with and without advocacy, so they do not establish that outside persuasion caused harm.
