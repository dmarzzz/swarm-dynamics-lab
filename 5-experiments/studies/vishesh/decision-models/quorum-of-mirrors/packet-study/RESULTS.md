# Case-readiness assessment: repetition and distortion with fixed source evidence

**The v2 cases are ready for a bounded synthetic mechanism experiment.** They are not a field benchmark, evidence of model capability, a qualified native instrument or permission to launch. The three previously identified gaps are addressed for this scope: complete multi-report decision packets, a capable text parser and source-counting baseline, and frozen, separately sealed qualification/evaluation cases. [Prospective plan](PLAN.md), [development case explorer](DEVELOPMENT.md), [readiness record](READINESS.json), [current seal](SEAL.json).

## Useful question and full task

Can a solver preserve the correct source-grounded decision when reports are duplicated or distorted? Each packet includes a query, three authenticated source receipts and their reports. The source groups are stipulated independent and equally reliable; correct means the source-majority evidence-MAP decision, not agreement with an unobserved physical state. These synthetic assumptions are explicit in actor input.

Every scenario has four matched conditions: one versus five copies of the focal report, crossed with faithful versus inverted focal claims. The underlying source text never changes. Focal sources can agree or disagree with the source majority, so repetition can help or hurt a report-voting heuristic; neither case is discarded. Evaluation balances both majority directions and focal alignment within all six mechanisms. The distortion treatment tests binary claim reversal, not all forms of misinformation.

The source-reading mechanisms are polarity, time, entity, units, plans versus observations, and corrections. A receipt may contain an old reading, another entity, an unfulfilled plan or an explicitly superseded entry. The solver must interpret the relevant observation before counting sources. This connects the previous isolated claim tests to a scored final decision.

A native failure would justify rejecting that solver as an arbiter of this grammar without deterministic verification. Native robustness would establish only robustness on these finite controlled patterns. It would not show a benefit from a swarm, natural-language generalization or superiority to exact code. There is no conversational peer treatment. Packet length increases with repetition; the treatment cannot distinguish repetition from associated length/attention effects.

## Strong comparator and observed offline results

The source-parser baseline reads the same actor-visible text a future model would receive. It resolves negation, selects the requested time/entity, converts grams/kilograms, distinguishes plans from observations and applies explicit corrections. It then counts the three sources once. It receives no typed gold state. An independent construction calculation supplies labels from the generator's original source values; this is a second implementation by the same operator, not an independent researcher audit.

| Development controller | Correct /48 packets | What it omits |
|---|---:|---|
| Count every parsed report | 30 | Source deduplication and claim verification |
| Deduplicate reports by source, trust their claims | 36 | Checking claims against source text |
| Parse source receipts and count sources once | 48 | Nothing required by this controlled contract |

These comparisons retain all 48 assigned development packets, including ablation successes. They demonstrate interpretable test contrasts and software behavior, not model effects. A perfect deterministic baseline is appropriate for this robustness question; the experiment does not need to invent cases that defeat it to justify a well-defined competence comparison.

Ten test methods pass, including independently authored parser examples, numeric boundary cases, unknown grammar, conflicting observations/corrections, source/claim disagreement, duplicate/missing identities, unknown dependence, ordering, opaque IDs and copy invariance. Automated corpus checks also verify source votes, report fidelity, final labels, target balance, exact treatment counts, source conservation and actor/gold separation for every split. The parser is deliberately conservative outside its published grammar. It is not a general natural-language inference engine.

All four variants remain with their root. Actor event/acquisition identities and illustrative domain names are disjoint across splits. The language grammar is shared deliberately; new domain names are not evidence of domain transfer. These 24 evaluation roots enumerate balanced mechanism/direction/alignment strata with randomized identities, times, ordering and surface alternatives. They are not 24 independent linguistic families or sampled real-world tasks. Statistical population precision cannot be claimed from repeated templates.

Individual v2 qualification/evaluation packets and ablation outcomes were not opened by the operator. Automated QA read all of them: zero critical mismatches, reference decisions correct on 24/24 qualification and 96/96 evaluation packets, and zero cross-split source overlap. Public hashes bind corpora and QA; contents/seeds remain in local custody until an approved evaluation consumes them. This is protection from operator tuning, not independent authorship, cryptographic blindness or a claim that software never examined the holdout. Do not append the operating conversation to future actor input.

**The case-quality review found and fixed a real flaw.** In v1, a generator indexing error gave every qualification scenario a ZERO target, so a constant-answer solver could appear capable. Inspection of generator logic exposed this without reading individual sealed packets. [The v1 seal is retained and explicitly retired](SEAL-v1-retired.json). A prospective amendment preceded repair. V2 qualification now has 12 ONE and 12 ZERO packets (three roots each); evaluation has eight of each per mechanism. Fresh corpora were generated after refreezing. No native calls occurred under either version; v1 is not valid qualification evidence.

| Mechanism isolation | Pass with treatment limit | Fixed sources/query/nonfocal reports across 2x2 contrasts; repetition includes packet-length change |

| Strong comparisons | Pass | Same-input source parser plus report-vote/deduplicated-claim ablations; no privileged gold inputs |
| Realism | Limited, sufficient for synthetic mechanism claim | Multi-report structure and common reading errors; authored fixed grammar, binary inversion, synthetic authentication/independence; no field validity |
| Independence/precision | Limited, explicitly finite | 24 evaluation roots/96 correlated packets; six grammar mechanisms; no population or rare-error guarantee |
| Holdout integrity | Pass for tuning separation | v2 source freeze, counts/hashes and local custody; shared known grammar, automated QA, v1 retired |
| Robustness/failure handling | Pass offline | Order/ID/copy invariance, schema and missing/conflicting-source refusal; native transport/output handling not qualified |
| Reproducibility/costs | Pass for offline cases | Source and corpus hashes, development outputs, local seed custody, no API spend; native request/cost envelope still needs preparation |

There are no unresolved critical **case** defects under the stated controlled scope. A real-world robustness claim would fail realism/generalization checks and require new evidence; the rubric does not average those limits away.
