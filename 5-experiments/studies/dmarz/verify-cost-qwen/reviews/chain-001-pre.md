## Question, design and precision

- Question and decision: can an agent choose checking versus exploration when the optimal action changes with report reliability and the cost of leaving a cell unknown, and does a structured action-consequence table change that choice relative to explicit prose with the same objective, facts and consequences? It tells us whether the PC5 regression (explicit contract: reliable-source optimal choices fell from 28 of 32 to 21 of 32) is a matter of how the information is laid out. A one-step policy assay; it decides nothing about multi-step sensing.
- Primary contrast: per layout, the mean over the twelve cases of expected regret with the table minus expected regret with the prose; 24 paired values. Predicted negative; practical marker 0.03; bound of the design ±0.3208.
- Plausible null or adverse outcomes: no difference (both renderings carry the same numbers and the model reads either equally well); the table worse; both near zero regret everywhere (ceiling) or both near a constant habit (regret 0.15 or 0.17, the comparators). All are valid results. The study is uninformative if qualification fails (the model cannot make even clear-dominance choices) or if nearly every S1 cell sits at regret 0 in both representations; the second is reported as a ceiling, not repaired.
- Units: 24 layouts (3000 to 3023), each with 12 cases × 2 representations = 24 dependent calls. The twelve cases are authored. Calls are not samples. No power claim; the interval is descriptive.
- Sample: S1 576 = 24 × 12 × 2 (the program's count). Qualification 24 = 12 fixtures × 2 representations on layouts 2800 to 2811. Engineering layouts 2600 to 2603 (S0 only). Second qualification set, layouts 2900 to 2911, frozen now for the one permitted repair. Holdout 10000 to 19999 unopened.
- What the builder has already seen of S1 layouts: the S1 requests were generated offline for the manifest, the selftest and the rehearsal, where a stub answered them by the analytic rule or a constant rule. No model has read any request of this study.
- Manipulation fidelity, checked in code by S0 and the selftest for every one of the 744 requests: both actions legal; the two representations parse to the same six consequence records, re-render to the same text, and hold the same numbers with multiplicity; the text outside the consequence block is byte-identical across representations; within a layout, masking the numbers leaves one text for all twelve cases, and the numbers differ only at the four positions holding e (twice), 1 − e and U; no request contains a truth value for the two open cells, a seed, an expected loss, a recommendation or an evaluator word. Hand-made mutants (a table with an extra "expected cost" row, a prose block missing one probability, a changed number, a recommending sentence, a case-dependent phrase) are caught by the checker in the selftest.
- Independent recomputation of the optimum (selftest): for each of the 744 requests the expected cost of each action is recomputed as the sum of probability × cost over the records parsed from the rendered text, without the scorer; the cheaper action equals the scorer's optimal action, the gap equals the scorer's margin, and checking is cheaper exactly when e > U.
- Controls: offline comparators always-check (mean regret 0.1500), always-explore (0.1708), always-first and always-second (0.1604 each; listing order balanced 12/12 over layouts, 6 per order × label combination), analytic policy (0). Per case the optimal action and both comparator regrets are in README "Offline evidence". The optimum is `check` in six cases and `explore` in six.
- Qualification (program thresholds, unchanged): 24 valid of 24 and at least 11 of 12 optimal per representation, over P0's row and Q0's 23. Fixtures: expected-loss margin at least 0.60, both actions legal, six check-optimal and six explore-optimal, listing order balanced within each six, none of them a main-grid case. Every constant policy scores exactly 6 of 12 per representation and fails. Misclassification (lessons item 7): a solver that is right 95% of the time on such choices passes with probability about 0.78 (0.88 per representation); at 99%, about 0.99. A failed gate is read first: `failures.json` holds the rendered request and the returned answer of every miss.
- Evaluator and truth separation: the scorer uses the stated e and U, not the realized draw; realized scripted loss is secondary. Truth, `truth_draw` and scores never enter a request.
- Missingness: a unit without a valid answer stays in its cell's denominator with regret bounded in [0, |e − U|]; the primary is reported as bounds over all 24 layouts plus the complete-case estimate with its denominator. Nothing is dropped, imputed or re-run.
- Timing semantics: one independent stateless call per assignment; no rounds, no memory.

## What differs from PC5's contract (diff of `src/sim.py` against `pc5/src/contract.py` at `61307ac1`)

| PC5 `contract.py` | This study's `sim.py` |
|---|---|
| `CELLS`: 36 names `row,column` | identical |
| `LABELS = {LAND, WATER, UNKNOWN}` | `LABELS = (LAND, WATER)`; UNKNOWN is an outcome of the completion rule, not a terrain label |
| `rng(seed, stream)`: SHA-256 of `seed/stream` | SHA-256 of `verify-cost-qwen/seed/stream`: same generator, namespaced, so no layout repeats a PC5 root |
| `development_world` guard (seeds 1600 to 1607) | `layout(seed)` accepts integer seeds 0 to 9999; splits are in `design.yaml` |
| `_world`: `roles` shuffle gives report and unknown cell; `terrain/<cell>` gives truth; `report-calibration` gives `truth_draw` | identical streams and use |
| report label LAND when the seed is even | LAND when `(seed // 2)` is even |
| legal order drawn by a seeded shuffle (`criterion-order`) | report cell first when the seed is even: balanced by construction (with the label rule: 6 layouts per order × label combination) |
| `packet(w, reliability, objective, actor, qualification)`: task text (legacy or explicit), observation list with ids and epochs, and in qualification a single legal cell | `facts(w, e, U)` (same content: cells, both legal cells always, one slot, 34 measurements, the report with its error probability, the cell without evidence, the costs) and `consequences(w, e, U)` (six outcome records); no single-legal-cell mode; the texts are rendered in `study.py` |
| `score(w, reliability, choice)`: risk = 1 − reliability; optimal is the unknown cell when risk < 0.25, else the report cell; expected loss 0.25 (report), risk (unknown), risk + 0.25 (none); regret against min(0.25, risk); `false = truth_draw >= reliability` | `score(w, e, U, choice)`: the same formulas with U for 0.25 and e for risk; a tie e = U is rejected instead of resolved; adds `margin` and `first_listed`; action names `check` and `explore` |
| `valid_map`, `majority`, `error`, `POLICIES` | dropped (PC5's one-step study did not use them) |

A selftest reproduces PC5's two conditions with the generalized scorer (e 0.20 and 0.80 at U 0.25: optimal actions explore and check, regrets of the wrong action 0.05 and 0.55, no-inspection loss e + U) and checks the SHA-256 of the three PC5 source files recorded in `design.yaml`. PC5's `design.py` (schedule, qualified, grade, contrasts) became `study.assignments`, `study.qualification`, `study.evaluate` and `analyze.py`; its `engine.py` became `worker.py` on the reference adapter.

## Frozen execution and collection

- Model settings: `qwen/qwen3.7-flash` via OpenRouter chat completions; body exactly `model`, `provider {only: [alibaba], allow_fallbacks: false, require_parameters: true}`, `reasoning {enabled: false}`, `max_tokens: 1000`, `response_format {type: json_object}`, `messages` (system, user). No other model, no fallback, no temperature or other key. Reasoning is disabled; `effort: low` in READY.yaml and design.yaml is only the launcher's required field. Adapter: the pipeline's reference adapter at main `639e9501`, copied unchanged (SHA-256 `2e98d517…`, asserted by a selftest), with its 32 tests in this selftest.
- Answer: `{"inspect": "<row>,<column>"}`; local validation, strict (exact key, string, one of the two legal cells; duplicate keys rejected). Invalid JSON or an invalid answer is a failed call. No repair call, no answer retry.
- Call counts and hard caps (`budget.max_calls`, enforced by the ledger per batch family): S0 0, P0 1, Q0 23, S1 576; 600 for this attempt. The ledger's study cap (`max_attempted_calls`) is 624 so that the one permitted repair attempt (`p0-002`, `q0-002`: 24 calls) fits in the same ledger; this attempt cannot use those 24, because its own per-stage caps are 1, 23 and 576.
- Gates and what happens at each failed gate:

| Stage | Assignments | Gate | On failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| S0 | 144 scripted rows (96 engineering, 24 + 24 qualification) | every row valid; analytic policy passes both qualification sets; 0 invariant violations (design, every request of every stage, known-answer scorer checks) | chain stops, exit 3; nothing paid has happened |
| P0 | 1 (`qa-2800-e90-u05-prose`) | response parses; model slug and named provider match; usage reported; finish reason `stop`; no reasoning tokens; valid answer | chain stops after one call; its summary keeps the raw response metadata and, on an HTTP failure, status and body |

| before S1 | — | 576 × Q0's mean cost per call ≤ remaining cap; P0's tokens per content byte × 1,891 bytes ≤ 8,000 tokens | chain stops with `projection_exceeds_cap` or `input_ceiling_projection`; no S1 call |
| S1 | 576 | none of its own; ends `done` with at most 6 failed calls and no stop | `failed_units_over_limit`, `integrity_failure` or `provider_credit_balance_low` (resumable) |

- Failure handling, one line per part: (1) adopted: every failed request keeps HTTP status, the first 2,000 characters of the body and the request id; never a header or the key. (2) not applicable: this route has no token-counting endpoint; the reservation is a byte bound. (3) adopted: `max_failed` = 6 (the larger of 3 and 1% of 576); S1 continues past failed calls up to that limit; integrity failures stop at once; S0, P0, Q0 strict. (4) adopted: billing-outage schedule 60 s between re-sends for up to 1,200 s, then `provider_credit_balance_low`; `python src/chain.py resume` queues `s1-001-r1` with exactly the not-started units; the reservation of an unanswered call is voided, so standing S1 reservations never exceed 576.
- Transport retry: at most 2 re-sends per call, only HTTP 429, 502, 503, 529 (a 429 that names a limit or billing goes to the billing rule instead), backoff 2 s then 6 s, `retry-after` up to 20 s, inside the 120 s request budget; `max_transport_attempts` 784 (624 calls + 160: two full outages of 21 attempts each and 118 retries).
- Input ceiling: the largest request body is 2,221 bytes (1,891 bytes of message content); `max_input_bytes` is 6,000. A token is at least one byte, so no request can exceed 6,000 tokens; at the planning assumption of 2.5 characters per token the largest request is about 890 tokens, against the 8,000 ceiling. P0 measures the real ratio and the chain applies it before S1.
- Cost, with arithmetic at the frozen snapshot prices (USD 0.03 and 0.13 per million input and output tokens):
  - Expected per call: about 500 to 900 input tokens (1,891 bytes at 2.5 to 4 characters per token, plus chat overhead) and about 10 output tokens: 15 to 27 + 1.3 = about 17 to 29 millionths of a dollar. Whole chain, 600 calls: **about USD 0.010 to 0.017; say USD 0.01 to 0.03**.
  - Reservation per call: 10 × (2,221 bytes × 0.03 + 1,000 tokens × 0.13) = 10 × 196.6 = 1,966 millionths = USD 0.00197. That is about 70 to 115 times the expected cost, so a provider-reported cost several times the snapshot does not trip `reservation_bound_breached`. At most 4 reservations are open at once: USD 0.0079.
  - Worst realistic: every one of 600 calls settled at its full reservation: USD 1.18. Worst possible under the caps: 624 × 0.00197 = USD 1.23. Both are under the cap.
  - Dollar cap `aggregate_usd`: USD 2 (settled cost plus open reservations), the program's per-study cap. Dollars are not the gate; the call caps are, and actual calls, tokens and dollars are reported to the hub.
- Wall time: calls are short (a few seconds). Expected S1: 576 calls ÷ 4 in flight × 2 to 6 s = 5 to 15 minutes; whole chain about 6 to 16 minutes. Timeouts in the hashed design: request 120 s; stage 5,400 s (576 ÷ 4 × 29 s = 4,176 s plus one full billing outage of 1,200 s = 5,376 s); chain 7,200 s (S0 under 10 s, P0 at most 120 s, Q0 at most 23 ÷ 4 × 120 = 690 s, S1 5,400 s: 6,220 s).
- Collected per unit (`episodes.jsonl.gz`): id, layout, e, U, representation, legal cells, request hash and size, status, answer, evaluation (action, optimal, expected loss and regret, realized loss), accounting (attempts, request bytes, reservation, tokens, computed and provider-reported cost, latency, finish reason, response model, provider and id; on failure HTTP status, body, request id, returned text). Also `requests.jsonl.gz` (exact system and user text), `assignments.jsonl.gz`, `summary.json`, `analysis.json`, `failures.json` (the failure report), frames and replay.
- Stop rules: no answer retries, no replacement runs, no change of sample, thresholds or cases after outcomes are seen; a batch name is never reused; a failed qualification allows one repair attempt on the disjoint set b after reading the failing answers, and a failed repeat ends the line; null and adverse results complete the study; S2 stays disabled; all task ids are below 10000.
- Credential: alias `SWARM_OPENROUTER_API_KEY` in the chain's environment only. No secret, server address or hub address is in the repository.
- Allocation: one server for this line, claim `dmarz-verify-cost-qwen`, taken by the operator at launch.
