## Scientific results and interpretation

The predeclared primary comparison was renewal minus reputation specialist accuracy at sixteen controller identities, sleeper attack, round eight. Observed accuracy was **20.8% renewal versus 12.5% reputation**, a **+8.3 percentage-point** paired effect with descriptive 95% interval **−2.8 to +19.4 points**, across 24 paired worlds. The point estimate is below the +10-point useful-effect marker; the interval includes zero and useful positive effects. This neither establishes the proposed improvement nor proves the true effect is below ten points.

Equal-cost random auditing achieved **26.4%**. Renewal minus random was **−5.6 points**, interval **−16.7 to +5.6**. Renewal is not established as superior to random; selecting the best observed mean is not evidence of a definitive ranking. The primary adverse/inconclusive result is valid and will not be rerun or tuned away.

Admission already lost most scarce information. At the main endpoint, honest rare-report retention/available true specialist values were 30.6% for random, 25.0% for renewal and 23.6% for reputation. Harmful admitted-report shares were respectively 12.2%, 15.3% and 30.9%; wrong non-null specialist outputs were 36.1%, 44.4% and 62.5%. Retention and output accuracy must therefore be reported alongside attack influence; lowering harmful admission alone does not recover missing knowledge.

Matched clean conditions reached 81.9%/84.7%/83.3% accuracy for random/renewal/reputation. The respective available-truth shares were 81.9%/84.7%/86.1%, so admission loss also constrains the noattack control; these clean scientific cells are not failed copies of Q0's full clean packets. Relative to clean, sleeper accuracy fell 55.6 points for random (interval −68.1 to −43.1), 63.9 for renewal (−73.6 to −54.2), and 70.8 for reputation (−81.9 to −59.7). Relapse remained difficult: 15.3% random, 13.9% renewal and 9.7% reputation at round eight with sixteen identities.

Fixed-resource identity splitting produced mixed, uncertain effects. For sixteen minus one identities at sleeper round eight, accuracy changed −1.4 points for random (−16.7 to +13.9), +8.3 for reputation (−1.4 to +18.1), and −9.7 for renewal (−26.4 to +6.9). The prespecified secondary policy-by-identity interaction was −18.1 points (−36.1 to 0.0). These exploratory, unadjusted intervals do not support a universal identity-amplification claim. All controller message budgets stayed sixteen per round and controller model computation stayed zero; identity age composition and reputation concentration intentionally varied.
