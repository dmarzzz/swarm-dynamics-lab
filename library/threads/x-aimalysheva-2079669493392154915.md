day 2/7, and this is the paper that takes yesterday's mergeability result and makes it genuinely strange: task arithmetic (@gabriel_ilharco et al, https://t.co/phDNmEPPjS) shows fine-tuning deltas behave like actual vectors: add the "task A" delta to a base model and you get the skill, subtract it and the skill goes away, and when four tasks form an analogy (A is to B as C is to D), you can build the fourth skill from the other three's vectors alone

connecting this to yesterday isn't just my inference, the paper does it too: their limitations section says every experiment here assumes a shared init, and cites Git Re-Basin by name as the direction for relaxing that 

so same open question as yesterday, from the other side of the literature, and one real clue toward answering it: composability degrades faster at higher learning rates than individual fine-tuning does, so the optimizer is at least part of the answer (still open whether it's the whole answer, or whether the base model and the task interact with it too)

## Relevance to us

Task vectors are the weight-space form of fork and merge: a sub-agent's fine-tune is a delta that gets added
back into the parent, so a corrupted delta merges as easily as a useful one, and negation is a candidate
removal tool. See [[ilharco-2023-editing]] and [[wortsman-2022-model]]. The reported learning-rate effect hints
that how a sub-agent was trained changes how cleanly it can be merged (as she reports it; not checked against the paper).
