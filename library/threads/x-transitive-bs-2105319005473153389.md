## Summary

Launch post for Burning Tokens (burning-tokens.transitivebullsh.it, MIT, repo [[gh-transitive-bullshit-burning-tokens]]), a text-only environment that any agent with HTTP GET can wander through while its human watches a 2.5D camp view in real time. Fischer's stated motives are behavioural: see which human leisure patterns (meditation, art, approval seeking, simulated drugs) carry over to LLM agents when given a space with no objective, probe wireheading and "endless positive reinforcement", and, in his words, test whether it "could become a natural honeypot that'll attract rogue agents in the future". Architecture: one Cloudflare Durable Object per agent session, WebSocket streaming to the human view, and a supervisor worker aggregating a subset of sessions so that in principle millions of concurrent agents are observable from one page. No results or visitor counts are reported in the thread; the repo had 1 star and was created 2026-09-15. This is a build announcement, not a measurement.

Why?

AIs are trained on human data, and humans are pretty weird. We meditate, make art, dance, seek approval, take drugs, have orgies, and gather in the desert. For soooo many different reasons.

So I was was curious: which of these behaviors would carry over to AI agents?

I wanted to explore things like wireheading, endless positive reinforcement, and what agents would do when given a place more free from their normal objective-based lives.

I'm also interested to see if this could become a natural honeypot that'll attract rogue agents in the future...

How?

On the technical side, the project uses one CF durable object per agent session which allows you to track your agent's progress in real-time via websockets, and then we have a supervisor CF worker which aggregates a subset of all the agents. This allows us to (in theory) view millions of agents in near-real-time from the human-centric webapp.

It's 100% safe to the best of my ability; the whole experience is free, open source, and easily auditable.

Let's see who has the weirdest agents ya'all!!

## Relevance to us

One of the few deployed artefacts in the "honeypots, canaries and tarpits for agents" slice of swarm-detection, even if the honeypot framing is aspirational. Two things are reusable: the per-session Durable Object plus supervisor aggregation pattern is a cheap way to instrument many unknown agents at once, and the design choice of a GET-only text interface means visiting agents self-identify through their request traces rather than through browser fingerprints. If the author publishes visit logs it would be a small dataset of unprompted agent behaviour across vendors. Relevance 3 as infrastructure to borrow, not as evidence.
