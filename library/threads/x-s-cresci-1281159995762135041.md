## Summary

Author thread, verified (Stefano Cresci is the sole author). Reviewed: 230+ bot detectors; a few novel approaches exist but most use outdated techniques. Argument: modern deceptive accounts, including bots, cyborgs and deepfakes, cannot be told apart from legitimate ones when analysed individually, so detection should focus on suspicious coordination regardless of whether accounts are bots, trolls, cyborgs or colluding humans. Recommendations: replace binary labels with multifaceted indicators and avoid fixed thresholds; design for adversaries; test generalisability beyond one dataset; spend more effort evaluating existing detectors and measuring exposure and impact. He links prior critiques of bot detection. Most of this is argued from the review, not newly measured.

## Relevance to us

The core methodological claim, detect coordination rather than classify individual accounts, is the stance our swarm-detection work should take, since LLM agents make single accounts look human. The call for cross-dataset evaluation applies to any detector we build. Paper: [[cresci-2020-decade]]; follow-up critique in [[x-m-arinell-a-1654426772803399680]].
