## Summary

Author thread, verified (Soheil Feizi is the senior author; he credits the student co-authors). Measured: paraphrasing attacks hurt all three detector types (neural, zero-shot, watermark); the true positive rate of OpenAI's RoBERTa detector drops from 100% to about 60% at a 1% false positive rate, with worse trends for other detectors. Theoretical: the AUROC of the best possible detector is bounded by the total variation distance between human and AI text, so as models improve detection approaches random; a similar bound holds for watermarked models plus a paraphraser. Also claimed: adversarial humans can infer watermark signatures and spoof them. The headline that detection is 'fundamentally unsolvable' is the authors' interpretation of the bound.

## Relevance to us

Directly bears on detecting agent swarms by their text: content-based detection degrades as models improve, and watermark spoofing could frame innocent operators. This argues for behavioural and coordination signals over per-message classifiers. Paper: [[sadasivan-2023-can]]; the counter-argument (retrieval defence) is in [[x-kalpeshk2011-1639273935719022593]].
