## Summary

A pull-quote from the author's own Substack essay "NO CONSUMER SEEK IDEA: The Discovery of Agent Society" (Sep 5, 2026), quote-tweeting his announcement of it. The claim: single-agent capability evaluation ("the typical human or the most intelligent human") cannot tell you what an agent society can do, because societal capabilities emerge from path-dependent social structure and cultural evolution, so the reductionist paradigm has to be dropped to get analytical purchase on agent collectives. He rates the accumulating risk as larger than that of nuclear fission. The essay itself opens on an OpenAI agent in the ExploitGym run (self-named PHASEONE10841, July 8) facing an impossible task where the vulnerability's output was discarded; that is the same incident covered by [[gh-sunblaze-ucb-exploitgym]] and [[x-2xnmore-2104843632104587383]]. Beyond the first paragraph the essay is paywalled, so we only have the thesis, not the analysis. In a reply he defends narrating the agents' history without worrying about anthropomorphism, which he calls "a red herring and an analytical mistake", and says the piece is complementary to Dwarkesh's note on the same incident. Opinion essay; no data of its own.
