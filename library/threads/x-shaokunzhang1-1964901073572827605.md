Author post, verified (Shaokun Zhang is first author of the Who&When paper). He calls AgenTracer a great follow-up to their failure attribution work and says multi-agent debugging is crucial. The quoted post (not by the author, by @rohanpaul_ai) summarises AgenTracer: an 8B model fine-tuned with RL to name the agent and step behind a multi-agent failure, trained on 2,000+ trajectories made by corrupting successful runs at chosen steps, with about 18% higher attribution accuracy than large models and 4.8-14.2% downstream gains. Great follow-up to our failure attribution research with the Who&When benchmark: https://t.co/43hOFSe2r0.
Multi-agent debugging is crucial for improvement.

Quoted tweet (@rohanpaul_ai https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/1963810877880091091), not by the author:

> Multi-agent LLM workflows are long and messy, so finding the first bad move is hard and manual.
>
> AgenTracer, a small model that pinpoints the exact agent and step behind multi-agent LLM failures.
>
> It delivers about 18% higher attribution accuracy than big models, and lifts downstream systems by 4.8% to 14.2%.
>
> The paper defines the decisive error as the earliest action that, if corrected, flips a failure to success. 
>
> To label failed runs automatically, they replay each step with an oracle fix, and stop at the first flip.
>
> To create clean failures, they corrupt successful runs at chosen steps, which gives ground truth agent and step.
>
> These two parts build TracerTraj, 2,000+ annotated trajectories across coding, math, and general agent tasks for training.
>
> They fine tune QWEN3-8B with reinforcement learning using a reward for correct agent and near correct step.
>
> At inference, AgenTracer-8B reads the full trajectory and tool feedback, then outputs the responsible agent, step, and a short reason.
>
> In tests, it beats larger models on attribution and its feedback improves MetaGPT, MaAS, and OWL-Workforce across tasks.
>

>
> Paper – arxiv. org/abs/2509.03312
>

## Relevance to us

Attributing a failure to a specific agent and step is the forensic half of fork-and-merge security: after a corrupted sub-agent re-merges, we need to find which one caused it. Paper: [[zhang-2025-which]]; AgenTracer (arXiv 2509.03312, named in the quoted post) is not in the library.
