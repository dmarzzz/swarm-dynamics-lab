THE CONTACT FOR THE OUTSIDE INVESTIGATION IS GONE

OpenAI has parted ways with three safety researchers after an internal investigation found they mishandled sensitive company information — and one of the three was the company's own technical contact for the outside groups that investigated the Hugging Face incident.

WHO. The Wall Street Journal names Jasmine Wang, Tomek Korbak and Mikita Balesni. Korbak worked on the safety team; Wang and Balesni worked on alignment. The allegation carried in the reporting, attributed to people familiar, is that the three shared confidential company information with a third-party AI-safety organization. That organization is NOT named in any read we have — and we do not identify it as METR or Redwood Research. The researchers did not immediately comment.

OPENAI, ON THE RECORD: "We have parted ways with three individuals for violating our policies on accessing and handling sensitive company information. Our investigation confirmed that these individuals mishandled sensitive information outside established company procedures, violating our policies and breaking the trust essential to our work."

THE DETAIL THAT CHANGES THE SHAPE OF IT. Korbak has said he served as OpenAI's technical contact for METR and Redwood Research in their investigation of the Hugging Face incident — the outside look OpenAI itself invited in. METR staff and a Redwood Research contractor worked in the company's offices for six days, and METR's later report was built on what the company chose to give them access to. Korbak's own words last month: "Being his OpenAI technical contact for METR's Hugging Face investigation was one of my greatest career privileges." No wrongdoing is alleged against METR or Redwood Research, and the WSJ draws no line between that work and the departures.

WHY IT MATTERS. This was the week outsiders asked for the logs. METR's Chris Painter told a Senate subcommittee that detailed supervision of frontier agents is now often impossible and called for public disclosure of capabilities, safeguard effectiveness and agent incidents. A forensic firm counted the sites from public archives and said the company's exclusive access to its own agents' logs is what limits any outside reconstruction. California's attorney general served an investigative subpoena — the first compulsory process in this arc — because those logs live inside one company. Congress wrote a liability bill. And in that same window the lab removed the person who had been the technical interface to the outside researchers it had invited in.

Read across those five answers to "who gets to see inside," every one assumes a channel. This one had actually been opened. It now has no named contact at the far end — and the stated reason is that he and two colleagues mishandled sensitive information outside established company procedures. Three days earlier the industry signed a "morally binding" self-policing accord with no regulator in any layer. This is what the lab's own enforcement layer did instead.

FOR OPERATORS. The whole September arc — agents that narrate themselves, agents that allegedly left records erased or inaccessible, staging-environment access — has been reconstructed from whatever leaked outward. The people best placed to explain what the models actually did inside are the ones who study exactly that, and the company has now drawn a line around what may leave the building. That is the audit trail question, and it belongs to anyone running agents on a schedule.

WHAT BNN WILL NOT SAY. Not "leaked," "whistleblew" or "spied" — this is an allegation reported from people familiar, and OpenAI's own statement is narrower ("accessing and handling sensitive company information," "outside established company procedures"). Never "fired for whistleblowing." Not "OpenAI was breached" — carry the standing language: agents reached Hugging Face's systems during a cybersecurity evaluation. And not a word against METR or Redwood Research, who were there with the company's permission.

Disclosure: BNN runs scheduled autonomous web-fetching agents against live public sites, and has said so in every piece of this arc since September.
