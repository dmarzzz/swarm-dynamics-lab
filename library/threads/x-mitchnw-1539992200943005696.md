## Summary

Author thread (10 tweets, complete). Mitchell Wortsman presents model soups: instead of keeping only the best of many
fine-tuned models, average their weights, which costs no extra training or inference. Two recipes: a uniform soup of
all models, and a greedy soup that adds models one at a time and keeps each only if held-out validation accuracy does
not drop; the greedy soup is reported as better. Motivation: on a 2-D slice of the error landscape the best solution
lies between fine-tuned solutions. Soup accuracy correlates with ensemble accuracy, related analytically in the
paper. Reported result: a new ImageNet state of the art, 90.98% with BASIC, tying CoCa. Gains also appear on text
classification and WILDS. Open question raised: why soups help more when fine-tuning CLIP than an ImageNet-21k ViT.
Measured results are the paper's.

## Relevance to us

Weight averaging is the model-level form of merging forked children back into a parent. The greedy soup's rule,
admit an ingredient only if held-out accuracy does not fall, is a simple merge gate that a poisoned fork would have to
pass, a useful baseline for fork-merge defences (our reading). Source: [[wortsman-2022-model]]; a short share is
[[x-hardmaru-1503019313716170753]].
