## Summary

Five-part author thread (plus a credits tweet) by the lead author of [[amayuelas-2024-multiagent]]. Setting: LLMs from different organisations collaborate by debate, and one participant is adversarial. Reported result: debating collaborations are vulnerable, with accuracy drops of 10-40% (stated without per-task breakdown in the thread). The thread says the attacker's persuasiveness is the key factor and that the paper explores ways to generate more compelling arguments for the adversary. Defences: prompt-based mitigations, which the author describes only as early results that suggest more sophisticated methods could work; no defence numbers are given.

## Relevance to us

Directly on the fork-and-merge question: one corrupted participant degrading a group answer through persuasion is the same failure as a corrupted sub-agent re-merging into a parent. The 10-40% drop is a baseline for how much one adversarial voice costs a debate-style aggregator, and the weak prompt defences argue for structural merge thresholds instead. See [[amayuelas-2024-multiagent]] and [[du-2023-improving]] for the debate protocol being attacked.
