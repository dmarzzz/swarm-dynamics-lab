## Summary

Author thread (the handle phildaian and the posts on "co-authors" and "1.5 years of work of mine" match Philip
Daian, the first author; the display name is only an emoji). Measured, per the thread: the authors deployed
infrastructure that recorded over 700M observations of arbitrage-bot transactions on the Ethereum p2p network,
including bids that are never mined; most priority gas auctions (PGAs) still leave the winner a median 65%
profit; bots converged over time on bidding the minimum raise (Parity enforces 12.5%, Geth 10%), which their
formal model predicts as tacit cooperation against miners; the market looks oligopolistic but dynamic; the
GasToken release shifted bots' gas optimisation; bid latency fell and bids per auction rose over time;
arbitrage revenue in some blocks exceeds block reward plus fees by orders of magnitude. Argued: order
optimisation fees (a form of miner extractable value) enable undercutting and a new "time bandit" attack that
rewrites history, and today's block reward being 25x lower than DEX volume makes 51% attacks potentially
profitable; he concludes Ethereum is insecure at current parameters. Replies defend the use of the term
"frontrunning" and suggest batch auctions help.

**3. @phildaian, 2019-04-11 01:45 UTC** (status 1116155238405345283)

**4. @phildaian, 2019-04-11 01:45 UTC** (status 1116155239411986432)

Worse still, DEXes in general threaten the consensus security of Ethereum, even today.  Miners could extract implicit "fees" from controlling the order of transactions in blocks, we call "order optimization fees".  These OO fees are one form of "miner extractable value" (MEV).

**5. @phildaian, 2019-04-11 01:45 UTC** (status 1116155240380760064)

MEV can subsidize two types of attacks, one of which is new.  Undercutting attack: in this attack, miners may choose to ignore a block with juicy MEV in the hopes that they can orphan it at potentially huge profits.  This destabilizes short-term consensus.

**6. @phildaian, 2019-04-11 01:45 UTC** (status 1116155241479667717)

Time bandit attack (new): in this attack, miners rewrite trade history to steal money from DEXes *in the past*.  They do this by inserting themselves on the other side of every trade it would have been profitable to take at today's market rate.

**7. @phildaian, 2019-04-11 01:45 UTC** (status 1116155242578677761)

Because miners get pure ETH and leave no traces on centralized exchanges, this is an attractive incentive to perform 51% attacks.  Today's Ethereum block reward is 25x lower than its DEX volume, making stealing MEV from DEXes possibly within grasp.

**8. @phildaian, 2019-04-11 01:45 UTC** (status 1116155244537421828)

In general, abstractions hide important, security-critical interactions.  Building your DEX assuming the underlying blockchain will keep working may actually stop this from happening in a self-fulfilling prophecy.

**9. @phildaian, 2019-04-11 01:45 UTC** (status 1116155246131208192)

**22. @phildaian, 2019-04-11 01:45 UTC** (status 1116155277248741376)

**23. @phildaian, 2019-04-11 01:45 UTC** (status 1116155278624468993)

## Relevance to us

A first measured study of an autonomous bot population in the wild: p2p-level monitoring separated competing
bots, and their bidding converged on tacit cooperation that nobody coded centrally. It is a template both for
in-the-wild swarm detection and for collusion in the MEV setting that our Sybil topic covers. See
[[daian-2019-flash]].
