## Summary

Author thread (Edward Hughes is a co-author of [[vallinder-2024-cultural]]). Setup: populations of LLM agents play the Donor Game over generations, testing whether a group can build trust through reputation. Reported results, from figures not archived and without numbers in the text: emergence of cooperation differs strikingly by base model; Claude 3.5 Sonnet populations bootstrap cooperation fairly reliably, Gemini 1.5 Flash gives mixed results, and GPT-4o populations converge to mutual defection. Drivers named for the Claude "cooperative bootstrap": higher initial cooperation, more effective punishment of free-riders, and a more cooperative bias when strategies mutate. Qualitatively, strategies grow more complex across generations. Stated limitations: idealised strict generation boundaries, only the Donor Game, and no exhaustive prompt search. The call to add such evals to industry benchmarks is the author's opinion. The thread appears complete (10 tweets).

## Relevance to us

A reputation-based cooperation game among LLM agents is a natural testbed for Sybil attacks: an agent that can reset its identity escapes the punishment of free-riders that drives cooperation here (our inference, not tested in the thread). The base-model dependence also means a mixed swarm can behave very differently from a single-model one. Links [[vallinder-2024-cultural]].
