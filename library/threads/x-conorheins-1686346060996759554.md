## Summary

Author thread (14 numbered tweets, all present). Conor Heins introduces a model class for collective behaviour in
which each agent runs active inference: it infers latent variables of its social environment (for example the
average distance to neighbours) and acts to minimise sensory prediction error. The claims: the model generalises the
"social force" rules of self-propelled particle models (Vicsek, Couzin-Aoki) and recovers attraction, repulsion and
alignment in limiting cases as gradients of a free-energy bound on surprise; it reproduces collective regimes,
information transfer and amplification of transient perturbations; a key mechanism is "behavioural plasticity",
agents updating their world-model parameters by the same free-energy gradient descent. He claims this reproduces
amplification and encoding of information that earlier models need extra mechanisms for. All are modelling claims
from simulation; no numbers are given. In later replies he says the formalism is not intrinsically spatial and doubts
active inference is necessary to explain ant bridge building.

We end up with a group of active inference agents that A) infer latent variables (for example, the average distance-to-neighbors) of their social environment and B) act upon their environment to minimize sensory prediction errors (aka surprise). (4/14)

**9. @conorheins, 2023-08-01 12:00 UTC** (status 1686346082454822912)

One of the key mechanisms that allows groups of active inference agents to amplify and collectively encode transient fluctuations, is what we call “behavioral plasticity”. We cast this plasticity as the ability of agents to flexibly update their world models over time (9/14)

**10. @conorheins, 2023-08-01 12:00 UTC** (status 1686346084535205889)

**11. @conorheins, 2023-08-01 12:00 UTC** (status 1686346086758170625)

## Relevance to us

A principled agent-level model of flocking that ties classic rules to inference, from an author thread on
[[heins-2024-collective]] with code linked. The "behavioural plasticity" mechanism, agents adapting their model of
neighbours, is a natural place to study how a few corrupted or adversarial agents propagate through a group, but that
is our inference, not the paper's. Low direct bearing on Sybil resistance.
