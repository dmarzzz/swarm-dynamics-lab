## Summary

Author thread (Stefano Cresci is the first author of [[cresci-2023-demystifying]]); all 10 numbered tweets plus the closing thanks are present. The paper takes one bot-dataset paper as a case study and finds information leakage, wrong data provenance, citation bias and misrepresentation of the literature, which it links to six misconceptions. Claimed corrections: 1, bot detection is far from solved; 2, it cannot be improved easily, it is intrinsically hard, and lack of platform collaboration and new technology make it worse; 3, social bots are of many types; 4, each detector's performance depends on the accounts it is applied to, for both general and specialised detectors; 5, there is no scientific consensus that bots drive disinformation, and paid trolls and colluding humans are also involved; 6, bot research, though imperfect, is useful against disinformation. These are argued positions from a literature review; the thread gives no new measurements. The tone is polemical ("uninformed or biased").

## Relevance to us

The practical lessons for a swarm detector: report per-bot-type performance, expect detectors not to transfer between populations, and watch for dataset leakage when evaluating. Coordinated humans are a confound that a swarm detector must separate from agents. Links [[cresci-2023-demystifying]]; for a case where existing detectors failed on an LLM botnet see [[x-yang3kc-1686751689078976512]].
