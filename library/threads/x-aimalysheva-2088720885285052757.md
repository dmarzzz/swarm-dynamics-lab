## Summary

Commentary and reading list, not by the paper's authors. The poster lists five multi-agent RL papers she says carry most of the field's vocabulary: RIAL/DIAL (Foerster et al. 2016, learned communication), CommNet (2016, continuous channels), MADDPG (2017, centralized training with decentralized execution), QMIX (2018, value factorization) and the Hanabi Challenge (2019, coordination with strangers). Quoted tweet (@aimalysheva https://x.com/aimalysheva/status/2086928827251413037), not by the author:

> day 7/7, and this is what the week was building toward
>
> classical game theory assumes fixed players: you design a mechanism around their incentives that are unchanged
> multi-agent learning is kind of next level as it has plastic players -- the agents change while the mechanism is running: so what is mechanism design for learning agents? what does an equilibrium mean when every participant is mid gradient step?
>
> everything from this week feeds into it: the protocols from days 1 and 2 are mechanisms, credit assignment on day 3 is payments, opponent modeling on day 4 is beliefs, and conventions on day 6 are the folk theorems -- and most of these pieces are usually from separate literatures that barely cite each other (assembling them is a career's worth of work for somebody, possibly several somebodies...)
>
> that's the week 🫡 thanks to everyone who argued with me in the replies!
