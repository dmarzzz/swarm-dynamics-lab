## Summary

A glucose-limited continuous-culture population founded from one E. coli clone diversified. Reconstruction experiments supported stable coexistence of three isolates with different growth and uptake properties. The abstract attributes coexistence to differences in acetate and glycerol secretion and uptake.

## Contribution

An experimental example of metabolic resource partitioning emerging without an externally structured habitat.

## Key results

- Three isolated clones coexisted in reconstructed populations.
- Acetate handling was linked to acetyl-coenzyme A synthetase regulation; the glycerol explanation is qualified as likely.

## Methods and models

Continuous culture, clone reconstruction and metabolic characterization, as described in the abstract.

## Limitations and open questions

Abstract-depth only; original replicate counts and detailed methods remain unchecked. Do not generalize this bacterial result to LLM collaboration or claim a measured speciation event.
