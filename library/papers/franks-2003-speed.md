## Summary

Experiments showing a speed-accuracy trade-off in collective decisions: house-hunting Leptothorax albipennis colonies choose faster but less discriminatingly in harsh conditions. Errors are of judgement rather than omission because colonies had already discovered all alternatives. Colonies lower their quorum threshold in harsh conditions and then rely much more on individual than on collective decision-making.

## Contribution

First demonstration that a collective adjusts a quorum parameter to trade speed for accuracy; contrast with [[pratt-2006-tunable]] and [[sumpter-2009-quorum]], which find acceptance rates more important than the threshold.

## Key results

- Faster, less accurate emigrations under harsh conditions (experiment).
- Quorum thresholds are lower in harsh conditions (experiment).
- Abstract-level reading only; numbers beyond the abstract were not checked.

## Methods and models

Laboratory emigrations of Temnothorax albipennis between artificial nests of different quality under benign versus harsh (exposed) conditions.

## Limitations and open questions

Laboratory colonies; the relative contribution of quorum versus acceptance changes was later disputed.

## Relevance to us

Classic empirical speed-accuracy result to cite for any tunable swarm decision. Related: [[marshall-2009-optimal]], [[valentini-2016-collective]].
