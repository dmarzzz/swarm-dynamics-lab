## Summary

Builds a database of 281 crowd accidents between 1900 and 2019 from more than 800 press and media sources, recording date, country, fatalities, injuries, crowd size, purpose of gathering and country income group. Accident frequency rises clearly over the 120 years; there is no indication that larger crowds raise per-person risk (if anything the opposite); sport events have declined as a share while religious gatherings have grown.

## Contribution

The first systematic, open dataset of crowd accidents, giving an empirical base for claims about trends and risk factors.

## Key results

- Measured: 281 accidents, 1900–2019; per-decade counts rising steadily; small accidents (fewer than 10 deaths) increasing.
- No evidence that larger crowds raise per-person risk (causality not established).

## Methods and models

Press-report compilation with cross-checking; dataset at https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.7523480. Safety Science 164, 106174 (CC BY).

## Limitations and open questions

Press coverage biases (language, era, country). Skimmed.
