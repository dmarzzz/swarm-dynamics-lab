## Summary

A simplified fish school model with asynchronous burst-and-coast swimming where each fish interacts only with
the one or two most influential neighbours (attraction and alignment) within a limited range. Above a
threshold interaction strength the model reproduces schooling, milling and swarming (one neighbour) or
schooling and swarming (two neighbours). Long simulations show that, except for swarming, these patterns do
not persist indefinitely: groups lose cohesion and disperse, and the dispersal mechanisms are identified. Read
at abstract level via Europe PMC.

## Contribution

Tests long-time stability of collective phases under realistic intermittent locomotion and selective
(one or two neighbour) attention, a recent line from the Theraulaz group following
[[gautrais-2012-deciphering]] and [[calovi-2014-swarming]].

## Key results

- Simulated: phases depend on number of attended neighbours (1 vs 2); schooling and milling eventually
  disperse over long runs.

## Methods and models

Burst-and-coast kicks, asynchronous decisions, selective interaction with most influential neighbours.
