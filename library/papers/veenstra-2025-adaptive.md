## Summary

Veenstra, Coulais and colleagues build active solids from centimetre-scale motorized building blocks coupled
elastically. The solids show odd (non-variational) elasticity whose moduli are predicted from microscopics by
coarse-grained theory and validated experimentally. In contact with an environment they spontaneously undergo limit
cycles of shape change that produce rolling and crawling; an emergent feedback loop through elastic stress lets them
accelerate, change gait and cross varied terrains with performance similar to neural-network controllers. Read from the abstract on the page in `url`; details beyond the abstract are not checked. The
Nature page showed only the abstract (subscription).

## Contribution

Shows decentralized, physics-based locomotion control of a robot collective, bridging active matter and robotics; part of
the odd-elasticity line with [[baconnier-2022-selective]] and [[baconnier-2025-self]].

## Key results

- Measured odd moduli agree with coarse-grained predictions (abstract).
- Spontaneous limit cycles yield locomotion (rolling, crawling) and adaptive gaits (abstract).
- Locomotion performance comparable to neural-network control strategies (claimed in abstract; comparison details
  not checked).

## Methods and models
