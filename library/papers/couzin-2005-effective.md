## Summary

A zonal model of moving groups in which a few individuals have a preferred direction (informed) and the rest
do not; nobody knows who is informed and there is no signalling. Information transfers through the group
anyway. The larger the group, the smaller the proportion of informed individuals needed to guide it, and a
very small proportion gives high accuracy. With conflicting preferences the group still reaches consensus
even though informed individuals do not know whether they are in a majority. Read at abstract level via
Europe PMC.

## Contribution

Classic result linking collective motion to collective decision-making and leadership without explicit
communication; the basis for many later informed-agent and "few leaders" swarm-control schemes.

## Key results

- Claimed (model): required informed proportion decreases with group size; small informed fraction yields
  high accuracy; consensus emerges with conflicting informed subgroups.

## Methods and models

Extension of the [[couzin-2002-collective]] zonal model with a preferred-direction term weighted by omega for
informed individuals.

## Limitations and open questions

Abstract-level read; model result, tested empirically later by others.

## Relevance to us

Directly relevant to steering a swarm with a few informed (or human-controlled) agents.
