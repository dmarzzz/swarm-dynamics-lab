## Summary

3D tracking of polarised schools of giant danios swimming continuously for ten hours tests whether fish adopt the planar diamond formation predicted by Weihs and Lighthill for hydrodynamic efficiency. Of fish pairs within four body lengths, only 25.2% were in the same horizontal plane; of those, 54.6% were inline, 30.0% staggered and 15.4% side by side. The diamond formation appeared in under 0.1% of frames. The most probable configuration was a vertical "ladder" formation (79% of pairs), which elongated at higher swimming speeds.

## Contribution

Direct 3D evidence against fixed planar formations as the organising principle of schools; argues hydrodynamic benefits may not require fixed formations.
