## Summary

Applies integrated information theory to tracked ayu (Plecoglossus altivelis) schools and finds group
integrity Phi is maximal at the critical state. Multiple levels of criticality coexist as distinct subgroups,
heterogeneously distributed in space and time, alongside group-level criticality; core fish in high-criticality
subgroups are less affected by internal and external stimuli than fish in low-criticality subgroups.

## Contribution

Links an integration measure (Phi) to criticality in real fish data and introduces the idea of
heterogeneous, subgroup-level criticality.

## Key results

- Phi maximal at the critical state of the school (measured).
- Coexisting critical subgroups with heterogeneous spatial and temporal distribution.
- Core fish of high-criticality subgroups less sensitive to stimuli.

## Methods and models

IIT applied to binarised motion of tracked ayu schools; subgroup-level criticality analysis.

## Limitations and open questions

IIT computational limits and binarisation choices; abstract-level read.
