## Summary

Artificial fish with embodied neural networks and pixel input were raised in virtual tanks mimicking biological rearing conditions. With deep reinforcement learning and curiosity-based intrinsic reward, they spontaneously developed fish-like social behaviour, including collective behaviour and in-group preference, and also did so in naturalistic ocean worlds. The authors argue that animal-like social behaviour can develop from generic learning algorithms.

## Contribution

Reverse-engineering the development of collective behaviour with image-computable RL agents, linking MARL emergence and animal schooling.

- Simulation (abstract): schooling-like collective behaviour and social preference emerge from curiosity-driven deep RL.
