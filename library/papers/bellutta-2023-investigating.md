## Summary

The authors examine fourteen months of tweets about the 2020 United States election and relate bursts of account creation to coordinated behavior. Burst-created accounts showed more similar behavior, more agreement on selected political topics, and greater likelihood of bot classification and sharing low-credibility links.

## Contribution

Account-creation bursts offer a group-level early-warning signal complementary to individual bot detection.

## Key results

- Fourteen months of election-related tweets were analyzed. Associations with behavioral similarity, bot labels, and low-credibility links are reported, without effect sizes in the abstract.

## Methods and models

Burst detection and network analysis of Twitter accounts and election discourse.

## Limitations and open questions

Associations do not identify operator intent or establish that every creation burst is malicious. The abstract proposes limiting burst-created accounts but does not evaluate that intervention.

## Relevance to us

This provides a prior-art comparison for detecting coordinated automation, not evidence that any observed population is an LLM swarm.
