## Summary

Short review comparing the two naturally occurring contagious cancers known at the time, devil facial tumour disease (DFTD) and canine transmissible venereal tumour (CTVT). Both pass between individuals as allografts and evade the adaptive immune response that should reject them, but their impact differs: DFTD causes 100 percent mortality among infected Tasmanian devils, while CTVT usually coexists with its host without killing it. The review compares how each interacts with its host and avoids rejection, and highlights epigenetic control of immune escape and a possible vaccine for devils. Abstract only.

## Contribution

Review that sets the two mammalian transmissible cancers side by side and points to marker suppression as the shared escape route with different outcomes for the host.
