## Summary

A Perspective on making swarms of artificial micromotors and nanomotors (MNMs) behave intelligently. It
first surveys swarming intelligence in nature from bacteria to animal groups, then propulsion and control of
artificial units (magnetic, electric, acoustic fields and onboard chemical fuel), then collective behaviours
(taxis, communication, cooperation, consensus-like decisions) and biomedical uses such as collective drug
delivery and tissue penetration. Its outlook names the missing pieces: material limits, information storage,
communication between units, and stigmergy-inspired designs (for example "seeker" particles that find a target
by chemotaxis and recruit "effector" particles).

## Contribution

A 2025 map of microrobot swarming from the materials-chemistry community, the field that
[[dorigo-2021-swarm]] flags as the miniaturisation frontier. It complements the robotics-side microrobot
entries [[gardi-2022-microrobot]], [[xie-2019-reconfigurable]] and [[heuthe-2024-counterfactual]].

## Key results

- Qualitative synthesis: artificial MNM swarms still fall short of the autonomy of biological swarms; most
  "intelligence" is imposed by external fields rather than by local rules.
- Proposes stigmergy and division of roles between particle populations as routes to autonomous swarming.

## Methods and models

Perspective with glossary (bioconvection, consensus decision-making, oscillatory behaviour, and others). Read:
abstract, introduction, section structure and outlook.

## Limitations and open questions

Biomedical framing; little quantitative treatment of collective dynamics. Claims about in vivo swarm
efficacy are summarised from the cited primary studies and were not checked here.

## Relevance to us

Background for the microrobot end of swarm robotics, and a source of open questions (communication,
memory) that are natural to test in active-matter simulations. See also [[gompper-2025-motile]].
