## Summary

Compares flow through bottlenecks in sheep herds, pedestrian crowds, granular silos and colloids. In all of them the distribution of time lapses between consecutive passages has a power-law tail whose exponent depends on conditions; the clogging transition is identified with the divergence of the mean time lapse, when the exponent drops below 2. This gives a unified clogging state diagram whose control variables are driving force, noise and outlet size.

## Contribution

A cross-system framework that recasts faster-is-slower ([[helbing-2000-simulating]]) and clogging as a transition in intermittent flow, applicable to living and inert particles.

## Key results

- Measured: power-law tails in time-lapse distributions across sheep, pedestrians, grains and colloids.
- Defined: an order parameter distinguishing states with finite versus divergent mean clog duration.
- Classified: routes out of clogging by reducing driving force (for example an obstacle before a door for sheep, lower desired velocity for pedestrians), increasing noise, or widening the outlet.

## Methods and models

Experimental data from several systems plus simulations. Sci. Rep. 4, 7324; open access PMC4255180.

## Limitations and open questions

Pedestrian data from competitive egress experiments; mapping of human "pressure" onto granular driving force is heuristic. Skimmed.

## Relevance to us

A universal statistic (time-lapse tail exponent) for diagnosing clogging in any swarm passing a constriction, including robots. Related: [[helbing-2000-simulating]].
