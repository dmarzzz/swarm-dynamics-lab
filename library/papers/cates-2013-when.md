## Summary

Cates and Tailleur show that when motility parameters depend on density but not on orientation, the coarse-grained
fluctuating hydrodynamics of interacting active Brownian particles (smooth rotational diffusion) and run-and-tumble
particles (sudden tumbles) map onto each other exactly, so both phase separate when v(ρ) decreases steeply enough, in 2D
and 3D, without attractions. The equivalence fails for orientation-dependent motility. Read from the abstract in the Semantic Scholar API record in `url`; details beyond the abstract are not checked.

## Contribution

Unified the two canonical active-particle models for MIPS; the general D = v²τ/d with τ⁻¹ = α + (d−1)D_r appears as
Eqs. 4–5 of [[cates-2015-motility]] (read in full).

## Key results

- Strict ABP/RTP equivalence for orientation-independent, density-dependent motility (abstract).
- MIPS generic in d = 2, 3 (abstract).

## Methods and models

Analytical coarse-graining. arXiv:1206.1805; full text not read.

## Limitations and open questions

Equivalence breaks for orientation-dependent motility (e.g. alignment, chemotaxis).

## Relevance to us

Means a swarm model's choice of tumbling vs smooth turning does not change MIPS predictions if speed depends only on
density. Related: [[cates-2015-motility]], [[tailleur-2008-statistical]].
