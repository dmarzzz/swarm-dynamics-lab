## Summary

Review that organises empirical and theoretical work on consensus decisions in animals by two axes: the degree of conflict of interest among group members and whether communication is local or global. For each category it asks who decides, by what mechanism, and with what fitness consequences, and concludes that consensus decisions are common and that cooperative decision processes are the norm even under substantial conflict.

## Contribution

The most-cited framing review of animal consensus decisions; introduced the distinction between combined and shared versus unshared decisions that later work (for example [[couzin-2011-uninformed]], [[strandburg-peshkin-2015-shared]]) builds on.

## Key results

- Classification of consensus decisions by conflict of interest and local versus global communication (conceptual).
- Conclusion that cooperation in the decision process is likely the norm even with significant conflict (authors' synthesis, not a quantitative meta-analysis).
- Abstract-level reading only; numbers beyond the abstract were not checked.

## Methods and models

Narrative review covering about 70 references across primates, ungulates, birds, fish, bats and social insects (reference list checked via Crossref).

## Limitations and open questions

2005 coverage; qualitative synthesis without effect sizes.
