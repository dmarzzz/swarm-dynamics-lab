## Summary

Field study of sulphur molly shoals that produce conspicuous, rhythmic surface waves when diving in
response to avian predators. Experimentally induced waves doubled the time birds waited before their next attack,
and in one bird species capture probability fell with wave number, supporting an anti-predator function.

## Contribution

Establishes the function of the fish waves whose dynamics [[gomez-nava-2023-fish]] later analysed
as a near-critical excitable system.

## Key results

- Induced waves doubled inter-attack waiting time (field experiment).
- Capture probability decreased with wave number for one predator species.

## Methods and models

Field observation and experimental wave induction in sulphidic springs (Mexico).

## Limitations and open questions

Function, not criticality, is measured here; abstract-level read.

## Relevance to us

Provides the functional payoff that a criticality account must explain. See
