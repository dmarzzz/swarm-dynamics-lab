## Summary

Controlled laboratory experiments on bidirectional pedestrian flow show that lane segregation is structurally unstable: organised and disorganised states alternate, and the lifetime of well-organised clusters follows a stretched exponential relaxation. Variability of comfortable walking speeds across individuals is identified as the source of the breakdowns; the collective benefit is maximal when everyone walks at the group mean speed.

## Contribution

First experimental measurement of the functional benefit and the instability of lane formation, showing that a "smart" collective pattern does not always maximise group payoff.

## Key results

- Measured (abstract): alternation of organised and disorganised states; stretched-exponential lifetime of organised clusters.
- Inferred: heterogeneity in preferred speed triggers global breakdowns; benefit maximised for homogeneous speeds.

## Methods and models

Controlled laboratory experiments with tracked participants, plus modelling (details not read). PLoS Comput. Biol. 8(3), e1002442.
