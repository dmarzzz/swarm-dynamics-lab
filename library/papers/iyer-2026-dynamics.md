## Summary

Proposes a minimal flocking model in which agents use vision-based steering to avoid collisions,
align and stay cohesive. In 2D simulations, collision avoidance acts as emergent noise and induces an
order-disorder transition; large vortices at the critical point hint at a Berezinskii-Kosterlitz-Thouless-like
transition. Deep in the ordered phase cohesion acts like surface tension; competing interactions cause large
shape and density fluctuations that may speed responses to predators or food.

## Contribution

Shows the order-disorder transition can arise from deterministic competing interactions rather
than imposed noise.

## Key results

- Collision avoidance generates emergent noise and an order-disorder transition (simulation).
- Large vortices at criticality suggest a BKT-like transition.
- Pronounced shape and density fluctuations from competing interactions.

## Methods and models

2D vision-based steering model with avoidance, alignment and cohesion.

## Limitations and open questions

Simulation only; abstract-level read.

## Relevance to us

Relevant to robot or drone swarms with collision avoidance: the avoidance rule itself may set the
distance to the transition. Compare [[verdoucq-2025-flocking]].
