## Summary

Review of the STARFLAG programme's data-first approach to bird flocks: use 3D field data on starlings as the
starting point to characterise flocking as a collective phenomenon in the sense of condensed matter physics,
and build models directly from the data rather than postulating rules. Read at abstract level only (via the
Semantic Scholar record).

## Contribution

Synthesis of the Rome group's results ([[ballerini-2008-interaction]], [[cavagna-2010-scale]],
[[bialek-2012-statistical]], [[attanasi-2014-information]]) into one condensed-matter narrative: topological
interactions, scale-free correlations, maximum-entropy inference and inertial (spin-wave) information
transfer.

## Key results

- Abstract-level claims only: experiments on macroscopic flocks had been scarce compared with micro-scale
  active matter; the review argues for empirically characterising flocking with statistical-physics tools
  and inferring models from data.
- Specific numbers live in the primary papers linked above.

## Methods and models

Review; data from stereometric reconstruction of starling flocks over Rome.

## Limitations and open questions

Not read beyond the abstract. Single-species (starlings) perspective; criticality interpretation is
contested in later work ([[romanczuk-2022-phase]]).

## Relevance to us

Good second review after [[vicsek-2012-collective]] for anyone measuring correlations in simulated or
robotic swarms.
