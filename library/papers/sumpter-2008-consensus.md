## Summary

Small groups of sticklebacks choosing which of two replica fish to follow make more accurate choices as group size increases, discriminating subtler differences in replica phenotype (for example body size or condition). A simple quorum rule explains the data, and occasional cascades of wrong choices emerge as a by-product of usually accurate consensus.

## Contribution

Empirical demonstration that decision accuracy increases with group size in animals, with a quorum mechanism rather than explicit comparison; complements [[ward-2008-quorum]].

## Key results

- Accuracy increases with group size (experiment).
- A quorum rule suffices to reproduce choice data; incorrect cascades occur occasionally (model fit).
- Abstract-level reading only; numbers beyond the abstract were not checked.

## Methods and models

Choice trials with Gasterosteus aculeatus groups of different sizes and replica leaders differing in phenotype; quorum-rule model.

## Limitations and open questions

Small groups; artificial replicas.

## Relevance to us

Group-size scaling of accuracy is a quick measurable for a swarm benchmark; contrast with [[kao-2014-decision]], which predicts small groups can be best when cues are correlated.
