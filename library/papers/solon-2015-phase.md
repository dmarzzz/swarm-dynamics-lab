## Summary

Solon, Chaté and Tailleur argue that the flocking transition of the Vicsek model is a liquid–gas transition rather
than an order–disorder one. Flocking models with discrete (Z2) symmetry (active Ising) fully phase separate, whereas the
Vicsek model with continuous symmetry microphase separates into a smectic of traveling bands. Deterministic continuum
equations cannot capture this difference, which appears only at the level of fluctuating hydrodynamics: scalar and
vector order parameters generate different number fluctuations that select the patterns. Read from the abstract on the page in `url`; details beyond the abstract are not checked.

## Contribution

The paper that reframed flocking onset as phase separation; the core of [[chate-2019-dry]] (read in full, where the
band-selection-by-noise mechanism is described in detail).

## Key results

- Liquid–gas picture of the Vicsek transition with microphase separation (bands) (abstract).
- Active Ising model shows bulk phase separation (abstract).
- Noise is essential for pattern selection (abstract).

## Methods and models

Simulations of Vicsek and active Ising models plus fluctuating hydrodynamics. arXiv:1406.6088; full text not read.

## Limitations and open questions

2D; mechanism of band-number selection discussed further in later work.
