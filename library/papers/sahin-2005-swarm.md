## Summary

The paper that gave swarm robotics its working definition. Şahin describes the desirable system-level
properties observed in social insects (robustness, flexibility, scalability), proposes a definition of swarm
robotics as the coordination of large numbers of relatively simple robots inspired by social insects, and gives
criteria to separate swarm robotics from other multi-robot research (autonomous robots, large numbers,
homogeneous groups, relatively incapable individuals, local sensing and communication). It reviews biological
sources of inspiration and lists promising application domains.

## Contribution

The standard definitional reference, cited by nearly every later review ([[brambilla-2013-swarm]],
[[schranz-2020-swarm]]).

## Key results

- Definition and criteria for swarm robotics (conceptual).
- Properties: robustness, flexibility, scalability as the motivating trio.

## Methods and models

Position and review chapter in the proceedings of the first swarm robotics workshop (SAB 2004). Abstract only
read; the criteria above are as summarised in the abstract.

## Limitations and open questions

The "simple, homogeneous robots" emphasis has been challenged by later perspectives
([[dorigo-2020-reflections]], [[kegeleirs-2025-towards]]).
