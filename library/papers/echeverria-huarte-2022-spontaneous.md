## Summary

Experiments with pedestrians roaming freely inside an enclosure show spontaneous vortex-like collective rotation that, strikingly, is always counterclockwise. A minimal model with long-range repulsion, dissipative interaction with walls and no explicit alignment term reproduces the vortices as a phase transition controlled by density and wall dissipation; a turning preference at walls (right-handed people tend to turn left when facing a wall) is the symmetry-breaking ingredient that selects counterclockwise rotation.

## Contribution

Shows that coherent milling in a human swarm can arise without any alignment (flocking) interaction, from repulsion, confinement and an individual turning bias, and offers an explanation for the counterclockwise preference of circle pits.

## Key results

- Measured: counterclockwise vortices in pedestrian assemblies in an arena; single-peaked angular-momentum distributions; weak dependence on number of pedestrians; enhanced density near walls.
- Simulated: vortices emerge without flocking terms; wall turning bias needed to reproduce the CCW preference.
- Cited observation: about 95% of circle pits at heavy metal concerts rotate counterclockwise ([[silverberg-2013-collective]]).

## Methods and models

Controlled arena experiments; minimal agent model with repulsion and wall turning preference. Sci. Rep. 12, 2647 (open access).

## Limitations and open questions

Sparse assemblies; minimal model does not capture decision-making. Skimmed.

## Relevance to us

A clean demonstration that milling can come from boundary interactions and individual chirality rather than alignment, a useful null model for robot swarm milling experiments. Compare [[gu-2025-emergence]] (spontaneous chirality with no preferred handedness).
