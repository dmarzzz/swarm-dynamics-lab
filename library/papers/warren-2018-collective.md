## Summary

Short review of an experiment-driven programme to decipher the local "rules of engagement" in human crowds using virtual crowds and real crowd data. It describes a dynamical model of how a pedestrian aligns their motion with a neighbour and how these binary interactions combine within a neighbourhood; simulations produce coherent global motion and reproduce individual trajectories.

## Contribution

Accessible synthesis, from a cognitive-science perspective, of the alignment-based (flocking-like) account of human collective motion, contrasting with avoidance-based social force models.

## Key results

- Review claims (abstract): pedestrian alignment rule plus neighbourhood averaging yields both local trajectory fit and global coherence; presented as the first experiment-driven bottom-up model of collective motion.
