## Summary

The authors introduce social honeypots to collect deceptive spam profiles and characterize their temporal, geographic, and content patterns. The retrieved abstract argues that these distinguishing properties could support automated detection, without claiming a trained classifier or contemporary LLM attribution.

## Contribution

Introduces social honeypots on MySpace to harvest spam profiles and characterize their temporal, geographic and content patterns.

## Key results

- Recognizable temporal and geographic patterns are reported qualitatively; no performance metric is in the abstract.

## Methods and models

Social honeypot collection and analysis of harvested spam profiles.

## Limitations and open questions

The historical platform population does not establish modern agent behavior. Proposed automatic detection should not be treated as an evaluated classifier.
