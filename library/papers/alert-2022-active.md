## Summary

Review of active turbulence: chaotic, self-sustained flows in bacterial suspensions, sperm, cytoskeletal extracts,
self-propelled colloids and tissues, which occur without inertia yet resemble inertial turbulence. Organised by polar vs
nematic order and wet vs dry. Compares power-law spectra (universal or not), transition scenarios from steady to chaotic
flow, and explains the absence of energy cascades by intrinsic length scales and self-organized energy injection. Read from the abstract on the page in `url`; details beyond the abstract are not checked.

## Contribution

The reference synthesis for chaotic collective flows (bacterial turbulence, [[wensink-2012-meso]],
[[dombrowski-2004-self]]; active nematic turbulence, [[sanchez-2012-spontaneous]], [[doostmohammadi-2018-active]]).

## Key results

- Power-law scaling with universal or non-universal exponents depending on class (abstract).
- No energy cascade, unlike inertial turbulence (abstract).

## Relevance to us

Dense swarms with alignment often fall into "swarming turbulence" rather than a clean flock; this review gives the
observables (energy spectra, vortex size) to recognise it. Related: [[wensink-2012-meso]], and [[das-2024-flocking]]
(same senior author).
