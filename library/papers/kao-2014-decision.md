## Summary

Two simple models show that when environmental cues are spatially or temporally correlated and multiple cues exist, collective accuracy is often maximised by a finite and frequently small group size rather than increasing monotonically as the wisdom-of-crowds view assumes. The noise inherent in small groups lets them avoid being dominated by correlated information while still pooling.

## Contribution

Challenges the assumption that bigger is better for collective decisions; contrast with the empirical group-size gains in [[sumpter-2008-consensus]] and [[ward-2011-fast]].

## Key results

- The classical wisdom-of-crowds increase appears only in a minority of environments (model).
- When it does not, a small finite group size maximises accuracy (model).
- Abstract-level reading only; numbers beyond the abstract were not checked.

## Methods and models

Majority-vote models with correlated and uncorrelated cues of differing reliability.
