## Summary

3D-printed robots on a vibrating table that spin clockwise or counter-clockwise ("rotors") are mixed. Experiments
show collective motion with super-diffusive interfacial motion and phase separation by spinodal decomposition
between the two spinning species; under confinement, long-time states favour symmetric demixing patterns.
Mapping rotor motion onto a Langevin equation with a constant driving torque, and comparing with simulations,
shows the macroscopic system behaves as active soft matter.

## Contribution

First experimental realisation of phase separation of oppositely rotating active particles, previously only
seen in simulations; establishes vibrated 3D-printed robots as a cheap active-matter platform.

## Key results

- Measured: spinodal decomposition and super-diffusive interfaces in a mixture of opposite rotors.
- Langevin model with constant torque reproduces the behaviour.

## Methods and models

Vibration-driven 3D-printed rotors; tracking; Langevin simulations. Abstract read.

## Limitations and open questions

No sensing or control; purely physical interactions.
