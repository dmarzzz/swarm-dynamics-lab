## Summary

A longitudinal measurement of text-repetition-based coordination in X's Japanese-classified stream: 734,173 Chinese-language coordinated accounts and about 495 million coordinated posts between May 2024 and March 2026. Most coordinated accounts are crude and keep classic automation marks; the same criterion applied to Japanese-language accounts gives a share six times lower. Content is overwhelmingly non-political, and most reactions in large cascades come from the coordinated accounts themselves.

## Contribution

A rare base-rate measurement of crude coordination in a live stream, and evidence that most of it talks to itself.

## Key results

- 734,173 coordinated accounts and about 495M coordinated posts over May 2024 to March 2026 (abstract).
- Coordinated share among Chinese-language accounts is six times that among Japanese-language accounts under the same criterion (abstract).
- In large observable cascades most reactions come from coordinated accounts; potentially harmful or illegal posts draw a comparatively high share of outside reactions (abstract).

## Methods and models

Text repetition across accounts as the coordination criterion; reaction cascade analysis. Precise thresholds not in the abstract.

## Limitations and open questions

Abstract only. Text repetition catches crude templates and will miss paraphrasing LLM swarms; the base rate is a lower bound for that reason (inference).

## Relevance to us

Gives a measured scale for crude coordinated activity on one platform in 2024 to 2026. The paraphrase gap is what LLM swarms exploit; see [[luceri-2026-coordinated]] for transcript-similarity failures.
