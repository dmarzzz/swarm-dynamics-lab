## Summary

Two-stage model in which each animal first makes a Bayesian estimate of which behaviour is best, using private information and the observed choices of others, and then chooses by probability matching. The resulting interaction rules depend on only two reliability parameters and reproduce a rich set of observed collective decision patterns in three-spined sticklebacks.

## Contribution

First-principles derivation of social decision rules from estimation; basis of [[arganda-2012-common]] and a precursor of [[mann-2018-collective]].

## Key results

- Derived rules quantitatively reproduce stickleback choice patterns from earlier experiments (model fit).
- Abstract-level reading only; numbers beyond the abstract were not checked.

## Methods and models

Bayesian estimation plus probability matching; comparison to published stickleback data.

## Limitations and open questions

Probability matching is assumed rather than derived; independence assumptions.

## Relevance to us

Gives agents a principled way to weight social versus private information. Related: [[sumpter-2008-consensus]].
