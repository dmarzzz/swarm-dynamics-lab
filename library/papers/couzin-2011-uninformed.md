## Summary

Theory and fish experiments (golden shiners trained to two colour targets) showing that a strongly opinionated minority can dictate group choice, but adding uninformed individuals returns control to the numerical majority. Uninformed individuals therefore promote democratic consensus rather than making groups more manipulable, contrary to earlier arguments.

## Contribution

Overturned the intuition that naive group members make groups vulnerable to extremists; the mechanism was later analysed in [[leonard-2012-decision]] and [[hartnett-2016-heterogeneous]].

## Key results

- A strongly opinionated minority wins in groups without uninformed members over a wide range of conditions (model and experiment).
- Adding uninformed individuals spontaneously inhibits minority control and restores majority choice (model and experiment).
- Abstract-level reading only; numbers beyond the abstract were not checked.

## Methods and models

Agent-based schooling model with two informed subgroups of different preference strength plus uninformed agents, and fish experiments with trained preferences (experimental details not checked beyond the abstract).

## Limitations and open questions

Preference strengths in the experiment were induced by training; the effect reverses with highly nonlinear interactions ([[hartnett-2016-heterogeneous]]).

## Relevance to us

Directly testable in any agent swarm with mixed informed and naive members. Related: [[couzin-2005-effective]], [[sridhar-2021-geometry]].
