## Summary

Argues that the "tsunami" of novel metaphor-based metaheuristics (insects, water flow, musicians, and so on) is
leading the field away from scientific rigour. Examines the historical context that led to metaphors being used as
inspiration and justification, why the field is vulnerable to this, and the fallacies involved, while noting that
genuinely innovative work continues; closes with properties of good research and promising avenues.

## Contribution

The founding critique of metaphor-driven swarm and evolutionary algorithms; every later critique
([[aranha-2022-metaphor]], [[camacho-villalon-2023-exposing]], [[campelo-2023-lessons]], [[kudela-2023-evolutionary]])
builds on it. Forward-citation chase (OpenCitations, 892 citing works) shows it is cited both by critics and,
ironically, by many new metaphor algorithms.

## Key results

- Position paper; no experiments. Claims: metaphors obscure algorithmic content, novelty is often terminological,
  and evaluation practices are weak.
