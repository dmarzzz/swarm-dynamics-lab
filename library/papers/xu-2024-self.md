## Summary

Dense bacterial populations spontaneously formed a centimetre-scale lattice of mesoscale, fast-spinning vortices, each containing about 10^4-10^5 motile cells, arranged with apparent hexagonal order, while cells within each vortex moved with strong polar and vortical order. Single-cell tracking and simulations point to self-enhanced mobility (cells move faster where local motion is already strong) as the mechanism, giving regular pattern formation from purely physical interactions without chemical signalling.

## Contribution

A new physical route to ordered large-scale patterns in living active matter, near the boundary between fluid-like and solid-like behaviour; extends collective-motion phenomenology from single vortices or turbulence to a regular vortex lattice.

## Key results

- Measured (abstract): vortex lattice over centimetre scales with apparent hexagonal order; 10^4-10^5 cells per vortex.
- Measured: strong polar and vortical order of cells within vortices (single-cell tracking).
- Model: simulations with self-enhanced mobility reproduce the patterning.

## Methods and models

Dense bacterial colonies, single-cell tracking, numerical simulations (details not read).

## Limitations and open questions

Abstract-level read; mechanism supported by simulations, and generality beyond the bacterial system is a claim.
