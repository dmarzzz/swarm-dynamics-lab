## Summary

This EC paper studies algorithmic repricing in e-commerce. The abstract describes initially falling prices after automation, followed by resetting strategies that induce competitors to raise prices. It distinguishes observed repricing effects from a model predicting increased cycling and possible future price increases.

## Contribution

Empirical algorithmic coordination can emerge through delegated pricing strategies rather than explicit communication.

## Key results

- Immediate price declines and competitor responses to resetting are reported qualitatively in this abstract; no numerical effect size is provided.

## Methods and models

E-commerce dataset, regression discontinuity evidence, plausibly exogenous variation in strategy execution, and a delegated-strategy equilibrium model.

## Limitations and open questions

This is a two-page EC publication; the abstract does not allow auditing identification assumptions. Its predictions should not be confused with measured coordination among LLM agents.

## Relevance to us

This provides a prior-art comparison for detecting coordinated automation, not evidence that any observed population is an LLM swarm.
