## Summary

The official post-mortem (often called the Silberman-Robb report) on why the US Intelligence Community wrongly judged that Iraq had weapons of mass destruction. I read the chapter overview and the biological warfare section through Finding 2 from a mirror of Chapter 1 (the official PDF is at govinfo.gov/app/details/GPO-WMD). The judgment that Iraq "has" biological weapons, made with "high confidence" in the October 2002 National Intelligence Estimate, rested almost entirely on one source, the Iraqi engineer codenamed "Curveball". A foreign liaison service debriefed him and passed reports to DIA's Defense HUMINT Service without giving the US direct access. Between January 2000 and September 2001 Defense HUMINT disseminated almost 100 reports from him. CIA formally deemed his reporting fabricated in May 2004. The Iraq Survey Group found no mobile BW programme.

## Contribution

A detailed, official case study of how a single unvetted source, filtered through an intermediary, became an institution's belief through repetition and apparent corroboration.

## Key results

- Volume masqueraded as confirmation: almost 100 reports from one source moved assessments from "could have" (late 1990s) to "has" (2002). The commission notes that a "daily drumbeat of reports on the same topic gives an impression of confirming evidence, even when the reports all come from the same source."
- The three "corroborating" sources each gave one report. One recanted in October 2003 and the report was never recalled. One (the INC source) had been formally judged a fabricator in May 2002, but his reporting re-entered finished intelligence, including the NIE and Secretary Powell's UN speech, because it was never recalled. The fourth is discussed only in the classified version.
- Defense HUMINT treated itself as a "conduit" with no duty to vet; the commission calls the failure even to attempt validation "a major failure in operational tradecraft".
- Analysts' validation can only check internal consistency, technical plausibility and fit with claimed access; it cannot establish bona fides or freedom from "hostile control", which requires operational checks.
- Analysts discounted early warning signs because the reporting fit their prior that Iraq was hiding programmes; contradicting evidence was read as further concealment.
- After the war, Curveball identified hydrogen-generation trailers as similar to his mobile labs, and a May 2003 CIA assessment cited them as evidence: the fabricated source kept "confirming" himself.
- The commission recommends a uniform requirement to reissue or recall reporting from sources later found to be fabricators.

## Methods and models

Official investigation with access to classified material, interviews with analysts and collectors, and comparison with Iraq Survey Group findings. The unclassified version omits some details.

## Limitations and open questions

A political-institutional document; findings reflect the commission's judgment. I read only the overview and the start of the BW section, not the chemical, delivery-system or recommendations chapters. Curveball was a fabricator for his own reasons, not (per this report) a doubled agent run by Iraq, so this is the "corrupted returning source" case without a controlling adversary.

## Relevance to us

Three direct lessons for fork-merge agents. Q2: apparent k-of-n agreement was really 1 source plus three weak, one-shot reports, one already known false; a threshold rule needs provenance of independence, not a count of reports, and repeated reports from one child must count once (as [[heuer-1999-psychology]] states in general terms). Q3: an intermediary that relays a child's output without vetting it (the "conduit") is where the merge goes wrong; in agent systems that is a summariser or orchestrator that forwards sub-agent output as fact, the confused-deputy pattern in [[liu-2026-safe]]. Defence: once a source is found corrupt, everything derived from it has to be recalled mechanically; human institutions failed to do this even after a formal fabrication notice, which argues for merge logs that support retraction by source, as in [[loven-2026-meld]]. The post-war self-confirmation by Curveball also shows why a suspect child should never be asked to adjudicate evidence about its own reports.
