## Summary

Three-choice experiments with schooling fish separate cohesion-driven from information-driven choices, showing that both shape consensus. Groups integrate two informational dimensions (colour and stripe orientation) to reach a consensus option that no individual specifically prefers, and fish in frontal positions balancing personal and social information are key.

## Contribution

Shows that binary-choice paradigms confound information and cohesion; complements [[couzin-2011-uninformed]].

## Key results

- Both information and social cohesion are needed to explain choices (experiment).
- Groups reach a consensus option that integrates multiple cue dimensions (experiment).
- Abstract-level reading only; numbers beyond the abstract were not checked.

## Methods and models

Schooling fish with trained cue preferences (colour, stripe orientation) in a three-choice paradigm; tracking of positions within the group.

## Limitations and open questions

Trained preferences; laboratory arenas.

## Relevance to us

Multi-option, multi-cue decisions are the realistic case for swarms; see also [[reina-2017-model]].
