## Summary

Historical and conceptual account of stigmergy, introduced by Pierre-Paul Grassé in 1959 to resolve the paradox that
insects in a society appear to work alone while their collective activity is coordinated. Describes the history of
the concept in social-insect research and distinguishes two mechanisms: quantitative stigmergy (stimulus intensity
modulates response, e.g. pheromone concentration) and qualitative stigmergy (qualitatively different stimuli trigger
different actions, e.g. building configurations).

## Contribution

Standard definition and taxonomy of stigmergy, the indirect environment-mediated coordination underlying ACO and many
swarm-robotics designs.

## Key results

- Conceptual paper; quantitative vs qualitative stigmergy distinction is the main output.
