## Summary

Uses publicly available online videos of heavy metal concerts to study highly energised human collective motion. Two states appear: a disordered, gas-like "mosh pit" and an ordered, vortex-like "circle pit". Both are reproduced by a flocking simulation of self-propelled agents with alignment, repulsion and noise, showing that human collective behaviour at these extremes is consistent with simple active-matter models.

## Contribution

The best-known demonstration that human crowds can be described by Vicsek-type flocking models, with a phase diagram between disordered and milling states controlled by noise and alignment.

## Key results

- Measured (abstract): mosh pits have gas-like velocity statistics; circle pits are coherent vortices.
- Simulated: a flocking model of self-propelled agents reproduces both states.

## Methods and models

Video analysis; agent-based flocking simulation with self-propulsion, repulsion, alignment ("flocking") and noise. arXiv 1302.1886 (title on arXiv: "Collective Motion of Moshers at Heavy Metal Concerts"; arXiv id left out of the arxiv field because the title differs from the published one). PRL 110, 228701.

## Limitations and open questions

Uncontrolled online video; quantitative comparison limited. [[gu-2025-emergence]] notes that dense crowd quakes are different from circle-pit vortices. Abstract only.

## Relevance to us

Human swarm in the regime where alignment dominates goal-seeking: the closest human analogue of Vicsek flocking ([[vicsek-1995-novel]]) and milling. A ready-made sanity check for a flocking simulator.
