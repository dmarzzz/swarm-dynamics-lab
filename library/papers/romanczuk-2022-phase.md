## Summary

Book chapter reviewing phase transitions in animal collectives and the "criticality hypothesis" (that groups
operate near a critical point to optimise collective computation), including methods to measure distance
from criticality and functional consequences. Highlights an emerging view that de-emphasises being exactly
critical in favour of groups tuning to an optimal distance from criticality. Read at abstract level from
arXiv.

## Contribution

The most recent critical review of criticality claims stemming from [[cavagna-2010-scale]] and
[[bialek-2012-statistical]], written by a modeller who also produced empirical tests
([[gomez-nava-2023-fish]]).

## Key results

- Abstract-level: points out difficulties of applying statistical-physics concepts to biological groups;
  argues for tunable distance from criticality.
