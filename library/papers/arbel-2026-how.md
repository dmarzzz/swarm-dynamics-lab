## Summary

A legal-theory article on identifying AI agents for liability. AIs lack bodies and can copy, split, merge, swarm and vanish, and a "single" agent is often an ensemble of instances on several models. The authors distinguish thin identification (tying every AI action to some human principal) from thick identification (sorting AI entities into discrete, persistent units with stable goals, needed when principals cannot fully control their agents). They propose the Algorithmic Corporation (A-corp), a legal entity owned by humans and run by AIs that can hold property including compute, contract and litigate; they argue incentives and selection push A-corps to self-organise into persistent entities with coherent goals that respond to liability.

## Contribution

States the individuation problem behind Sybil resistance for AI agents in legal terms and proposes resource ownership (compute) as the anchor for agent identity.
