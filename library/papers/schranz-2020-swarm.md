## Summary

A review asking why swarm robotics has not reached industry. The authors catalogue "basic swarm behaviours"
in four groups (spatial organisation, navigation, decision making, miscellaneous), use this taxonomy to classify
research and industrial applications, and list research platforms, commercial systems and market-targeted
projects. Their finding is that genuine swarm applications are rare: many industrial multi-robot systems keep
centralised control and drop the core swarm idea of distributed decision making.

## Contribution

An applications-oriented complement to [[brambilla-2013-swarm]], with an inventory of platforms and products.

## Key results

- Reasons identified for low uptake: emergent behaviour is hard to predict and certify; communication
  architectures do not fit swarm needs, pushing systems back to central infrastructure; testing in productive
  environments is risky and simulations are not accurate enough.
- Research platforms are seen as the route from theory to prototypes.

## Methods and models

Narrative review and classification; abstract read.

## Limitations and open questions

Classification is qualitative; no quantitative evidence on deployment.
