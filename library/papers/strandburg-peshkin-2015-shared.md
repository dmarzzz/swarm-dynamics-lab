## Summary

High-resolution GPS tracking of a wild baboon troop shows shared decision-making in collective movement: baboons are more likely to follow when several initiators agree, rather than preferentially following dominant individuals. When initiators conflict, baboons choose one direction if the angle between options is large and compromise if it is small, matching predictions of collective-motion models.

## Contribution

Field confirmation, in a socially stratified primate, of the compromise-versus-choice transition predicted by [[couzin-2005-effective]] and [[leonard-2012-decision]]; key evidence for democratic decisions in [[conradt-2005-consensus]]'s terms.

## Key results

- Following probability increases with the number of agreeing initiators; dominance does not predict influence (field data).
- Angular threshold between compromise and choice in conflicts (field data; numeric threshold not in abstract).
- Abstract-level reading only; numbers beyond the abstract were not checked.

## Methods and models

High-resolution GPS tracking of members of a wild baboon troop; analysis of who follows whom as a function of the number and dominance of initiators and the angle between their directions.

## Limitations and open questions

One troop; observational.
