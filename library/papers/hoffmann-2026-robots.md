## Summary

The authors measure web permission and descriptor files across roughly four million domains. The abstract compares robots.txt, ai.txt, llms.txt, and llms-full.txt, finding dominant robots.txt use, emerging AI-specific files, restrictive directives, rare cross-file permission conflicts, and some descriptor links pointing to blocked content.

## Contribution

Large-scale measurement of permission and descriptor files across domains.

## Key results

- Approximately 4M domains; robots.txt remains dominant and robots.txt/ai.txt conflicts are infrequent.

## Methods and models

Large-scale measurement of permission and descriptor files across domains.

## Limitations and open questions

Permission files signal intended policy rather than demonstrate crawler compliance; the abstract lacks adoption percentages and enforcement outcomes.

## Relevance to us

A comparison source for coordinated automation and security measurement. Full-text methods and replication have not been checked.
