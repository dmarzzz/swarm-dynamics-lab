## Summary

Analyses video of the crowd disaster at the Jamarat bridge in Mina during the Hajj on 12 January 2006. The recordings show two sudden transitions as density increases: from laminar to stop-and-go flow, and then to irregular "turbulent" flow with sudden, earthquake-like mass displacements that make people fall and be trampled. The study informed organisational changes credited with a safe Hajj the following year.

## Contribution

The key empirical study of crowd disasters; introduced "crowd turbulence" and the crowd-pressure measure P = ρ Var(V) used to locate danger zones. Its turbulence interpretation has been revisited by [[gu-2025-emergence]], which finds periodic chiral oscillations rather than turbulence in similar dense crowds.

## Key results

- Measured (abstract): laminar to stop-and-go transition consistent with a bottleneck-flow model; subsequent transition to turbulent flow with pressure releases comparable to earthquakes.
- Not extracted here: specific densities and displacement statistics (abstract only). [[moussaid-2011-simple]] cites a displacement power-law exponent consistent with about 2 from this analysis.

## Methods and models

Video tracking and local field estimation (speed, density, crowd pressure) from surveillance footage; PRE 75, 046109.

## Limitations and open questions

Single event; density estimates from video are uncertain; "turbulence" was descriptive rather than a spectral or statistical identification. Abstract-level read only.

## Relevance to us

Defines operational early-warning observables for dense swarms and is the empirical benchmark for crowd-quake models ([[moussaid-2011-simple]], [[gu-2025-emergence]]). See also [[helbing-2012-crowd]].
