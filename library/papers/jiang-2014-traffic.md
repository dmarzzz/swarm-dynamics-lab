## Summary

Large-scale car-following experiment with a platoon of 25 cars on an open road section, studying how disturbances grow along the platoon. The observed growth pattern contradicts traditional car-following theory: optimal velocity, full velocity difference, intelligent driver and inertial models reproduce qualitatively different growth. New models in which the traffic state can span a two-dimensional region of the velocity–spacing plane (removing the assumption of a unique equilibrium speed for each spacing) reproduce the observed growth qualitatively or quantitatively.

## Contribution

An experimental challenge to the fundamental-diagram assumption underlying most car-following models, supporting multi-state (Kerner-style) views of congested traffic.

## Key results

- Measured: growth pattern of speed oscillations along a 25-car platoon differs from predictions of four standard models.
- Modelled: allowing a 2D region of steady states in the velocity–spacing plane reproduces the experiment.
- Limitation noted by authors: at 55 km/h leader speed the stationary state lasted only about 2 minutes due to road length.

## Methods and models

Open-road platoon experiment (measurement details not read); simulations of OV, FVD, IDM and inertial models with noise (IDM parameters used: v_max = 80 km/h, T = 1.6 s, a = 0.73 m/s^2, b = 1.67 m/s^2, s0 = 2 m). PLoS ONE 9(4), e94351 (CC BY).

## Limitations and open questions

Single platoon size; high-speed behaviour not revealed. Skimmed.
