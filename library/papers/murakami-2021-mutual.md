## Summary

Bidirectional flow experiments in which some pedestrians at the front of one group walk while typing on a mobile phone. Distracting a few individuals delayed the onset of global lane formation and slowed the whole crowd, and both distracted and non-distracted pedestrians made more abrupt local collision-avoidance manoeuvres. The authors conclude that avoidance is normally cooperative and that mutual anticipation between pedestrians facilitates efficient self-organisation.

## Contribution

A causal, manipulation-based test of the role of anticipation in emergent order in a human swarm, complementing correlational evidence such as [[karamouzas-2014-universal]].

## Key results

- Measured: distraction of a subset delays lane formation and slows overall movement; local avoidance increases for distracted and for nearby non-distracted pedestrians.
- Cited context: walkers typing look at the phone about 75% of the time (prior study).
- Interpreted: reduced anticipation propagates through the crowd and disturbs self-organisation.

## Methods and models

Controlled bidirectional flow experiments with tracking, by authors at the University of Tokyo and Nagaoka University of Technology. Sci. Adv. 7(12), eabe7758; open access via PMC7968841.

## Limitations and open questions

Laboratory groups of moderate size; distraction may change more than anticipation (for example gait). Skimmed (abstract, results headlines, discussion).

## Relevance to us

Evidence that predictive, mutually anticipating agents self-organise faster than reactive ones, a direct design principle for robot swarms and for agents in mixed human–robot crowds. Compare [[bonnemain-2023-pedestrians]] (game-theoretic anticipation) and [[moussaid-2011-simple]].
