## Summary

Controlled experiments with pedestrians performing simple avoidance tasks provide a large trajectory dataset from which a behavioural map is computed: the average change in direction and speed as a function of interaction distance and angle. Pedestrians show a side preference when choosing which way to evade, amplified by mutual interaction; a binary interaction model built on these findings reproduces two asymmetric lanes observed in a crowded street.

## Contribution

One of the first individual-level experimental calibrations of pedestrian interaction laws, following the approach used to infer interaction rules in fish schools and other animal groups.

## Key results

- Measured (abstract): behavioural map of speed and direction changes; side preference amplified by mutual interaction.
- Simulated vs observed: two asymmetric opposite-direction lanes in quantitative agreement with street observations.

## Methods and models

Laboratory avoidance experiments with tracking; Proc. R. Soc. B 276, 2755–2762. The binary interaction model is built from the measured behavioural map (exact form not read).

## Limitations and open questions

Simple two-body tasks; extension to multi-body interactions is the problem addressed in [[moussaid-2011-simple]]. Abstract only.

## Relevance to us

Template for inferring swarm interaction rules from controlled pairwise experiments, the same programme as in animal collective motion. Feeds [[moussaid-2011-simple]] and [[helbing-1995-social]] calibration.
