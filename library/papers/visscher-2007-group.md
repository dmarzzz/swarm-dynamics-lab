## Summary

Annual Review of Entomology article on nest-site selection by swarm-founding and emigrating social insects, chiefly honeybees and Temnothorax ants. Scouts discover sites and integrate multiple properties into quality assessments, sites compete for a limited pool of scouts with attrition of less-favoured ones, and a quorum at one site triggers the colony's move, which is coordinated differently in different species.

## Contribution

Concise comparative review establishing the shared algorithm (independent assessment, competitive recruitment, quorum trigger) across bees and ants; complements [[franks-2002-information]].

## Key results

- The main features of collective nest choice are strikingly similar across the few species studied in detail (synthesis).
- Abstract-level reading only; numbers beyond the abstract were not checked.

## Methods and models

Review of field and laboratory experiments.

## Limitations and open questions

Few species covered; 2007 coverage predates the discovery of stop-signal cross-inhibition [[seeley-2012-stop]].

## Relevance to us

Biological ground truth for the algorithm most swarm decision designs copy; see [[reina-2015-design]] and [[valentini-2017-best]].
