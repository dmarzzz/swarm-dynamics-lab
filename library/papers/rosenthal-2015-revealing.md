## Summary

Tracks positions, body postures and reconstructed visual fields of all fish in schools of about 150 golden shiners during startle cascades, and infers the sensory interaction network through which behavioural change spreads. Networks are weighted, directed and heterogeneous; individuals with few but strongly connected neighbours are most influential and most susceptible, and cascades can be predicted at initiation.

## Contribution

Moves from metric or topological neighbourhood assumptions to measured visual networks for information transfer; complements [[chase-2025-physics]] on information propagation.

## Key results

- Schools of about 150 fish; simple visual measures predict response (experiment).
- Cascade size is predictable at initiation from the network (model fit).
- Abstract-level reading only; numbers beyond the abstract were not checked.

## Methods and models

Automated tracking of positions and body postures, reconstruction of visual fields, fitted sensory-response model, network analysis.

## Limitations and open questions

One species and one behaviour (evasion).
