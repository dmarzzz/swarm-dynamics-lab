## Summary

Repeats the indicative-word method on the full text of published papers across all fields in the Dimensions database and concludes that LLM tools were likely involved in producing more than 10% of all papers published in 2024. Reconciles this with earlier estimates and argues that LLM text generation is still being concealed or downplayed by authors, so disclosure requirements are needed.

## Contribution

A whole-literature, full-text follow-up to [[gray-2024-chatgpt]], growing the floor from about 1% (2023) to more than 10% (2024).

## Key results

- Measured (abstract): more than 10% of all 2024 published papers likely involved LLM tools, by disproportionate use of indicative words.
- Claimed (abstract): evidence that use of LLMs for text generation is concealed or downplayed.

## Methods and models

Indicative-word frequency growth in full text of Dimensions-indexed papers; synthesis with prior estimates. Abstract read only.

## Limitations and open questions

Same marker-word fragility as the 2024 version. Abstract depth.

## Relevance to us

Shows the scale-up of machine text in one large corpus over one year; the concealment claim is relevant to adversarial evasion of detection.
