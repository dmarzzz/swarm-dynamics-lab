## Summary

Long review of the hydrodynamic (continuum) theory of flocks. Flocks have phases classified by symmetry like
equilibrium matter (polar or "ferromagnetic", nematic, disordered), and all flocks in one phase share the same
long-wavelength hydrodynamics. The polar phase shows true long-range order in 2D despite Mermin-Wagner;
the nematic phase shows giant number fluctuations growing faster than sqrt(N); coupling to a surrounding
fluid makes polar order unstable in low-Reynolds suspensions. Read: abstract and introduction of the
journal PDF hosted by ICTP.

## Contribution

The reference review for Toner-Tu theory ([[toner-1995-long]], [[toner-1998-flocks]]) and its extension to
nematic and wet (momentum-conserving) flocks, bridging into active matter ([[marchetti-2013-hydrodynamics]]).

## Key results

- Claimed: polar flocks break a continuous symmetry in d = 2, violating the equilibrium Mermin-Wagner
  theorem, because self-propulsion advects order.
- Claimed: active nematics have number fluctuations scaling faster than sqrt(N) ("giant number fluctuations").
- Claimed: polar order is always unstable for low-Reynolds-number suspensions (hydrodynamic backflow);
  experiments appeared consistent, quantitative comparison pending in 2005.

## Methods and models

Symmetry-based fluctuating hydrodynamic equations (velocity and density fields), linear analysis and dynamic
renormalization group.

## Limitations and open questions

Only abstract and introduction read. The 2D exponent predictions inherited from 1995 were later shown to be
incorrect for Vicsek flocks ([[mahault-2019-quantitative]], [[chate-2024-dynamic]]).

## Relevance to us

Theory background for interpreting density fluctuations and long-range order in any large simulated swarm.
