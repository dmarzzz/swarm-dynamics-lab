## Summary

Treats self-organisation of collective motion in simulated self-propelled particles as a
thermodynamic process. Using curvatures of generalised internal energy and work with respect to two alignment
control parameters, the authors find that across the kinetic phase transition configuration entropy falls, rates
of change of work and internal energy fall, their curvatures diverge, and the entropy reduction per unit of work
peaks at criticality. Fisher information appears as the curvature of free entropy.

## Contribution

Introduces thermodynamic efficiency of collective motion and shows it peaks at the flocking
transition; the basis for the super-efficiency principle in [[chen-2025-why]].

## Key results

- Entropy reduction per unit of work (thermodynamic efficiency) peaks at criticality (simulation).
- Curvature of internal energy = curvature of free entropy (Fisher information) minus curvature of configuration entropy.

## Methods and models

Self-propelled particle model with two alignment control parameters; Fisher information and
configuration entropy estimated from simulations. arXiv 1708.04004.

## Limitations and open questions

Quasi-static equilibrium-style thermodynamic treatment of an active system; abstract-level read.

## Relevance to us

Fisher information with respect to a control parameter is a robust criticality indicator for
simulated swarms, unlike transfer entropy ([[brown-2020-information]]).
