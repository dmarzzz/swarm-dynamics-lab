## Summary

Wensink and colleagues combine experiments on dense Bacillus subtilis suspensions (quasi-2D and 3D), simulations of
self-propelled rods, and a minimal continuum model to characterize meso-scale bacterial turbulence through energy spectra
and structure functions. The bacterial flow statistics agree with the rod model, suggesting short-range interactions
dominate at high concentration; they propose a generalized Navier–Stokes (Toner–Tu–Swift–Hohenberg) continuum model for
incompressible bacterial flow that reproduces many observed features in 2D. Read from the abstract on the page in `url`; details beyond the abstract are not checked.

## Contribution

Introduced the widely used continuum model of active turbulence (the "Wensink" or Toner–Tu–Swift–Hohenberg
equation), see [[shaebani-2020-computational]] (generalized Navier–Stokes, read in full) and [[alert-2022-active]].

## Key results

- Measured energy spectra and structure functions of bacterial turbulence in 2D and 3D (abstract).
- Rod simulations match the flow statistics (abstract).

## Methods and models

Experiment (PIV on B. subtilis), self-propelled rod simulations, continuum PDE. arXiv:1208.4239; full text not read.
