## Summary

Devil facial tumour disease (DFTD) is a cancer passed between Tasmanian devils as a living cell line, emerged about 16 years before the paper and causes 100 percent mortality among infected devils with no apparent rejection, even though the cells are an allograft. The authors show DFTD cells express no cell-surface MHC molecules in vitro or in vivo, because genes of the antigen-processing pathway (beta2-microglobulin, TAP transporters) are down-regulated. This is not due to structural mutation but to regulatory change including histone deacetylation. Treating DFTD cells with recombinant devil interferon-gamma restores MHC class I to the surface in vitro, and MHC class I can appear in vivo during lymphocyte infiltration. They propose MHC-positive or epigenetically modified DFTD cells as a vaccine and suggest reversible regulatory down-regulation lets transmissible cancers stay evolvable. Abstract only.

## Contribution

Explains the immune escape of a transmissible cancer as reversible hiding of the identity markers, not loss of the genes, and shows the markers can be forced back.

## Key results

- DFTD cells lack surface MHC in vitro and in vivo (abstract).
- Cause is regulatory and epigenetic, not structural mutation (abstract).
- IFN-gamma restores MHC class I in vitro (abstract).

## Methods and models

Expression analysis of antigen-processing genes, flow cytometry or staining for MHC, IFN-gamma treatment (abstract only).
