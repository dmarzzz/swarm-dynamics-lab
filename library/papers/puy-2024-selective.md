## Summary

Introduces a force-map technique for alignment forces, validated on an agent-based model, and applies it to schooling fish data. Fish show an effective alignment with faster neighbours and an apparent anti-alignment with slower ones; the authors explain the latter not as explicit anti-alignment but as selective attention, with fish paying less attention to slower neighbours. This implies transient, speed-based leadership, supported by agent-based modelling and leader-follower analysis of the data.

## Contribution

Empirical evidence for selective, speed-dependent attention in fish schools and a method (alignment force maps) to detect it.

## Key results

- Measured (abstract): alignment with faster neighbours; apparent anti-alignment with slower neighbours.
- Model and data (abstract): speed-induced temporary leadership.

## Methods and models

Force maps of focal-fish acceleration vs neighbour variables; agent-based model with selective attention (details not read).

## Limitations and open questions

Abstract only; force maps are correlational and confounded by group geometry.
