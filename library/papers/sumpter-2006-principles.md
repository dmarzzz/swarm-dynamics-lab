## Summary

Review of where the self-organization approach succeeds in explaining collective animal behaviour (ant
trails, cockroach aggregation, applause, fish school migration) and where it is insufficient (honeybee
foraging with complex signals). Argues that the field should identify recurring principles of individual
behavioural algorithms and information flow, such as positive feedback, response thresholds and individual
integrity. Crossref lists the online date as 2005; the issue is 2006. Read at abstract level via Europe PMC.

## Contribution

An influential biology-side synthesis that reframes collective behaviour around a short list of
mechanistic principles, complementary to the physics-side review [[vicsek-2012-collective]].

## Key results

- Qualitative claims: simple repeated interactions explain many group patterns; animals are complex
  individuals, so some phenomena need individual complexity; principles recur across taxa.

## Methods and models

Narrative review.

## Limitations and open questions

Abstract-level read. Pre-dates high-resolution tracking; principles are proposed, not tested.

## Relevance to us

Useful vocabulary (positive feedback, thresholds, integrity) for describing what makes our agents coordinate.
