## Summary

Analyses about 1,500 pedestrian groups (friends, couples, families) walking under natural conditions. At low density group members walk side by side in a line perpendicular to motion; as density increases the line bends forward into a V shape. A model based on social communication between members reproduces these formations; the V shape facilitates conversation but reduces flow.

## Contribution

Showed that up to 70% of people in crowds walk in groups and that groups are mesoscale structures with their own dynamics, which pair-interaction crowd models had ignored.

## Key results

- Measured (abstract): up to 70% of pedestrians move in groups; line abreast at low density, V-like at higher density.
- Inferred: V formation trades flow efficiency for social interaction.

## Methods and models

Video observation of natural crowds; a model based on social communication between group members (exact form not read). PLoS ONE 5(4), e10047.

## Limitations and open questions

Observational; group relations inferred visually. Abstract only.

## Relevance to us

A concrete case of sub-group structure inside a swarm, analogous to multi-scale cohesion in robot swarms and flocks. Corbetta's review discusses later group models ([[corbetta-2023-physics]]).
