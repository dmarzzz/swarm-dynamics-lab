## Summary

Records spontaneous local field potentials from rat cortical cultures and slices on 60-channel arrays
and finds that activity propagates in "neuronal avalanches" whose size distribution follows a power law with
exponent -3/2 and whose branching parameter is close to the critical value 1. Simulations show a branching
parameter of 1 optimises information transmission while preventing runaway excitation.

## Contribution

The seminal empirical paper for criticality in living networks, which introduced avalanche
statistics and the branching ratio as measurements later borrowed for animal groups.

## Key results

- Avalanche size distribution power law with exponent -3/2 (measured).
- Branching parameter close to 1 (measured).
- Branching parameter 1 optimises information transmission in feedforward network simulations.

## Methods and models

Organotypic cultures and acute slices of rat cortex; 60-channel multielectrode arrays; avalanche
detection by binning; branching process model.

## Limitations and open questions

The exponent depends on binning and measurement method (noted in [[mora-2011-biological]]);
power laws alone are not proof ([[touboul-2017-power]]). Abstract-level read.

## Relevance to us

Avalanche-size statistics and branching ratio are directly applicable to cascades in swarms
(startle cascades in [[poel-2022-subcritical]], dive waves in [[gomez-nava-2023-fish]]).
