## Summary

Ring-arena experiments with desert locust nymphs test the SPP prediction that increasing density causes a rapid
transition from disordered to aligned collective motion. They report such a transition with a critical
density for the onset of coordinated marching, and at field-typical densities a dynamic instability in which
groups switch direction spontaneously, which may speed directional information transfer. Read at abstract
level via Europe PMC.

## Contribution

The flagship empirical support for the density-driven Vicsek transition in animals, now contested:
[[sayin-2025-behavioral]] re-analysed these data and reports alignment independent of density once
group-size statistics are accounted for.

## Key results

- Claimed (measured): rapid density-dependent disorder-to-order transition, a critical density for marching,
  spontaneous direction switching at intermediate densities.
- Re-analysis in 2025 disputes the density dependence.

## Methods and models

Locust nymphs (Schistocerca gregaria) at varying densities in an annular arena; alignment order parameter vs
density; comparison with a 1D SPP model.

## Limitations and open questions

Abstract-level read. Ring geometry is effectively 1D; density and order are confounded (the point made by
[[sayin-2025-behavioral]]).

## Relevance to us

Classic example to cite carefully: pair it with the 2025 re-analysis. The 1D ring is also an easy experiment
to replicate in simulation.
