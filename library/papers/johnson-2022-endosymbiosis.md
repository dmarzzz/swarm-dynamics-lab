## Summary

The authors extend Symbulation to compare pathways toward obligate mutualistic endosymbiosis. Their abstract reports that allowing facultative ectosymbiosis broadens the environmental conditions supporting endosymbiosis, while reducing its evolution in some conditions where it otherwise occurs.

## Contribution

Tests how an intermediate interaction state changes evolutionary accessibility in an agent-based biological model.

## Key results

- The reported direction depends on environmental conditions; ectosymbiosis is not uniformly beneficial.
- Evidence comes from simulation, not an LLM system or a wet-lab organism experiment.

## Methods and models

Evolutionary agent-based Symbulation; primary repository abstract read through direct HTTPS retrieval after browser-tool fetch failures.

## Limitations and open questions

Abstract-depth only. Replicate counts, effect sizes, parameters and code were not audited or run. This entry describes the March 8 preprint; its landing page identifies it as not peer reviewed. Do not conflate it with the later conference version.
