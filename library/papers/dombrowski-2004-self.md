## Summary

Dombrowski, Goldstein, Kessler and colleagues observe aerobic bacteria in drops: chemotaxis plus buoyancy generate
flows that concentrate cells at the drop edge (sessile) or bottom (pendant); the concentrated regions show transient,
reconstituting high-speed jets flanked by vortex streets on scales much larger than a cell, which they attribute to
hydrodynamic interactions between swimming cells. Read from the PubMed abstract (via the PubMed E-utilities and the Europe PMC REST record in `url`; publisher page blocked automated access); details beyond the abstract are not checked.

## Contribution

Early observation of bacterial turbulence ("zooming bionematic"), the experimental motivation for wet active-matter
theory ([[simha-2002-hydrodynamic]]) and later [[wensink-2012-meso]].

## Key results

- Measured: self-concentration by bioconvection and large-scale coherent jets and vortices (abstract).
- Proposed: hydrodynamic mechanism for coherence (abstract).

## Methods and models

Experiment on Bacillus subtilis in sessile and pendant drops, PIV. PRL 93, 098103.

## Limitations and open questions

Mechanism proposed, not quantified; geometry-specific.
