## Summary

A tightly packed collective of robotic units acts like a material that can be both strong (bear loads) and fluid (change shape). Modulating the tangential forces between units controls topological rearrangements (T1-like neighbour exchanges), which gives local, spatiotemporal control of rigidity transitions between solid-like and fluid-like states. The collective forms and heals structures and supports 700 N, 500 times a robot's weight, before 'melting' under its own weight when fluidised.

## Contribution

It realises 'robotic matter' with controllable jamming and unjamming, borrowing the physics of tissue fluidisation (Campàs) for swarm robotics. It is a physical counterpart to programmable self-assembly ([[rubenstein-2014-programmable]]).

## Key results

- Local control of solid-to-fluid rigidity transitions via inter-unit tangential forces (measured).
- Supports 700 N, 500 times a single robot's weight; shape forming and self-healing demonstrated.

## Methods and models

Robotic units with actuated tangential (gear-like) edge interactions in a dense 2-D packing (per the abstract; details not checked).
