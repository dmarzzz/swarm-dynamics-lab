## Summary

Experiments with golden shiner schools tracking a dynamic light field (the fish prefer dark) show that group-level gradient tracking emerges from individuals slowing down in darker regions and interacting socially, not from pooling individual gradient estimates. Collective sensing improves sharply with group size while single fish barely respond to the gradient.

## Contribution

Established 'emergent sensing' as distinct from wisdom-of-crowds averaging; it motivated the evolutionary model [[hein-2015-evolution]] and is a standard example of distributed computation in moving groups.

## Key results

- Individuals modulate speed in response to local scalar light level and turn towards neighbours; group-level gradient climbing emerges (experiment plus model).
- Emergent problem solving, not enhancement of personal estimates, is the predominant mechanism (authors' conclusion from model comparison).
- Abstract-level reading only; numbers beyond the abstract were not checked.

## Methods and models

Golden shiners (Notemigonus crysoleucas, species confirmed in [[hein-2015-evolution]]) in groups of various sizes tracking a dynamic light field; tracking and agent-based model.

## Limitations and open questions

One species and one cue; the mechanism was inferred from model fits.

## Relevance to us

A cheap mechanism to put on robots (speed modulation by a scalar sensor plus cohesion), noted by the authors as appropriate for robotic agents. Related: [[hein-2015-evolution]], [[kao-2014-decision]], [[chase-2025-physics]].
