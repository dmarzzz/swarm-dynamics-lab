## Summary

This review argues that social-bot definitions and detection methods often mismatch. It organizes bots by intention, coordination, and rule-based versus generative operation, and evaluates four detection-method families on a Bluesky dataset containing 4,071 bots and 6,386 human accounts.

## Contribution

A three-axis typology links bot definitions to comparative detection methodology.

## Key results

- The abstract reports 4,071 bots and 6,386 humans and comparison of rule-based, supervised, unsupervised, and LLM-based detection; no accuracy table is available here.

## Methods and models

Systematic review, multiclass Bluesky dataset, and comparison of four detection-method families.

## Limitations and open questions

Dataset labeling, performance differences, and cross-platform generalization cannot be assessed from the abstract. A method-label mismatch warning is not itself a demonstrated swarm detector.

## Relevance to us

This provides a prior-art comparison for detecting coordinated automation, not evidence that any observed population is an LLM swarm.
