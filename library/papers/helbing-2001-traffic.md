## Summary

A 75-page Reviews of Modern Physics article treating vehicle traffic, pedestrians and other self-driven many-particle systems with the tools of statistical physics and nonlinear dynamics. It covers empirical traffic data, microscopic (particle), mesoscopic (gas-kinetic) and macroscopic (fluid) models, the micro–macro link, and connections to bacterial colonies, bird flocks, panics and markets.

## Contribution

The standard physics review that unified highway traffic, pedestrian flows and flocking under "self-driven many-particle systems". It answers questions such as why phantom jams form, why congestion appears below capacity, why counterflowing pedestrians form lanes while similar systems "freeze by heating", and why panicking crowds deadlock.

## Key results

- Review-level claims (from abstract): phantom jams and stop-and-go traffic arise from instability of homogeneous flow; several congested states exist and are related; temporary reductions in volume can cause lasting jams; speed limits can speed up traffic; lanes form in pedestrian counterflow ([[helbing-1995-social]]), freezing by heating ([[helbing-2000-freezing]]); panics produce deadlocks ([[helbing-2000-simulating]]).
