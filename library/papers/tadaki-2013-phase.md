## Summary

Larger follow-up to [[sugiyama-2008-traffic]]: an indoor 314 m circuit in the Nagoya Dome, identical cars (Toyota Vitz), positions measured with a laser scanner, sessions with 10 to 40 cars. Jammed flow appears at high densities and free flow persists at low densities, with signs of metastability in between. The critical density, estimated from speed fluctuations and density–flow relations, lies between 0.08 m^-1 (25 cars) and 0.09 m^-1 (28 cars).

## Contribution

Turns the qualitative jam demonstration into a density sweep with an estimate of the critical density, supporting the interpretation of jam formation as a dynamical phase transition.

## Key results

- Measured: critical density between 0.08 and 0.09 m^-1 at free-flow speeds of about 40 km/h; jam clusters move backward at about 20 km/h.
- Argued: consistent with an expressway critical density of about 25 cars/km at 120 km/h, because required headway scales with speed.
- Observed: indications of metastability at intermediate density.

## Methods and models

Indoor circuit, laser scanner tracking, space-time diagrams, speed variance and fundamental diagram analysis. New J. Phys. 15, 103034 (open access, CC BY-NC-SA via Osaka repository).

## Limitations and open questions

Single car model and trained drivers; finite ring. Skimmed (abstract, setup, key results, conclusions).

## Relevance to us
