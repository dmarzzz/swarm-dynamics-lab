## Summary

Zhou and Brady use theory and large-scale active fast Stokesian dynamics of squirmers to show that collision-induced
pusher force dipoles, present even for neutral squirmers, destroy MIPS once hydrodynamic interactions are included.
Rotational HIs shorten persistence (reducing swim pressure) and translational HIs create confinement-scale advective
flows; collisional dipoles near boundaries can create attractive flows explaining aggregation in some experiments. MIPS
can be recovered by weakening HIs with brush-like coatings. Read from the abstract in the Semantic Scholar API record in `url`; details beyond the abstract are not checked.

## Contribution

Addresses the long-standing question raised in [[cates-2015-motility]] (read in full) of whether hydrodynamics
suppresses MIPS, answering yes for squirmers.

- HIs suppress MIPS via rotational and translational mechanisms (abstract; simulation and theory).
