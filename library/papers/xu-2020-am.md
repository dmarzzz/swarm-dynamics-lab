## Summary

Proposes ETH-EDS, a detector for eclipse attacks on Ethereum nodes (an attacker monopolising a victim's outgoing peer connections so it sees only attacker-controlled views of the network). The authors collect normal and attack traffic on the Ethereum P2P layer, find that attack packets differ in packet size, access frequency and access time, and train a random-forest classifier on those features; the abstract reports that the classifier identifies the malicious node "with high probability" and "with high accuracy" but gives no numbers. Abstract only (publisher paywalled; the NTNU open-access handle listed by Unpaywall did not resolve to a PDF from this box; abstract confirmed on the Surrey and Macquarie repository pages). Reference list (via Exa) shows it builds on Sit and Morris 2002, the DHT security survey of Urdaneta et al. and DDoS/flash-crowd discrimination work.

## Contribution

A traffic-feature machine-learning detector for eclipse attacks on a live blockchain client, framed from the victim's side ("am I eclipsed?") rather than as a protocol redesign.

## Key results

- Three discriminative features: packets_size, access_frequencies, access_time.
- Random forest achieves "high accuracy" on collected traffic (abstract; no figures visible).

## Methods and models

Traffic capture from Ethereum nodes under normal and attack conditions; random-forest classification. Dataset size, attack generation method and evaluation protocol not visible.

## Limitations and open questions

Abstract-level, and the abstract is vague on results; feature set is shallow and likely evadable by an attacker that pacing its traffic; Ethereum's discovery protocol has changed since. The 95 citations are more about the topic than the method.

## Relevance to us

Marginal. It is one of the few papers that treat eclipse/Sybil as a detection problem using behavioural traffic features, which is the posture swarm-detection takes toward agent populations, so it is tagged there as a weak precedent. Better anchors: [[heilman-2015-eclipse]] (the Ethereum/Bitcoin eclipse mechanics), [[tan-2019-toward]] (Tor), [[puttaswamy-2009-securing]] (self-certifying detection in overlays), and [[urdaneta-2011-survey]] (which this paper cites).
