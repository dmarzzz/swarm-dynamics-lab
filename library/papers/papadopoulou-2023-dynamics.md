## Summary

Comparative study of collective motion in four species (stickleback shoals, homing pigeon flocks, a goat herd
and a chacma baboon troop): local patterns (neighbour distances and positions) and group patterns (shape,
speed, polarization) are compared and each species is placed in a shared "swarm space"; intraspecific
variation over time is analysed to advise on how long observations must be for confident inference. Read
at abstract level via Europe PMC.

## Contribution

One of very few cross-species, long-time-scale comparisons; proposes an extensible swarm space for
comparative work.

## Key results

- Abstract-level: species differ systematically in local and group patterns; observation time scale affects
  inference about species-typical collective motion.

## Methods and models

Trajectory data from four species; comparative metrics; part of the 2023 theme issue "Collective behaviour
through time".

## Limitations and open questions

Abstract-level read; four species, one group each for some.

## Relevance to us

The swarm-space idea (place simulated and real groups in one metric space) is directly reusable for
benchmarking agent swarms against animals.
