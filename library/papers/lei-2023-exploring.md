## Summary

Tests the criticality hypothesis with up to 50 programmable robots running Vicsek-like interactions,
under time-varying stimulus-response and hazard-avoidance tasks. Not all order-disorder transitions bring
functional benefits: collective response is maximised near the critical state when the transition is induced by
alignment weight or alignment range, not by noise or other non-alignment factors, which instead highlight the
benefits of alignment-induced criticality.

## Contribution

Physical-robot evidence that the functional benefit of criticality depends on which control
parameter drives the transition.

## Key results

- Collective response peaks near criticality induced by alignment weight or scale (measured on robots).
- Noise-induced and other non-alignment transitions do not give the same functional advantage.

## Methods and models

Swarm of up to 50 programmable ground robots; Vicsek-like alignment with tunable weight, scale,
noise; stimulus-response and hazard avoidance tasks.

## Limitations and open questions

Abstract-level read; small swarm sizes.
