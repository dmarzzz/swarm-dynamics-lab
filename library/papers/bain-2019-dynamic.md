## Summary

Uses tens of thousands of road-race participants waiting in starting corrals (for example the Chicago Marathon) to probe how polarised crowds respond to motion of their boundaries. Speed information propagates over system-spanning scales through the crowd while orientational fluctuations are suppressed locally. On this basis the authors build a hydrodynamic theory of polarised crowds from conservation laws and symmetry, without behavioural assumptions, and show it predicts the observed response.

## Contribution

First quantitative hydrodynamic (active continuum) description of real human crowds validated on large-N data, opening the line continued by [[gu-2025-emergence]] and reviewed in [[corbetta-2023-physics]].

## Key results

- Measured (abstract): system-spanning propagation of speed information; local suppression of orientational fluctuations.
- The Science editor's summary reports non-damped linear waves travelling at the same speed across corrals and races (speed value not checked here).
- Theory: hydrodynamic equations of a polarised active fluid predict the response to boundary forcing.

## Methods and models

Video of starting corrals, PIV-like velocity fields, linear response analysis, active hydrodynamics. Science 363(6422), 46–49. Paywalled; read via the Europe PMC abstract record (PMID 30606837).

## Limitations and open questions

Polarised, unidirectional, moderately dense crowds only; behaviour under multidirectional or panicked conditions not addressed. Abstract only.

## Relevance to us

Shows that a coarse-grained hydrodynamic description, with response coefficients fitted from data, can predict human swarm dynamics; a model for how to build and test continuum theories of robot swarms. See [[gu-2025-emergence]].
