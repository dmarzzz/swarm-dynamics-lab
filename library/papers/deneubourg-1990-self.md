## Summary

Argentine ant (Iridomyrmex humilis) workers explore unmarked territory at random at first; as the exploratory front
advances, more explorers are recruited and a trail extends from the front to the nest. Unlike two-point recruitment
trails, these exploratory trails have no fixed destination and resemble army-ant foraging patterns. A minimal model
shows how the pattern can arise from individual trail-laying and trail-following, illustrating self-organisation of
complex collective structures.

## Contribution

Classic biological evidence and minimal model for pheromone-mediated self-organisation, the empirical basis cited by
ACO ([[dorigo-1996-ant]]) and by [[bonabeau-1999-swarm]].

## Key results

- Observed (abstract): random initial exploration followed by recruitment and a trail network from front to nest.
- Modelled: simple trail-laying/following rules reproduce the pattern (parameters not read).

## Methods and models

Laboratory observation of Argentine ant exploration plus a minimal individual-based model (abstract only).
