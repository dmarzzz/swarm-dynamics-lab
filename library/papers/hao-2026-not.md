## Summary

The study compares self-reflection, peer stance and peer reasoning from the same initial answers. Its primary setting reports substantial answer changes without peers, showing why raw flip counts do not isolate social influence.

## Contribution

Operationally separates spontaneous changes from changes associated with stance and reasoning exposure.

## Key results

The primary MMLU-Pro screen retains 1,040 disagreement questions from 4,900, producing 5,200 nested agent-question observations. These observations are not independent question roots.

## Methods and models

Abstract and sections 3–4 inspected; conservatively abstract depth, not full audit. Independent condition continuations from Round 0 support controlled contrasts.

## Limitations and open questions

Selection on initial disagreement narrows the population. Operational labels do not identify internal psychology; stochastic branching and within-question dependence still require care. No local reproduction.

## Relevance to us

Dissenter and Influence should report correction and corruption separately and distinguish peer evidence from extra inference. Do not retrofit missing controls into old cohorts.
