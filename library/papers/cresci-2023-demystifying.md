## Summary

A position paper by leading bot-detection groups that catalogues methodological and conceptual errors in social bot research, from hyped findings to flawed critiques, and refutes fallacious arguments made by both proponents and opponents of the field. It is in part a response to critiques such as [[gallwitz-2022-investigating]] and [[rauchfleisch-2020-false]], and closes with directions for sound methods.

## Contribution

The field's own account of what bot studies can and cannot claim, useful as the counterweight to the 'bots are mostly false positives' critique.

## Key results

- Lists methodological issues (dataset bias, threshold misuse, generalisation) with examples from recent studies (abstract).
- Identifies and refutes fallacious arguments from both sides of the bot-prevalence debate (abstract).

## Methods and models

Argumentative review. Abstract-level read.

## Limitations and open questions

Written by tool authors, so not neutral. Pre-dates most LLM-bot evidence.
