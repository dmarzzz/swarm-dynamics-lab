## Summary

Rod-like robots made motile by self-vibration are confined in circular arenas. Dilute assemblies behave like a
gas; as surface fraction increases, polar clusters form along the boundary while the bulk stays gas-like. This
gas-cluster coexistence is shown by simulation to be a consequence of inertia, and a theoretical model based on
surface-mediated transport explains the role of the boundaries. Using deformable, free-to-move arenas, the
surface clusters produce directed motion, and the topology of surface states can be controlled by biasing the
particles' motility.

## Contribution

Shows that boundaries and inertia, often neglected in overdamped active-matter models, control collective states
of real robots; seeds [[boudet-2021-collections]].

## Key results

- Measured: gas in bulk coexisting with polar boundary clusters above a surface-fraction threshold.
- Simulated: coexistence requires inertia.
- Measured: deformable arenas convert boundary clusters into directed motion.

## Methods and models

Self-vibrating rod robots in circular arenas; simulations with inertia; surface-transport theory. Abstract read
on the APS page.

## Limitations and open questions

Specific to vibration-driven rods; generality across robot types not checked.

## Relevance to us

Reminder that arena boundaries and inertia change collective outcomes, important when comparing simulated and
real swarms (compare walls in [[vasarhelyi-2018-optimized]]).
