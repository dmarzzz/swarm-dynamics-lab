## Summary

GPS tracks of homing pigeons flying in flocks of up to 10 analysed with directional delay correlations reveal a well-defined leadership hierarchy in pairwise interactions. A bird's average position in the flock correlates with its rank, and birds respond faster to neighbours seen mainly through the left eye.

## Contribution

Introduced delay-correlation methods for inferring leader-follower networks from tracks; widely reused for influence inference.

## Key results

- Consistent hierarchy of leadership among flock members (field data).
- Hierarchy rank correlates with front position; left-eye bias in response.
- Abstract-level reading only; numbers beyond the abstract were not checked.

## Methods and models

Lightweight GPS loggers at high sampling rate; pairwise directional correlation with time delay.

## Limitations and open questions

Small flocks; hierarchy may differ in other tasks (later work found context dependence).
