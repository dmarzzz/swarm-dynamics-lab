## Summary

High-resolution Ultra-Wide-Band tag tracking of 14 sheep and a border collie during a driving task between two targets. Sheep positions along the front-back axis strongly correlate with their influence on collective movement; even though the dog chases from behind, directional information on short timescales propagates from front to back, and the dog adjusts to the flock's dynamics. An agent-based model captures the data; under chasing, sheep change relative positions less often and front-to-back information flow appears, disappearing without chasing.

## Contribution

Quantifies predator-driven information flow in a mammal herd with field-grade tracking and a model.

## Key results

- Measured (abstract): front-to-back directional information flow despite rear chasing; reduced position swapping under chasing.

## Methods and models

UWB tags at high rate, time-lagged correlation leader-follower analysis, agent-based model.

## Limitations and open questions

Abstract only; one flock of 14.
