## Summary

A short Comment introducing the Nature portfolio collection "Swarm Intelligence - Collective Motions from Biology to Robotic". It argues that evolved collectives combine robustness to noise with sensitivity to salient change through redundancy and nonlinear feedback, and surveys three themes: collective decision-making via cross-inhibition (honeybee stop signals, Reina et al. robot swarms of 100, March-Pons et al. on nonlinear inhibition), "intelligent matter" (robot swarms with matter-like phases, micro-robotic ants), and mutual feedback between robotics and biology (cyborg insects, drone tracking swarms, robots and VR as experimental tools). It stresses that animals are not self-propelled particles and that perception-based models (salience, surprise, neural representation) are gaining traction, citing [[zheng-2024-body]] and [[xiao-2024-perception]].

## Contribution

Agenda-setting perspective from a leading lab; no new data. Useful as a map of where the field's attention is in 2025.

## Key results

- No new measurements; positions perception-driven and neurally grounded models as the next step beyond fixed-rule SPP models.
- Highlights robots and virtual agents embedded in animal groups as tools for causal tests.

## Methods and models

Perspective/commentary, two figures (decision feedback loops; milling in fish and robots).

## Limitations and open questions

Selective, collection-focused; no systematic review of evidence. Claims about tuning of interaction rules by natural selection are asserted, not evidenced here.
