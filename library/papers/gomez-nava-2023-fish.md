## Summary

Wild sulphur molly shoals of many thousands of fish produce repetitive, rhythmic collective dive cascades
(wave-like) under high bird predation. The dynamics resemble a stochastic excitable system driven by
environmental perturbations. An agent-based model fitted to the data suggests the best-fitting regime sits at
a critical point between high and low individual diving activity, where information about perturbations
such as predator attacks propagates most effectively. Read at abstract level via the Semantic Scholar record.
Code: https://github.com/RobertTLange/automata-perturbation-lstm (seen in search results, not opened).

## Contribution

Rare field evidence for the criticality hypothesis in a wild animal collective, using an excitable-media
model rather than an alignment model.

## Key results

- Measured: rhythmic dive cascades in the wild; model fit places shoals near criticality; critical model
  maximises information propagation (model claim).

## Methods and models

Field video of sulphur mollies (Poecilia sulphuraria), Mexico; cellular-automaton/excitable agent model;
inference with neural network surrogates (per the code repo name).

## Limitations and open questions

Abstract-level read; criticality inferred through a model, not directly measured.
