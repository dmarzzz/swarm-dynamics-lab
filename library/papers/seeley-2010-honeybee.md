## Summary

Book by Thomas Seeley (Princeton University Press, 280 pages, published October 2010, copyright 2011) gathering decades of field experiments on how honeybee swarms choose a new nest site: scouts evaluate cavities, advertise them by waggle dance, debate through competing recruitment, reach a choice by quorum sensing and then guide the swarm in flight. It closes by drawing lessons for human group decision-making.

## Contribution

The canonical accessible account of honeybee nest-site selection, the best-studied natural collective decision. Primary results are in papers such as [[seeley-2012-stop]], [[britton-2002-deciding]] and Seeley and Visscher (2004, not catalogued). We read only the publisher page.

## Key results

- Publisher description lists the principles Seeley draws for effective group decisions: shared interests, minimal leader influence, diverse options, robust debate, and quorum-based aggregation.
- No quantitative claims checked at this read depth.

## Methods and models

Book synthesising field experiments with Apis mellifera swarms (artificial nest boxes on islands, labelled scouts, video of dances).
