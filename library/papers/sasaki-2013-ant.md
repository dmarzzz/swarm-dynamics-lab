## Summary

Temnothorax colonies and isolated ants chose between two nest sites of varying quality difference. Colonies beat individuals when the difference was small (hard task) but individuals beat colonies when it was large (easy task). A model shows that positive feedback sharpens discrimination of fine differences but can lock colonies into a suboptimal choice on easy tasks.

## Contribution

Direct test of when crowds are wise, with the same task given to individuals and groups; key evidence in [[sasaki-2018-psychology]].

## Key results

- Colonies more accurate than individuals for small quality differences; reversed for large differences (experiment).
- Abstract-level reading only; numbers beyond the abstract were not checked.

## Methods and models

Binary nest choice by Temnothorax colonies and by isolated workers as the quality difference between sites was varied; mathematical model of recruitment with positive feedback.

## Limitations and open questions

One task dimension; laboratory.

## Relevance to us

Predicts a crossover in swarm-versus-individual accuracy with task difficulty, measurable in simulation. Related: [[kao-2014-decision]].
