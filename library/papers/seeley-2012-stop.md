## Summary

Field experiments on honeybee swarms showing that nest-site scouts deliver stop signals (head-butts with a brief vibration) to scouts waggle-dancing for other sites, and preferentially target dancers for competing sites. An analytic model shows that this cross-inhibition between scout populations makes the swarm's decision reliable by breaking deadlock between equal sites, mirroring cross-inhibition between neural populations in brains.

## Contribution

Discovery of cross-inhibition in swarms; it supplied the empirical basis and the model later analysed in [[pais-2013-mechanism]] and generalised in [[reina-2017-model]].

## Key results

- Stop signals are directed mainly at dancers advertising sites other than the signaller's own (field measurement).
- Model: cross-inhibition above a threshold breaks deadlock over equal sites (analytical).
- Abstract-level reading only; numbers beyond the abstract were not checked.

## Methods and models

Swarms presented with two nest boxes; scouts labelled by site; video analysis of dances and stop signals; ODE model of committed scout populations.

## Limitations and open questions

Small number of swarms (detail not in abstract); model parameters not fitted to data.

## Relevance to us

Cross-inhibition is the single most transferable mechanism from bees to robot swarms. Related: [[seeley-2010-honeybee]], [[marshall-2009-optimal]], [[talamali-2021-when]].
