## Summary

Review and conceptual framework on when collective decisions in birds and mammals rely on acoustic signals versus movement cues. It argues that decisions about timing or specific actions should rely on acoustic signals, while multi-option decisions about direction or destination should use movement cues because sounds suffer more from masking and Weber's-law discrimination limits.

## Contribution

Recent framework for communication modality in group decisions; complements the movement-based decision literature such as [[strandburg-peshkin-2015-shared]].

## Key results

- Prediction: complex multi-option choices favour movement cues; timing decisions favour acoustic signals (conceptual).
