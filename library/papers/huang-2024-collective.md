## Summary

Adds geometric confinement, via both flow (hydrodynamic) and wall interactions, to data-driven fish behavioural rules. New phases appear, including wall-following and double milling, and confinement drives repeated spontaneous switching between schooling and milling. A probabilistic description reveals bistable states and unintuitive bifurcations, supporting the idea that collective transitions can occur with no change in individual behaviour.

## Contribution

Shows that boundaries, routinely ignored in models but always present in experiments, can create and switch collective states.

## Key results

- Simulation (abstract): wall-following and double-mill phases; spontaneous schooling-milling switching; bistability.
