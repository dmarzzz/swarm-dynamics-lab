## Summary

Field 3D tracking of wild midge swarms measures how one midge's change of direction affects others. Despite no
global order, swarms show strong correlations incompatible with non-interacting particles; correlation grows
sharply with swarm density, indicating a metric interaction, and simulations show such growing correlation is
typical of a system close to an ordering transition. Concludes correlation, not order, is the hallmark of
collective behaviour. Read at abstract level from arXiv.

## Contribution

Extends the correlation approach of [[cavagna-2010-scale]] to disordered swarms and shows metric (not
topological) interaction in insects; precursor to [[cavagna-2017-dynamic]].

## Key results

- Measured: strong velocity correlations without polarization; correlation length increases with density;
  metric interaction inferred.

## Methods and models

Stereo tracking of midge swarms (Cladotanytarsus, Chironomidae) in parks in Rome; connected correlation
functions; comparison with Vicsek-type simulations near the transition.
