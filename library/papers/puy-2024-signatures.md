## Summary

Analyses spontaneous turning avalanches (large heading changes propagating through the group) in freely swimming schools in a tank, using tools from condensed-matter avalanches and seismology. Avalanche metrics follow power laws with data collapses and scaling relations, a necessary condition for criticality. Avalanches are linked to collective choice of a new direction; tank walls and boundary individuals matter; avalanches cluster in time below a timescale and follow an Omori law that decays faster than for earthquakes.

## Contribution

Quantitative avalanche statistics for fish schools, extending the criticality hypothesis from static correlations to dynamic cascades.

## Key results

- Measured (abstract): power-law avalanche size and duration distributions with scaling collapse; Omori-law aftershocks with faster decay than earthquakes; boundary effects.

## Methods and models

Tracking of schooling fish (black neon tetra per the group's other work, not checked), avalanche definitions by turning-rate thresholds.

## Limitations and open questions

Abstract only; power laws in small tank groups are hard to separate from finite-size effects.
