## Summary

A unidirectional crowd experiment that tracks each pedestrian's feet rather than heads. Three dynamical regimes appear with density: free, slow-moving (speed constrained but step direction free) and jammed (speed and step direction both constrained), with transitions at about 0.75 and 1.80 persons/m^2. Speed decays exponentially with decreasing specific volume; in the jammed regime both step length and frequency fall. Spontaneous step synchronisation between neighbours is most likely at the onset of jamming, and more with the person in front than to the side.

## Contribution

Adds a gait-level (submicroscopic) view of crowd regimes and shows that synchronisation emerges near the jamming transition.

## Key results

- Measured: regime transitions at about 0.75 and 1.80 persons/m^2.
- Measured: synchronisation peaks at jamming onset; front–back synchronisation exceeds lateral.

## Methods and models

Large-scale laboratory experiment with foot tracking. Sci. Adv. 11(26), eadw2688 (CC BY); data at https://zenodo.org/records/14737521.

## Limitations and open questions

Unidirectional laboratory flow; participant pool not representative. Skimmed.
