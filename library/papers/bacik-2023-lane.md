## Summary

Develops a kinetic theory of lane formation in active two-component flows (pedestrians, driven colloids, complex plasmas, molecular transport) that explains its physical origin and quantifies the propensity for lane nucleation. In the low-density regime it predicts lanes not parallel to the flow, which human crowd experiments confirm: tilted lanes when chiral symmetry is broken, and lanes along elliptic, parabolic and hyperbolic curves near sources or sinks.

## Contribution

A first-principles theory of laning that makes new, tested predictions about lane geometry, rather than only reproducing parallel lanes.

## Key results

- Predicted and observed (abstract): tilted lanes under broken chiral symmetry; curved lane nucleation near sources and sinks.

## Methods and models

Kinetic theory, low-density; controlled human crowd experiments. Science 379(6635), 923–928. Read via Europe PMC record (PMID 36862784).
