## Summary

Analyses 82,076 misleading posts on X flagged through Community Notes, separating AI-generated from conventional misinformation. AI-generated misinformation is more often entertaining and positive in sentiment, is judged less believable and less harmful, comes more often from smaller accounts whose authors are more partisan and more exposed to misinformation, and is significantly more likely to go viral.

## Contribution

Uses crowd fact-checking as the detector, giving a prevalence and characterisation of AI-generated misinformation without an automatic classifier.

## Key results

- Measured (abstract): four characterisation findings over 82,076 noted posts, including higher virality for AI-generated misinformation.

## Methods and models

Community Notes data; notes labelled for AI generation (labelling procedure not read). Abstract read only.

## Limitations and open questions

Only posts that attracted notes; crowd labels of "AI-generated" are themselves noisy. Abstract depth.

## Relevance to us

Crowd notes as a distributed detector; account-size and partisanship features are the kind of side information that separates operations from organic posting. Multimodal follow-up: [[chrysidis-2026-synthetic]].
