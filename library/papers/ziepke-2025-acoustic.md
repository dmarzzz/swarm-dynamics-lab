## Summary

Ziepke, Maryshev, Aranson and Frey model swarms of agents that each emit and detect acoustic waves, adjust their
emission frequency and migrate toward the strongest signal. The swarms self-organize into snake-like self-propelled
entities, localized aggregates and spinning rings, and show emergent functions: self-regeneration after distortion
(passing through narrow constrictions), collective sensing of reflecting objects, collective decision-making and
response to acoustic control inputs. Read from the abstract on the page in `url`; details beyond the abstract are not checked.

## Contribution

Brings explicit wave-based communication into active-matter swarms, closer to robotic and biological signalling than
contact or alignment rules; links active matter to collective decision-making.

## Key results

- Distinct morphologies (snakes, aggregates, rings) and emergent functionality (abstract; simulation-based).

## Methods and models

Agent-based model with acoustic field coupling. arXiv:2410.02940; full text not read.
