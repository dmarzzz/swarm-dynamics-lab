## Summary

California blackworms braid their bodies into entangled 3D "blobs" of thousands of individuals that behave as a
living material: the blob changes shape to reduce evaporation and can break symmetry and crawl away from heat
(negative thermotaxis). The authors hypothesise a rudimentary differentiation of function across the blob that,
combined with entanglement dynamics, produces persistent directed locomotion, and test it with a robophysical
blob of physically linked robots that shows emergent locomotion without sophisticated control of any robot.

## Contribution

Introduces entangled active matter as a class of collectives and uses robots as a physical model to test a
biological mechanism, a template for biology-robotics feedback.

## Key results

- Measured in worms: shape transformations under desiccation; collective negative thermotaxis.
- Robophysical blobs show emergent locomotion without central control (from abstract).

## Methods and models

Laboratory experiments with Lumbriculus variegatus; theoretical and robophysical models. Abstract read.

## Limitations and open questions

Robot blobs are small; mapping between worm and robot interaction rules is qualitative.
