## Summary

This study examines AI use in romance-baiting scams through insider interviews, a blinded week-long conversation comparison, and safety-filter tests. The abstract reports greater trust and compliance for an LLM agent than human operators and failure of tested filters to recognize scam dialogues.

## Contribution

Combines insider interviews, a blinded week-long conversation comparison and safety-filter tests to measure LLM use in romance-baiting scams.

## Key results

- 145 insiders and five victims interviewed; compliance 46% versus 18%; trust difference p=0.007; tested filters detected 0.0% of dialogues.

## Methods and models

Interviews, blinded long-term conversation study, and commercial safety-filter evaluation.

## Limitations and open questions

Controlled conversations and selected filters do not establish prevalence of fully autonomous criminal operators.

## Relevance to us

Shows single LLM agents outperforming human operators at building trust (46% vs 18% compliance) and evading filters (0% detected); relevant to the threat model, not to multi-agent coordination.
