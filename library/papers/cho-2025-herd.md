## Summary

Controlled experiments on herd behaviour, the tendency of an LLM agent to align its output with peers. The gap between an agent's self-confidence and its perceived confidence in peers strongly predicts conformity; the presentation format of peer information modulates herding; and herding can be tuned so that calibrated herd tendencies improve collaborative outcomes.

## Contribution

Identifies confidence asymmetry and information format as control knobs of social influence in LLM collectives.

## Key results

- Confidence gap predicts conformity (abstract).
- Format of peer information changes herding strength (abstract).
- Calibrated herding improves outcomes (abstract).

## Methods and models

Prompted peer-influence experiments with manipulated confidence and format. Code not checked.
