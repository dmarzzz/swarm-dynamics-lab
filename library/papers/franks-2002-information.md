## Summary

Experimental and mathematical comparison of house hunting in honeybees and the ant Leptothorax (Temnothorax) albipennis. Bees advertise sites by waggle dance but rarely compare sites directly; ant scouts often compare sites but cannot report quality, so they start recruiting sooner to better sites. Both use forms of opinion polling (unbiased dance following in bees, a nest-mate quorum in ants) and built-in time lags that trade survey thoroughness against speed.

## Contribution

Established the comparative 'opinion polling' view of insect house hunting and the role of time lags and quorums in balancing speed and accuracy, which [[franks-2003-speed]] and [[sumpter-2009-quorum]] later quantified.

## Key results

- Honeybee colonies are about 100 times larger than L. albipennis colonies yet use similar fundamental algorithms (as stated in the abstract).
- Ants switch to faster recruitment when a threshold population of nest-mates is present at the new site.
