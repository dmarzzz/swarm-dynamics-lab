## Summary

Using ant foraging models, argues that the point where two discontinuous (bistable) transitions merge
into a continuous one is a codimension-2 bifurcation equivalent to a critical point. Locating a continuous
transition needs tuning two parameters whereas a discontinuous transition needs one; heterogeneity blurs
transitions and recruitment without positive feedback removes bistability.

## Contribution

A bifurcation-theory classification of collective transitions that explains why tuning to
criticality is harder than tuning to a bistable switch.

## Key results

- Bistable transitions appear across ant foraging models with different mechanisms (analysis).
- Continuous transition requires two-parameter tuning; discontinuous requires one.
- Heterogeneity blurs transitions; no positive feedback, no bistability.

## Methods and models

Bifurcation analysis of ant foraging/recruitment ODE models.
