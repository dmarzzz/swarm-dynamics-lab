## Summary

Field study of sulphur-adapted fish in Mexico whose schools respond to bird attacks with synchronized, repeated
collective dives (escape waves). Across bird species, birds waited longer to attack as they encountered more
waves, both before the first attack and between attacks; post-attack all species triggered similar numbers of
waves, while pre-attack triggering differed by species. Simulated bird arrivals indicate pre-attack birds may
be perceived as less threatening or overlooked depending on size, colour and contrast. The generic effect is
argued to target a weakness in visual processing shared by predators. Read at abstract level via Europe PMC.

## Contribution

Functional follow-up to [[gomez-nava-2023-fish]]: evidence that the same collective behaviour is protective
against multiple predator species.

## Key results

- Measured: attack latency increases with number of waves for all bird species; species differences in
  pre-attack wave triggering.

## Methods and models

Field observation of sulphur molly shoals and multiple bird species; simulated bird stimuli.
