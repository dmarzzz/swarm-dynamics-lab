## Summary

Reviews leadership in vertebrate groups and reports three experiments with people instructed to walk without talking: in groups of eight, group size and uninformed individuals affect decision speed and splitting when two informed factions conflict; in groups of ten, mixing informed individuals at the centre and edge increases speed and accuracy; in crowds of 100 and 200, a small informed minority can guide a large uninformed group.

## Contribution

Human test of the informed-minority predictions of [[couzin-2005-effective]], in the same theme issue as [[sumpter-2009-quorum]].

## Key results

- Small minority of informed individuals guides crowds of 100 and 200 people (experiment).
- Spatial position of informed individuals matters for speed and accuracy in groups of 10 (experiment).
- Abstract-level reading only; numbers beyond the abstract were not checked.

## Methods and models

Human walking experiments with secretly informed participants; group sizes 8, 10, 100, 200.

## Limitations and open questions

Verbal and gestural communication forbidden by design; artificial task.

## Relevance to us

Evidence that minority-steering principles carry over to humans; links collective decision to crowds.
