## Summary

Review of dry aligning dilute active matter (DADAM): self-propelled particles with alignment and noise but
no momentum-conserving fluid. Focuses on the collective phenomenology of minimal particle models (Vicsek-like
polar and nematic classes) and on solutions of the hydrodynamic theories derived from them. The unifying
point: orientational order is advected by the particles. Read at abstract level via the Semantic Scholar
record; the longer Les Houches notes are Chaté and Mahault, arXiv 1906.05542 (abstract seen, not catalogued).

## Contribution

The authoritative modern summary of what Vicsek-class models actually do at large scale, superseding the
early "continuous transition" picture.

## Key results

- (a) direct transitions to orientational order are not observed; (b) generic phase separation instead,
  with a coexistence phase of inhomogeneous nonlinear structures (bands); (c) orientational order can be long
  range even in 2D and comes with long-range correlations and anomalous (giant) fluctuations; (d) defects
  are not point-like topologically bound objects.

## Methods and models

Review of particle simulations and Boltzmann-Ginzburg-Landau derived hydrodynamics.

## Limitations and open questions

Abstract-level read. Excludes wet systems, cohesion and attraction (animal-like groups).
