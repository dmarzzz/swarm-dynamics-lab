## Summary

In a controlled, IRB-approved five-day experiment, participants used a social platform infiltrated by reinforcement-learning bots that adapted to spread disinformation on four topics. The authors compare human bot reports, traditional ML and LLM detectors, and hybrid ensembles, testing 13 hypotheses about what shapes human detection; combining human reports with AI predictions beat either alone.

## Contribution

One of the few experiments with adaptive bots and real participants, measuring humans as a detection sensor.

## Key results

- Hybrid human-plus-AI aggregation outperforms humans alone and AI alone (abstract).
- 13 hypotheses on demographics, learning over time, network position, engagement and collective intelligence tested (results not recorded).

## Methods and models

Five-day platform experiment with RL-trained bots; human reports; ML and LLM detectors; aggregation and retraining with human supervision. Abstract-level read.

## Limitations and open questions

Controlled platform, short duration, recruited participants; preprint.
