## Summary

In groups of 10 to 100 cichlid fish (Etroplus suratensis), schooling (high polarization) is shown to be
noise-induced: it arises from the intrinsic (multiplicative) stochasticity of finite numbers of interacting
fish, so smaller groups are more likely to be aligned. Fitting stochastic differential equations to the
polarization time series and comparing simulations suggests fish align with one neighbour at a time,
contrary to local direction averaging. Read at abstract level from arXiv.

## Contribution

Shows order in small groups can be a finite-size noise effect rather than a deterministic consequence of
alignment; methodologically, introduces data-derived drift and diffusion of the order parameter.

## Key results

- Measured: polarization distribution and its group-size dependence consistent with noise-induced order;
  inferred pairwise (one-at-a-time) copying.

## Methods and models

Tracked fish groups; Langevin (drift/diffusion) inference on the polarization; agent-based models with
pairwise vs averaging interactions.

## Limitations and open questions

## Relevance to us

Warns that apparent order in small agent groups (N ~ 10-100) may be noise-induced; fit drift and diffusion of
polarization before claiming coordination.
