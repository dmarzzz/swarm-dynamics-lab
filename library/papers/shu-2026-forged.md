## Summary

In vision-language judge panels, quoting a peer judgment creates anchoring gaps of 19 to 26 points under both self and peer framing; a matched label-only control changes the broken rate by only -0.17 points. Deliberately forged concise wrong quotes overturn correct verdicts 1.5 to 2.7 times more often than naturally occurring wrong peer statements, across two datasets and seven VLM judges. Cross-checking quotes against independently collected blind votes blocks 84.9% of forged attacks.

## Contribution

Measures anchoring on quoted peer judgments as an attack surface, with a blind-vote defence.
