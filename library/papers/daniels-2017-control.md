## Summary

Uses fight-joining data from a captive macaque group (n=48) to ask whether a finite, heterogeneous
social system sits near a critical point and whether it can control its distance from criticality (DFC).
Criticality is operationalised with Fisher information and an instability measure in maximum entropy and
branching-process models. The group sits near criticality, individuals contribute unequally to DFC, and
third-party policing can dampen the most influential individuals.

## Contribution

Moves the debate from "is the system critical" to "how is distance to criticality controlled",
and gives finite-size definitions of criticality usable in small groups.

## Key results

- Macaque conflict system sits near a critical point (inferred from models fitted to data).
- Individual contributions to DFC are quantifiable and heterogeneous.
- Policing by a few individuals plausibly controls DFC (model-based).

## Methods and models

Maximum entropy (equilibrium) and branching-process (dynamic) models of fight participation in
Macaca nemestrina; Fisher information as a finite-size susceptibility. arXiv 1601.03449.

## Limitations and open questions

Social system, not a moving swarm; abstract-level read.

## Relevance to us

Fisher information as a finite-N criticality measure and the idea of per-agent contribution to DFC
transfer directly to agent swarms. Related: [[poel-2022-subcritical]], [[romanczuk-2022-phase]].
