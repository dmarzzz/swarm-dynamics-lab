## Summary

Introduces Synch.Live, an experimental paradigm in which 10 people walk together and receive group
feedback on an unspecified task that can be solved by moving collectively like a flock. In a pilot with 16
groups, more than half self-organised into flocking motion; winning players reported higher connectedness to
others, and individuals aware of working strategies were more likely to be in winning groups.

## Contribution

Brings information-theoretic emergence measures (the Psi family of [[rosas-2020-reconciling]], per
the authors' framing and search snippets) into human collective-motion experiments as a live feedback signal.

## Key results

- More than half of 16 groups of 10 self-organised into collective flocking motion (measured).
- Winning players showed higher connectedness to others; strategy awareness associated with group success.

## Methods and models

Real-time position tracking of 10 walking participants; group feedback; psychometric, behavioural and
trajectory data. Abstract read from the Crossref record because the publisher page returned 403; that the
feedback is the emergence measure Psi comes from a search-result description, not from the paper text.

## Limitations and open questions

Pilot study, small sample; details of the feedback measure not verified from the full text.

## Relevance to us

A precedent for using an emergence metric as a live objective for a swarm, directly transferable to
robot or agent swarms (optimise Psi). Related: [[sas-2026-improved]], [[mediano-2022-greater]].
