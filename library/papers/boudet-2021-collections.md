## Summary

Simple self-propelled rod-like robots enclosed in a flexible scaffold form a mobile, deformable superstructure
that can squeeze through constrictions narrower than itself and move around obstacles. The authors measure the
forces the superstructure develops and exerts (for example when crossing a constriction), relate them to the
self-organisation of the robots inside, and show that designing the environment can bias the superstructure's
mobility into directional motion. Tasks demonstrated include pulling a load, traversing an obstacle course and
cleaning an arena, with rudimentary light-based control.

## Contribution

Turns boundary-mediated collective motion of active rods ([[deblais-2018-boundaries]]) into a functional robot,
with force measurements linking microscopic organisation to macroscopic function.

## Key results

- Measured forces exerted by the superstructure and their link to internal self-organisation (from abstract).
- Environment design rectifies motion into directed transport; tasks demonstrated.

## Methods and models

Vibration-driven rod robots inside a flexible ring scaffold; force measurements; simulations. Abstract read.
Cite volume/issue from Crossref (6(56)).

## Limitations and open questions

Control is rudimentary; scaling with robot number not checked.

## Relevance to us

Physical example of emergent function from mindless units in a deformable boundary; related to
[[savoie-2019-robot]] and [[ben-zion-2023-morphological]].
