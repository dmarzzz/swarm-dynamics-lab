## Summary

Asks when a crowd crossing a concourse in many directions organises into lanes and when it stays disordered. A kinetic (hydrodynamic) theory of collisional flows of heterogeneous populations gives a linear stability criterion for the disordered state; agent-based simulations and controlled human experiments confirm an order–disorder transition controlled by the angular spread of preferred directions.

## Contribution

Extends laning theory from perfectly binary counterflows to realistic multidirectional crowds and gives a quantitative threshold for when self-organisation should be expected.

## Key results

- Predicted and confirmed: for a bimodal distribution of preferred directions, ordering is lost when the standard deviation of directions exceeds γ_crit ≈ 13°.
- Measured: loss of order reduces walking efficiency.
- Practical claim: widening a crossing need not raise throughput because the transition can lower speed.

## Methods and models

Kinetic theory with stability analysis, hard-sphere and agent-based simulations, controlled crowd experiments (Katowice). PNAS 122(14), e2420697122 (CC BY). Commentary in PNAS ("Lane formation in criss-crossing crowds").

## Limitations and open questions

Low-density kinetic theory; dense regime not covered. Skimmed.

## Relevance to us
