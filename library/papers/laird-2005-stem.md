## Summary

Follow-up to [[stoner-1996-somatic]] in Botryllus schlosseri. From the abstract: vascular fusion between genetically distinct colonies results in parasitism of somatic tissues, gametes or both, and the authors show that the genetic hierarchies of which genotype wins can be replicated by transplanting cells alone, without whole-colony fusion. They prospectively isolate a population of multipotent, self-renewing stem cells that keep their competitive (winning) phenotype after transplantation. Single transplanted cells contributed to either somatic or germline fates, not both. The authors conclude that histocompatibility genes in Botryllus evolved to protect the body from parasitic stem cells that usurp asexual or sexual inheritance. Numbers are not given in the abstract and I did not read the full paper.

## Contribution

Moves the unit of the takeover from "colony" to an identifiable, transplantable cell population with a heritable competitive phenotype, and shows the winner-loser ordering is reproducible (a hierarchy), not random.

## Key results

- Somatic and gametic parasitism hierarchies after fusion are reproduced by cell transplantation (abstract).
- A prospectively isolated stem cell population retains its competitive phenotype on transplantation (abstract).
- Single-cell contribution goes to soma or germline, not both (abstract).
- Specific frequencies and effect sizes not checked (full text not read).

## Methods and models

Colonial ascidian Botryllus schlosseri; cell transplantation between colonies; prospective isolation of stem cells (method details not read). Abstract only.

## Limitations and open questions

I read only the abstract, so sample sizes, how the hierarchy was scored, and how strongly "winning" is heritable are not checked. Laboratory transplantation may not reflect field encounter rates.

## Relevance to us

Q3: shows the takeover in [[stoner-1996-somatic]] is carried by a small, specific population that is transplantable and keeps its competitive behaviour wherever it lands. The agent analogue is a compact payload (a memory entry, a tool, an adapter) that wins whatever merge it is carried into, independent of the rest of the returning sub-agent. The existence of a stable winner hierarchy suggests that the most effective attack is a lineage with a general "win the merge" property rather than a host-specific trick.
Q2: the defence is again a single compatibility gate at fusion, see [[de-tomaso-2005-isolation]].
