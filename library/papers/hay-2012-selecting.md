## Summary

The authors formalize choosing computations as a decision problem: spend effort when its expected decision value warrants its cost. They derive properties and evaluate approximation policies in selection problems and Go.

## Contribution

A framework for reasoning about the cost of deciding what to compute.

## Key results

Section 6.2 discusses carrying unused samples into later search states; this is relevant prior art for retaining a future verification opportunity.

## Methods and models

Belief-state models, sampling policies and game-tree experiments. Introduction, selected theory, sections 6.1–6.3, figure captions and conclusion skimmed from the author-hosted PDF; no reproduction.

## Limitations and open questions

Search computation differs from external testimony and changing-world verification. The paper does not validate our dissent protocol.
