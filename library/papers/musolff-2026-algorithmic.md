## Summary

The Management Science version analyzes automated e-commerce pricing using an event study and a delegated-strategy model. The abstract reports initial seller and market price declines, followed by price increases where resetting strategies are adopted with fewer than six serious competitors. Cycling remains relatively rare in the sampled data.

## Contribution

A journal treatment quantifies when algorithmic repricing lowers prices and when resetting strategies facilitate tacit collusion.

## Key results

- Reported initial seller-price decline: 16.93%; market-price decline: 9.67%; eventual competitor and market-price increase under resetting with fewer than six serious competitors: 11.4%.

## Methods and models

E-commerce event study and equilibrium in delegated repricing strategies.

## Limitations and open questions

The convenience sample includes products with at least one repricing merchant. The abstract says cycling is rare; the monopoly-price conclusion belongs to the model rather than all observed markets.

## Relevance to us

This provides a prior-art comparison for detecting coordinated automation, not evidence that any observed population is an LLM swarm.
