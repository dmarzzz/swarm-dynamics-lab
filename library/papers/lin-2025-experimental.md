## Summary

Combines experiments and a data-driven burst-and-coast model of rummy-nose tetras to study how group
size and stress shape collective state, quantified by polarisation, milling and cohesion. Smaller groups show
higher baseline stress; under stress, fish adjust social interaction strengths so that the group moves into a
critical state with enhanced sensitivity. Large groups need an external stressor to reach criticality, small
groups are already near it.

## Contribution

Experimental evidence that stress drives fish schools towards criticality by modulating
interaction strength, complementing [[poel-2022-subcritical]]'s finding that alarm moves schools closer to the
transition.

## Key results

- Group size modulates stress, with smaller groups more stressed (measured).
- Under stress, inferred interaction parameters move the group into a critical state (model inference).
- Large groups require an external stressor to reach criticality; small groups are near it already.

## Methods and models

Hemigrammus rhodostomus groups of varying size; polarisation, milling, cohesion metrics; burst-and-coast
model with attraction and alignment fitted to data. PRX Life 3, 033018.

## Limitations and open questions

Abstract-level read; criticality is inferred through the fitted model.

## Relevance to us

Supports a "context-tuned distance to criticality" hypothesis and gives the order parameters to
use. Same species as [[crosato-2018-informative]].
