## Summary

Bäuerle, Fischer, Speck and Bechinger implement synthetic quorum sensing: each light-activated colloid's motility is set
by a feedback loop (particle detection plus scanning laser) as a function of local neighbour density. Depending on how
motility varies with density, the system self-organizes into aggregates of different size, density and shape; the rule
is almost freely programmable. Read from the abstract on the page in `url`; details beyond the abstract are not checked.

## Contribution

Experimental realization of density-dependent motility, the input of MIPS theory ([[cates-2015-motility]],
[[tailleur-2008-statistical]]), with programmable rules.

## Key results

- Measured: aggregate morphology controlled by the motility–density rule (abstract).

## Methods and models

Experiment and simulation; feedback-controlled active colloids (Nature Communications, open access). Full text not read.

## Limitations and open questions

External feedback, not autonomous sensing.
