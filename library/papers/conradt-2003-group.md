## Summary

Theoretical model comparing the fitness costs of 'despotic' decisions (one individual decides for the group) with 'democratic' decisions (the group follows the majority or an average) when group members hold different information about the best activity timing or direction. Democratic decisions cost subordinates and the group less under most conditions, mainly because they avoid extreme outcomes.

## Contribution

Started the modern functional analysis of shared versus unshared consensus decisions in animals and motivated the later review [[conradt-2005-consensus]]. It is a fitness-cost argument, not a mechanism model.

## Key results

- Despotic decisions incur considerably higher consensus costs than democratic ones under most parameter values (model result).
- Even when the despot is the most experienced member, accepting its decision pays only when group size is small and its information advantage is large.
- The benefit of democracy comes mainly from less extreme decisions, not from each individual having influence per se.
- Abstract-level reading only; numbers beyond the abstract were not checked.

## Methods and models

Analytical and numerical fitness model of synchronisation decisions with members differing in their optimal timing; costs measured as deviation from each member's optimum.

## Limitations and open questions

No mechanism for how votes are counted; assumes the group stays together; predictions were proposed as testable but this paper contains no data.

## Relevance to us

Framing for leader-versus-crowd designs in swarms; contrast with the mechanistic leadership models [[couzin-2005-effective]] and the empirical baboon result [[strandburg-peshkin-2015-shared]].
