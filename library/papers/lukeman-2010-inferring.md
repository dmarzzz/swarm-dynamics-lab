## Summary

Field data on flocks of hundreds of surf scoters swimming on the water surface are used to reconstruct
positions, velocities and trajectories, revealing concentric neighbour structure, a preference for neighbours
directly in front, and strong alignment with neighbours to the sides. Fitting zonal models shows the data
require strong short-range repulsion, intermediate-range alignment, longer-range attraction (circular
zones) and a weak but significant frontal interaction with one neighbour; removing or altering any one rule
fails. Read at abstract level via Europe PMC.

## Contribution

Unlike the fish studies, finds that zonal models with explicit alignment do fit this 2D bird system,
showing that the "right" rules are species-specific.

## Key results

- Measured: concentric positional structure, front preference, side alignment; best-fit zonal model with
  repulsion, alignment, attraction and frontal interaction.

## Methods and models

Aerial imaging of surf scoter (Melanitta perspicillata) flocks; zonal model fitting.
