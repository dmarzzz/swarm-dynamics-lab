## Summary

Pre-registered analysis of 69,632 medRxiv preprints (2020-2025): the share of Discussion sections containing at least one em-dash rose from 4.23% before ChatGPT to 11.58% after (+7.35 points, 95% CI 6.94-7.77), reaching 20.3% in 2025. Placebo and falsification tests show no change within the pre-LLM era or in boilerplate sections. The author stresses this is a population-level indicator, not a per-paper detector.

## Contribution

A pre-registered, falsification-tested punctuation marker for population-level LLM influence.

## Key results

- Measured (abstract): em-dash presence 4.23% to 11.58%; 8.0% in 2024 and 20.3% in 2025; placebo +0.13 points (CI -0.33 to +0.58).

## Methods and models

Logistic model with first-author clustered errors; six supporting, six sensitivity analyses and two falsification tests frozen in advance. Abstract read only.

## Limitations and open questions

Cannot establish causality; single-character marker is trivially removable. Abstract depth.

## Relevance to us

Methodological model for marker-based prevalence claims: pre-registration and placebo windows, which most estimators here lack.
