## Summary

Deseigne, Dauchot and Chaté vibrate a monolayer of disks with built-in polar asymmetry so each disk moves
quasi-ballistically over a long persistence length. Alignment emerges from collisions through self-propulsion plus hard
core repulsion. Increasing vibration amplitude produces large-scale collective motion and giant number fluctuations with
an exponent matching the theoretical prediction. Read from the abstract on the page in `url`; details beyond the abstract are not checked.

## Contribution

First experimental polar flock of non-living, non-communicating particles with alignment arising only from collisions,
a direct physical realization of Vicsek-class behaviour and a precursor of self-alignment ([[baconnier-2025-self]]).

## Key results

- Measured: onset of large-scale collective motion with vibration amplitude (abstract).
- Measured: GNF exponent consistent with theory (abstract).

## Methods and models

Experiment: vibrated polar disks (asymmetric mass distribution). arXiv:1004.1499; full text not read.

## Limitations and open questions

Small systems (hundreds of disks) and boundaries matter; exponent agreement limited by range (inferred, not checked).
