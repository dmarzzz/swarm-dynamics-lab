## Summary

Virtual-reality experiments in which a participant walks inside a virtual crowd whose speed and heading are manipulated reveal how a pedestrian combines the influence of neighbours. Influence is combined linearly (a weighted average) and decreases with distance but not with lateral position. The resulting neighbourhood model (circularly symmetric, weighted average, one-directional influence, weights decaying exponentially to zero by 5 m) reproduces the experiments and predicts individual trajectories in observational data of a human "swarm".

## Contribution

The first experiment-driven, bottom-up model of collective motion in human crowds, establishing velocity alignment with a metric, distance-weighted neighbourhood as the human analogue of flocking rules.

## Key results

- Measured (abstract): linear combination of neighbour influence; decay with distance, not eccentricity; influence negligible beyond about 5 m.
- Validated: model predicts trajectories in observational swarm data.

## Methods and models

Three VR experiments with manipulated virtual neighbours; alignment model derived from earlier pairwise following experiments. Proc. R. Soc. B 285(1878), 20180611. Read via Europe PMC record (PMID 29769363; publisher page blocked).

## Limitations and open questions

VR presence may alter behaviour; the "omniscient" neighbour weighting was later replaced by a visual model based on optical expansion and occlusion (Warren lab, not catalogued here). Abstract only.

## Relevance to us

Gives measured alignment weights for humans, directly comparable to Vicsek/Couzin rules ([[vicsek-1995-novel]], [[couzin-2002-collective]]) and to topological versus metric debates in bird flocks. Summary in [[warren-2018-collective]].
