## Summary

The authors propose that losing a costly function can benefit an organism when neighboring organisms provide its products. For functions whose benefits leak into the environment, selection can favor dependent beneficiaries while retaining enough helpers to support the community.

## Contribution

A theory of adaptive dependency through gene loss, distinct from genome reduction driven only by drift.

## Key results

- Predicts frequency-dependent limits to loss of essential shared functions.
- Uses microbial helper/beneficiary relationships to motivate the hypothesis.

## Methods and models

Opinion/hypothesis article; abstract and selected introductory passages read on the publisher page. This entry is conservatively abstract-depth.

## Limitations and open questions

Not a new replicated test of LLM specialization. Detailed mathematical assumptions and supplementary methods were not audited in this session.

## Relevance to us

Foundational biological prior art for dependency and division of labor in heterogeneous collectives. It motivates discriminating experiments; it does not establish benefits for agent swarms. The publisher page overcame the earlier PMC access failure.
