## Summary

Motion capture of head and body orientation of pigeons foraging in a flock during simulated predator attacks, reconstructing each bird's visual field and foveal projection. Pigeons used their fovea to inspect predator cues; earlier foveation followed vigilance-related behaviours (head up, head scanning) and predicted earlier escape flights at individual and collective levels, but long and variable delays between foveation and escape blurred that link.

## Contribution

Connects individual gaze (detection) to collective escape with fine-scale 3D tracking, testing assumptions of collective vigilance models.

## Key results

- Measured (abstract): earlier foveation predicts earlier escape at individual and group level; detection-to-escape delays are long and variable.

## Methods and models

Fine-scale motion capture of head and body orientation; reconstruction of visual fields and foveal projections (facility details not read).

## Limitations and open questions

Abstract only; captive setting, simulated predator.
