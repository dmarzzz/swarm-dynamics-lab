## Summary

In three-spined stickleback shoals, the first fish to reach an ephemeral food source get there faster
when the shoal is disordered, because collectively their visual fields cover more of the environment, while
later arrivals are faster in ordered, polarised shoals that transmit social information better. First
responders keep larger heading differences to neighbours, suggesting conflict between private and social
information drives changes in group order.

## Contribution

Empirical evidence that the costs and benefits of order vs disorder differ across individuals,
offering an information-based explanation for fluctuating group order.

## Key results

- First responders faster in disordered shoals; subsequent arrivals faster in ordered shoals (measured).
- First responders maintain larger heading differences with neighbours.

## Methods and models

Tracked stickleback shoals with food presentation; polarisation; visual field coverage.
