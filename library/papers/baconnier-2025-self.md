## Summary

Review of self-alignment: a polar active unit rotates its heading toward (or away from) its own velocity, as opposed to
mutual alignment with neighbours in Vicsek models. Self-alignment couples rotation and displacement of each unit, lets a
single particle orbit in a harmonic trap, changes the nature of the flocking transition even at mean-field level, and
produces collective actuation in dense or solid elastic assemblies. The review unifies independently introduced models
and identifies experimental realizations, with implications for dense biology, metamaterials and swarm robotics. Read from the abstract on the page in `url`; details beyond the abstract are not checked.

## Contribution

Names and unifies a mechanism that is ubiquitous in robots (wheeled or vibrated bots turn toward where they are pushed)
and was overlooked by Vicsek-type theory. Builds on [[baconnier-2022-selective]] and links to
[[paramanick-2024-programming]] and [[das-2024-flocking]].

## Key results

- Single self-aligning particle can orbit in a harmonic potential (abstract).
- Self-alignment modifies the transition to collective motion already in mean field (abstract).
- Leads to collective actuation in elastic active solids (abstract).
