## Summary

Measures the cost side of allorecognition in Neurospora crassa. Allorecognition restricts social parasitism, but if recognising and rejecting is costly relative to fusing, common alleles are favoured and the diversity recognition depends on is eroded (Crozier's paradox). The authors show that fusion among colonies is mutually beneficial compared with allorecognition-triggered non-fusion, because rejection causes mutual antagonism and because fused colonies are larger. They then show experimentally that this benefit of fusion selects against allorecognition diversity, as Crozier predicted. Abstract only.

## Contribution

Experimental confirmation of Crozier's paradox in a fusing organism: the benefit of merging works against the diversity that protects merging.

## Key results

- Fusion is mutually beneficial relative to rejection (abstract).
- Benefit comes from avoided antagonism and from larger size (abstract).
- The benefit of fusion selects against allorecognition diversity in experiments (abstract).

## Methods and models

Fusion and non-fusion pairings among N. crassa colonies with defined allorecognition genotypes; short-term evolution of label frequencies (abstract only).

## Limitations and open questions

Abstract only; short-term lab evolution.
