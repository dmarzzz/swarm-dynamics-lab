## Summary

Analyses in vivo spike recordings from awake rats and monkeys, anaesthetised cats and human LFPs and
compares them with Bak-Tang-Wiesenfeld and branching models. Matching the data requires subsampling, stronger
input (no separation of timescales) and a slightly subcritical branching ratio, so in vivo activity looks driven
and slightly subcritical rather than self-organised critical.

## Contribution

Influential evidence that real neural systems sit slightly below criticality, paralleling the
subcritical fish schools of [[poel-2022-subcritical]].

## Key results

- In vivo neural activity is best matched by subsampled, driven, slightly subcritical models (measured vs model).
- Subcriticality may provide faster processing and a safety margin from supercritical (epileptic) dynamics (interpretation).

## Methods and models

Avalanche size distributions, branching parameter estimates, mean avalanche size and single-spike
frequency across bin sizes; comparison with BTW and branching models.

## Limitations and open questions

Neural setting; abstract-level read.

## Relevance to us

Subsampling matters: tracked swarms often observe only part of a group. Also supports the
"slightly subcritical" view. Related: [[poel-2022-subcritical]], [[beggs-2003-neuronal]].
