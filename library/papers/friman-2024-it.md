## Summary

First direct metabolic measurement of the energetic cost of group flight. Fourteen European starlings flew solo, in pairs and in trios in a closed-circuit wind tunnel while their 3D positions were tracked and their CO2 production was measured by 13C-bicarbonate washout. Followers continually shifted position but on average sat in a V-formation-like offset behind the leader (modal 0.81 wingspans lateral, 0.91 wingspans downstream), about 40% closer than expected from solo positions. Time spent as a follower predicted lower flight cost relative to each bird's solo benchmark, but followers that held a less consistent position relative to the leader did worse, sometimes above solo cost.

## Contribution

Moves the aerodynamic-benefit-of-formation argument from biomechanical proxies (logger data on ibises and pelicans) and models to measured metabolic rate, and shows the benefit depends on positional precision, in small birds whose cluster flocks were thought to raise costs.

## Key results

- Measured: 121 flights and 80 metabolic measurements; 44 group-flight recordings with positions.
- Measured: modal follower offset [0.81, 0.91] wingspans (spanwise, streamwise); median follower positions 0.75-1.25 wingspans lateral, 0.5-1.75 downstream.
- Measured: fraction of time as follower correlated with lower cost (mixed model P = 0.013); among frequent followers, lower variability of horizontal distance to the leader correlated with lower cost (P = 0.009).
- Measured: some highly variable followers had costs above solo flight.

## Methods and models

Wind tunnel working section 1.2 x 1.2 x 2.8 m (starling wingspan about 34 cm); four machine-vision cameras at 25 Hz; IMU backpacks (200 Hz) with LEDs for identity; 13C-labelled sodium bicarbonate injection and CO2 washout in a respirometry chamber; linear mixed-effects models with bird identity as random effect. Only one bird per group flight could be measured metabolically.

## Limitations and open questions

Small groups (2-3) in a confined tunnel; whether results transfer to large cluster flocks is open. Metabolic data for one bird per flight limits pairing analyses; the wake mechanism is inferred, not measured. Read at skim depth.
