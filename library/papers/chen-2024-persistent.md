## Summary

Collective structures face a trade-off between persistence to noise and responsiveness to real stimuli. Inspired by adaptive behaviour in fish schools, the authors give each agent an adaptive time delay with a threshold, and test it with optothermally driven colloidal particles under optical feedback control. With a suitable threshold, responsivity increased about 1.6 times without loss of persistence; they then combine it with long-distance transport and obstacle avoidance as a prototype adaptive micro-robot swarm.

## Contribution

An experimental demonstration, in synthetic active matter, that a simple adaptive delay can break the persistence-responsivity trade-off often discussed for animal groups.

- Measured (abstract): responsivity enhanced by about 1.6x with persistence unchanged.

## Methods and models

Optothermal particle actuation with an optical feedback loop that implements virtual interaction rules with time delay (details not read).
