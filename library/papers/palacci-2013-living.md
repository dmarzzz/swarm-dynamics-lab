## Summary

Palacci and colleagues make light-activated colloids (hematite cube partly encapsulated in a polymer sphere) that
self-propel under light. They form two-dimensional "living crystals" that form, break, explode and re-form. The assembly
comes from competition between self-propulsion and a light-activated attraction of osmotic/phoretic origin; a transition
from normal to giant number fluctuations is measured, and simple simulations describe the experiments quantitatively.

## Contribution

Landmark experiment on synthetic active clustering; with [[buttinoni-2013-dynamical]] it anchors the experimental side
of [[cates-2015-motility]], though here phoretic attraction contributes.

## Key results

- Measured: transition from normal to giant number fluctuations (abstract).
- Living crystals intrinsically related to out-of-equilibrium collisions of self-propelled particles (claimed).

## Methods and models

Experiment: photoactivated catalytic colloids in H₂O₂; particle tracking; numerical simulations. Science 339, 936.

## Limitations and open questions

Attraction and activity are entangled, so the result is not pure MIPS; finite clusters (arrest) unexplained at the time.

## Relevance to us

Shows that switchable activity (light) can toggle collective assembly, an analogue of switching a swarm's behaviour by a
global signal. Related: [[bauerle-2018-self]], [[lavergne-2019-group]].
