## Summary

Mixed groups of cockroaches and socially integrated autonomous robots made shared shelter choices through nonlinear feedback in local interactions. Animals and robots were treated as equivalent; even as a minority, robots could steer the collective choice, for example towards a shelter the cockroaches would not otherwise prefer.

## Contribution

Landmark mixed animal-robot society experiment showing that self-organised decisions can be controlled by socially integrated robots; source of the aggregation rules later used in robot-only studies reviewed in [[valentini-2017-best]].

## Key results

- Robots in the minority modulated the collective shelter decision and produced a pattern not seen without them (experiment).
- Abstract-level reading only; numbers beyond the abstract were not checked.

## Methods and models

Cockroach groups mixed with small autonomous robots socially accepted by the insects, in an arena with two shelters (hardware details not checked).

## Limitations and open questions

Specific species and odour-based integration; 2007 robot hardware.

## Relevance to us

Proof that a few engineered agents can steer a self-organised collective decision, an obvious hackathon theme (steering a swarm with a minority of controlled agents). Related: [[couzin-2005-effective]], [[bose-2017-collective]].
