## Summary

Field study of starling flocks performing collective turns. Information about direction changes propagates
across the flock with a linear dispersion law and negligible attenuation, contrary to current models
(including Vicsek), which predict diffusive, damped transport. A new theory based on symmetry breaking and
conservation laws, with behavioural inertia (a spin variable), reproduces the linear undamped propagation
and predicts faster transfer for stronger orientational order, which the data confirm. Read at abstract
level via Europe PMC; arXiv preprint 1303.7097 has the different title "Superfluid transport of information in
turning flocks of starlings" (arxiv field left null for verification).

## Contribution

Empirical motivation for the inertial spin model ([[cavagna-2015-flocking]]) and the strongest evidence that
first-order (overdamped) alignment models miss the dynamics of real flocks.

## Key results

- Measured: linear (not diffusive) propagation of turning information with negligible attenuation; speed of
  propagation increases with polarization, as predicted.

## Methods and models

3D tracking of starling flocks during turns; arrival times of turning across birds; theory with conserved
spin current (analogous to superfluid helium).

## Relevance to us
