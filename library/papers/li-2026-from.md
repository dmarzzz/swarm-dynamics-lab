## Summary

Argues that airdrop-hunter detection (heuristics and clustering, lightly supervised community partitioning, graph learning) suffers from inconsistent definitions, weak explainability and poor generalisation across contexts. Using open and axial coding of expert narratives followed by two Delphi rounds, the authors produce a consensus operational definition of an airdrop hunter with six contrasts to regular users, 15 measurable indicators covering operations and fund flow, tempered by 'human-ness' counter-evidence, and reference threshold distributions (medians and quartiles) for labelling and evaluation, with why-flagged rationales for audit.

## Contribution

A shared labelling standard for on-chain Sybil swarms, addressing the problem that every detector in this area is scored against different, heuristic labels.

Qualitative coding of expert interviews or narratives; two-round Delphi consensus; indicator operationalisation.
