## Summary

Nautilus essay (19 Sep 2023) surveying crowd science from physical crowds to online ones. It opens with the October 2022 Itaewon crush (159 dead) and earlier disasters. Physical crowds: pedestrians avoid collisions based on time-to-collision rather than distance [[karamouzas-2014-universal]], spontaneously form lanes, and lane formation is disrupted when only 3 of 27 people walking in one group are distracted by phones [[murakami-2021-mutual]]. Keith Still's density thresholds: about 1 person per square metre free movement, 2 slows walking, 4 involuntary contact, 6 to 7 movement difficult, 10 movement virtually impossible; at extreme density a crowd behaves like soft matter rather than a fluid. Psychological layer: emotional-contagion models treated like epidemics (Durupinar et al. 2016) and a 2021 model combining psychology, physics and physiology that matched videos of real emergencies better than partial models. Online crowds: Ramos et al. 2015 found extremist share grows nonlinearly above about 20 percent of respondents; Manrique et al. 2023 (PRL) model anti-X hate communities on Facebook and VK as fusion-fission bubbles that grow and collapse like shockwaves, fitting membership curves from data since 2014.

## Key claims

- Pedestrian avoidance is governed by time-to-collision (measured, Karamouzas 2014).
- A small number of distracted walkers (3 of 27) breaks lane self-organisation for both groups (measured, Murakami 2021).
- Density thresholds of roughly 4, 6 to 7 and 10 people per square metre mark contact, difficulty and immobility (Still, empirical guidance).
- Online hate communities show shockwave-like fusion-fission growth curves that distinguish them from ordinary groups (Manrique 2023, model fit to data).

## Evidence quality

Popular-science essay citing six peer-reviewed papers (listed in its references). No new data. The physical-crowd claims rest on experiments; the online-crowd parts rest on model fits to platform data.

## Relevance to us

Mostly background for crowds. Two hooks: the Murakami result is a small-perturbation sensitivity of self-organisation (a few deviant agents disrupting a whole pattern) that we may want to replicate in agent populations; and the Manrique fusion-fission growth signature is a candidate population-level feature for detecting coordinated online groups, relevant to swarm detection. Note this Manrique is also a co-author of the stubbornness work in [[quanta-2019-smarter]]. Related: [[helbing-2000-simulating]], [[moussaid-2011-simple]].
