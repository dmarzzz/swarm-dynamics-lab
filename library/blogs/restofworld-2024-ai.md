## Summary

News article (2024, before Ghana's 7 December election) on NewsGuard research that found 171 X accounts using ChatGPT to post in favour of the incumbent New Patriotic Party and its candidate Mahamudu Bawumia, and against opposition candidate John Mahama, active since February 2024. NewsGuard's Dimitris Dimitriadis said the accounts posted at regular, predictable intervals, often ten or more times a day, between 8 a.m. and 6 p.m. Ghana time, and mostly received likes and reposts from other accounts in the network; these timing and style regularities first raised suspicion. NewsGuard ran posts from all 171 accounts through Pangram Labs' AI-text detector, which rated all as "highly likely" AI-generated. After NewsGuard reported the network to X and OpenAI, two accounts had been suspended and two restricted at the time of writing. Accounts used apparently AI-generated profile photos and generic names.

## Key claims

- Office-hours posting schedules and within-network engagement are practical tells of a small LLM account swarm.
- Measuring the network's impact is "inherently difficult" because it pushed hashtags thousands of real accounts also used.
- NewsGuard describes it as the first secretly partisan AI network found in a Ghanaian election.

## Evidence quality

Journalism relaying a NewsGuard investigation (the NewsGuard report itself was not opened). Uses a commercial AI-text detector without stated error rates; the platform response (4 of 171 actioned) is a dated snapshot.

## Relevance to us

A second independent example (with [[nbcnews-2024-ai]]) of the same detection recipe used by civil-society researchers: temporal regularity, network-internal amplification, then an AI-text classifier for confirmation. It also records low platform follow-through after detection, which matters for anyone measuring the lifetime of agent swarms in the wild.
