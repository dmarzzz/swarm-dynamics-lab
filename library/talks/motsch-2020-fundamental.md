## Summary

Talk by Sebastien Motsch (Arizona State University). Only the abstract in the YouTube description was read; transcript unavailable (YouTube bot wall), no timestamps. Per the abstract, the talk studies fundamental diagrams (flux as a function of density), experimentally and analytically, for two systems. (1) Pedestrians: a "bi-directional fundamental diagram" relating pedestrian fluxes to both the co-moving and the counter-moving densities; the resulting macroscopic model is not always hyperbolic, which produces complex dynamics. (2) Ants: despite simple individual behaviour, their fundamental diagram shows no traffic jam even at high density, and individual-level measurements (number of contacts, travel time) are used to explain how they avoid jamming. The ant part corresponds to [[poissonnier-2019-experimental]], on which Motsch is a co-author.

## Relevance to us

The ant result is the interesting one: a collective of simple agents whose throughput keeps rising with density where human crowds and cars jam, because individuals adapt their interaction behaviour at high density. That is a concrete design target for agent swarms sharing a bottleneck resource. The loss of hyperbolicity in two-population counterflow models is a warning for anyone writing continuum models of two-species agent traffic. Related: [[poissonnier-2019-experimental]] (primary ant data), [[taherifar-2019-macroscopic]] (pedestrian MFD with hysteresis), [[helbing-1995-social]], [[motsch-2011-new]].
