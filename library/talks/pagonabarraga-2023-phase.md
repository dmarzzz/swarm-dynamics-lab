## Summary

At 07:00-17:00, active hard disks are mapped across density and persistence, connecting MIPS to hexatic and solid phases. Defect-cluster percolation offers an additional view of hexatic-liquid transitions; its relation to standard unbinding theory remains unsettled. At 18:00-26:00, continuous Kuramoto-like alignment produces flocking and band or lane morphologies whose asymptotic status and boundary sensitivity remain open. At 27:00-39:00, chemical fields mediate long-range interactions between surface-bound active and passive colloids. Individually non-translating particles can form self-propelling dimers; relative size can reverse propulsion direction. Calibrated pair motions and magnetic interactions reproduce aspects of cluster, gel and glass behaviour semi-quantitatively. At 40:00-47:00, magnetic colloids transported on a patterned substrate regain collective synchrony at drive frequencies where isolated particles slip. Switching off hydrodynamic interactions in simulations reverses the density-speed trend, identifying medium coupling as essential.

## Relevance to us

Strong example of using controlled model variants to identify mechanisms rather than relying on images. The final magnetic case is externally driven and explicitly distinguished from strict self-driven activity. Its successful entrainment is not evidence for generic agent consensus.
