## Summary

Couzin links animal-group computation to neural-network questions. At 08:00-15:00, speed-dependent fish states and light-gradient tracking illustrate computation distributed through physical interactions rather than individual gradient estimates. At 16:00-26:00, 205 single-initiator escape experiments motivate weighted, directed visual networks; reinforcing contagion reverses the intuitive expectation that high-degree nodes are always most influential. At 26:00-30:00, networked holographic fish environments provide intervention control analogous to a dynamic patch clamp. At 34:50-46:40, angular disagreement yields averaging below a critical angle near 135 degrees and winner-takes-all above it; preference strength and uninformed participants reshape consensus. A network example uses 10,000 simulated nodes and nonlinear multi-neighbour reinforcement, with mathematical analysis supporting a switch from minority to majority control. Trained-fish experiments and wild-baboon trajectories supply empirical comparisons. Questions at 55:00-66:00 distinguish biological evidence, unpublished human studies and scaling conjectures; observed biological systems are limited to roughly 1000-2000 individuals, and very large-scale empirical behaviour remains uncertain.

## Relevance to us

Complements [[couzin-2011-uninformed]] and [[rosenthal-2015-revealing]] by explaining nonlinear reinforcement, network interventions and the limits of transferring small-group findings to much larger networks. Treat the neuroscience implications as proposals, not measured outcomes in neural tissue.
