## Summary

Marchetti contrasts defect motion in fluids with remodelling in active solids. At 02:00-22:00, nematic hydrodynamics explain self-propelling +1/2 defects, passive -1/2 defects and activity-driven unbinding; active length controls both vortex size and defect spacing. At 22:00-25:00, light-patterned activity provides experimental and numerical defect manipulation. At 26:00-40:00, hydra regeneration motivates a solid model because cells do not substantially exchange neighbours while fibre orientation reorganizes. Spherical topology requires net charge +2, but geometry alone fails to reproduce the head texture; prescribed morphogen gradients plus elastic alignment on a fixed shape improve it. At 40:00-51:00, an active nematic elastomer model couples orientation to strain. Compression locally orders and dilation can melt the material; an asymmetric strain profile then moves a defect through texture remodelling without transporting cells. Unlike in the fluid, its direction depends on both activity and a strain-ordering coefficient. At 52:00-57:00, closing the morphogen-strain feedback loop, allowing shape change and explaining suppression of defect proliferation in actual hydra are explicitly unresolved.
