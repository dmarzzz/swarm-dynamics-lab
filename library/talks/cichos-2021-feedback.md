## Summary

At 00:00-07:00, Cichos describes dark-field tracking, a 532-nm laser, acousto-optic steering and real-time feedback for independently controlling particle propulsion. At 07:00-20:00, photon nudging activates a Janus particle only when its orientation lies within an acceptance cone toward a target. Drift-diffusion balance produces localization without a static trapping potential. At 21:00-29:00, finite imaging intervals cause overshoot near the target, creating a nonzero localization optimum rather than unlimited improvement with propulsion speed. The two-dimensional optimum acceptance half-angle is stated as pi/3. At 30:00-36:00, symmetrically gold-coated particles allow direct steering by off-centre illumination instead of waiting for rotational diffusion. Localization around 200 nm and control of up to 100 particles with roughly 50-ms time resolution are described. Python-notebook and paper pointers are mentioned, but exact URLs are not supplied in the transcript.

## Relevance to us

Concrete example of how feedback latency changes collective control limits. Distinguish physical self-propulsion from independent sensing: this setup uses global imaging and centralized per-particle feedback, not fully decentralized autonomous agents.
