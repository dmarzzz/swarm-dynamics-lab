## Summary

Albert Kao discusses the conditional value of collective computation with host Michael Garfield. Read the complete approximately 53-minute Deepgram transcript. This is a research interview, not a new experimental report; numerical examples and policy speculation are distinguished below.

- [10:49] Correlated information breaks the assumption of independent estimates underlying naive wisdom-of-crowds arguments. Kao reports models with an intermediate optimal group size rather than monotonic improvement; the mentioned 10-15 members are an example, not a universal design prescription.
- [16:24] Garfield reports that 231 of 602 participants ignored supplied social information in an estimation experiment. At [17:05], Kao explicitly says the explanation is speculative: confidence, task context, or stable individual differences could contribute, and confidence need not imply competence.
- [21:39] Hierarchical majority decisions within subgroups lose information by suppressing minority opinions. [23:10] With correlated inputs this loss can nevertheless improve accuracy by reducing effective group size, reconciling benefits of a physically large group with smaller-group computation. At [41:38], he emphasizes that separated modules must eventually exchange results; indefinitely isolated factions do not complete that process.
- [43:13] Allowing an abstention/stalemate option can avoid decisions that would otherwise be wrong. Its value depends on delay costs: hiding while uncertain about food differs from failing to flee an approaching predator. [49:19] Kao proposes responses of group size to scarce resources as clues to the benefits that selected for sociality, not a settled classification of every species.

## Relevance to us

Strong conceptual companion for agent-swarm scaling and fork/merge aggregation: distinguish raw agent count from effective independent information, test modular voting against correlated errors, and include the cost of abstention. The interview does not study adversarial delegates or provide secure merge thresholds. Direct transfer to LLM agents is a proposed analogy, not evidence from this source.
