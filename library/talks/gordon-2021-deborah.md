## Summary

Deborah Gordon discusses ant task allocation with host Michael Garfield. The episode distinguishes flexible, interaction-driven allocation from fixed worker castes and cautions against transferring human identity and learned specialization to ants. Gordon proposes ecological constraints on distributed algorithms, not a universally optimal swarm rule. - [32:37] Gordon describes network modularity as containment: a damaged cluster need not disable weakly connected clusters. This is a conceptual resilience mechanism, not an adversarial reintegration experiment.
- [35:02] Gordon reports that dopamine administration increases foraging even under dry conditions; colonies that reduce foraging more strongly in drought have workers that lose water faster and respond more strongly to dopamine. At [47:20] and [50:13] she rejects a simple evolutionary story in which initially independent individuals sacrifice themselves to become progressively interconnected.

## Relevance to us

Useful for choosing allocation and feedback rules according to resource patchiness, operating cost, and rupture risk. The modularity discussion supplies a biological analogy for fault containment in forked agent groups, but does not establish secure merging or a Byzantine threshold. The scented-bead result also warns that identity-blind interaction rates can be affected by synthetic contacts; that security implication is ours, not a demonstrated Sybil attack in this episode.
