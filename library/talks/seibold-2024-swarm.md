## Summary

Workshop talk by Benjamin Seibold (Temple University). Only the abstract in the YouTube description was read; the transcript could not be fetched (YouTube bot wall), so no timestamps. Per the abstract, the talk studies how micro-scale agent behaviour maps to macro-scale swarm performance, with emphasis on heterogeneous systems mixing agent types. Examples: (1) road traffic, where automation and connectivity change the total energy footprint of urban traffic, and where both system-level and individual benefits depend on whether automated vehicles are evenly distributed; (2) vehicles moving supplies in 2D/3D, e.g. to a disaster area; (3) mixed human/robot groups and animal swarms; (4) swarms where a few agents are degraded or disruptive, or enhanced and leading. The stated aim is to characterise the interplay between agent strategies and emergent swarm performance, contrasting success metrics such as "survival of the fittest" versus "support the weakest".

## Relevance to us

Directly on our framing: a small set of agents with different capability or intent (disruptive, or leading) shaping the outcome of a mostly homogeneous swarm, and the choice of collective metric (mean versus worst-off agent) changing which strategy looks best. Useful for experiments on LLM agent swarms where a few agents are adversarial or privileged.
