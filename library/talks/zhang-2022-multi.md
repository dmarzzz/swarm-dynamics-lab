## Summary

The institutional abstract surveys theoretical guarantees for infinite-horizon zero-sum stochastic games. It claims near-optimal tabular sample complexity for model-based RL when a generative environment model is available, equilibrium convergence for independently run Q-learning, and policy-gradient solutions for linear-dynamics quadratic-cost games. The last connection yields robust-control interpretations and implicit regularization preserving robustness during optimization. These guarantees are setting-dependent, not universal MARL convergence claims.

## Relevance to us

A useful boundary on what decentralized learning theory actually proves, compared with unconstrained claims of spontaneous collective intelligence. Zero-sum games and a generative model are crucial assumptions. Read the institutional abstract only; the recording and proofs were not read.
