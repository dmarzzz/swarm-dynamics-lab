## Summary

Dickman reviews dry-active-matter modelling rather than wet hydrodynamics. At 08:00-16:00, he specifies a parallel-update, fixed-speed two-dimensional Vicsek model with periodic boundaries, metric neighbours and no excluded volume. Small systems can conceal density bands and make the transition appear continuous; distributions, hysteresis and Binder cumulants are needed beyond an order-parameter curve. At 17:00-21:00, ongoing birth-death variants compare slow population dynamics with faster turnover, which appears to smooth the transition but is not yet conclusively classified. At 21:00-26:00, excluded-volume triangular-lattice models permit immobilized bands and traffic jams as well as mobile states. A mean-field treatment can predict a discontinuous transition even where an equilibrium comparator is continuous. At 28:00-30:00, questions clarify that plotted mean-field thresholds are stability limits or spinodals, not a free-energy determination of coexistence.

## Relevance to us

Practical diagnostic reference for avoiding finite-size and mean-field overclaims. Birth-death turnover changes conservation laws, while excluded volume changes mechanisms, so these are not interchangeable implementation details. Complements [[puzzo-2025-short]] on distinguishing spinodals and transition points.
