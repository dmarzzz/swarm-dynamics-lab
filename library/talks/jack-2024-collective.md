## Summary

Jack surveys interacting active Brownian and Ornstein-Uhlenbeck particles without assuming direct biological fidelity. At 12:00-18:00, crowded, polydisperse systems exhibit velocity correlations and chaotic advection even without explicit alignment interactions; arrest depends on relaxation-time criteria. At 19:00-31:00, active droplets near penetrable wedges show apparent contact angles together with steady currents. A statistical interface model partly captures fluctuations, but mechanical and fluctuation-defined surface tensions need not agree out of equilibrium. At 32:00-42:00, weakly biased, slowly tumbling two-species exclusion models admit exact hydrodynamic limits. Particle swapping and parameter scaling are essential assumptions; fluctuation corrections are described as exact physically but not all rigorously proven. At 43:00-55:00, informatic entropy production compares forward and reversed path probabilities. Its value depends on observed degrees of freedom and the chosen time reversal. In the specific weak-bias lattice model, microscopic and hydrodynamic descriptions give the same mean irreversibility, which is not asserted as a general property.

## Relevance to us

Especially relevant to swarm detection: specify observation level and reversal convention before comparing irreversibility metrics, and distinguish statistical from mechanical response. The exact lattice result cannot be transferred to arbitrary agent logs. Compare [[tailleur-2020-thermomechanics]] on force-based measurements.
