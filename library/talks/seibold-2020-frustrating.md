## Summary

Public lecture by Benjamin Seibold (Temple University, Center for Computational Mathematics and Modeling). Only the abstract on the IPAM event page and the YouTube description were read: the video transcript could not be fetched (YouTube bot wall), so no timestamps. Per the abstract, the talk (1) presents traffic waves as structured patterns that can arise "without apparent reason" as phantom jams from the collective behaviour of drivers, (2) draws an analogy to detonation waves, cloud patterns and galaxies, which in his research corresponds to the jamiton / travelling-wave solutions of second-order traffic models, and (3) shows, through simulations and experiments, that a small number of automated vehicles on the road is enough to prevent such waves, improving safety and energy use for all drivers. The experiments referred to are the ring-road field tests in [[stern-2018-dissipation]] (Seibold is a co-author), where one controlled vehicle among about 20 damped stop-and-go waves.

## Relevance to us

Clean public-facing statement of a result we care about: a small fraction of controlled agents can stabilise an emergent instability in a large collective of uncontrolled ones. That is the same shape as steering an LLM agent swarm with a few designated agents.
