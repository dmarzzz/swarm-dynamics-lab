## Summary

Vicsek presents three experimental directions. At 09:00-16:00, zebrafish-embryo cell flows and mutant controls suggest that mechanical friction between migrating tissue layers affects morphogenesis. At 16:00-24:00, groups of rats search a hierarchical maze for water faster than individual rats; the example last-arrival times are about 180 versus 300 seconds, while a broader twofold-efficiency claim is asserted without a precisely defined metric in the transcript. At 24:00-34:00, four groups of seven rats are monitored for ten months, accumulating roughly 30 TB of video; group mixing changes behaviour and chasing relationships help infer dominance. These rat studies are described as not yet written up. At 35:00-42:00, evolutionary optimization tunes a multi-parameter drone flocking model against a task fitness function, explicitly including delays, noise, walls and obstacles. A thirty-drone field demonstration is distinguished from simulation.

## Relevance to us

Useful measured-versus-unpublished split and a practical optimization strategy for realistic swarms. Do not equate parameter-rich engineered controllers with the simplest Vicsek rule, or turn an illustrative rat timing comparison into a validated universal collective-intelligence gain.
