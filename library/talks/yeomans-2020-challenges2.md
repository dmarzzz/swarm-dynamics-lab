## Summary

At 00:00-14:00, Yeomans connects molecular-motor-driven microtubule bundles to continuum nematic hydrodynamics with active stress proportional to the alignment tensor. Distortions induce flows that further distort order, destabilizing the homogeneous nematic and producing turbulent-like motion at low Reynolds number. At 19:00-27:00, +1/2 defects self-propel, -1/2 defects remain stationary to leading order, and pair creation as well as annihilation maintains the active state. At 29:00-42:00, channel width relative to the active vortex scale selects no flow, laminar flow, a dancing vortex state or active turbulence. Initial simulations conserve defect patterns more cleanly than experiments: softer anchoring lets defects enter or leave at walls. Closed experimental channels also require return flow, unlike ideal periodic channels. At 43:00-47:30, bacterial and cellular examples are previewed without claiming that all have the same wet-hydrodynamic mechanism.

## Relevance to us

Particularly useful boundary and validation guidance: a visually attractive simulation can preserve the wrong quantities when its anchoring is too rigid. Complements [[marchetti-2021-topology]] and [[vlahovska-2025-quincke]] on environment coupling.
