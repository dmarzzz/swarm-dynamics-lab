## Summary

This roughly fifteen-minute public lecture moves from sensory influence networks to decision geometry. At 01:40-06:00, Couzin explains that simple local movement rules can coordinate collectives, while AI-assisted tracking reconstructs weighted, directed communication networks. Both individual brains and collective connectivity can compute, and group-level gradient sensing need not imply individual gradient sensing. At 07:30-10:30, perspective-correct virtual environments allow real animals to interact with virtual neighbours or remotely located animals. At 11:00-14:45, a coupled movement and neural-consensus model predicts target-choice bifurcations: movement changes sensory geometry, rather than merely following a decision already made. The model decomposes three or more options into binary decisions. Fly, locust, fish and baboon trajectories are presented as supporting examples. The proposed common algorithm is a broad interpretation across those systems, not a demonstrated mechanism in every kind of collective.
