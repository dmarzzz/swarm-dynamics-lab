## Summary

Slide deck (about 2,500 words of slide text, no timestamps since only the PDF was read). Sutton argues that understanding intelligence is the grand prize, that the main technical bottleneck is deep learning's poor continual learning, and that the main societal obstacles are fear of AI and calls for centralized control. The late slides set out his view of cooperation: "Decentralization is many agents, each pursuing own goal"; "Cooperation is agents with different goals interacting to mutual benefit"; "Humanity's greatest failures are failures to cooperate: war, theft, corruption"; "Cooperation is not always possible — it takes two trustworthy agents" (dash in original); "There are always some who benefit from not cooperating: cheats, thieves, con men"; "Cooperation needs institutions to facilitate it and to punish cheaters"; "A centralized authority can help cooperation in the short term, but poison it in the long run". The 2023 "AI Alignment and Decentralization" slides make the same case in shorter form ("in a decentralized, uncontrolled way. Like an ecosystem."). Neither deck discusses copying or merging agents.

## Relevance to us

Context for reading [[sutton-2025-father]]. Sutton's general stance favours many agents with different goals cooperating through institutions that punish cheaters, over a single central purpose. His fork-merge remark instead describes copies reporting to a "central master", and he locates the danger exactly there. The slides suggest the design alternative he would likely prefer: children that stay separate agents and trade knowledge through institutions, rather than being reabsorbed. That reframes Q2: a k-of-n merge rule is one such institution, and "punish cheaters" maps to reputation or slashing for children whose returned knowledge is later found to be harmful. This is an inference from the slides, not something Sutton says about fork-merge.
