## Summary

Vicsek surveys common motion patterns across bacteria, ants, fish, birds and humans before asking an audience to synchronize hand movements without a conductor. At 08:30-13:00, noisy local heading alignment is offered as a minimal explanation for ordered, rotating and disordered motion; coupling to the environment means momentum of the moving-agent subsystem need not be conserved. At 14:50-19:40, GPS trajectories from a ten-pigeon group are used to infer directed leader-follower relationships through time-lagged correlations. The inferred graph has overlapping leadership relations rather than a single leader or uniform equality. Whether this hierarchy optimizes navigation is posed as an open question, and robotic-flock applications are proposals rather than results demonstrated in the lecture.

## Relevance to us

Accessible early framing of minimal models and inferred leadership. Do not treat similarity of circular patterns across taxa as proof of a common mechanism, or trajectory correlation as an intervention-based causal test. Later critiques in [[couzin-2024-cognitive]] make that distinction important.
