## Summary

At 02:00-09:00, independently energy-consuming particles are distinguished from particles driven uniformly by an external field. At 10:00-23:00, Vicsek and Toner-Tu models connect local alignment to flocking and propagating density waves despite overdamped, non-momentum-conserving dynamics. A broken-symmetry transverse velocity mode supplies the second slow variable needed for sound-like propagation. At 29:00-37:00, placing a flocking fluid on a sphere changes the linear mode spectrum: curvature generates a bulk gap, while an equatorial density disturbance can propagate unidirectionally along the effective boundary. At 38:00-42:00, patterned channel networks with a net chirality offer a second theoretical route to protected edge transport. At 43:00-56:00, open questions include nonreciprocity, emergent chirality, living-tissue rheology and objective measures of irreversibility. Questions explicitly distinguish topological protection from absence of viscous damping.

## Relevance to us

Adds a continuum explanation behind the curved-surface teaser in [[marchetti-2018-physics]]. Good distinction between robust directionality and zero dissipation: protected edge waves can still damp. The sphere and patterned-network results are model predictions, not a demonstrated autonomous-agent communication protocol.
