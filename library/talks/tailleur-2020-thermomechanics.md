## Summary

At 04:00-21:00, Tailleur distinguishes mechanical pressure from hydrodynamic and free-energy definitions. Active-particle forces on a wall generally depend on wall-induced torques, so bulk density and propulsion do not define a universal equation of state. Torque-free spheres with pairwise repulsion can retain one; quorum-sensing and alignment interactions can destroy it. At 21:00-33:00, asymmetric partitions produce spontaneous compression in simulation and robot demonstrations; flexible boundaries show instabilities and chain-dependent pressure measurements. At 35:00-55:00, an active-impulse construction explains the exception: the expected future integrated propulsion is independent of confinement when propulsion evolves independently of other degrees of freedom. Its flux supplies an effective conservation law even in an overdamped model. At 56:00-73:00, a two-material boundary junction tests tangential forces. Even when bulk pressure has an equation of state, junction-generated currents dissipate active impulse and make those forces depend on the junction details.

## Relevance to us

A particularly clear warning that measurable pressure or a stable-looking interface does not imply equilibrium thermodynamics. The wall is part of the measurement and sometimes part of the mechanism. Complements [[jack-2024-collective]] on statistical versus mechanical surface tension.
