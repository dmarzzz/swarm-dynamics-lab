## Summary

Nagpal studies complex group behavior built from simple local interactions. At 25:43-29:07, a tactile bridge-state hypothesis uses congestion to recruit ants and disappearance of traffic to release them, giving a self-stabilizing feedback explanation rather than a central planner. At 50:38-53:49 she describes minimalist milling from a binary forward sensor; an equispaced circle is a fixed state, but she explicitly distinguishes that result from an unproved general convergence claim. Q&A at 71:02-74:57 stresses that natural colonies tolerate individual loss while manufactured swarms still lack self-maintenance and require human babysitting.

## Relevance to us

Strong global-to-local design cases and a precise distinction between stability and convergence. Cooperative robots and biological colonies are not adversarially selected populations; redundancy does not imply Byzantine resilience.
