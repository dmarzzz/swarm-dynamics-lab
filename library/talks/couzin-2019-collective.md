## Summary

Couzin follows a common trajectory from golden-shiner sensing to primate decisions, but the questions expose important limits. At 09:00-15:00, individual light-dependent speed changes yield only a 10-15% improvement over random motion, whereas group interactions allow substantially better gradient tracking without individual gradient estimates. At 16:00-29:00, 205 single-initiator escape experiments motivate weighted, directed sensory networks and reinforcing, rather than infection-like, contagion. At 30:00-34:00, networked virtual environments offer controlled social inputs. At 35:00-50:00, informed minorities, angular conflict and unbiased participants are tested against fish and baboon data. At 53:00, stork leadership prediction is described for only 32 birds, with a larger tagging effort proposed. At 70:00-73:30, audience discussion challenges the independent-estimate and common-cue assumptions behind the wisdom of crowds. The speaker describes theoretical environments in which larger groups lose that advantage, with optimal groups around two to eleven, and suggests reconsidering learning algorithms in a social context. Those final claims lack enough parameter detail here for independent replication.
