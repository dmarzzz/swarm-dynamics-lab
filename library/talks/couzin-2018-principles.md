## Summary

Couzin combines locust fieldwork, fish-school computation and primate consensus. At 09:00-23:00, 54 eight-hour arena trials are described as supporting density-dependent locust ordering; nerve-cut experiments and cricket nutrient preferences separately support cannibalism-driven marching. At 28:00-35:00, golden shiners achieve gradient tracking through speed and density differences, with individual kinesis alone giving only about a 10% benefit in the described task. At 35:00-43:00, visual-network reconstruction and networked virtual fish connect inferred influence to interventions. At 43:00-56:30, leadership without recognition and uninformed-participant effects lead to trained-fish consensus tests. At 56:30-63:40, baboon habitat and tracking data are described as improving movement prediction when combined with social information. Future applications of tiny medical robots and new field-tracking infrastructure are not presented as completed results.

## Relevance to us

Useful historical account of modelling-to-experiment iteration. The old locust density-order interpretation is explicitly questioned by [[couzin-2024-cognitive]], so cite that later account alongside this lecture rather than treating the 54-day series as uncontested model validation.
