## Summary

Bredeche describes distributed online evolution: robots exchange controller genomes during encounters, select received genomes and mutate them between lifetimes. Unlike offline evolution, this learns after deployment. Encounter frequency creates an environmental selection pressure distinct from user fitness; landmark aggregation and wall-following can spread genomes without improving the intended task. Two-resource experiments retain specialization when geography separates populations but can collapse when resources are colocated. The final stag-hunt experiments show physical coordination invalidating simple payoff-model expectations; duplicating controller networks permits asymmetric leader-follower strategies that turn a difficult joint switch into a simpler individual decision.

## Relevance to us

Direct evidence that population structure, communication density and representation alter selection and cooperation. More explicit task-selection pressure is not automatically better. The simulated leader-follower result does not establish a general solution to strategic-agent cooperation.
