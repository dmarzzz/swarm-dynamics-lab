## Summary

Three related lectures move from phase dynamics to network approximation and abrupt collective transitions. In class 1 at 28:02-29:48, a fixed phase difference is carefully distinguished from motionless original oscillators; at 42:49-44:38 the all-to-all Kuramoto interaction is rewritten through a mean field. Class 2 at 22:51-24:08 explicitly warns that sparse-network onset calculations use approximations rather than exact identities, and at 45:38-47:20 distinguishes a graph-local coherence measure from the standard global order parameter. Class 3 at 21:02-22:46 explains why hubs can nucleate early coherence and motivates suppressing that pathway to delay synchronization. Later spectral stability reasoning reduces large linearized systems to mode-specific checks; closing Q&A separates using synchronization to infer an ecological network from demonstrating a natural synchronization process there.

## Relevance to us

Strong model and measurement boundaries: local graph-weighted order, global phase order and frequency agreement are distinct; network onset approximations do not imply exact guarantees.
