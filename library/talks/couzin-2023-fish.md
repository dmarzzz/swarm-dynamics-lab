## Summary

At 19:00-29:00, tracking and reconstructed sensory networks connect collective computation to local movement. At 30:00-42:00, neural ring-attractor and flocking models predict geometry-dependent decisions; a forgetting mechanism is needed to reproduce multi-option group choices rather than always averaging toward the middle. At 43:00-57:00, the lecture pivots to human conformity, misinformation and political polarization, citing other studies and describing the speaker’s group’s findings. Those political claims are not accompanied by sufficient methods or source identifiers here to independently assess them. At 61:00-68:00, recommendations include diverse and weak opinions, egalitarian turn-taking, relatively small decision groups around two to ten people, and interconnected groups of groups. These are survey-level interpretations, not a benchmark of autonomous agents.
