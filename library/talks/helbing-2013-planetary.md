## Summary

Hour-long seminar by Dirk Helbing (ETH Zurich, FuturICT coordinator), read in full from YouTube auto-captions (no timestamps kept; positions below are approximate fractions of the talk). It is a vision talk, not a results talk. (1) Opening third: pitches a "Planetary Nervous System", a participatory, privacy-respecting sensor network built from the roughly ten sensors in every smartphone, which would answer real-time queries (noise in a Hong Kong street, waiting time in a London jam) by aggregating over many people and discarding individual data at collection time; plus a "Living Earth Simulator" that runs what-if scenarios, likened to weather forecasting. (2) Argues against a single "global brain": because system complexity grows factorially while compute grows exponentially, real-time central optimisation becomes infeasible, so decentralised, multi-perspective control and preserved diversity are required. (3) Cites his own wisdom-of-crowds experiment: repeatedly showing people others' estimates lowered the accuracy of the group on factual questions, so social influence undermines crowd wisdom (this is [[lorenz-2011-how]]). (4) Middle: examples of data-driven observatories (epidemic spread on mobility networks, a war-risk index from sentiment in news archives; an audience member challenges the coverage bias of older news and Helbing concedes he does not have an answer). (5) Final third: self-organisation as an "invisible hand" that works in pedestrian counterflow lanes at moderate density but breaks down when density is too high; decentralised traffic-light control in which each intersection minimises local queues while watching its neighbours, which he says beat both central control and purely local optimisation with shorter queues; pluralistic, user-defined recommender filters as an antidote to filter bubbles. Closes with "data is the new oil, models are the new gold."
