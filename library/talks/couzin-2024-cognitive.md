## Summary

At 00:00-12:00, Couzin revisits the apparent density-dependent locust ordering used to support the Vicsek model. Pairwise alignment and an activity-aware binomial null suggest that classifying trials with fewer than two moving insects as disordered generates a sample-size artifact: locusts spend about 52% of time inactive. The reinterpretation is identified as unpublished or embargoed work at the time of the talk. He also questions explicit neighbour alignment as a biological premise. At 13:00-22:00, a ring-attractor model with local excitation and long-range inhibition couples neural consensus to movement-dependent target geometry. It predicts transitions from averaging directions to choosing one target, with enhanced sensitivity near the bifurcation and hysteresis that discourages renewed indecision. At 23:00-37:00, fly, locust and virtual-fish trajectory tests support geometry-dependent branching and sequential binary choices among multiple targets. Audience questions distinguish trajectory agreement from direct neural validation; the neural data are described but not shown or published. The available transcript ends during a critique of assumed optomotor mechanisms, at about 40:00.

## Relevance to us

A load-bearing caution for interpreting [[couzin-2018-animal]] and phase-transition claims in swarms. Use active-agent-count-aware nulls before inferring density dependence. The ring-attractor framework suggests testable geometric decision protocols, but claimed neural universality and unpublished human results require independent primary-source checking.
