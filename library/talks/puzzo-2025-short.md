## Summary

Puzzo applies short-time dynamics, originally developed for equilibrium critical phenomena, to a three-dimensional Vicsek model with vector noise. At 04:00-09:30, she describes disordered, flocking and band-coexistence regimes and polarization as an order parameter. At 09:30-17:00, early-time power-law growth or decay from ordered and disordered initial conditions estimates critical points, exponents and metastability limits without long equilibrium runs. Different apparent transition values from the two initial states identify first-order behaviour. At 17:00-22:00, zero-speed controls on a lattice and random spatial graph yield continuous transitions but different exponents, cautioning against assuming the zero-speed model always has Heisenberg behaviour independent of coordination. At 22:00-28:00, mobile particles require carefully prepared ordered initial conditions, including bands, and attention to parallel versus sequential updates. The reported approach uses about 27,000 particles and approximately 1000 Monte Carlo steps, with conventional finite-size and Binder-cumulant checks confirming first-order behaviour and metastability limits. No numeric critical exponents are legible in the transcript.

## Relevance to us

Practical candidate for fast phase-transition diagnostics in simulation. The important controls are initialization, update semantics and geometry, not just fitting a straight line on log-log axes. This is a numerical model result, not direct evidence that animals obey Vicsek interactions.
