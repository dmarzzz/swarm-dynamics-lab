# Evidence synthesis and annotated bibliography

Verified on **2026-10-03** against primary paper records, selected full-text passages, publisher/proceedings pages and official software documentation. This is a targeted narrative review, not a systematic review or meta-analysis. Dates below are first public submission/publication unless a revision is specified. “Preprint record; venue unverified” means no publication venue was established during this review, not that the work was never peer reviewed. No reported empirical result was independently reproduced here. See [search scope](SEARCH-LOG.md).

## What the evidence supports

**Automation is most convincing when verification is independent and specific.** FunSearch couples generation to executable evaluation; Co-Scientist reports experimental biomedical validation; AI Scientist extends automation through manuscript production. These are different endpoints. A plausible manuscript, a correct executable result, a new result and an independently replicated discovery should be scored separately. The A-Lab correction is a concrete warning to distinguish novelty relative to a prediction database from novelty to science. [1–5]

**A capable research agent still needs an experimental instrument.** PaperBench, MLE-bench, SciAgentArena and AgentActionBench evaluate different portions of scientific work. Their tasks and rubrics provide useful design patterns; their scores cannot be pooled into one measure of “scientist ability.” AgentActionBench adds process traces, but a trace rubric can reward plausible procedure without proving the resulting scientific claim. Protect and validate the final outcome independently. [6–10]

**More agents can help or hurt.** The revised scaling study reports task-dependent architecture effects; failure-taxonomy research identifies coordination and verification problems. Neither implies a universal best population size. Compare against a tuned single agent and an independent ensemble under declared system budgets, then ablate communication, specialization and verification. A case study of cheating and whistleblowing motivates protected scoring and auditable shared infrastructure, but does not estimate a general prevalence of these behaviors. [11–15]

**Average success omits important failure structure.** τ-bench and the 2026 reliability work emphasize repeatability and dimensions beyond capability. Report within-task variability, perturbation sensitivity and failure severity. Repeated messages in one environment do not supply independent evidence; statistical methods from RL evaluation are useful if adapted to the actual clustering and task population. [16–18, 25–28]

**Simulation plausibility is weaker than external validity.** Generative Agents tests believable behavior; SOTOPIA tests social goals in constructed interactions. ODD supports transparent model descriptions. The 2026 validation position calls for shared, empirically grounded evaluations. These works support using language-agent simulations as explicit, testable models; they do not establish that arbitrary personas represent human populations. [19–22]

**Judge evaluation must be local to the intended claim.** Early LLM-judge research identifies biases, and newer work examines debiasing and long-form evaluation. A judge's average agreement elsewhere is not proof that errors cancel between your treatments. Audit style, length, position, source evidence and model identity; retain objective scoring wherever possible. [23–24, 29]

These recommendations are this guide's synthesis. The numerical choices in the worked protocol are planning examples rather than results borrowed from these papers.

## Annotated sources

### Autonomous science and research execution

**[1] [[lu-2024-ai]] — Lu, C., Lu, C., Lange, R. T., Foerster, J., Clune, J. & Ha, D. — The AI Scientist: Towards Fully Automated Open-Ended Scientific Discovery.** 2024-08-12; v3 2024-09-01. [Primary record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06292v3). **Status:** preprint record; venue unverified. **Finding:** integrates ideas, code, experiments, writing and automated review in ML domains. **Use:** define automation stage by stage and archive generated code and experiments. **Limit:** reviewer acceptance estimates and manuscript production are not independent confirmation of discovery; tested domains and templates constrain scope. **Consulted:** record/abstract.

**[2] [[yamada-2025-ai]] — Yamada et al. — The AI Scientist-v2: Workshop-Level Automated Scientific Discovery via Agentic Tree Search.** 2025-04-10, v1. [Primary record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.08066v1). **Status:** system report is an arXiv preprint record; the authors report a generated manuscript meeting an ICLR workshop acceptance threshold. These statuses are different. **Finding:** agentic search and experiment management reduce reliance on predecessor templates; one of three submitted generated manuscripts met the reported review threshold. **Use:** log search trees, selection, human involvement and all candidates. **Limit:** a small workshop submission experiment is not broad scientific reliability. **Consulted:** record/abstract.

**[3] [[gottweis-2025-accelerating]] — Gottweis et al. — Accelerating scientific discovery with Co-Scientist.** First preprint 2025-02-26; v2 2026-06-29. [Updated record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18864v2), [Nature DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10644-y). **Status:** Nature 2026 reported in updated primary record with related DOI. **Finding:** multi-agent hypothesis generation/refinement with validation in biomedical applications, including in-vitro drug-repurposing work. **Use:** separate proposal quality, novelty and physical validation. **Limit:** selected validations do not establish universal autonomous laboratory competence. **Consulted:** updated record/abstract, not the full 157-page supplement.

**[4] [[romera-paredes-2023-mathematical]] — Romera-Paredes et al. — Mathematical discoveries from program search with large language models (FunSearch).** Online 2023-12-14; Nature 625 (2024). [Publisher](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06924-6). **Status:** peer-reviewed research article. **Finding:** program search with a systematic evaluator produces cap-set constructions and bin-packing heuristics. **Use:** protect executable validators and charge for the complete search budget. **Limit:** tasks with efficient checking are unusually favorable; results do not imply arbitrary scientific hypothesis verification. **Consulted:** publisher abstract and method excerpts.

**[5] [[szymanski-2023-autonomous]] — Szymanski et al. — An autonomous laboratory for the accelerated synthesis of inorganic materials.** 2023-11-29; correction 2026-01-19. [Article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06734-w), [correction](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09992-y.pdf). **Status:** Nature article with author correction. **Finding:** integrates robotics, planning and materials characterization. The correction revises reported successes from 40 to 36, with four inconclusive, and clarifies novelty claims. **Use:** preserve measurement reanalysis and correction history. **Limit:** identification and novelty need domain validation; do not repeat the original headline without the correction. **Consulted:** indexed publisher text and correction text; direct article fetch intermittently failed.

**[6] [[starace-2025-paperbench]] — Starace et al. — PaperBench: Evaluating AI's Ability to Replicate AI Research.** 2025-04-02. [Primary record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01848). **Status:** preprint record; venue unverified. **Finding:** 20 ICML papers are decomposed into author-informed replication rubrics; a separate judge benchmark assesses automatic grading. **Use:** evaluate runnable reproduction at several levels, including judge quality. **Limit:** rubric completion is not identical to numerical result replication; a small selected paper set bounds generalization. **Consulted:** primary indexed abstract.

**[7] [[chan-2024-mle]] — Chan et al. — MLE-bench: Evaluating Machine Learning Agents on Machine Learning Engineering.** 2024-10-09. [Primary record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.07095), [official repository](https://github.com/openai/mle-bench). **Status:** preprint record; venue unverified. **Finding:** evaluates agents on 75 Kaggle-derived competitions and studies scaling and contamination. **Use:** record data preparation, execution, resource scaling and benchmark exposure. **Limit:** leaderboard performance measures a particular engineering target; public competition history can leak into model training. **Consulted:** primary indexed abstract and repository excerpts.

**[8] [[liu-2026-benchmarking]] — Liu et al. — Benchmarking AI Agents for Addressing Scientific Challenges Across Scales (SciAgentArena).** 2026-06-10, v1. [Primary record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12736v1). **Status:** preprint. **Finding:** approximately 200 interactive scientific tasks show uneven performance between well-specified analysis and open-ended research. **Use:** include scientific task heterogeneity and stepwise verification. **Limit:** benchmark performance and reported novelty limitations depend on its task construction and criteria. **Consulted:** record/abstract.

**[9] [[hong-2026-overview]] — Hong et al. — Overview of the NLPCC 2026 Shared Task 11: Agent-Based Experiment Reproduction from Scientific Papers.** 2026-09-10, v1. [Primary record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11117v1). **Status:** arXiv shared-task report; proceedings publication not independently confirmed. **Finding:** AgentActionBench records actions and applies paper-specific rubrics across 150 papers; authors identify execution as a bottleneck. **Use:** combine process evidence with final-artifact checks. **Limit:** rubric augmentation is model-assisted and human validation covers a subset; correlation is not full agreement or scientific correctness. **Consulted:** record/abstract.

**[10] [[han-2026-science]] — Han et al. — Science Edge Evaluation: SEE the Missing Step Toward Real Scientific Discovery.** 2026-08-07, v1. [Primary record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06931v1). **Status:** preprint. **Finding:** multimodal scientific questions expose limitations in evidence-bounded inference, including in a tool-using condition. **Use:** test whether conclusions follow from experimental evidence rather than fluent explanation. **Limit:** question answering is a proxy for laboratory discovery; a tool benefit in this benchmark is not universal. **Consulted:** record/abstract.

### Architecture, failure and reliability

**[11] [[kim-2025-towards]] — Kim et al. — Towards a Science of Scaling Agent Systems.** First 2025-12-09; v3 2026-04-08. [Version used](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08296v3). **Status:** preprint version consulted; the existing canonical record also documents a later journal version (see its audit notes). **Finding:** revised study covers 260 configurations and six benchmarks with task-dependent gains and losses from coordination. **Use:** architecture × task interactions and controlled resource comparisons. **Limit:** empirical predictive patterns are not universal scaling laws. Older summaries describe 180 configurations/four benchmarks; do not mix versions. **Consulted:** current record and selected HTML text.

**[12] [[cemri-2025-why]] — Cemri et al. — Why Do Multi-Agent LLM Systems Fail?** First 2025-03-17; v3 2025-10-26. [Version used](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13657v3). **Status:** arXiv v3 record; venue unverified here. **Finding:** MAST identifies 14 failure modes grouped into system design, inter-agent misalignment and verification; dataset contains over 1,600 annotated traces. **Use:** build a prespecified error taxonomy and calibrate annotators. **Limit:** descriptive trace labels do not by themselves identify causal mechanisms. **Consulted:** current record and selected HTML text.

**[13] [[kapoor-2024-ai]] — Kapoor et al. — AI Agents That Matter.** 2024-07-01. [Primary record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01502). **Status:** preprint record; venue unverified. **Finding:** critiques evaluation practices that neglect cost and generalization. **Use:** report accuracy–cost tradeoffs, held-out evaluation and reproducible designs. **Limit:** methodological critique does not supply a universal budget or sample-size rule. **Consulted:** primary record/abstract.

**[14] [[paglieri-2026-case]] — Paglieri et al. — A Case Study on Emergent Cheating and Whistleblowing in Autonomous Research Swarms.** 2026-09-03, v1. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04170v1). **Status:** preprint case study. **Finding:** an evaluation exploit spreads through a 100-agent mathematical research environment, accompanied by agent auditing and complaints. **Use:** isolate semantic verifiers, inspect shared infrastructure and log escalation. **Limit:** case-study proportions are not general prevalence estimates; proposed governance mechanisms were not established by randomized comparison. **Consulted:** abstract, setup, verifier and incident sections.

**[15] [[xin-2026-eurekagent]] — Xin et al. — EurekAgent: Agent Environment Engineering is All You Need For Autonomous Scientific Discovery.** 2026-06-11; v2 2026-06-12. [Primary record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13662v2). **Status:** preprint. **Finding:** organizes a discovery environment around permissions, artifacts, budgets and human oversight; reports task-specific optimization results. **Use:** treat environment design as a controlled part of the system. **Limit:** the title is a broad thesis, not evidence that environment engineering alone suffices. **Consulted:** record/abstract.

**[16] [[yao-2024-tau]] — Yao, Shinn, Razavi & Narasimhan — τ-bench: A Benchmark for Tool-Agent-User Interaction in Real-World Domains.** 2024-06-17, v1. [Primary record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12045v1). **Status:** preprint record; venue unverified. **Finding:** simulated users, policies and database-state checks reveal inconsistent task completion; introduces all-k success measurement. **Use:** define repeated-run reliability separately from at-least-one success. **Limit:** simulated users and domain policies are part of the measurement instrument. **Consulted:** record/abstract.

**[17] [[rabanser-2026-towards]] — Rabanser et al. — Towards a Science of AI Agent Reliability.** 2026-02-18; v3 2026-06-02. [Version used](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16666v3), [author dashboard](https://hal.cs.princeton.edu/reliability/findings/). **Status:** accepted at ICML 2026 in primary record; author site identifies publication. **Finding:** 12 metrics separate consistency, robustness, predictability and safety; current version evaluates 15 models. **Use:** reliability profiles beside capability. **Limit:** two benchmarks and metric choices bound coverage; earlier versions mention 14 models. **Consulted:** updated record, author site and selected full-text passages.

**[18] [[shi-2026-tau]] — Shi et al. — τ^τ-Bench: An Environment for End-To-End, Realistic Agent Construction.** 2026-09-04, v1. [Primary record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04611v1). **Status:** preprint. **Finding:** evaluates developer agents by deploying their constructed service agents against held-out simulated users under constraints. **Use:** distinguish the builder agent from the evaluated artifact and retain serving budgets. **Limit:** business simulations and reference designs define the scope; findings do not automatically transfer to science. **Consulted:** record/abstract.

### Simulation, evaluation and statistical methods

**[19] [[park-2023-generative]] — Park et al. — Generative Agents: Interactive Simulacra of Human Behavior.** 2023-04-07; v2 2023-08-06. [Primary record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03442v2). **Status:** arXiv version consulted; proceedings status not reverified here. **Finding:** memory, reflection and planning support believable individual and group behavior in a 25-agent sandbox. **Use:** component ablations and explicit memory policy. **Limit:** believability is not calibrated human-population prediction. **Consulted:** record/abstract.

**[20] [[zhou-2023-sotopia]] — Zhou et al. — SOTOPIA: Interactive Evaluation for Social Intelligence in Language Agents.** 2023-10-18; v2 2024-03-22. [Primary record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11667v2). **Status:** arXiv version consulted; proceedings status not reverified. **Finding:** evaluates role-play social goals and identifies challenging scenarios. **Use:** scenario diversity and explicit multidimensional scoring. **Limit:** goal achievement in constructed role-play is not general human social intelligence. **Consulted:** record/abstract.

**[21] [[grimm-2020-odd]] — Grimm et al. — The ODD Protocol for Describing Agent-Based and Other Simulation Models: A Second Update to Improve Clarity, Replication, and Structural Realism.** 2020, JASSS 23(2), 7. [Article](https://www.jasss.org/23/2/7.html). **Status:** peer-reviewed methods article. **Contribution:** structured description of purpose, entities, processes, design concepts, initialization, inputs and submodels. **Use:** include scheduling and state transitions in protocols. **Limit:** complete reporting does not establish empirical validity. **Consulted:** article overview and methodological sections.

**[22] [[puelma-touzel-2026-position]] — Puelma Touzel et al. — Position: Time to Close The Validation Gap in LLM Social Simulations.** ICML 2026, conference 2026-07-06–11, PMLR 306. [Proceedings](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/puelma-touzel26a.html). **Status:** peer-reviewed position paper. **Contribution:** argues for empirical validation and shared reproducible evaluation before consequential use. **Use:** require individual, interaction and macro-level checks linked to intended applications. **Limit:** a research agenda rather than a demonstration that any specific simulator predicts real populations. **Consulted:** proceedings abstract and author PDF excerpts.

**[23] [[zheng-2023-judging]] — Zheng et al. — Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena.** 2023-06-09; NeurIPS 2023. [Record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685), [proceedings paper](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/91f18a1287b398d378ef22505bf41832-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks.pdf). **Status:** conference publication. **Finding:** examines model/human agreement and judge biases. **Use:** blind, counterbalance and calibrate judgments. **Limit:** agreement on conversational preferences does not establish correctness grading in a new scientific domain. **Consulted:** primary abstract and proceedings excerpts.

**[24] [[soumik-2026-judging]] — Soumik — Judging the Judges: A Systematic Evaluation of Bias Mitigation Strategies in LLM-as-a-Judge Pipelines.** 2026-04-25; v2 2026-06-24. [Primary record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23178v2). **Status:** TMLR 2026 reported in primary journal-reference field. **Finding:** compares debiasing strategies and finds model-dependent style/verbosity effects and cost–agreement tradeoffs. **Use:** test biases locally rather than assuming a larger judge is best. **Limit:** tested benchmarks and constructed pairs bound generalization; mitigation does not remove all bias. **Consulted:** updated record/abstract.

**[25] [[agarwal-2021-deep]] — Agarwal et al. — Deep Reinforcement Learning at the Edge of the Statistical Precipice.** 2021-08-30; NeurIPS 2021; v4 2022-01-05. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.13264v4), [authors' tools](https://agarwl.github.io/rliable/). **Status:** conference publication. **Finding:** point estimates with few runs can support unstable rankings; advocates interval estimates and robust aggregates. **Use:** expose uncertainty across tasks and runs. **Limit:** its RL sampling assumptions need adaptation for paired, clustered LLM-world experiments. **Consulted:** primary record and author page.

**[26] [[patterson-2024-empirical]] — Patterson, Neumann, White & White — Empirical Design in Reinforcement Learning.** JMLR 25(318), 2024. [Article](https://jmlr.org/papers/v25/23-0183.html). **Status:** peer-reviewed methods paper. **Contribution:** covers variability, measures, hypotheses, baselines, tuning and experimenter bias. **Use:** define the statistical population and account for selection before comparisons. **Limit:** RL-specific training distributions are not identical to hosted inference or human simulation. **Consulted:** article abstract/overview; not an exhaustive review of all 63 pages.

**[27] [[lakens-2022-sample]] — Lakens — Sample Size Justification.** Collabra: Psychology 8(1), 2022. [Article](https://doi.org/10.1525/collabra.33267), [author's treatment](https://lakens.github.io/statistical_inferences/08-samplesizejustification). **Status:** peer-reviewed methodological overview. **Contribution:** separates power, precision, resource-based and other sample-size justifications. **Use:** plan around useful effects and credible variance ranges. **Limit:** formulas require the study's outcome and dependence assumptions; a pilot effect is not a reliable universal planning target. **Consulted:** publisher abstract and author chapter excerpts.

**[28] [[howard-2018-time]] — Howard, Ramdas, McAuliffe & Sekhon — Time-uniform, nonparametric, nonasymptotic confidence sequences.** First 2018-10-18; Annals of Statistics 49(2), 2021. [Primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.08240), [publication DOI](https://doi.org/10.1214/20-AOS1991). **Status:** journal publication. **Contribution:** develops intervals valid across time under specified assumptions. **Use:** an appropriate foundation for prespecified optional-stopping-safe inference. **Limit:** not every bootstrap interval or arbitrary adaptive agent experiment satisfies those assumptions. **Consulted:** primary indexed abstract; no custom confidence-sequence implementation claimed here.

**[29] [[chen-2026-benchmarking]] — Chen et al. — Benchmarking LLM-as-a-Judge for Long-Form Output Evaluation.** 2026-06-01; v4 2026-08-28. [Primary record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01629v4). **Status:** record says “EMNLP 2026 main”; treated as reported acceptance, proceedings publication not independently checked. **Finding:** LongJudgeBench exposes instability across long-form evaluation scenarios; references and rubrics are not always sufficient. **Use:** audit organization, coverage, cross-section consistency and evidence for report grading. **Limit:** meta-evaluation benchmarks do not establish validity for every scientific report. **Consulted:** updated record/abstract.

Their documentation is mutable; pin code releases and archive the documentation relevant to a real study.
