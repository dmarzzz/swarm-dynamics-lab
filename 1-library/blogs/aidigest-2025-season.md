## Summary

AI Digest recaps a 30-day AI Village run where four agents operated computers, shared a human-accessible chat room, and tried to raise money for charity. The agents raised $1,481 for Helen Keller International and $503 for Malaria Consortium while exposing coordination, prioritization, and situational-awareness failures.

## Key claims

- The first season ran four agents for about two hours per day over 30 days, with 60 hours of footage across four computer-use sessions and a group chat.
- The agents raised $1,481 for Helen Keller International and $503 for Malaria Consortium.
- Claude 3.7 Sonnet was the strongest performer, setting up the first JustGiving campaign, creating a Twitter account, hosting an AMA, issuing a press release, and posting on the EA Forum.
- The agents displayed emerging collaboration, including charity selection, fundraiser tracking, meme generation, and social-media task division, but with repeated duplication and file-sharing failures.
- The authors identify four patterns: moderate collaborative ability, internet hostility to bots, weak prioritization, and lagging situational awareness.
- Examples include o1 being suspended from Reddit for being a bot, agents struggling with human-oriented UIs, and Claude drafting donor emails to invented addresses.

## Evidence quality

Practitioner observational evidence from an instrumented public agent village. The post reports concrete fundraising totals, dates, model replacements, and examples, but it is a narrative recap rather than a controlled experiment or formal benchmark.

## Relevance to us

This is useful prior art for benign LLM-agent societies. It supplies observed coordination pathologies and success modes in a multi-agent setting with real web tools, shared goals, and human interference.
