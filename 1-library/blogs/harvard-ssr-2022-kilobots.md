## Summary

Lab project page for the Kilobot swarm. The live page (https://ssr.seas.harvard.edu/kilobots) returned 403 Access Denied on 2026-10-03, so this entry is from the Wayback snapshot of 18 March 2022; the page itself is undated, and the year field is the snapshot year. The Kilobot is a 1024-robot swarm built from low-cost parts and mostly assembled automatically, with a programmable controller, vibration locomotion and local communication, designed so one user can program, power and charge the whole collective hands-off. The lab uses it for collective behaviours (synchronisation, collective transport, self-assembly) and for theory linking minimal individual capability to achievable swarm behaviour. Listed topics: collective perception and decision-making (AAMAS 2018, ICRA 2020 "Bayes Bots"), programmable self-assembly and disassembly (Science 2014, DARS 2016), error cascades and robustness (AAMAS 2017), collective transport (2013). The page says the robots are sold by K-Team, over 8,000 exist across dozens of labs, the design is open under CC BY-NC-SA 3.0, and the Kilobotics web programming environment is provided. Funding: DARPA DSO, NSF, Wyss Institute.

## Key claims

- A thousand-robot swarm is operable by one person when programming, power and charging are all broadcast.
- Over 8,000 Kilobots are in use across dozens of labs, including biology groups (UCSF Lim lab, NJIT Garnier lab) (lab's own claim).
- Science named the Kilobot swarm a top-10 breakthrough of 2014 (as stated on page).

## Evidence quality

Institutional project page, self-reported. Technical results live in the linked papers: [[rubenstein-2012-kilobot]] and [[rubenstein-2014-programmable]] are catalogued. Adoption numbers are the lab's claim and not checked.

## Relevance to us

Hub page for the main physical-swarm platform; useful when choosing a hardware-validated benchmark. The "error cascades and robustness" line (AAMAS 2017) is relevant to how one faulty unit propagates through a collective, a robotics counterpart to corruption propagation in agent swarms.
