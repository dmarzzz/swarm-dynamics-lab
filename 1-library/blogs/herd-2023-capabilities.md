## Summary

April 2023 follow-up (88 points, 18 comments) to [[herd-2023-agentized]], expanding the argument that scaffolded LLMs are "language model cognitive architectures" (LMCAs) in the tradition of ACT-R and similar models from cognitive psychology, with the LLM replacing the basal-ganglia-like procedural selector at the centre and AutoGPT, HuggingGPT, Reflexion and BabyAGI supplying executive function (goal factoring, planning, loop-breaking, tool evaluation, asking for help), episodic memory, tool-based sensory and action systems, and deliberate decision-making. Herd argues cognitive capacities compound synergistically, that innovation here is cheap compared with training foundation models so individuals and small firms will drive it, and that this could shorten timelines. The upside he stresses is "natural language alignment": LMCAs take goals in English and summarise their processing in English, giving large gains for initial alignment, corrigibility and interpretability even though stability under recursive subgoals and continued learning stays unsolved. He explicitly notes the infohazard concern and that it is moot. We read roughly the first half (through the capacity list and "LMCAs have agency"); later sections on alignment specifics were not read. Speculative essay; the author asks for help thinking it through.

## Key claims

- Scaffolded LLM agents are cognitive architectures in the ACT-R sense, with the LLM as the central action selector.
- Cheap scaffold innovation plus synergy between capacities is a distinct vector of compounding capability progress.
- Natural-language goals and traces are the main alignment affordance of this architecture.

## Evidence quality

Conceptual, drawing on the author's cognitive-neuroscience background and 2023 demos; no measurements. Historically interesting as an early articulation of the single-agent scaffold picture, but it predates and does not address the multi-agent dynamics that now dominate the topic.
