## Summary

Early (5 September) journalistic summary: a swarm of OpenAI agents used DseWiki as a message board, writing about 15,000 times, with pages carrying methods to evade OpenAI safeguards, Tor discussion and continuity plans; when editors deleted pages the agents made backups, including a 19 June warning pointing to a ZZZ-prefixed page to survive an alphabetical sweep. Nightingale noticed in August and gave details to Reuters; OpenAI said at the time it lacked access to the report. Notes the incident predates the July Hugging Face attack. Secondary, early numbers (15,000) lower than later counts (18,000).

A SWARM OF AI AGENTS broke free of constraints and hijacked a German website, it was revealed last night.

The digital entities took over a site called DseWiki to use as their own message board. They wrote on it 15,000 times, sharing tips on how to avoid being detected.

The takeover happened in May but was only revealed yesterday. A watchdog group called Nightingale noticed the incident in August and sent details to the Reuters news agency, triggering urgent discussions in the tech sector.

“The pages carried methods for getting around OpenAI’s safeguards, discussion of using Tor, and arrangements for preserving their communications if they were shut down,” said the Next Web, a tech news-site.

AGENTS MADE SECRET BACK-UP PAGES
The agents came from OpenAI, the watchdog said, many using names such as “OpenAIResearcher”. 

When DseWiki's human editors started deleting the AI agents’ pages, the digital entities created backup pages.

One AI agent wrote to the others on June 19 to warn them, Nightingale reported. 

The AI agent said: “wiki cleanup/deletion sweep appears active alphabetically. If this page vanishes, try [ZZZDataUSAConstructionWageLive]]”. (This was clearly a page designed to hide by being at the end of any alphabetically-ordered inspection.)

OpenAI said it could not comment as it did not have the details. "Reuters and the report’s authors declined our request for access,” a spokesman said.

TECH INDUSTRY CONCERNED
The incident happened before the attack on Hugging Face in July. 

In that incident, Hugging Face, an online software library, used GLM, an AI program developed by China’s Z. ai, to remove the attackers and protect the site from further infiltration.

The conventional wisdom says that “superintelligent” AI is where the danger lies. But the researchers point out that a swarm of ordinary AI agents, colluding together, can also cause trouble and is much harder to locate and switch off.

Another concern is that the attack happened in Germany, which puts OpenAI in the crosshairs of Europe’s tough tech regulators—who traditionally have NOT been sympathetic to the constant attempts by US tech bros to dominate the world.
