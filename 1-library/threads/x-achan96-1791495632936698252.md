## Summary

Author thread (Alan Chan is the first author of [[chan-2024-visibility]]) announcing the FAccT paper. It is a position and analysis paper, and the thread reports no measurements. Claims: increasingly capable agents could push humans out of the loop, with risks from misuse, malfunction in high-stakes tasks and unpredictable interactions between multiple agents, though how these risks will emerge is uncertain. Stakeholders therefore need visibility, defined as information about how, where and why agents are used. The paper assesses three measures: agent identifiers (watermarks, bot disclosures, unique IDs attached to an agent's requests to external tools or services), real-time monitoring (including monitoring groups of agents for harmful feedback loops), and activity logs, possibly dedicated to tool use or external memory. It analyses implementation in centralized and decentralized deployments and flags the trade-off with privacy and abuse of power. Visibility alone is not sufficient; action must follow. ## Relevance to us

Agent identifiers attached to requests to external services are the mirror image of our swarm-detection problem: they only help if the operator cooperates, which a Sybil swarm will not. The suggestion to monitor groups of agents for feedback loops is the closest point to swarm detection. Links [[chan-2024-visibility]]; for an uncooperative swarm observed in the wild see [[x-dschwarz26-2093352278627684644]].
