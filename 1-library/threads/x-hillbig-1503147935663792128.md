## Summary

Commentary in Japanese; the poster is not a paper author. He explains model soups: when fine-tuning a
pretrained model, average the weights of several models fine-tuned with different hyperparameters. Because the
fine-tunes fall into the same basin, taking their centroid improves generalisation and robustness. He adds that
adding models greedily, starting from the best-performing one, works well. No numbers.

## Relevance to us

Weight averaging of sibling fine-tunes from one parent is the plainest form of fork and merge
([[wortsman-2022-model]]). The greedy recipe, which adds an ingredient only when it helps, is a merge threshold
in miniature, and a natural place to test whether one corrupted sibling can slip into the average (our framing).
