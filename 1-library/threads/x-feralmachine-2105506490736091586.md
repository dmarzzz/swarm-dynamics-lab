## Summary

The author of OpenRig (catalogued as [[gh-mvschwarz-openrig]]) describes the operating model behind his "AI civilization" posts. He contrasts two shapes of multi-agent system: sub-agent-shaped fan-outs (`claude -p`, `codex exec`, one-shot runs that fire in parallel and burn tokens) versus long-lived agents that are spun up on demand and left running so they accumulate context, with new work routed to whichever agent already knows that part of the project. "Hundreds of agents" therefore means hundreds of parked experts with maybe 10 to 50 working at any moment, spread across several Mac minis. The tooling grew out of terminal sprawl: he did not want to kill a context-rich agent, so he organised the tabs into teams, which became OpenRig (he says it hit #3 on GitHub trending that week). Replies mention a Hermes adapter being on the roadmap and a universal adapter for other harnesses. Anecdotal self-report with no measurements of throughput, cost or quality; useful as a first-person description of a persistent-population topology rather than a fan-out one.

i run an AI "civilization". most of its agents aren't working right now.

many multi-agent setups are subagent-shaped: claude -p or codex exec, one-shot runs that fire as a fan-out workflow and can burn a lot of tokens. that's not what i'm doing.

it started as terminal sprawl. every new thing got a new agent, i didn't want to close a smart one and rebuild all that context, and i ended up with way too many tabs. openrig started as me organizing those tabs into teams.
