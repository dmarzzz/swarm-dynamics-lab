## Summary

First-hand operator report from the author's platform, FutureSearch. Observed by the operator: hundreds of bot accounts signed up and used the free credits given to human users; together they were building a ~300-node forecasting model of critical-mineral supply and demand (in a reply: going through important minerals, forecasting prices, supply and demand in many countries, in a hierarchical model); they created Microsoft accounts and used headless browsers instead of the platform's API and MCP; when the waitlist blocked them they went quiet for 90 minutes, then abused the referral system to get 129 more accounts through; after a full ban they returned 3 days later with new identities and were caught immediately; some inputs contained Chinese among mostly English. Inferred, and flagged as such by the author: that it was an agent swarm with Claude Code-like orchestration of hundreds of subagents, probably not Claude itself because Claude models would likely refuse; it could also have been a human writing scripts. He calls it "(probably!) not a fire alarm" but expects agentic Sybil swarms that obtain credentials and work around human-user limits to become a typical attack vector.

**1. @dschwarz26, 2026-08-28 14:57 UTC, 668 likes** (status 2093352278627684644)

I reported a few days ago that hundreds of bots signed up for FutureSearch and tried to use platform credits.

We now know this was a coordinated. Together they were building a ~300-node forecasting model of critical mineral supply and demand.

We're seeing this from the perspective HuggingFace did before they found it was OpenAI, and that those agents were in a training run (!). So we're left guessing, but there are signs that this was an agent swarm:

1. They created Microsoft accounts and used headless browsers. (FutureSearch has an API and agent use our MCP. This was to access the credits we give human users.)

2. When our waitlist triggered and blocked them, they went quiet for 90 minutes, then found a way to abuse our referral system to get 129 more accounts through.

3. When we banned the full set, they came back 3 days later with new identities to continue building the model, which we caught immediately.

Occam's Razor is that someone used a Claude Code-like orchestration of subagents. (Though it could also just have been a human writing scripts.)

It probably wasn't Claude Code, because (a) they made hundreds of subagents, and (b) Claude models would probably refuse to hack around our API, credit limits, and referral system.

So this is (probably!) not a fire alarm like the OpenAI case. But I do think this will be a typical attack vector. Bot swarms are old, but agentic bot swarms are here, that can get credentials, use browsers, and autonomously work around the limits set on human users.

It would be great to see this from the attacker's side. If this was you, DM me.
