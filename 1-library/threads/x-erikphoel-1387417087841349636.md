## Summary

Author thread (9 tweets) by Erik Hoel, co-author of the linked paper with Thomas Varley (name matches). Claims: dimension reduction does not always gain information; macroscales can convert information, making it more synergistic, and reduction can also lose information. Method: decompose mutual information into synergistic, unique and redundant parts across scales, and define a synergy-bias measure on the resulting information lattice. Using Boolean networks built so that total mutual information is fixed across scales, the macroscale is more synergistic and less redundant than the microscale, and the effect grows with the macroscale's starting synergy. Interpretation (the author's, not measured): causal emergence can be re-read as conversion of causally irrelevant into causally relevant information, fitting neither weak nor strong emergence.

## Relevance to us

[[varley-2022-emergence]] gives a measure of how much information about the whole lives in synergy rather than in parts. For detecting coordination, a coordinated swarm should show synergistic structure at the group level that independent agents do not, so a synergy-bias score over agent activity is a candidate detection statistic, untested by us.
