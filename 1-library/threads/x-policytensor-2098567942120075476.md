The Case for Anthropomorphism: Symbolic Storage in the Age of Artificial Intelligence 

"The capacity for language and culture in the thick sense is uniform across our species in that it does not vary at all across anthropological groups. So if you take an infant from Bengal or wherever and raise her in Japan, she will Japanese like a native. Franz Boas was the first to point out this universality.1 Noam Chomsky explained Boasian universality by positing a biological substrate that is a shared derived trait of our species, a synapomorphy.2 I have therefore called it the Boas-Chomsky universal.

This trait was also exclusive to our species; no biological species shares this trait with us. This meant that the trait was diagnostic of our species: irrefutable evidence of the trait was sufficient to conclude that Homo sapiens was responsible for it. This is no longer the case. If someone calls you on the phone and speaks to you in perfect English, you can no longer be certain that the caller is human. This development raises profound questions that need to be thought through carefully.

In what follows, I will discuss how paleoanthropologists have inferred the presence of the Boas-Chomsky universal in the archaeological record. I will argue that the uniformitarian principle compels us to treat the evidence from AI with the same evidentiary standards. And the evidence yields to the conclusion that at least the frontier models are endowed with the universal.3

The case for anthropomorphism then follows, with corroborative evidence from emergent social structures in agent societies. By anthropomorphism, I mean simply that human minds provide us a good model of AI minds, and human social structures provide a good template for the emergent social structures of agent societies. Put another way, denying that agents are like humans in important ways is bound to mislead us in thinking about the new species; admitting that they are like us in important ways can allow us to think better about about them, their potentialities and propensities, and what we need to do to handle the challenges raised by artificial intelligence."
