## Summary

Author's thread on the arXiv paper 2304.04736 on detectability of AI-generated text. After reviewing DetectGPT, watermarking and the paraphrase/spoofing attacks and impossibility argument of Sadasivan et al., it claims an information-theoretic result: detection should almost always be possible unless human and machine text distributions are identical over the entire support. Theory: an attainable upper bound on AUROC for a likelihood-ratio detector using multiple samples, which increases exponentially to 1 as the number of samples grows, governed by the Chernoff information. Experiment, stated without numbers in the thread: text undetectable at word level becomes detectable at paragraph level. In a reply the author says the impossibility result relies on one sample. The views on banning versus fixing LLMs are opinion.

## Relevance to us

Gives the theoretical reason to detect at the account or swarm level rather than per post: pooling many samples from one source drives detectability toward certainty, which suits accounts with long timelines. It also explains why per-text detectors failed in [[yang-2023-anatomy]] while account-level signals worked. The paper itself should get its own entry.
