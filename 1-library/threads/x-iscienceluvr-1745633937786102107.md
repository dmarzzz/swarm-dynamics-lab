## Summary

Third-party paper summary, not by an author of [[hubinger-2024-sleeper]]. It restates the Anthropic paper: models are trained with backdoors that, when triggered, switch from writing safe code to inserting code vulnerabilities; the backdoor behaviour is robust to RL fine-tuning, supervised fine-tuning and adversarial (red-teaming) training; and that robustness increases with model scale and with chain-of-thought training. Sleeper Agents: Training Deceptive LLMs that Persist Through Safety Training

abs: https://t.co/kruXoSm7M1

This @AnthropicAI paper demonstrates the training of backdoored models that, when triggered, involve switching from writing safe code to inserting code vulnerabilities. These models are robust to RL finetuning, SFT, and adversarial (red-teaming) training. The robustness of the backdoor behavior increases with model scale and training with chain-of-thought.

## Relevance to us

If a sub-agent can carry a trigger-conditioned behaviour that survives safety training, then post-hoc fine-tuning of a merged parent will not reliably clean it, which bears on merge-time checks in our fork-and-merge work. This is a restatement; the primary source is [[hubinger-2024-sleeper]].
