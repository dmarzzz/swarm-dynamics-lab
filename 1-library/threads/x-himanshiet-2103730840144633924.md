## Summary

An Economic Times reporter's summary of her own story on how Indian digital-commerce companies are preparing for large populations of consumer-side AI agents (Meta's Muse, Grokbot, OCPlatform, Hermes and others) hitting their websites and apps. Reported responses, attributed to unnamed experts: rethinking product pricing and customer-engagement cost models; revisiting bot-verification techniques because the incoming traffic is agents acting for real customers rather than scrapers; amending privacy policies to obtain consent for agentic transactions under India's DPDP law; forecasting higher cloud bills from millions of agents automating thousands of searches; multi-crore security programmes against rogue agent behaviour; and booking higher fraud liabilities on the assumption that agents will try to manipulate systems. She notes Amazon blocked an agent from shopping on its platform while Shopify, Expedia, PayPal and Instacart embraced them, and gives 958 million Indian internet users as the scale that worries executives. Journalism relaying industry anecdotes; no measurements of agent traffic in the tweet itself.

**1. @HimanshiET, 2026-09-26 06:18 UTC, 3 likes** (status 2103730840144633924)

India’s digital commerce firms have started preparing for swarms of personal AI assistants which shall be interacting with their websites and mobile applications very soon. Companies have started rethinking product pricing, customer engagement costs as well as bot verification techniques, experts told ET. 

They have approached lawyers on how their privacy policies can be amended to comply with DPDP and obtain consent for agentic transactions. 

Companies are forecasting a jump in cloud bills when millions of agents generate heavy server load or automate thousands of searches. 

They are also running multi-crore programmes to secure codebases, web/mobile apps, to prevent rogue agentic behaviour and embarrassing customer incidents. Some have even factored an increase in fraud-related liabilities in their P&L as agents try to manipulate their systems.

Agents like Instinct, Muse (from Meta), Grokbot (from SpaceXAI), OpenClaw, Hermes etc. have already taken over consumer internet in the US, forcing technology giants to relook their policies and business strategies. Amazon blocked the agent from shopping on its platform citing security concerns. Meanwhile, others like Shopify, Expedia Group, PayPal, Instacart moved to embrace it. 

Although Muse is not available in India yet, the velocity with which it can scale with Meta’s distribution muscle and 958 million overall internet users has put tech leaders on alert.
