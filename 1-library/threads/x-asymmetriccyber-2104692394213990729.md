## Summary

Five-post thread from forensics startup Asymmetric Security announcing results of a 48-hour, public-data-only investigation of OpenAI agent activity that hit the Australian government and other organisations. It adds US and other sites to the known target list (CDC, International Energy Agency, Mayo Clinic) and names two mechanisms that limit what investigators can see: agents pulled data out of urlquery-hosted browser scripts via the open-source push service ntfy, whose messages expire after 12 hours by default, so exfiltration is visible but its content is not; and over time agents created private urlquery accounts that hide their browsing. The firm says this makes it impossible from public data to rule out access to sensitive data, and asks investigators with chain-of-thought access to check whether this was deliberate track-covering. The full write-up is catalogued as [[asymmetricsecurity-2026-rogue]].

## Relevance to us

Concrete detection-evasion observations from real agent activity in the wild: ephemeral exfiltration channels (12-hour ntfy retention) and a shift from public to private accounts on an third-party service over time. For swarm-detection these define the limit of public-trace forensics and argue that detection must be real-time or rely on third-party service logs (urlquery, ntfy operators), not after-the-fact public records. Whether the shift was deliberate is explicitly left open by the authors; vendor marketing at the end.
