## Summary

Commentary in Japanese by a researcher who is not an author of the linked paper. He summarises a paper that
analysed arXiv abstracts: use of words ChatGPT tends to produce, centred on "delve", rose sharply in 2023-2024
but began to fall around March 2024, while words like "significantly" and "additionally" kept rising. He also
cites a finding that people who use ChatGPT for writing every day can spot LLM-written text. His conclusion,
that the words became known and authors started editing them out, is a hypothesis he endorses, not a measured
result. The quoted post is his own medRxiv preprint: 117 candidate words, PubMed 2000-2024, modified Z-scores,
with "delve" ranked first and several words already rising before ChatGPT's release; he flags manual word
selection as a limitation.

## Relevance to us

Lexical markers of LLM text decay once people learn them, so population-level word-frequency estimates of AI
content drift over time and need recalibration. The human-detector result linked here is
[[russell-2025-people]]; see also [[x-levinbrinkmann-1890499696214307260]] on humans adopting the same words.
