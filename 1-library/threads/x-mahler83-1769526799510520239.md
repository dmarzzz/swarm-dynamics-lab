## Summary

Long Korean-language reading-notes thread by a non-author (display name 말러팔삼) on [[liang-2024-monitoring]], ending with a link to his blog post. Restated method: build human and AI review corpora, compute per-adjective (and adverb) frequencies in each, model a mixed corpus as (1-α)P+αQ and estimate α by maximum likelihood, validated on synthetic data. Restated findings: α rose after ChatGPT's release; ML conference reviews show roughly 7-15% LLM use while Nature reviews show no such evidence; α is higher for reviews submitted in the last 3 days before the deadline, lower when reviews contain "et al.", lower for reviewers who replied more in discussion, higher where reviewers agree, and higher for low-confidence (1-2) than high-confidence (3-5) reviewers; ChatGPT proofreading barely changes α, while outline-to-prose expansion is detected. His own opinions, labelled as such: it is a warning about LLM-written papers and reviews, homogenised reviews may reduce diversity, and using an LLM to turn bullet-point reviews into prose is acceptable.

## Relevance to us

The distributional estimator is a direct template for swarm detection at population level: estimate the fraction of agent-written content in a feed without classifying any single post. The behavioural correlates (deadline timing, low engagement, higher agreement) are the kind of side signals a swarm detector can add. See [[liang-2024-monitoring]] and the word-frequency approach in [[x-gengmingmeng-1891579742706409728]].
