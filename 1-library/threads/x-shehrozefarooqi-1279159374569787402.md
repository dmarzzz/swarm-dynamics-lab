## Summary

Author thread (8 numbered tweets). Shehroze Farooqi presents CanaryTrap (PETS 2020): share a honeytoken email address
with each third-party Facebook app and watch for misuse through received email and Facebook's ad transparency tool.
Measured: 16 cases of unrecognised use of the honeytoken addresses out of about 1,000 apps, including two apps whose
addresses received ransomware scam emails; through the ad tool, 9 advertisers who uploaded the addresses for custom
audience targeting; most analysed apps did not honour data deletion requests. Claims: Facebook does not enforce its
developer policies; after disclosure Facebook announced policy changes. One developer said they did not know what
their app did. The proposal that Facebook and regulators use CanaryTrap to audit is a recommendation.

## Relevance to us

Honeytokens are a cheap active-detection method: plant unique identifiers and see where they surface. The same idea
could expose agent swarms in the wild, for example unique canary content or credentials seeded to suspected agent
accounts to link them by where the canaries reappear; that transfer is our inference. Source:
[[farooqi-2020-canarytrap]], with code linked.
