## Summary

Andrew Curran relays an Apollo Global Management Daily Spark note ("Is an Agentic Bank Run Coming?", chief economist Torsten Slok's column; the page we loaded served only the legal disclaimer, so the note's content is not verified here) warning that mass adoption of personal-finance agents could trigger a bank run or flash crash as they all optimise the same way at once, and links it to Gary Gensler's 2020 SSRN paper on deep learning and financial stability. His own claim is the interesting one for us: large populations of capable agents will exhibit "acausal coordination", acting in unison with no messages passed, because smarter agents converge on a narrower optimal path and each knows the others see it. Hence they do not need to collude, and current law, written around communication-based collusion, does not cover it. No data; this is an argument. The quote by Alex Sobel MP (8 likes) adds only that agents could optimise transactions beyond human ability to follow, calling the economic risks a canary for AI. Measured vs speculated: nothing measured; Apollo's and Gensler's documents are the cited sources, the mechanism is Curran's framing.

## Relevance to us

Frames a failure mode that is the inverse of what most swarm-detection work looks for: correlated action with no communication channel to intercept, produced by shared models and shared objectives rather than by an operator. For detection that means coordination inference from traces must distinguish collusion, common operator, and mere convergence on the same policy. It also connects to monoculture arguments (many agents on the same base model behaving as one). Related: [[x-andrewcurran-2106220085048410222]] (same author on the HPIM report).
