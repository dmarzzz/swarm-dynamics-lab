## Summary

Single-tweet announcement of an updated version of the Sybil-proof mechanisms paper [[pan-2024-sybil]], now with Minghao Pan (Caltech), Bruno Mazorra and Christoph Schlegel (both Flashbots). The reported new result: there exist mechanisms that are non-wasteful, symmetric, Bayesian incentive compatible and Bayesian Sybil-proof but do not always allocate to the highest bidder. The author concludes that under Sybil constraints Bayesian implementation is strictly weaker than dominant-strategy implementation and gives a larger design space, unlike the classical setting without Sybils. This is a theoretical claim reported from the paper; no numbers are given.

We updated a paper on Sybil-proof mechanisms (https://t.co/C6NU5vXhc8), for quite some time now. It is a joint work with Minghao Pan (from @Caltech), Bruno Mazorra (@0xBrMazoRoig) and Christoph Schlegel (both from Flashbots). The main addition is that there are mechanisms which are non-wasteful, symmetric, Bayesian incentive compatible, and Bayesian Sybil-proof -- yet they do not always allocate to the highest bidder. Therefore, under Sybil-constraints and in contrast to the classical mechanism design setting without Sybils, Bayesian implementation is strictly weaker than dominant strategy implementation and allows for a larger design space.

## Relevance to us

Extends the earlier announcement [[x-kakia1989-1815703685269274823]]: the "only second price auction" characterisation holds in dominant strategies, while Bayesian Sybil-proofness admits other allocation rules. For an agent collective that allocates work or reward among members whose identity count is unverifiable, this widens the set of usable mechanisms if we accept prior assumptions. Read with [[pan-2024-sybil]] and [[conitzer-2010-using]].
