## Summary

Author thread by Manoel Horta Ribeiro, a co-author of the linked paper (name matches). Measured: re-running an earlier MTurk task (summarising New England Journal of Medicine abstracts), the authors estimate 33-46% of crowd workers used LLMs. Method: a bespoke classifier trained on answers from the original study plus ChatGPT-generated summaries. Validation with keystroke data: summaries typed entirely in the MTurk textbox were all classified as real, and pasted summaries split clearly between extractive copying and LLM use. In replies he adds that task-specific classifiers worked far better than generalist detectors, which 'sucked' for this study, and that the setting was easy because human workers tended to extract chunks of the abstract. Speculation: adoption will rise as models improve; synthetic data may carry biases into trained models.

## Relevance to us

[[veselovsky-2023-artificial]] is a template for measuring AI agents hidden inside a population that is supposed to be human: a task-specific classifier plus behavioural validation from keystrokes. The finding that generic detectors failed while bespoke ones worked is directly useful for designing swarm detectors. Commentary on this thread: [[x-emollick-1669040772665188352]].
