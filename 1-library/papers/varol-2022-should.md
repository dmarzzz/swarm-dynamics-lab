## Summary

Written during the Musk-Twitter dispute over Twitter's claim that under 5% of monetizable daily active users were bots. Varol argues that bot-prevalence numbers depend on three choices that are rarely stated together: what counts as bot-like behaviour, which detection approach is used, and which population is inspected, so different estimates are not comparable without them. He also calls on platforms to be transparent about their own estimates.

## Contribution

A short methods note that names the free parameters of any prevalence estimate of automated accounts.

## Key results

- Twitter's public estimate: under 5% of monetizable daily active users are bots (as reported in the abstract).
- Argues that definition, detector and sampled population each change the measured bot share.

## Methods and models

Argument and illustration; no new dataset. Abstract-level read.

## Limitations and open questions

Position piece; does not resolve the 5% versus 9-15% gap with data.
