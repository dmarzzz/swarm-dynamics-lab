## Summary

Re-analyses a published data set tracking thousands of honeybees inside a hive over days. Occupancy
density fluctuations show long-range spatial and temporal correlations despite short-range interactions, and
density versus flow is non-monotonic, resembling traffic near a jamming transition where throughput is maximal.
The hive appears self-adjusted near an optimal density.

## Contribution

Extends scale-free correlation evidence to social insects inside a nest and connects it to
jamming/traffic criticality.

## Key results

- Long-range spatial and temporal correlations in occupancy density fluctuations (measured).
- Non-monotonic density-flow relation like traffic near jamming; hive near optimal density (measured/interpreted).

## Methods and models

Correlation analysis of tracked bee positions from a public data set.
