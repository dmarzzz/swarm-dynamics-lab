## Summary

WormLab applies heterogeneous mean-field theory and continuous-time event-driven dynamics to agent-network worm propagation. WormGuard is a decentralized per-agent containment sidecar, evaluated on scale-free networks. The work emphasizes timing and deployment heterogeneity rather than assuming a centralized guardian is always reachable.

## Contribution

Analysis of propagation, lifecycle security, containment or trust-boundary assessment.

## Key results

The indexed abstract reports synchronous simulations overestimating propagation speed by up to ninefold, central defences losing effectiveness around 80% locally running agents, and 94-97% containment efficacy for decentralized WormGuard. These statements await primary-method verification.

## Methods and models

Crossref publisher metadata and Semantic Scholar indexed abstract read; ACM primary page returned a client challenge. Mean-field model and event-driven experiments, according to indexed abstract.

## Limitations and open questions

Full model assumptions, efficacy denominator and implementation were not read; no unconditional real-world containment claim is justified.
