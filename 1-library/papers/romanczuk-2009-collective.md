## Summary

Motivated by cannibalism-driven marching in locusts and crickets, introduces Brownian particles interacting
only through escape (from those approaching from behind) and pursuit (of those ahead) responses, without
explicit alignment. Shows that collective motion emerges and that macroscopic observables depend strongly
on the ratio of escape to pursuit strength; analyses their different effects on motion statistics and
derives the scaling of migration speed. Read at abstract level from arXiv.

## Contribution

Shows alignment is not necessary for collective motion: non-cooperative interactions can produce it. A
mechanism tested directly in [[sayin-2025-behavioral]], which finds pursuit ("pull") matters in locusts.

## Key results

- Claimed: onset of collective motion from escape and pursuit; strong dependence on escape/pursuit ratio;
  scaling of migration speed with parameters.

## Methods and models

Active Brownian particles in 1D/2D with velocity-dependent escape and pursuit interactions.
