## Summary

Active colloids whose motility is set by an external feedback loop according to a "visual perception" of
peers (how many others are within a vision cone) form cohesive groups without any active reorientation.
Narrow fields of view give cohesive, non-polarized groups; for wider fields of view cohesion requires a
lower response threshold. Proposes motility-induced cohesion as a mechanism relevant for living systems and
scalable autonomous systems. Read at abstract level via Europe PMC.

## Contribution

Experimental demonstration that a perception-dependent speed rule alone (no alignment, no attraction force)
can produce group cohesion; complements [[bastien-2020-model]].

## Key results

- Measured: cohesive groups from perception-dependent motility; dependence on vision cone angle and threshold.

## Methods and models

Light-activated Janus colloids with feedback-controlled propulsion; vision-cone perception rule.

## Relevance to us

A minimal rule (move fast when you see few others, slow when you see many) worth trying in agent swarms.
