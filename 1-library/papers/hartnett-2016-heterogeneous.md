## Summary

Formulates collective decision-making as a spin system in which spin state is preference between two options and a local bias field is opinion strength. Unbiased (uninformed) spins maximise the probability of consensus and minimise its time through a stochastic-resonance-like effect, but which option wins depends on the nonlinearity of local interactions: near-linear responses let unbiased individuals reinforce the majority, strongly nonlinear responses let them favour a strongly biased minority.

## Contribution

Physics formulation reconciling [[couzin-2011-uninformed]] with cases where uninformed individuals help minorities; makes interaction nonlinearity the control variable.

## Key results

- Unbiased individuals enhance consensus probability and speed (model).
- Linear social response: democratic outcomes; highly nonlinear response: minority wins (model).

## Methods and models

Ising-like spin model with heterogeneous fields and tunable local nonlinearity; simulation and analysis.

Abstract spin model; mapping to real interaction rules is qualitative.
