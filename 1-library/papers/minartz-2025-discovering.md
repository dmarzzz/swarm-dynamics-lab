## Summary

Trains a data-driven Neural Crowd Simulator (NeCS) on large real-world pedestrian datasets and validates it against key statistics of crowd dynamics, then uses it to run surrogate "virtual experiments" with laboratory-like control. It reproduces known pairwise avoidance results and reveals that N-body interactions are vision-guided and topological.

## Contribution

Proposes generative neural simulators as instruments for scientific discovery in crowd dynamics, combining the statistical power of field data with controllability.

## Key results

- Reported (abstract): reproduces known pairwise avoidance; uncovers vision-guided, topological N-body interactions.

## Methods and models

Generative neural simulator trained on large-scale real-world tracking data. Published in Scientific Reports 15, 10385 under the title "Discovering interaction mechanisms in crowds via deep generative surrogate experiments"; preprint arXiv 2412.01491 ("Understanding complex crowd dynamics with generative neural simulators"). arxiv field left empty because the titles differ.

## Limitations and open questions

Surrogate experiments inherit training-data biases. Abstract only.

## Relevance to us

A method for discovering interaction rules in any swarm with large trajectory data; the topological finding connects to the bird-flock debate on metric versus topological neighbours. Related: [[karamouzas-2014-universal]], [[rio-2018-local]].
