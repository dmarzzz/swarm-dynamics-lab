## Summary

Compares single- and mixed-species shoals of threespine sticklebacks, ninespine sticklebacks and roach
in the lab. Single-species groups are more polarised and, for some pairs, more cohesive; mixed groups show some
segregation, and pairwise transfer entropy reveals species differences in information use and in responses to
conspecifics vs heterospecifics.

## Contribution

Applies transfer entropy to heterogeneous groups, showing information flow depends on identity of
neighbours.

## Key results

- Single-species shoals more polarised than mixed (measured).
- Mean pairwise transfer entropy differs between treatments; species differ in information use (measured).

## Methods and models

Lab tracking; polarisation, cohesion, inter-individual distances; pairwise transfer entropy.

## Limitations and open questions

Abstract-level read.

## Relevance to us

Relevant for heterogeneous swarms (mixed robot types): information flow is type-dependent.
