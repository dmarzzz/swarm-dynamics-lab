## Summary

Shows that time-delayed mutual information and transfer entropy, widely used to identify leaders and
followers in cells, birds, fish and other animals, conflate distinct modes of information flow, with pitfalls
even for two interacting self-propelled particles. Decomposing transfer entropy into intrinsic, shared and
synergistic modes reveals true effective interactions and the role of individual and group memory, and the
decomposition of a single pair reveals many-body interactions without conditioning on more agents.

## Contribution

Methodological correction to the transfer-entropy leadership literature, with a tractable
pairwise decomposition.

## Key results

- Transfer entropy and time-delayed mutual information can misattribute influence even for two particles (simulation).
- Intrinsic/shared/synergistic decomposition recovers effective interactions and exposes memory effects.

## Methods and models

Self-propelled particle models with known interactions; information decomposition of transfer
entropy. arXiv 2012.00293.
