## Summary

Short review arguing that collective decision-making in functionally integrated groups (social insect colonies, shoals) should be analysed with the same economic and optimality tools as individual decisions. It covers quorum rules that improve true-positive and false-positive rates together, confidence weighting, value-sensitive decisions and the speed-value trade-off from the honeybee stop-signal model, nutritional decisions in the Geometric Framework, and the use of robots and mixed animal-robot societies to test collective-behaviour models.

## Contribution

Brings the value-sensitivity argument of [[pais-2013-mechanism]] into the behavioural-science literature and connects it to human and monkey evidence for magnitude-sensitive choice; positions robots as a test bed for biological models rather than only an application.

## Key results

- Cites Wolf et al. for quorum rules letting groups raise true positives while lowering false positives, against the individual signal-detection trade-off (reported, not new).
- Equal high-value options need less cross-inhibition to break deadlock than equal low-value options, motivating a speed-value trade-off in place of a speed-accuracy trade-off.
- Argues that implementing models on physical robots exposes assumptions about sensing, noise and communication topology that simulations hide.

## Methods and models

Narrative review (Current Opinion format, about 5 pages) of theory and experiments; no new data.

## Limitations and open questions

Brief and selective; mostly the authors' own research line. Nutrition section is only loosely tied to consensus decisions.

## Relevance to us
