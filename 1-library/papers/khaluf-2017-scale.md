## Summary

Reviews scale invariance in natural and artificial collectives, classifying it into scale-invariant
spatial structures, scale-invariant topologies and scale-invariant dynamics. It gives examples across domains
(including scale-free correlations in flocks), discusses origins and features, and outlines challenges and
approaches for engineering scale invariance into artificial systems such as robot swarms.

## Contribution

The main review that bridges the biological criticality literature to swarm robotics design.

## Key results

- Three classes of self-organised scale invariance identified (review).
- Argues that size-independent collective integration increases robustness and adaptivity (claim).

## Methods and models

Literature review spanning physics, biology and robotics.

## Limitations and open questions

Abstract-level read; engineering guidance is qualitative.
