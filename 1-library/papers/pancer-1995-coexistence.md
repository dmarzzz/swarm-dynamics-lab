## Summary

An independent laboratory study, published a year before [[stoner-1996-somatic]], of what happens after Botryllus schlosseri colonies fuse. Twenty-two chimeras and subclones from 12 genotype combinations were typed at one microsatellite locus 45 to 130 days after fusion. Measured: both partners coexisted somatically in 73 percent of subcloned chimeras (83 percent of chimeric entities) up to 100 days after the genotypes were disconnected, and in all chimeras where one colony had been resorbed. Both genotypes were present in 23 percent of sampled gonads, and in 22 percent of cases only the second partner's germ cells were detected. Injecting compatible blood cells into three recipients led to donor cell proliferation in one case after 100 days. The authors propose four features that characterize cell lineage competition: somatic embryogenesis, independent existence of stem cells, disproportionate gametic share within chimeras, and hierarchical responses.

## Contribution

Early molecular confirmation that somatic and germ cell lines persist and can take over gonads after fusion, and that a partner's cells persist even after the partner colony is resorbed or disconnected.

## Key results

- Somatic coexistence: 73 percent of subcloned chimeras, persisting up to 100 days after disconnection (measured).
- Gonads with both genotypes: 23 percent; gonads with only the partner's genotype: 22 percent (measured).
- Donor proliferation after blood cell injection: 1 of 3 recipients (measured).

## Methods and models

Lab chimeras of B. schlosseri, PCR typing at one microsatellite locus of blood, zooids and gonads; blood cell injection into compatible recipients. Abstract only.

## Limitations and open questions

Abstract read only; one marker locus; small injection sample (n = 3). Lab conditions.

## Relevance to us

Q3: persistence after disconnection is the key point. A merged-in lineage survives even after the parent "resorbs" or cuts off the returning part, so rolling back the connection does not roll back the contamination. For agents this means a merge must be treated as irreversible unless state is restored from a pre-merge snapshot. Also shows that even a small injected population can expand over time (1 of 3), a low-dose infection model. Companion to [[stoner-1996-somatic]] and [[laird-2005-stem]].
