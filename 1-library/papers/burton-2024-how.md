## Summary

A Perspective by 28 authors from industry and academia on how LLMs change collective intelligence: the distributed cognition and coordination through which groups, markets and societies outperform individuals, often mediated by technologies such as prediction markets, deliberation forums and crowdsourcing platforms. LLMs transform how information is aggregated, accessed and transmitted online; the paper identifies benefits, risks, policy considerations and open research questions for humans' ability to tackle problems collectively.

## Contribution

The main collective-intelligence-science review of LLMs, connecting the wisdom-of-crowds and collective decision-making literature to LLM-mediated groups.

- Perspective; no new data.

## Limitations and open questions

Focus on human collectives augmented by LLMs, not populations of LLM agents.
