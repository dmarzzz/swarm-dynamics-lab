## Summary

Inspired by Minsky's society of mind and Schmidhuber's "learning to think", the paper assembles natural-language-based societies of mind (NLSOMs) of up to 129 LLM and other neural-network experts that "interview" each other in a "mindstorm" to solve multimodal tasks (visual question answering, captioning, text-to-image, 3D generation, egocentric retrieval, embodied AI). It raises organisational questions: monarchical vs democratic structure, and economic (reward-sharing) mechanisms for reinforcement-learning NLSOMs.

## Contribution

A large-membership (129) early demonstration and a research agenda on the social structure of agent societies.

## Key results

- NLSOMs improve multimodal zero-shot reasoning over single models on the listed tasks (abstract claim).

## Methods and models

Heterogeneous modular agents communicating in natural language; monarchy vs democracy organisational comparisons. Code not checked.

## Limitations and open questions

Mostly qualitative about organisation; no dynamics measurements.

## Relevance to us

Background; the governance-structure questions (monarchy vs democracy) map onto centralised vs decentralised topology studied quantitatively in [[kim-2025-towards]].
