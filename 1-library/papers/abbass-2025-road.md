## Summary

This is the editorial introduction to a 2025 Phil. Trans. A theme issue of 11 papers. It argues that the classical definition of a swarm ('autonomous, non-synchronized, non-intelligent robots') is outdated. It proposes layered definitions (individual, collection, group, team, swarm as 'a self-synchronized, efficient, effective team') and 'swarm analytics'. The 11 papers are grouped into scientific, technological and social-integration lenses. Examples include Kaminka's 'Swarms can be rational' (game-theoretic rational agents producing swarm phenomena), open-ended evolution in swarms, signalling and social learning ([[cazenille-2025-signalling]]) and human-swarm interaction.

## Contribution

A 2025 positioning statement for the field that explicitly allows 'intelligent' agents in swarms. This bridges classical swarm robotics and LLM/agentic swarms.
