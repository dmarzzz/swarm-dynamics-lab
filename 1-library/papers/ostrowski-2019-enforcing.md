## Summary

Review of conflict and its resolution in Dictyostelium discoideum, where starved cells aggregate into a fruiting body in which some die as stalk and the rest become spores, so there is conflict over who dies. The abstract says recent studies point to solutions to ensuring fairness, including suppressing selfishness and recognising and avoiding unrelated individuals; the work confirms a central role for kin selection but suggests further explanations; and genomic approaches are starting to reveal the evolutionary history of cooperation and conflict. Abstract only.

## Contribution

Recent review of the mechanisms that keep aggregative multicellularity cooperative despite mixing, updating [[strassmann-2011-kin]].

## Relevance to us

Q2: lists the two families of defence a merging collective can use, which also apply to fork-merge agents: recognition and avoidance before merging ([[hirose-2011-self]], [[ho-2013-kin]]) and suppression of selfish behaviour inside the merged body (policing).
