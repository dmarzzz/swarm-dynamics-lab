## Summary

Proposes the Supply-Chain Backdoor (SCAB) attack on reinforcement learning: a pre-trained external agent, supplied to a victim as an opponent or partner during training, implants a backdoor using only legitimate in-game actions. The attacker's policy has two parts, one that plays to win and one that implicitly rewards the victim (by letting it gain) whenever the victim responds to a trigger action sequence (here four consecutive no-ops) with the attacker's chosen backdoor actions, plus a detector that infers the victim's actions from observations. All parts are trained before the victim's training and stay fixed. Evaluated on two-player Atari games (Pong, Surround, Boxing) with PPO and DQN victims, CNN and LSTM policies, with multi-player, cooperative and continuous-action cases in appendices. Read: abstract, introduction, threat model, experiment setup and evaluation design; result tables and appendices not read, so numbers below are from the arXiv abstract.

## Contribution

Shows that backdoors in RL do not require write access to the victim's rewards, observations, transitions or weights; interacting legitimately with a supplied agent is enough.

## Key results

- Poisoning about 3% of training experiences activated over 90% of triggered backdoor actions and reduced the victim's average episodic return by 80% (measured, per abstract).
- The attack is designed to be stealthy: normal training metrics and normal behaviour when no trigger occurs (design goal; I did not check the stealth measurements).

## Methods and models

PettingZoo / Gymnasium Atari environments; PPO and DQN victims; trigger injection probability as the training-time attack knob. Forty-Third International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML 2026), per the arXiv comment.

## Limitations and open questions

Two-player competitive games in the main text; triggers are action sequences the victim can observe; defences not covered in the parts read.

## Relevance to us

Q3: the threat model matches a parent that continues learning alongside a returned sub-agent, or that trains on experience generated by interacting with it. The returned part needs no access to the parent's internals: by behaving normally most of the time and conditionally rewarding the parent's responses to a signal, it can install a triggered behaviour from a few percent of interactions. This is a third returned-object type beside text memories and weight edits: shaped experience. Q2: because only legitimate actions are used, data-level checks on the returned experience do not flag it, and a k-of-n rule only helps if the parent never trains against a single unverified part.
