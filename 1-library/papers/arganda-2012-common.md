## Summary

Derives a decision rule for animals in collectives from Bayesian estimation that uses others' behaviour as information about the world, and shows that one rule explains apparently different counting systems: dependence on absolute differences in numbers choosing each option in some species and on relative differences (Weber's law) in others. Tested on zebrafish experiments and existing data from Argentine ants and sticklebacks.

## Contribution

Unifies quorum-like and Weber-like social responses under one estimation principle; extends [[perez-escudero-2011-collective]].

## Key results

- A single Bayesian rule quantitatively fits zebrafish, Argentine ant and stickleback decision data (fits reported in paper).
- Abstract-level reading only; numbers beyond the abstract were not checked.

## Methods and models

Bayesian decision model with reliability parameters; binary choice experiments with Danio rerio; reanalysis of Linepithema humile and Gasterosteus aculeatus data.

## Limitations and open questions

Assumes independence of others' decisions in the estimation; binary choices.

## Relevance to us

Principled alternative to hand-tuned quorum curves for agent social-response rules. Related: [[mann-2018-collective]], [[ward-2008-quorum]].
