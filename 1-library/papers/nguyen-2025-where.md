## Summary

A review arguing that the physics of the body and the environment, not only neural decision rules, shapes the behaviour of animal groups. It opens with the dead trout that moved upstream by passively resonating with vortices, and then covers three case systems: hydrodynamic interactions in fish schools, thermoregulatory huddling in penguins, and self-assembled rafts of fire ants during floods.

## Contribution

Positions a 'physics of social interactions' agenda in which individual-environment coupling (flows, heat, buoyancy) is part of the interaction rule; complements the sensing and decision review by the same senior author.

## Key results

- Review, no new data. Case studies: fish-school hydrodynamics, penguin huddles, ant rafts.
- Claim: body and environmental physics can produce behaviour-like outcomes without neural control, so models should couple agents to physical fields.
