## Summary

Proposes two dimensionless numbers for pedestrian flows, the Intrusion number and the Avoidance number, by analogy with the Reynolds number. On an extensive dataset, these delineate regimes with distinct structural signatures: at low Avoidance number structure is best described by distances, at low Intrusion number by times-to-collision. A perturbative expansion of agent dynamics in each regime gives generic models that work well in, and only in, the regime where they were derived.

## Contribution

Explains why distance-based ([[helbing-1995-social]]) and anticipatory time-to-collision ([[karamouzas-2014-universal]]) models each succeed in different settings, and offers a regime map for model choice.

## Key results

- Reported (abstract): two dimensionless numbers separate crowd regimes; regime-specific generic models perform well only in their own regime.

## Methods and models

Dataset analysis of pair statistics; perturbative agent-based modelling. PNAS Nexus 3(4), pgae120.

## Limitations and open questions

Abstract only; definitions of the numbers not checked here.

## Relevance to us

A principled way to classify swarm regimes before choosing an interaction model; the same logic could apply to robot swarms (dimensionless ratio of avoidance to propulsion). Related: [[corbetta-2023-physics]].
