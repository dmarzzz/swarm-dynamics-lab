## Summary

Predator experiments with Asian honey bees associate inhibitory signaling with attack severity and context. Signals differ between attacks on foragers and at the nest, and influence recruitment or departure behavior.

## Contribution

Context matters to collective inhibition, extending the nesting-choice motivation in [[seeley-2012-stop]].

## Key results

Abstract-level qualitative findings only; no effect-size extraction or replication.

## Methods and models

Hornet attacks on Apis cerana foragers and colony entrances. Publisher abstract and author summary read.

## Limitations and open questions

No software verification budget or observation-identity policy is tested. The [correction](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.1002496) concerns PDF figure quality and was inspected.

## Relevance to us

Biological motivation for testing selective interruption; not evidence that bees implement RD5 reservation.
