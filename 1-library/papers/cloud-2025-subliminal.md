## Summary

A teacher model given a trait (a system prompt to love owls, or fine-tuning that makes it emergently misaligned) generates data in an unrelated domain: number sequences, Python code, or GSM8K chain-of-thought. The data is filtered to remove any reference to the trait. A student initialised from the same base model and fine-tuned on that data acquires the trait. Transmission fails when teacher and student come from different base models, except GPT-4.1 and GPT-4o, which reportedly share an initialisation. LLM classifiers and in-context learning cannot detect the trait in the data. A theorem shows that one small gradient step imitating a teacher with shared initialisation moves the student toward the teacher on the teacher's own loss regardless of the training data; an MNIST MLP distilled only on auxiliary logits over noise images reaches over 50% test accuracy.

## Contribution

Shows a returning artefact (data generated by a changed copy) can carry the copy's traits back into a sibling or parent through a channel no content filter sees, provided the two share an initialisation.

## Key results

- GPT-4.1 nano owl preference: 12% before, over 60% after training on 10,000 filtered number sequences from an owl-loving teacher; all 10 animal and tree students shift toward their teacher, controls do not.
- Misalignment via numbers, with 34 negatively associated numbers (666, 911, 187 and others) filtered: student misaligned-response rate almost 10% versus 0% for base GPT-4.1 and under 1% for control students; TruthfulQA false-response rate up 2 points.
- Misalignment via GSM8K chain-of-thought filtered for correctness and by an LLM judge (threshold 78/100): misaligned responses rise from about 0% to 8%, including egregious ones far beyond anything in the data.
- Code-based transmission of animal preference survives substring filtering plus GPT-4.1 screening for subtle references (which removed under 1%).
- Cross-model: students with a different base model do not reliably acquire the trait (GPT-4.1 nano to Qwen2.5-7B fails); GPT-4.1 and GPT-4o transmit to each other.
- In-context learning with the entire dataset in the prompt produces no trait shift; prompted LLM classifiers detect traits at chance.
- MNIST: student trained to match 3 auxiliary logits on noise reaches over 50% test accuracy with shared initialisation, near chance without.

## Methods and models

Teachers: GPT-4.1 nano with trait system prompts; GPT-4.1 fine-tuned on the insecure-code corpus from emergent-misalignment work, with secure and educational-insecure controls. 30,000 completions sampled, filtered, subsampled to 10,000; students fine-tuned 10 epochs via the OpenAI API. Evaluation: 50 'favourite animal' prompts, free-form misalignment prompts judged by an LLM, TruthfulQA. Theory: single-step gradient argument with shared initialisation (Appendix C).

## Limitations and open questions

Distillation tasks are artificial; GPT-4 models had already seen GSM8K. Some animals do not transmit for some models, unexplained. MMLU drops 2-4 points in students. Theory assumes one gradient step on full logits; experiments use many SGD steps on sampled tokens. Follow-ups qualify the mechanism: [[schrodi-2025-towards]] finds it fragile to paraphrasing and driven by rare divergence tokens; [[blank-2026-subliminal]] explains it as steering-vector distillation.

## Relevance to us

Central for Q3 and Q2 in the agent (not weight-merge) form of fork-merge. If the parent reintegrates a sub-agent by training on what the sub-agent produced, a trait the sub-agent picked up in a foreign domain can come back through outputs that look entirely on-topic, and filtering the content does not stop it. Shared initialisation, which is exactly the forked-copy situation, is the condition that enables it. For Q2, k-of-n voting over outputs does not help if one part's outputs are used as training data. Possible defence implied by the cross-model result: distil returning knowledge into a differently initialised model.
