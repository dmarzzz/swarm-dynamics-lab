## Summary

Introduces a statistical-physics framework for anticipatory agents whose present dynamics depend on the future state they anticipate. Anticipation is written through a cost function built from observations, and the dynamics of an anticipating agent in d dimensions maps onto a non-anticipating chain in d + 1 dimensions with transverse fluctuations representing uncertainty about the future. Polymer physics then defines an anticipation horizon beyond which the future can be treated in mean field. Applied to pedestrians, a minimal cost reproduces difficult scenarios such as crossing cluttered spaces and alighting from a crowded train.

## Contribution

A general theory of anticipation in active matter, extending the mean-field game approach of [[bonnemain-2023-pedestrians]] (same group) into a polymer mapping.

## Key results

- Theoretical (abstract): anticipatory dynamics map to a chain in d + 1 dimensions; an anticipation horizon emerges.
- Simulated: reproduces experimental crowd scenarios that challenge state-of-the-art models.

## Methods and models

Cost-function dynamics, polymer analogy, agent-based pedestrian model. Preprint, 2026.
