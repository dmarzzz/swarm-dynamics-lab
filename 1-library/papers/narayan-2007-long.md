## Summary

Narayan, Ramaswamy and Menon vibrate a monolayer of macroscopic rods into an active nematic and measure number
fluctuations: the standard deviation grows linearly with the mean (ΔN ∝ N), violating central-limit scaling, and the
fluctuations are long-lived, decaying only logarithmically in time. Flocking-like coherent motion and large-scale
inhomogeneity therefore arise from contact interactions alone. Read from the abstract on the page in `url`; details beyond the abstract are not checked.

## Contribution

First experimental observation of giant number fluctuations predicted for active nematics (Ramaswamy, Simha, Toner
2003); a template for vibrated-granular "robots" ([[deseigne-2010-collective]], [[deblais-2018-boundaries]]).

## Key results

- Measured: ΔN ∝ ⟨N⟩ (exponent ≈ 1) in the nematic phase (abstract).
- Measured: logarithmic decay of density fluctuations in time (abstract).

## Methods and models

Experiment: vertically vibrated copper-wire rods in a monolayer, image analysis of sub-box counts.
arXiv:cond-mat/0612020; full text not read.

## Limitations and open questions

Whether these fluctuations come from active nematic physics or from inelastic clustering has been debated (not
checked here). The measured nematic GNF exponents in simulations do not match theory ([[chate-2019-dry]]).

## Relevance to us

GNF is the most accessible signature of active collective order; a robot swarm can be tested the same way with an
overhead camera. Related: [[chate-2019-dry]], [[deseigne-2010-collective]], [[mahault-2019-quantitative]].
