## Summary

Uses a data-driven fish school model (burst-and-coast style interactions fitted to Hemigrammus
rhodostomus) to ask how one or a few perturbing individuals, with attraction and alignment parameters different
from the rest, change the school's long-term behaviour. The school is most responsive near the transition between
milling and schooling, where it is multistable and switches between the two states, and only responds
significantly when the noise-to-social-interaction ratio is below a threshold.

## Contribution

Early model-based demonstration that responsiveness peaks at a collective-state transition other
than the order-disorder one (milling-schooling), cited by [[klamser-2021-collective]] as support for the
criticality hypothesis.

## Key results

- Response to a few perturbing fish is maximal near the milling-schooling transition region (simulation).
- Significant response only below a threshold of noise relative to social interactions.

## Methods and models

Data-driven fish school model with attraction and alignment terms; perturbing agents with different
parameters; long-run state statistics. arXiv 1409.6430.

## Limitations and open questions

Model-only; abstract-level read.

## Relevance to us

Reminds us that swarms have several transitions (milling vs polarised, see
[[tunstrom-2013-collective]]) and responsiveness can peak at any of them. Same group as
