## Summary

Bibliometric and critical review of the influence of [[helbing-2000-simulating]]: where it is cited, its intellectual foundations, how it propagated terms such as "panic" and "herding", and how its assumptions match later empirical evidence. It finds discrepancies in key assertions about panic behaviour, while acknowledging the lasting influence of its simulations (for example faster-is-slower), and argues the field should move away from that terminology towards empirical evidence.

## Contribution

A field-level reality check: separates the paper's well-supported simulation insights from its contested behavioural assumptions.

## Key results

- Reported (abstract): nearly 3,000 Web of Science citations at the time; mismatches between assumed panic behaviour and empirical findings.

## Methods and models

Citation and terminology analysis plus literature review. Collective Dynamics 9, 1–11.

## Limitations and open questions

Abstract only.

## Relevance to us

Guards against importing "panic" assumptions into swarm models; a reminder to separate measured from assumed behaviour.
