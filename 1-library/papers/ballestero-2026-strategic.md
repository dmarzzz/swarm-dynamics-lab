## Summary

Separates primary algorithmic monoculture (baseline action similarity) from strategic monoculture (agents adjusting similarity to incentives) in coordination games run on human and LLM subjects. LLMs show high baseline similarity and, like humans, regulate it with coordination incentives; they coordinate very well on similar actions but lag humans at sustaining heterogeneity when divergence is rewarded.

## Contribution

A human-comparison experiment on LLM action similarity under incentives.
