## Summary

Shows that power-law statistics and universal scaling functions, often used to identify critical
states experimentally, emerge from networks in irregular self-sustained regimes away from criticality and even
from independent stochastic surrogates of the units.

## Contribution

A key negative result: power laws and scaling collapses are not sufficient evidence of
criticality.

## Key results

- Power-law avalanche statistics and scaling collapse reproduced by non-critical networks and by independent surrogates (simulation).

## Methods and models

Large-scale stochastic network models in asynchronous irregular regime; surrogate analysis. arXiv
1503.08033.

## Limitations and open questions

Neural setting; abstract-level read.
