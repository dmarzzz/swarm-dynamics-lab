## Summary

A short review proposing two quantitative properties of collectivity in biological aggregates:
amplification, the sensitivity of the large scale to small-scale information (related to criticality and
measured with Fisher information), and decomposability, the extent to which aggregate behaviour reduces to
individual contributions versus synergistic subgroups.

## Contribution

Gives an information-theoretic and statistical-physics vocabulary for "how collective" a system
is, used later in [[daniels-2017-control]] and [[poel-2022-subcritical]].

## Key results

- Defines amplification (criticality-related) and decomposability (synergy-related) as complementary measures (conceptual).
- Argues these measures identify causally important components and subgroups for manipulation (claim).

## Methods and models

Review drawing on maximum entropy models, Fisher information and information decomposition; examples
from neural and primate social data.

## Limitations and open questions

Conceptual; read at abstract level.

## Relevance to us

A compact checklist for what a hackathon "collectivity metric" should capture: response
amplification and irreducibility. Connects criticality ([[mora-2011-biological]]) to synergy-based emergence
([[rosas-2020-reconciling]]).
