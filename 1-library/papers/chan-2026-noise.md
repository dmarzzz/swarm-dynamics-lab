## Summary

In the classic Couzin zonal model (avoidance, alignment, attraction) the group shows collective memory: the pattern reached depends on past configurations. Combining simulations with bifurcation theory, the authors show the milling-to-schooling transition is a noisy transcritical bifurcation where the two states intersect and exchange stability, not structural bistability. A phenomenological model of group polarisation captures the bifurcation character, transient milling and memory.

## Contribution

Resolves the origin of hysteresis (collective memory) in a canonical model.

## Key results

- Simulation/theory (abstract): noisy transcritical bifurcation explains collective memory.

## Methods and models

Zonal SPP model simulations, bifurcation analysis, reduced polarisation SDE.
