## Summary

Experiments with three-spined sticklebacks choosing a movement direction in the presence of replica conspecifics (and sometimes a replica predator) show a quorum response: the probability of following rises steeply with the number of others already going that way. The quorum prevents the amplification of non-adaptive following; models predict that quorum responses improve both speed and accuracy over independent or linearly responding decision-makers.

## Contribution

The first clear demonstration of quorum decision-making in a vertebrate; central evidence in [[sumpter-2009-quorum]].

## Key results

- Solitary fish were strongly influenced by a single replica; groups of four or eight under predation risk largely ignored one replica and needed two to be swayed (experiment).
- Simulations predict quorum responses improve accuracy and speed over independent choice and weak linear responses.
- Abstract-level reading only; numbers beyond the abstract were not checked.

## Methods and models

Y-maze or arena choice trials with Gasterosteus aculeatus, replica conspecifics moved on lines, replica predator; individual-based model.

## Limitations and open questions

Replica fish, laboratory setting, binary choice.
