## Summary

Controlled single-file walking experiments measuring the relation between density and velocity. Velocity is linear in inverse density, interpretable as the length each pedestrian requires to move, and the single-file fundamental diagram agrees surprisingly well with literature data for planar movement in the range 1 to 5 m^-1, suggesting lateral interference has little effect there.

## Contribution

Started the series of Jülich laboratory experiments that put the pedestrian fundamental diagram on a measured footing and introduced automatic recording of flow characteristics.

## Key results

- Measured (abstract): linear relation between velocity and inverse density in single-file motion.
- Comparison: agreement with planar fundamental diagrams for 1 m^-2 < ρ < 5 m^-2 (as stated).

## Methods and models

Laboratory single-file ring experiments; J. Stat. Mech. (2005) P10002.

## Limitations and open questions

Single-file only; cultural and age composition effects were studied later. Abstract only.

## Relevance to us

The 1D crowd analogue of the traffic fundamental diagram, a basic calibration target for any pedestrian swarm model ([[helbing-1995-social]], [[moussaid-2011-simple]]).
