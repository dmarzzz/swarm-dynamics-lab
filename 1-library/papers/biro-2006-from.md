## Summary

GPS-tracked pairs of homing pigeons with distinct habitual routes averaged their routes when the conflict between preferences was small but either split or had one bird lead when conflict exceeded a critical threshold. A simple model reproduces both compromise and leadership from the same rules, and pairs flew more efficiently than either bird alone.

## Contribution

Early empirical evidence for the compromise-to-choice transition later formalised in [[leonard-2012-decision]] and seen in [[strandburg-peshkin-2015-shared]].

## Key results

- Averaging below a critical route conflict, leadership or splitting above it (field data).
- Pairs more efficient than individuals, even if the leader was not the more efficient bird.
- Abstract-level reading only; numbers beyond the abstract were not checked.

## Methods and models

High-precision GPS on pigeons (Columba livia) released in pairs after individual route training.

## Limitations and open questions

Pairs only.
