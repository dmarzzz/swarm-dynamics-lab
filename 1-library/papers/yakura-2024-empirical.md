## Summary

A synthetic-control analysis of 737,083 hours of conversation from 824,634 podcast episodes, screened for unscripted speech, finds that words ChatGPT prefers (delve, showcase, boast, intricacies, meticulous) increased abruptly in spontaneous human speech after ChatGPT's release. A preregistered experiment (N = 496) shows a brief chatbot interaction makes people adopt its words.

## Contribution

Evidence that humans internalise LLM vocabulary, which contaminates marker-word estimators of machine-written text.

## Key results

- Measured (abstract): abrupt rise of ChatGPT-preferred words in spontaneous podcast speech, causally linked by synthetic control.
- Measured (abstract): experimental adoption of chatbot words after brief interaction (N = 496).

## Methods and models

Synthetic control over podcast transcripts; preregistered behavioural experiment. Abstract read only.

## Limitations and open questions

Effect sizes not in abstract. Abstract depth.

## Relevance to us

A confound for every lexical prevalence estimator ([[kobak-2024-delving]], [[gray-2024-chatgpt]]): rising marker words can be humans talking like models, not models writing. Detectors that flag a population for LLM vocabulary will increasingly flag humans.
