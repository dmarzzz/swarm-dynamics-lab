## Summary

Uses topological data analysis on laboratory midge swarms and finds a dense "condensed" core
surrounded by a dilute "vapour" phase that coexist in equilibrium while individuals move freely between them.
The authors define a pressure and chemical potential for these phases.

## Contribution

Thermodynamic (equation-of-state style) description of an animal swarm as material-like
phases, an alternative order-parameter approach to correlation-based criticality.

## Key results

- Coexisting condensed and vapour phases in lab swarms (measured with topological data analysis).
- Pressure and chemical potential defined for the phases.

## Methods and models

3D tracking of Chironomus riparius lab swarms; persistent homology / topological data analysis.

## Limitations and open questions

Lab swarms, which [[poel-2022-subcritical]] notes may differ from wild swarms near criticality.
Abstract-level read.

## Relevance to us

Alternative macroscopic measurement framework (phases, equations of state) for swarms.
