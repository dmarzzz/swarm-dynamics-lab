## Summary

Luo, Li, Yang and Peng study millimetre-sized magnetic rollers whose motility is tunable and whose dynamics is
inertia-dominated. They observe first-order flocking phase separation: a uniform flock propagates through an isotropic
gas, with the two phases showing different particle-exchange dynamics and different effective temperatures. Experiments,
agent-based simulations and phenomenological theory attribute it to inelastic collisions creating positive feedback
between density and polar motion. Read from the abstract in the Semantic Scholar API record in `url`; details beyond the abstract are not checked.

## Contribution

Extends the phase-separation view of flocking ([[solon-2015-phase]], [[chate-2019-dry]]) to inertial, macroscopic
agents, the regime of most robots.

## Key results

- Measured: flock–gas coexistence with distinct effective temperatures (abstract).

Experiment with magnetic rollers plus simulation and theory (PRL 2025). Full text not read.

## Limitations and open questions

Externally driven rollers; how far it transfers to autonomous robots is open.
