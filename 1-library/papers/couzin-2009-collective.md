## Summary

Review in Trends in Cognitive Sciences arguing that collective decision-making in animal groups can be understood through social interactions, individual state, environmental modification, and the amplification and decay of information, and that these processes have close parallels in neural computation. It proposes studying animal collectives within the framework of cognitive science.

## Contribution

Coined the widely used framing of 'collective cognition'; it bridges the leadership models [[couzin-2005-effective]] and the brain-colony analogy formalised in [[marshall-2009-optimal]].

## Key results

- Identifies informational amplification (positive feedback) and decay (forgetting, inhibition) as the levers that tune adaptive group response (conceptual).
- Abstract-level reading only; numbers beyond the abstract were not checked.

## Methods and models

Narrative review of fish, insect and bird collective behaviour and neural decision models.

## Limitations and open questions

Qualitative; written before most high-resolution tracking studies.
