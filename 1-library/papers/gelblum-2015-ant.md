## Summary

Combines experiments and theory on cooperative transport by Paratrechina longicornis ants. Individual
decision rules balance individuality and compliance, which poises the ant-load system at the transition between
random-walk and ballistic motion, where the response to a single informed ant steering the load is maximal. The
peak is linked to diverging susceptibility at a phase transition, and the theory predicts, with supporting
experiments, that load size moves the system through the critical point.

## Contribution

One of the clearest experimental links between a criticality-like susceptibility peak and a
functional collective task, and a direct ancestor of [[chatterjee-2025-maximal]].

## Key results

- Collective response to a single informed ant is maximised at the random-walk/ballistic transition (experiment plus theory).
- Varying group (load) size moves the system through the critical point, as predicted (experiments).

## Methods and models

Tracked cooperative transport experiments; Ising-like model of carriers as spins coupled through load
forces; informed-leader perturbations.

## Relevance to us

A physical task (cooperative transport) where criticality is functionally defined and controllable
by group size; strong template for robot cooperative-transport demos. See [[feinerman-2018-physics]].
