## Summary

Follow-up to the stickleback predation-risk study showing that shoals of threespine sticklebacks also use a quorum rule when first moving to and then leaving a foraging patch. Following in both directions happened once a threshold number of initiators was exceeded, and the threshold depended on group size.

## Contribution

Extends the quorum mechanism of [[ward-2008-quorum]] from risky movement decisions to foraging decisions, supporting the generality argued in [[sumpter-2009-quorum]].

## Key results

- Quorum rule explains movement towards and away from a food patch (experiment plus model fit).
- The quorum threshold scales with group size (experiment).
- Abstract-level reading only; numbers beyond the abstract were not checked.

## Methods and models

Gasterosteus aculeatus shoals of different sizes with a food patch; analysis of initiator numbers versus following.

## Limitations and open questions

Abstract-level reading; laboratory setting.

## Relevance to us

Group-size-dependent thresholds also appear in ants [[gal-2022-emergence]]; a scaling law worth testing in agent swarms.
