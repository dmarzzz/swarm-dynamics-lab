## Summary

Shows that a new generation of 'social spambots' that mimic humans had appeared on Twitter, and measures that Twitter's own systems, human annotators and published detection methods all failed to detect them reliably. The authors survey the literature, point to collective-behaviour analysis as the promising direction, and release the datasets (cresci-2017).

## Contribution

Classic evidence of an evasion step in the bot arms race and the source of the cresci-2017 benchmark.

## Key results

- Neither Twitter, nor human annotators, nor state-of-the-art tools accurately detected the new social spambots (abstract).
- Recommends detection based on collective behaviours.
- Datasets publicly released.

## Methods and models

Measurement of platform suspension, crowdsourced human labelling and benchmark of published detectors on new spambot sets. Abstract-level read.

## Limitations and open questions

Pre-LLM. The released human accounts were later shown to share a collection artefact (all tweeted 'earthquake'; [[hays-2023-simplistic]]).
