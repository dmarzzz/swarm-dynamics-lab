## Summary

R^3-Bench gives a model six problems that share one token budget. The problems come from Olympiad math, competitive programming or abstract reasoning, with 50 six-problem contests per domain, run tool-free and agentic at two budget pressures. Each model's contest score is compared with two references built from its own single-problem runs. The first is an equal-allocation replay. The second is an offline "oracle" that picks the problems the model demonstrably solved in isolation, subject to the same total budget. In 72 main-table cells for six frontier models, the oracle matches or beats the contest score every time and is strictly higher in 71. Models do not convert the competence they show on single problems into suite-level results when budget spent on one problem is lost to the others.

## Contribution

The first shared-budget benchmark that calibrates multi-task performance against the same model's demonstrated single-task competence, which separates allocation failure from capability failure.

## Key results

- Measured: oracle at or above the contest score in 72 of 72 cells, strictly above in 71.
- Measured: under moderate tool-free pressure, the equal-split replay beats the model's own allocation for four of six models (DeepSeek-V4-Pro, Qwen3.7-Max, GLM-5.2, Claude-Opus-4.8), per the abstract and Section 4.2. The introduction says "three of six"; the paper is internally inconsistent on this count. Under strong pressure the equal split scores near zero.
- Measured: spending follows presentation order, not value. Position 6 accuracy is below position 1 in all 12 model-pressure series. Under strong pressure the first position absorbs 20.5 to 52.9% of attributed output, against 16.9 to 23.5% under moderate pressure.
- Measured: five of six models put substantive work into more than half the problems, but only DeepSeek-V4-Pro exceeds 50% on "resource-rational strategy updates". Hy-3 and GPT-5.5 make none in the agentic setting.
- Measured: failure causes change with pressure. Under strong pressure, oracle-selected misses are mostly "budget spent elsewhere". Under moderate pressure they are mostly "stopped after partial progress" (for example 67% for DS-Pro and 55% for GPT).
- Measured: allocation quality is not predicted by the Epoch Capabilities Index. Models close in ECI differ widely in Gap Ratio.
- Measured: a context-length control (all six problems shown, one designated) costs at most 5 points in 9 of 12 cells, a mean of 1.1 points.
- Measured: two fixed external schedulers (forced initial coverage, plus a verification gate) beat the contest in 6 of 9 model-domain cells. No policy wins across domains: Code improves for every model, Math and AR depend on the model.
- Speculated: a trained inner scheduling policy could close the gap (future work).

## Methods and models

Models: DeepSeek-V4-Chat, -Reasoner, -Pro, Qwen3.7-Max, GLM-5.2, Hy-3, GPT-5.5, Claude-Opus-4.8 (six flagship in the main table, eight in the appendix). Budgets are calibrated per model from single-problem response curves. Gap Ratio is the normalized contest-to-oracle gap. The oracle is offline and not an executable policy. Read: abstract, introduction, Sections 4 and 5, conclusion. Appendices not read.

## Limitations and open questions

The oracle is built from observed successes, so it upper-bounds what reallocation could have achieved and ignores stochastic variation in solve rates. The tasks are independent problems, not collaborating agents. Whether the gap persists when separate agents each hold a slice of the budget (rather than one model scheduling itself) is not tested. Contrast with [[paliskara-2026-worse]], which tests exactly that and finds it worse.

## Relevance to us

The cleanest measurement that LLMs misallocate a shared budget even when they know how to solve every part. A naive equal split already beats the model's own allocation for some models. Pairs with [[amayuelas-2025-self]] (LLM as allocator for others), [[jin-2025-controlling]] and [[dang-2025-multi]] (trained allocators), and the arrival-order bias in [[bansal-2025-magentic]].
