## Summary

A review aimed at behavioural ecologists on using information-theoretic tools (mutual information,
transfer entropy, causation entropy) to infer interactions from group movement data. Through ecologically
motivated examples it illustrates benefits and pitfalls, summarises workarounds for estimation and confounding,
and ends with an optimistic but cautionary perspective.

## Contribution

Bridges the information-dynamics methods community and collective-behaviour ecologists; a
practical guide to what these tools can and cannot show.

## Key results

- Catalogues approaches and known challenges (sample size, non-stationarity, indirect effects) for IT tools on group movement data (review).

## Methods and models
