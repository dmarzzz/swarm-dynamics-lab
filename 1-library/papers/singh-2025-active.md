## Summary

Weakly-electric-fish-like agents with biophysically inspired electrosensing and actuation are trained with MARL to forage collectively. Trained agents reproduce real-fish hallmarks such as curvilinear homing trajectories and heavy-tailed electric organ discharge interval statistics, and show emergent active sensing, social foraging, dominance-like asymmetries and aggression. In-silico sensor ablations, discharge silencing and food-distribution changes identify causal drivers of social foraging, and recurrent dynamics encode task and social variables.

## Contribution

A 2025 example of MARL as a neuroethology model organism: emergent group behaviour compared against animal data, with interventions impossible in vivo.

## Key results

- Agents reproduce heavy-tailed discharge interval statistics and homing trajectories of real fish (claimed in abstract).
- Emergent social foraging, dominance asymmetries and aggression (claimed).

## Methods and models

MARL with recurrent policies on a biophysical electrosensing simulator; ablation experiments. Abstract-level read.

## Limitations and open questions

Preprint; quantitative match to animal data not checked; group sizes not checked.
