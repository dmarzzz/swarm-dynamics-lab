## Summary

Experiments on small numbers of migrating cells in circular confinement find that each cell's speed is paced by
nucleating waves of polymerising actin, an internal oscillator, and that the onset of collective rotation
coincides with synchronisation of these wave-nucleation frequencies across the group. A mechanical analogue,
active spheres with internal oscillators, reaches the same collective state only when the internal oscillators
synchronise, and nearest-neighbour coupling suffices to produce global synchrony.

## Contribution

Experimental evidence, in living cells and in a mechanical toy system, that collective motion can require
synchronisation of internal oscillators; an empirical counterpart to the swarmalator idea
([[okeeffe-2017-oscillators]]).

## Key results

- Measured: onset of collective migration coincides with synchronisation of actin-wave nucleation frequencies.
- Measured (mechanical model): global synchrony via nearest-neighbour coupling is needed for the collective state.
- Claimed by analogy: local coupling and actin-wave sync drive self-organised motion of cell collectives.

## Methods and models

Confined dendritic-cell-like cells (Sixt lab) imaged in circular micro-confinements; macroscopic active-sphere
experiments (Hof lab). Abstract read from the Nature page; methods not read.

## Limitations and open questions

Small groups in confinement; the cell-to-sphere analogy is argued, not derived.

## Relevance to us

Rare experimental demonstration that internal-clock sync gates collective motion; a strong citation for any
hackathon claim that "sync is a precondition for coordinated movement", and a candidate system to model with
swarmalators ([[yoon-2022-sync]]).
