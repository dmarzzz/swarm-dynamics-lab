## Summary

Argues local neighbour interactions alone cannot explain how large bird flocks control their density.
Large flocks self-organize to the maximum density at which a typical bird can still see out of the flock in
many directions, so flocks are "marginally opaque": an outside observer sees a substantial fraction of sky
through them. Proposes that individuals respond to the projection of the flock they see, giving a global
interaction and faster information transfer than local models. Read at abstract level from arXiv.

## Contribution

A long-range, vision-based alternative to purely local (metric or topological) interaction models, with a
measurable signature (marginal opacity) in real starling flocks.

## Key results

- Claimed and measured from flock images: large flocks sit near marginal opacity.
- Model: projection-based interaction reproduces this.

## Methods and models

Analysis of starling flock images plus a projection-based SPP model.
