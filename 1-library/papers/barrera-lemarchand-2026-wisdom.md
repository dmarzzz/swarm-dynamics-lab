## Summary

Adapts the three-stage human deliberation paradigm (independent estimate, small-group deliberation to consensus, revised individual estimate) to LLMs from three families, across visual numerical estimation, peer review of ML papers, detection of hidden malicious behaviour by an AI agent, and sports forecasting against a real prediction market. Across domains, averaging the consensus of small deliberating groups reduced collective error beyond passive aggregation of independent answers, and post-deliberation individual judgments kept the gain. Groups of clones of one model did not benefit from deliberating.

## Contribution

The LLM version of the Navajas et al. human result that deliberation in small groups beats the independent wisdom of crowds, with model diversity as a necessary ingredient.

## Key results

- Deliberation beats passive aggregation in all four domains (reported; magnitudes not read).
- Clone groups show no deliberation benefit (reported).

## Relevance to us

Independent support (a cognitive-science group) for the homogeneity ceiling and the diversity lever, from a deliberation rather than voting design. A counterpoint to the pure-ceiling reading: diverse small groups can add evidence through interaction.
