## Summary

A personal, essay-style review of self-organisation. Gershenson declines to define "self-organizing system" (the problems of defining system, organisation and self), argues that calling something self-organising is partly an observer's choice that is useful when it helps describe the system, and then walks through properties and related concepts (information, complexity, emergence, adaptivity) and applications across physics, chemistry, biology, collective behaviour, ecology, networks, robotics, AI, linguistics, social science, urbanism and engineering. The conclusion poses open questions on why self-organising designs remain rare in practice and promotes "guided self-organization".

## Contribution

A recent, citable conceptual frame for the words "self-organisation" and "emergence", from a long-time author on guided self-organisation. It appears among the 2024+ citers of [[vasarhelyi-2018-optimized]] and [[rubenstein-2014-programmable]], which it cites in its robotics section.

## Key results

- Conceptual review; no new results. Its main position is pragmatic: ask when it is useful to describe a system as self-organising, not whether it "is" one.
