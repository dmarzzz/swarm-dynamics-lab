## Summary

A school of about 50 rummy-nose tetras (Hemigrammus rhodostomus) is observed while ambient light intensity
is cycled to alter visual range. At low light the fish cannot form a cohesive group; at higher illuminance
alignment correlates with light intensity, and increasing light moves the school from a polarized state to a
highly regular, stable milling state. Vision is necessary for cohesive collective motion; lateral-line
sensing alone is insufficient. Read at abstract level via Europe PMC.

## Contribution

Experimental control of the sensory channel (vision) as a tuning parameter for collective state, a clean
analogue of tuning interaction range in models.

## Key results

- Measured: cohesion lost at low light; order increases with illuminance; polarized then milling as light
  rises.

## Methods and models

~50 fish in a tank, controlled light cycles, polarization and milling order parameters.

## Limitations and open questions

Abstract-level read; single species, one group size.
