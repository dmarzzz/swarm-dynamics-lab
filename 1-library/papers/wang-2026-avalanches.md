## Summary

Three years of high-resolution trajectory data at a route split inside a busy train station show that pedestrians strongly tend to follow the route of the person directly in front of them, even without social ties and even when that route is longer. This imitation produces bursty "avalanches" of identical choices. A stochastic model with route costs, randomness and local imitation reproduces the statistics, indicating that local imitation dominates collective route choice.

## Contribution

Field evidence, at scale, of social imitation between strangers driving collective decisions in crowds, the human analogue of information cascades in animal groups.

## Key results

- Measured (abstract): imitation of the immediately preceding pedestrian, including when it increases travel time; bursty sequences of identical decisions.
- Modelled: cost plus noise plus imitation reproduces the data.

## Methods and models

High-resolution trajectory data from a route split in a busy train station over three years; stochastic choice model with route costs, noise and imitation. PNAS 123(8), e2528167123 (2026).

## Limitations and open questions

One site; causal identification of imitation versus shared information not checked here. Abstract only.

## Relevance to us

Quantifies a copying rule that can be tested in swarm decision experiments; links crowd dynamics to collective decision-making and herding ([[helbing-2000-simulating]]).
