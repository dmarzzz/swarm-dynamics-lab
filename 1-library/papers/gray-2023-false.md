## Summary

A review of false alarms (alarm behaviour with no real threat) in prey groups. False alarms are common across insects, amphibians, fish, mammals and birds; in some bird flocks they outnumber true alarms. They cost energy and opportunities (abandoning a safe feeding patch, lost rest, lost mating), and models treat them as a substantial, under-appreciated cost of using social information. The authors propose a two-stage framework: individual perception and response (misclassification, e.g. in low light) and group processing (whether an alarm is copied and propagated, e.g. damped by quorum responses).

## Contribution

Organises the animal false-alarm literature into individual-detection and group-propagation stages, which maps directly onto agent-level d′/c and swarm-level spread.

## Key results

- False alarms are frequent across taxa and in some bird flocks exceed true alarms (reviewed).
- Costs include abandoning safe patches (reviewed).
- Quorum-type group processing can limit the spread of false alarms (reviewed).
