## Summary

Evaluates widely used GPT detectors on writing from native and non-native English writers and finds they consistently misclassify non-native writing as AI-generated while classifying native writing correctly. Simple prompting both removes this bias and bypasses the detectors, so the detectors penalise constrained linguistic expression rather than machine authorship.

## Contribution

Key negative result on fairness of per-item detectors: false positives are not uniform across human populations.

## Key results

- Measured (abstract): consistent misclassification of non-native English writing samples as AI-generated by several detectors.
- Measured (abstract): simple prompting strategies mitigate the bias and evade detection.

## Relevance to us

Any population estimate built on per-item flags inherits demographic false positives, so a "swarm" flagged by a detector may be a non-native community. This is a direct false-alarm risk for swarm detection.
