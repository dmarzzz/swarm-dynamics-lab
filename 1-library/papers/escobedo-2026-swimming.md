## Summary

Tests causally whether a neighbour's speed changes social interaction strength. Freely swimming rummy-nose tetras (Hemigrammus rhodostomus) were shown a single virtual conspecific moving on circular trajectories at controlled speeds in an immersive open-loop VR system. Real fish followed the virtual partner at all speeds and matched its speed, but their spatial position, perception angles and leader-follower configuration changed systematically with partner speed. Fitting a data-driven 3D interaction model shows that higher neighbour speed weakens horizontal and vertical attraction and strengthens alignment and wall repulsion.

## Contribution

Turns the long-observed correlation between speed and order in fish schools into a controlled experiment and attributes it to speed-dependent interaction weights, identifying speed as a control parameter for transitions between collective regimes.

## Key results

- Measured (abstract): fish follow and speed-match the virtual fish across all tested speeds.
- Measured: position, perception angles and leader-follower configuration vary systematically with partner speed.
- Model inference: attraction (horizontal and vertical) decreases and alignment and wall repulsion increase with neighbour speed.

## Methods and models

Open-loop immersive VR for fish (same group's platform as the closed-loop study), one virtual conspecific on circular paths at several fixed speeds; reconstruction with a data-driven 3D model of burst-and-coast fish interactions. Abstract read via Europe PMC (PMID 42229927).

## Limitations and open questions

Abstract-level read; speeds, sample sizes and effect sizes not checked. Open-loop means the virtual fish does not respond, so reciprocity effects (see [[amichay-2024-revealing]]) are excluded by design. Pairs only.

## Relevance to us

Speed-dependent interaction weights are easy to add to a simulator and give a mechanism for speed-order coupling.
