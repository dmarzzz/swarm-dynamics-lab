## Summary

Distinguishes intrinsic criticality (interactions fine-tuned to a critical point) from extrinsic
criticality (observables coupled to unobserved fluctuating variables). Both produce diverging mutual information
between system halves and can be cast as learning problems, connected by a smooth crossover. Zipf's law is
robust for extrinsic criticality but can be hard to observe for some intrinsically critical systems; global
oscillatory dynamics can also produce Zipf's law.

## Contribution

Unifies the "criticality without fine tuning" critiques into an information-theoretic
framework and concludes fine tuning is neither the only nor the most likely explanation.

## Key results

- Intrinsic and extrinsic criticality lie on one continuum (theory).
- Zipf's law is a robust feature of extrinsic criticality, not reliably of intrinsic criticality (theory and simulation).

## Methods and models

Analytic information theory of latent-variable and mean-field models. arXiv 2309.13898.
