## Summary

Simha and Ramaswamy write hydrodynamic equations for suspensions (wet, momentum-conserving) of self-propelled
particles with orientational order and predict: true nematic suspensions are always unstable at long wavelengths; polar
suspensions support new propagating modes coupling orientation, flow and concentration; at low Reynolds number polar
suspensions are convectively unstable in an accessible wavenumber range; number fluctuations grow anomalously, with
variance/mean ~ ⟨N⟩^(2/3) in polar suspensions. Read from the abstract on the page in `url`; details beyond the abstract are not checked.

## Contribution

Founding paper of wet active hydrodynamics and of the "generic instability" of active ordered suspensions, the
counterpart of [[toner-1995-long]] for systems in a fluid; basis of [[marchetti-2013-hydrodynamics]].

## Key results

- Active nematic suspensions: absolutely unstable at long wavelength (claimed, theory).
- Polar suspensions: propagating modes; convective instability at low Re (theory).
- Giant number fluctuations: ΔN²/⟨N⟩ ~ ⟨N⟩^(2/3) (theory).

## Methods and models

Symmetry-based linear hydrodynamics with active stress ∝ order tensor. arXiv:cond-mat/0108301; full text not read.

## Limitations and open questions

Linear theory; nonlinear fate of the instability (active turbulence) left open, later reviewed in
[[alert-2022-active]].

## Relevance to us

Explains why ordered states of swimmers in a fluid do not stay ordered; for robot or drone swarms coupled through air or
water flows the same active-stress logic can apply. Related: [[wensink-2012-meso]], [[dombrowski-2004-self]].
