## Summary

Millions of Quincke colloidal rollers self-organize into coherent motion along a single direction with very
small density and velocity fluctuations (a polar liquid). Identifying the microscopic interactions allows a
theoretical description; comparison suggests hydrodynamic interactions promote collective motion as a single
macroscopic flock at low density or a homogeneous polar phase at higher density, and protect the polar
liquid from giant density fluctuations. Read at abstract level from arXiv.

## Contribution

The cleanest synthetic realisation of Vicsek-like flocking with known microscopic interactions; shows
propagating bands at onset in a real system.

## Key results

- Measured: macroscopic directed motion in populations of millions; polar liquid with weak fluctuations.

## Methods and models

Quincke rollers (electro-rotating PMMA beads) in microfluidic racetracks; kinetic theory from measured
interactions.

## Relevance to us

Physical benchmark showing flocking without cognition; contrast with animal studies.
