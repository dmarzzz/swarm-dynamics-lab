## Summary

Comprehensive review of active (self-propelled) Brownian particles, biological and synthetic microswimmers, with a
focus on how they interact with crowded and complex environments: walls, obstacles, external fields and other particles.
It covers basic principles, the development of artificial micro- and nanoswimmers, their use to study nonequilibrium
phenomena, and applications in healthcare, sustainability and security. Read from the abstract on the page in `url`; details beyond the abstract are not checked.

## Contribution

The single-particle and environment-centred counterpart to [[marchetti-2013-hydrodynamics]]; the most-cited

## Key results

- Frames active particles as converting environmental energy into directed motion, hence requiring nonequilibrium
  physics (abstract).
- Surveys artificial propulsion mechanisms and interactions with complex environments (abstract).
- Lists open challenges (abstract; not read in detail).

## Limitations and open questions

Emphasis on microscale swimmers; collective alignment phenomena and macroscopic swarms are covered less than
single-particle physics (inferred from abstract scope).
