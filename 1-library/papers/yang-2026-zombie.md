## Summary

The authors study persistent control of agents that update long-term state across sessions. One-time exposure to untrusted material during an ordinary task can be retained and later influence tool decisions. They examine persistence across common memory mechanisms while evaluating both unauthorized actions and benign-task quality.

## Contribution

Analysis of propagation, lifecycle security, containment or trust-boundary assessment.

## Key results

Abstract reports persistent compromise in representative agent setups, but no numeric effect sizes. It does not establish an autonomous multi-agent cascade.

Primary arXiv abstract and metadata read.

## Limitations and open questions

Full methods and uncertainties were not checked; do not infer cross-agent infection merely from persistent memory compromise.
