## Summary

LLM bidders in repeated Dutch auctions decide when to accept platform-posted payouts, without communication. A minimal repeated-auction model gives an incentive-compatibility condition and closed-form threshold for sustainable collusion in subgame-perfect equilibrium. Simulations with several LLMs show supra-competitive prices in small auctions and a return to competition as the number of bidders grows, consistent with the model; LLMs coordinate through focal-point acceptance timing or patient strategies.

## Contribution

Claimed first evidence of bidder-side tacit collusion by LLMs, with a theory matching the bidder-count effect.

## Key results

- Reported (abstract): supra-competitive prices with few bidders; competitive with more bidders. No numbers in the abstract.
