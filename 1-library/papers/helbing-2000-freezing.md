## Summary

Studies particles driven in opposite directions on a periodic strip, interacting by a repulsive potential and subject to random forces. Increasing the noise drives the system from a fluid lane-formed state into a crystallised, jammed state with higher total energy, a paradoxical transition the authors name "freezing by heating".

## Contribution

A minimal driven-diffusive model of pedestrian counterflow showing that fluctuations can destroy lanes and cause gridlock; frequently cited for why nervous or erratic crowds can jam.

## Key results

- Simulated (abstract): a noise-induced transition from a fluid state to a crystallised state with higher energy.

## Methods and models

Off-lattice particles, two species with opposite driving, repulsive interactions, Langevin noise; PRL 84, 1240–1243.

## Limitations and open questions
