## Summary

Identifies three interaction rules in mosquitofish shoals: attraction maintains cohesion while there is only
weak evidence for alignment with neighbours' orientation; repulsion is mediated mainly by speed changes; and
although positions and headings of all shoal members are correlated, individuals respond only to their
single nearest neighbour. Read at abstract level via Europe PMC.

## Contribution

Companion to [[katz-2011-inferring]]; together they shifted the field from assumed zonal rules to rules
inferred from data, and both find weak or no explicit alignment.

## Key results

- Measured: attraction important; weak alignment; speed-mediated repulsion; response to the single nearest
  neighbour.

## Methods and models

Mosquitofish (Gambusia holbrooki) tracked in pairs and shoals; inference of acceleration and turning
responses vs neighbour position.

## Relevance to us

Evidence that k = 1 nearest neighbour can suffice in small fish groups, contrasting with k ~ 7 in starlings
