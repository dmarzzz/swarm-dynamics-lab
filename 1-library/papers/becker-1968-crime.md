## Summary

Treats enforcement as minimising social loss: damages from offences plus the costs of apprehension and punishment. The decision variables are the probability of conviction p and the punishment f. At the optimum, when punishment is costly, offences must respond more elastically to p than to f, which implies offenders are risk preferrers at the margin. More harmful offences should be both detected more often and punished more severely. I read the introduction and the summary section of the NBER reprint (1974 volume).

## Contribution

The founding economic model of deterrence, cited by both the audit-games and AI-agent oversight papers in this lane.

## Key results

- Optimality conditions linking p, f and offence elasticities (derived).
- Fines conserve resources and compensate victims, so they are preferred where feasible.
- Evidence on US probabilities and sentences is described as consistent with the prediction that more harmful offences get higher p and f (descriptive, 1960 data).

## Methods and models

Static welfare optimisation with behavioural supply-of-offences function.

## Limitations and open questions

No strategic interaction with the enforcer and no avoidance; wealth limits cap fines.
