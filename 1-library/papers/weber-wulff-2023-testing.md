## Summary

Tests 12 publicly available AI-text detectors and two commercial systems (Turnitin, PlagiarismCheck) on an original document set, including machine-translated and obfuscated texts. Concludes the tools are neither accurate nor reliable, are biased toward labelling output as human-written, and degrade further under content obfuscation.

## Contribution

Independent audit of deployed detectors showing high false negatives, especially after obfuscation.

## Key results

- Measured (abstract): available tools neither accurate nor reliable; bias toward classifying text as human-written.
- Measured (abstract): obfuscation significantly worsens performance.

## Methods and models

Controlled document set; accuracy and error-type analysis across 14 tools. Abstract read only.
