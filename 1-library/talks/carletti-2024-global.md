## Summary

Carletti extends synchronization analysis from network nodes to signals on edges, faces and volumes of cell or simplicial complexes. At 12:15-13:55 he linearizes around a common solution and uses symmetry of an undirected coupling matrix to separate transverse modes. At 25:08-26:48 time variation and higher-order interactions motivate a move beyond static graph results. At 37:57-39:26 a torus example contrasts edge and face signals: negative master-stability values alone do not suffice when the invariant synchronized manifold fails to exist. Final Q&A at 48:43-50:28 emphasizes that gluing through faces rather than edges changes incidence operators and requires rechecking existence conditions.

## Relevance to us

Precise separation of existence of a collective state from stability of perturbations around it. Higher-order topology is not interchangeable with a simple graph and matters when agent interactions involve teams or shared higher-dimensional resources.
