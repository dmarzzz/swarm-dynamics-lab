## Summary

Monnot derives transaction-supply-chain roles from user preferences rather than treating them as fixed protocol components. At 2:23-6:40, EIP-1559 allocates fungible gas but transaction value depends on execution state and position. At 8:14-13:54, atomic bundles lead to trusted miners, then whole-block builders and relays as proof of stake makes validator whitelisting impractical. At 14:08-18:19 he compares in-protocol PBS with MEV-Boost: reducing relay custody, rent seeking and liveness risk trades against protocol complexity and enshrined market structure. At 18:27-22:51 he warns that searchers may recreate a virtual builder through coordination and speculates about user-searcher coalitions and order-flow payments. Q&A discusses concentrated stake, censorship and limits of whitelists.

## Relevance to us

Explains why identifiable repeat players and one-shot pseudonymous validators support different trust mechanisms. Useful when assessing whether splitting or recombining agents simply recreates a concentrated intermediary rather than decentralizing control.
