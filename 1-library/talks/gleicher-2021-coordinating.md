## Summary

Nathaniel Gleicher, then Facebook's head of security policy, discusses influence-operation enforcement with Evelyn Douek and Quinta Jurecic. Read the complete approximately 67-minute Deepgram transcript. The audio identifies the original Lawfare/Arbiters of Truth broadcast as 2021-07-01; the Scaling Laws feed republishes it on 2022-02-04. Statements are a platform representative's account, contested by the hosts, not independently verified effectiveness estimates.

- [07:52] Gleicher defines defensive success as increasing an adversary's operating cost rather than catching every campaign. [10:46] Perception hacking exploits public fear of ubiquitous operations by exaggerating the reach or impact of small campaigns. Exposure can therefore inform defenders while inadvertently amplifying an attack narrative.
- [14:41] He describes more than 150 public takedowns, approximately two-thirds beginning with internal investigations and one-third wholly or partly with external tips. [24:50] For 16 campaigns targeting discussion around the 2020 US election, he attributes five to Russian actors, five to Iranian actors, one to Chinese actors, and five to domestic actors. These are detected campaigns, not prevalence denominators.
- [25:47] The actor/behavior/content framework motivates enforcing on deceptive coordination rather than content similarity. [38:52] His narrow CIB definition involves a coordinated network concealing its operator and centrally relying on fake accounts. The hosts argue that public usage is much broader and that harmful authentic-account campaigns escape this category.
- [30:30] Gleicher acknowledges the unknown denominator and investigation priorities; Douek raises the streetlight/detection-bias problem. [33:39] Partial network removals can leave surviving accounts to reconstitute. [58:32] Platform diversification requires threat sharing; he cites Secondary Infektion using more than 300 platforms, while distinguishing sharing evidence from jointly making enforcement decisions.

## Relevance to us

Important detection-scoping source: coordination, automation, deception, operator identity, and harmful content are different labels. Account-level classification can miss campaign-level persistence, and observed takedown counts are selection-biased. Useful for measuring reconstitution and cross-platform links without equating all aligned discussion with an AI swarm. This predates widespread LLM agents and does not evaluate an LLM detector.
