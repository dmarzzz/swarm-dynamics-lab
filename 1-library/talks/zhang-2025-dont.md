## Summary

Zhang and Murag describe skills as shared folders of procedural instructions, scripts, and assets that specialize a general-purpose agent. Progressive disclosure loads only metadata until a task needs the full skill, reducing persistent context overhead. They distinguish MCP connectivity from workflow expertise and identify testing, versioning, and explicit dependencies as unresolved engineering needs. Their collective-learning vision concerns transferable procedural artifacts, not demonstrated autonomous self-improvement or a complete memory system.

## Relevance to us

Skill sharing is a concrete mechanism by which one agent's procedural knowledge can influence others. That suggests a relevant artifact provenance and contamination boundary, an inference rather than a security result in the talk. Compare the simpler agent-loop framing in [[zhang-2025-how]].
