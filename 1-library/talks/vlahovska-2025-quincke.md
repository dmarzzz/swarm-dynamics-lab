## Summary

Vlahovska presents electrically driven Quincke rollers as a controllable wet-active-matter platform. At 14:00-20:00, charging relaxation supplies memory without inertia, a field threshold starts spontaneous rotation and field strength controls speed. At 22:00-29:00, pulsed fields implement correlated, run-and-tumble and Levy-like walks; changing only memory and timing yields distinct collective clusters. Standard dry active-Brownian models fail to capture these experiments. At 31:00-39:00, rollers inside a deformable liquid bridge can induce fluctuations, flow-symmetry breaking and translation of the container. Adding far-field hydrodynamic interactions alone does not reproduce that motion when feedback from the moving boundary is missing. At 41:00-48:00, an analytic single-singularity-in-droplet calculation shows how internal active flow couples to a fluid interface to drive translation. Many-particle, freely swimming and controllable delivery extensions remain ongoing. Questions clarify that the interface is nonporous and Marangoni or elastic stresses can suppress motility.

## Relevance to us

Load-bearing example of why local-agent rules and fixed-boundary simulations can miss the actual mechanism. Environment feedback and finite polarization memory matter. Unlike [[cichos-2021-feedback]], this system controls activity globally while spontaneous orientations and hydrodynamics determine local motion.
