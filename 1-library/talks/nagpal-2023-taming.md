## Summary

At 3:05-7:00 Nagpal introduces collective intelligence through ant bridges and other biological examples. At 28:36-33:29 soft-robot prototypes detect crossing through vibration, while larger simulations apply bridge-state rules under varying traffic; some simulated pairs deadlock, unlike observed ants. The closing discussion at 79:25-82:44 notes that food-carrying ants cross bridges rather than forming them and explicitly separates cooperatively designed colonies from incentive problems involving independent goals. The speaker does not claim that her mechanisms solve multi-owner robot cooperation.

## Relevance to us

Valuable explicit failure case and assumption boundary: local rules can create a deadlock even while reproducing much natural behavior. Cooperative embodiment is distinct from collusion-resistant incentive design.
