## Summary

Zarei studies Sakaguchi-Kuramoto oscillators with phase-lag frustration and rewiring rules inspired by Hebbian and anti-Hebbian learning. At 5:20-7:10, rewiring removes links between dissimilar phases in the Hebbian case, and similar phases in the anti-Hebbian case. At 7:11-11:08 anti-Hebbian rewiring enhances global coherence while evolving heterogeneous connectivity; nonzero frustration produces asymmetric frequency-degree structure. At 11:09-13:50 Hebbian rewiring instead yields locally synchronized clusters that remain mutually asynchronous. Q&A distinguishes these structural update rules from literal spike-timing-dependent plasticity and reports qualitative robustness across initial networks.

## Relevance to us

A useful counterintuitive mechanism: removing locally synchronized connections can improve global synchrony in the studied adaptive model. It does not establish a universal intervention rule, and the transcript's time-reversal terminology is not independently validated.
