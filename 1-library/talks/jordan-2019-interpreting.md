## Summary

Jordan uses the cleaner-wrasse mirror-test controversy to explain why observing behaviour is not equivalent to establishing its cognitive meaning. At 03:20-08:00, small samples and species-specific sensory ecology complicate cross-species comparisons. At 09:00-25:00, he describes mirror aggression, unusual movements, postures and throat scraping, with transparent-mark, no-mirror and conspecific controls. Passing behavioural phases is not presented as proof of self-consciousness. At 26:20-33:40, conflicting reviewer interpretations motivate a quantitative rather than purely descriptive ethology. At 35:00-47:00, supervised and unsupervised tracking distinguish changes in movement repertoire, changes in how movements affect other animals, and changes in interaction distance. At 48:00-51:00, proposed cross-laboratory annotation comparisons address incompatible ethograms, with examples of 35, 19 and roughly 15 categories. Questions through 66:00 emphasize that computational categories remain interpretive and cannot capture unmeasured features.

## Relevance to us

Useful measurement guidance for distinguishing genuine policy differences from annotation artifacts in swarms. Retain raw trajectories and experimental controls; separate kinematics from inferred social function. The talk is methodological background, not a flocking model or evidence of machine self-awareness.
