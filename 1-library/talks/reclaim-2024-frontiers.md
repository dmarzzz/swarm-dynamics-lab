## Summary

Transcript coverage: 0:00-29:50, including closing discussion when present. Participants from Polygon ID, Gitcoin Passport and an identity-scoring project discuss standards, cost of forgery, privacy and adoption. Costly credentials can raise the expense of farming many accounts, but aggregation of shopping, work and social histories creates surveillance risk. The panel notes markets for legal identities and the possibility that a costly account holder still controls other accounts. Polygon ID profiles are proposed to separate application identifiers from a persistent uniqueness credential. Identity staking is discussed as an experiment linking attestations to slashable collateral. Regulation, government credentials and network effects remain unsettled, with no consensus on a universal personhood score.

## Relevance to us

Useful cautions against equating an estimated dollar cost of forgery with a calibrated probability of uniqueness. Privacy, credential resale, correlated evidence and cross-application linking need independent evaluation. Related foundational definitions: [[reclaim-2024-why]].
