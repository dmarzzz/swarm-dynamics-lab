## Summary

Talk by Mehdi Moussaid. Only the abstract in the YouTube description was read; transcript unavailable (YouTube bot wall), no timestamps. Per the abstract: walking in a crowd is a cognitive task of tracking others, avoiding collisions and updating a route; in emergencies people also decide whether and where to escape. Simple navigation heuristics give efficient navigation, and at crowd scale they produce collective patterns, some beneficial and some maladaptive, leading to disasters. Evidence covered: traffic flows recorded in crowded streets, panics and stampedes at mass events, and emergency evacuations studied in virtual worlds (networked VR/game experiments). Main claim: simple rules describe pedestrian behaviour well and the collective patterns follow from them. The bio notes his PhD (2010) on experimental study and modelling of crowds, supervised by Dirk Helbing and Guy Theraulaz.

Background, same programme as [[moussaid-2014-social]] four years on, adding the virtual-world evacuation experiments.
