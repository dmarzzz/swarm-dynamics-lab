## Summary

Strogatz starts with spontaneous audience applause and asks what minimal ingredients create synchrony. At 3:54-7:33 local attraction, alignment, separation and predator avoidance reproduce flocking, but he distinguishes simulation from mathematical understanding. At 9:52-11:20 he notes that pathological neural synchrony can be harmful. At 11:24-13:58 metronomes share mechanical forces through a movable support. The closing Millennium Bridge example at 17:33-21:32 illustrates an unintended feedback loop: pedestrians adjust gait to lateral motion and collectively amplify that motion without intending to drive the bridge.

## Relevance to us

Primary explanation of a shared-environment coupling mechanism that can create harmful collective order. This is a feedback example, not proof that observing synchrony establishes purposeful collaboration or a single controller.
