## Summary

Couzin connects local movement rules, collective sensing and decision-making to experiments rather than treating visually coordinated movement as proof of cooperation. At 07:15-13:10, attraction, alignment and repulsion models produce swarm, milling and polarized states. Speed sweeps exhibit hysteresis even without individual memory. Fish tracking, including groups of 800 and more than 100 hours of filming, supports the three-state description; speed changes and stochastic fluctuations have different roles in transitions.

At 13:40-18:35, golden shiners track changing light gradients despite no detectable individual gradient sensing. Individuals slow down in dark areas, creating density and attraction differences that let the group compute a response. At 19:30-27:10, 205 single-initiator alarm experiments motivate weighted, directed visual interaction networks. Low-degree initiators with clustered neighbours can be influential because behavioural contagion requires reinforcement, unlike simple infection. At 27:20-31:05, controllable virtual fish are proposed as an intervention tool for disentangling causal influence.

At 31:10-44:40, informed minorities guide groups without recognition or explicit signalling. In one simulation, six versus five preferred directions produces majority choice about 98% of the time after a geometry-dependent bifurcation. Strong minority preferences can reverse control; adding uninformed individuals restores majority choice in models and trained-fish experiments. At 45:00-50:00, GPS tracking of roughly 85% of a wild baboon troop supports movement consensus unrelated to dominance rank. Tracking previous locations improves movement prediction; scent-following is presented as a hypothesis, not a demonstrated mechanism.

## Relevance to us

A high-value bridge between [[couzin-2002-collective]], [[hein-2015-evolution]], [[rosenthal-2015-revealing]] and [[couzin-2011-uninformed]]. The relevant design lessons are to measure state and history separately from individual policy, reconstruct actual sensory influence rather than proximity alone, and distinguish simple from reinforcing contagion. Biological results do not establish that uninformed LLM agents or duplicate identities improve consensus. Those are separate, untested transfers.
