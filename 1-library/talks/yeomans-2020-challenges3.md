## Summary

Yeomans emphasizes that most of this lecture concerns unpublished research. At 03:00-23:00, faster hyperpiliated Pseudomonas cells spread less effectively than slower wild-type cells: fast motion encourages defect-pair encounters that nucleate out-of-plane, immobilized clusters. Rod simulations and measured local velocity fields motivate a connection to nematic defect physics, but locally nematic defect-like patterns are distinguished from globally ordered nematics. Similar division rates help exclude growth as the main explanation. At 25:00-43:00, phase-field models keep individual cell shape and distinguish single-cell polar forcing from intercellular active forces. Either force can unjam a layer, but velocity-aligned polarity specifically supports flocking. At 45:00-51:00, epithelial and Drosophila egg-chamber experiments motivate questions about flocking, chemical feedback and reversals. Matching collective flow does not identify a unique microscopic force model.

## Relevance to us

Useful counterexample to increasing individual speed as a strategy for collective performance. Also shows why a continuum visual match is insufficient to determine cell-level mechanics. Companion to [[yeomans-2020-challenges2]], with clearly separated established methods and unpublished mechanistic interpretations.
