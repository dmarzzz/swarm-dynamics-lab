## Summary

Transcript coverage: 0:00-17:48, including closing discussion when present. Obadia uses cross-rollup arbitrage to explain capital and coordination advantages. Native bridges are too slow for a fleeting price discrepancy; faster bridges lend capital and assume risk; maintaining inventory on both domains enables much faster execution but adds rebalancing and leg risk. He then asks whether joint ordering control on two domains yields more than separate control and argues that some opportunities reward sequencer collusion. Reducing trust-sensitive communication costs by owning validators on both domains creates a pressure toward overlapping operators. The closing research agenda explicitly calls this a prediction with unmeasured assumptions, not a finding that every current multichain validator set is centralized.

## Relevance to us

Useful economic account of correlated control across nominally independent populations. Requires measuring beneficial joint opportunities, coordination costs and countervailing user preferences before extrapolating a collapse into one operator. Related supply-chain analysis: [[hasu-2022-threat]].
