## Summary

In this five-and-a-half-minute presentation, Couzin contrasts visually coordinated marching with cooperation. At 01:00-02:30, locust tracking in an annular arena reveals biting and consumption among individuals. At 03:45-05:25, Mormon-cricket observations and nutritional preference tests support cannibalism as a driver of forced marching: individuals attack those ahead and avoid being attacked from behind. A demonstrated preferred salt concentration of 0.25 molar is identified as matching insect blood. Using nutrient-limitation estimates and satellite data to predict outbreaks is presented as ongoing work, without forecast accuracy or validation results.

## Relevance to us

Concise evidence that a swarm can coordinate through antagonistic incentives. Supports the distinction between coordinated traces and cooperative intent, with experimental context expanded in [[couzin-2018-animal]].
