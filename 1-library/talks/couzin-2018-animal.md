## Summary

At 06:40-21:30, Couzin separates the appearance of cooperative marching from the incentives that produce it. Locust arena experiments are described as showing density-dependent ordering; abdomen-denervation experiments impair marching, while Mormon-cricket nutritional tests show strong protein and salt preferences. These support cannibalism-driven forced marching rather than cooperative intent. At 25:00-31:00, fish groups track light gradients through individual speed modulation, despite no detected individual gradient sensing. At 32:00-40:00, reconstructed visual networks show why reinforcing behavioural contagion differs from simple infection. At 43:00-54:00, informed minorities, preference strength and uninformed individuals determine group consensus; adding five or ten untrained fish restores majority control in the described experiment. At 54:00-61:00, wild-baboon tracking and three-dimensional habitat mapping reveal movement decisions not determined by dominance rank. Trail-following improves prediction; an olfactory explanation remains a suggested mechanism. Tracking and virtual-reality tools connect these observations across laboratory and field settings.
