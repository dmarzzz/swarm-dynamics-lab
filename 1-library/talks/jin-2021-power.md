## Summary

Jin's abstract describes a two-player zero-sum Markov-game algorithm that learns a Nash-equilibrium policy with polynomial sample complexity when multi-agent Bellman-Eluder dimension is low. An exploiter deliberately targets the main player's weaknesses to drive improvement. The framework is said to cover tabular games and linear, kernel, or rich-observation approximation models. The accessible text gives neither the polynomial bound nor all conditions required for the guarantee.

## Relevance to us

A theoretical alternative to treating emergent strategies as unexplained phenomena: an explicitly adversarial training role can produce stronger policies under structural assumptions. The result is not evidence of cooperative swarm emergence or arbitrary many-player convergence. No useful theorem-specific timestamps were available in the chapter list read.
