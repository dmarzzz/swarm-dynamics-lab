## Summary

At 06:00-17:00, Marchetti connects nematic half-charge defects to active flows and possible biological organizing roles. At 18:00-32:00, continuum order and force-balance equations lead to a defect model with motile +1/2 defects and passive -1/2 defects. The sign of active stress determines propulsion direction, and the active length controls both defect separation and vortex size. At 33:00-37:00, persistence corrects a naive one-dimensional escape picture to yield a finite activity-driven defect-unbinding threshold. At 39:00-49:00, defect alignment and viscous screening allow model states with polar defect order or vortex lattices containing circulating defect pairs. Those ordered states are numerical predictions, not yet seen experimentally. At 55:00-59:00, questions expose limitations of isolated-defect approximations: surrounding defects and retarded texture relaxation can modify inferred interactions. Free-boundary and energy-consumption questions remain unresolved.

## Relevance to us

A strong model-to-measurement reference for distinguishing patterns in the medium from the particles or defects driving them. The ordered-defect predictions and biological functions need separate experimental validation. Compare [[yeomans-2020-challenges2]] on soft boundaries and confinement.
