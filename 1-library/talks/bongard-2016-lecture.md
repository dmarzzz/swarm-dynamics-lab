## Summary

The lecture first revisits crowdsourced symbol grounding and tests learned labels on unseen robot actions. The swarm section begins around 53:46: coordination and communication are separated as related evolutionary problems. At 72:09-76:48 a predator-prey example fixes gazelle behavior while evolving slower lion controllers on a toroidal arena. The gazelle flees an inverse-distance-weighted combination of predator vectors, making nearby predators more influential. This is a lecture setup, not evidence that both populations coevolve or a reported benchmark of adversarial robustness.

## Relevance to us

Concrete agent-interaction testbed with an explicit fixed-opponent assumption and boundary conditions. Useful caution that evolving coordination against one scripted prey need not generalize to strategic opponents.
