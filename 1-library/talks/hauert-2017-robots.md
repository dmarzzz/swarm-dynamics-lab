## Summary

Hauert describes bio-inspired, manually explored and machine-learned local rules. At 3:12-5:26 attraction, repulsion, alignment and a migration target produce demonstrated aerial flocking, alongside trail formation and decision-making examples. At 5:41-9:15 nanoparticle swarms extend the collective-design problem: making each particle highly sticky can trap delivery at the tumor periphery instead of improving penetration. Size and binding propensity must therefore be tuned against group dynamics. At 10:42-12:51 million-scale robotic seeds and emergency networks are explicitly future visions rather than implemented systems.

## Relevance to us

Clear negative example of individually effective behavior harming population-level performance, plus a path from simulation/crowdsourced design to physical tests. Avoid equating nanoparticle counts with autonomous communicating robot agents.
