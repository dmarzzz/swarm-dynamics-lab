## Summary

Leonard frames engineered and animal groups through feedback rather than literal biomimicry. At 12:00-27:00, a sensing graph, constant-speed planar agents and local steering laws connect individual responses to heading and spatial synchrony. Taking gradients of synchrony measures yields implementable rules, with gain signs selecting synchronized versus balanced motion and additional terms producing clustered circular patterns. At 27:00-31:00, underwater gliders use related rules to sample a 20-by-40-km region more than 1000 m deep near Monterey Bay for almost 24 days in August 2006 despite ocean currents. At 34:00-37:00, preliminary noise-robustness comparisons show that adding reciprocal connections to a directed star can worsen performance, rather than simply improving it. At 37:00-41:00, magnetically coupled fish replicas and overhead tracking provide a prospective platform for controlled animal-robot interaction. Questions discuss unknown real sensing graphs, redundant sampling, energy budgets and heterogeneous agents.

## Relevance to us

A concrete bridge from mathematical local controllers to a long-duration field system. The graph-robustness example warns that more edges are not automatically better; redundant measurements also need explicit spatial and temporal scales. Suitable controller ideas do not amount to empirical identification of animal policies.
