## Summary

At 07:00-15:00, virtual prey exposed to real predators evolve a balance of attraction and aligned motion, and Couzin explains three fish-school states and collective hysteresis. At 16:00-22:00, speed modulation enables group-level light-gradient tracking; an audience exchange reports replication in golden shiners and a different fish species that combines this mechanism with individual sensing. At 23:00-39:00, single-initiator escape trials and visual-field reconstruction support predictive weighted, directed influence networks. Strong local reinforcement and position at the group edge matter; inferring causality after the first response remains difficult. At 39:00-43:00, networked virtual fish offer sensory control. At 44:00-67:00, leadership, target geometry and uninformed participants reshape consensus, with a forgetting term identified as important for realistic multi-option behaviour. At 67:00-77:00, baboon movement data and habitat reconstruction support group decision predictions. Importantly, this is one baboon troop, not replicated independent populations. Olfactory trail-following remains inferred, not directly measured.

## Relevance to us

The unusually detailed questions expose assumptions hidden in public summaries, including replication limits, visual-only communication and forgetting.
