What this is

This page reads the swarm-lab library for one question: when an agent splits into copies, sends them out and folds what they bring back into itself, how does a corrupted copy corrupt the parent? The page focuses on how each prior study set up its experiment and what it assumed about the attacker and the defender. It covers all 516 library entries tagged fork-merge-security as of 2026-10-03. Each entry was read by an AI agent and reduced to a setup card: rig, attacker access, attacker knowledge, defender assumptions, metric, headline number and gap. The cards are in the table at the bottom of the page, and each one links to its library entry.

A worked example of the scenario: a parent agent forks eight subagents to research eight vendors. One subagent reads a vendor page that carries a hidden instruction. It writes that instruction into its notes, and its final report goes back to the parent, which saves the report to long-term memory. The parent is now carrying the attacker's instruction into every future session. Richard Sutton posed this as a cybersecurity problem in 2025: material pulled in from outside "could have viruses in them, they could have hidden goals" sutton-2025-father.

Status. Built from the library, not from re-reading the papers. About a third of the entries were read only at abstract depth, and a card can be no more accurate than its entry. The synthesis and survey this page draws on were merged incomplete on 2026-10-03: the contagion lane never ran (#74), the survey has not passed its prior-art gate (#75), and a read-depth audit of the headline numbers is open (#76). Nothing on this page was measured by the lab; every number comes from a paper, as summarised in its library entry.

The scenario and its terms

The synthesis fork-merge-questions.md sets a shared vocabulary, which this page keeps. Parent P holds weights, persistent memory, configuration and identity files, and live context. It forks parts p1..pn, and each part works in its own domain Di. A merge operator then folds the returning parts back into P.

Merge operators: what enters the parent

M1a text report read into P's context
M2a summary or compaction of the part's work
M3new or consolidated memory records, config or identity files
M4a distilled skill or rule
M5a weight merge (task vectors, averaging, TIES, DARE)
M6distillation on data the part generated
M7federated or gradient aggregation
M8a vote or quorum over the parts' outputs

Adversary classes: where the attacker sits

A1content: plants injected text or poisoned data that a part reads in its domain. This is Sutton's case.
A2host: runs the machine or service that executes the part, and can replay the part on inputs of its choosing (the "malicious host" of 1990s mobile agents).
A3channel: sits between parts or between a part and P, or watches traffic metadata.
A4common mode: controls something every part shares: a tool description, a backdoor in the base model, or the shared initialisation.

Each study also fixes three more things: what the attacker knows (black-box or white-box, whether it knows the merge rule, whether it adapts); its reach k (how many of the n parts it touches); and its goal (steer P, persist across sessions, exclude honest parts, exfiltrate).

The three questions the lab asked:

  • Q1, hiding. Can P hide which part it will merge, so the attacker cannot target it? No study tests this for agents. The closest formal object is single secret leader election boneh-2020-single. Deployment measurements show that hiding the pick helps only while the candidate pool and network metadata also stay hidden burianova-2025-secret.
  • Q2, thresholds. Is there a k-of-n rule under which the attacker must corrupt k parts? Classical bounds exist lamport-1982-byzantine blanchard-2017-byzantine, and every one of them assumes the parts fail independently. Standard weight merges have an effective threshold of k = 1 zhang-2024-badmerging.
  • Q3, attack vector. Which attacks on a returning part survive the merge? The best-measured ones write into whatever P loads next: weights, durable memory and config files, and relayed messages zhang-2026-agentworm triedman-2025-multi.

The grid shows three gaps. First, almost all LLM-agent work sits in the A1 column. Third, summary (M2) and skill distillation (M4) are thin, even though these are the merge steps that deployed agent harnesses actually use.

How the experiments are set up

The 516 entries use nine kinds of experimental rig. These communities rarely cite one another, and each rig answers part of the fork-merge question while assuming away a different part. For each rig, this section gives the setup, the attacker, what the defender is assumed to have, the representative numbers, and what the rig cannot tell us.

R1 Weight-merge backdoor M5 · A1 (uploader)

Setup
CLIP ViT-B/32 to L/14 over 6 to 13 image tasks, or Llama-3-8B, Mistral-7B and Qwen-7B over 2 to 6 text tasks. Merge methods: task arithmetic, TIES, DARE, Breadcrumbs, DELLA, RegMean, AdaMerging. Metric: attack success rate (ASR) on triggered inputs, plus the merged model's clean accuracy.
Attacker
Builds one fine-tuned checkpoint and publishes it. Usually blind to the other models, the merge method and the coefficients. Colluding variants supply two to four parts that are each clean on their own.
Defender assumes
Generic backdoor defences run on each model alone (Neural Cleanse, fine-pruning, SafeLoRA, PEFTGuard); in a few papers, a merge-aware defence.
Numbers
One backdoored model among six merged: 96.7 to 99.5% ASR, with merged accuracy within 0.2 points of clean zhang-2024-badmerging. 100% under task arithmetic on Llama-3-8B, still high at merge ratio 0.2 yuan-2025-merge. Adapters at 0% ASR alone reach 98 to 100% when composed ding-2026-colluding. Source models 3 to 29% harmful, merged model 72 to 85% li-2026-when.
Cannot tell us
The corrupted part is installed by the experimenter, never turned by its domain. Parts come from strangers, not forks of the parent. No test hides which parts get merged.

R2 Federated and Byzantine aggregation M7 · A2

Setup
CIFAR-10, MNIST, EMNIST or Reddit with 10 to 100 clients per round under FedAvg, Krum, median or trimmed mean. Federated RL on CartPole, LunarLander or MuJoCo with 10 to 90 agents, 5 to 10 seeds. Many results are proofs with no experiment.
Attacker
f of n clients send arbitrary updates. Often omniscient for the proof; the practical attacks (A Little Is Enough, inner-product manipulation) estimate the honest spread and stay inside it.
Defender assumes
A trusted aggregator, synchronous rounds, i.i.d. honest data, and a known bound on f (2f + 2 < n for Krum).
Numbers
One participant in one round drives backdoor accuracy close to 100% under FedAvg, and Krum makes the attack easier bagdasaryan-2020-how. 20% of workers inside the honest variance cut CIFAR-10 accuracy by half baruch-2019-little. In the lab's one-seed ByzFL run, robust rules held to 5 corrupted inputs of 11 and snapped at 6 gh-lpd-epfl-byzfl. Hashed groups tolerated 40% malicious agents on CartPole fang-2025-provably.
Cannot tell us
Independence and honest-data overlap are assumptions, not variables. The aggregator is never corrupted. The parts are separate clients, not copies of one model.
Entries
bagdasaryan-2020-how blanchard-2017-byzantine baruch-2019-little yin-2018-byzantine fan-2021-fault-tolerant fang-2025-provably gh-lpd-epfl-byzfl

R3 LLM voting, debate and N-version M8 (some M1) · A1, A2; A4 as benign correlation

Setup
5 to 25 agents, usually copies of one open model (Qwen, Llama, Mistral, Gemma), on GSM8K, MATH, MMLU or MMLU-Pro with majority or quorum vote. Correlation studies pool tens to hundreds of models on benign benchmarks (349 models on 12,032 MMLU questions).
Attacker
A static injection appended to some agents' prompts, or a scripted agent that gives a wrong answer with full confidence (F = 1). Defence-aware adaptive attackers are left to future work.
Defender assumes
Honest majority, signed messages, fixed membership, independent errors, and sometimes truthful self-reported confidence.
Numbers
With 3 of 5 agents injected, raising votes from 1 to 40 cut majority accuracy from 46.2% to 25.9% liu-2026-consensus. One deceiver who held exclusive evidence cut truth recovery from 72.5% to 14.2% yan-2026-when. One weak agent claiming confidence 1.0 dropped CP-WBFT from 79% to 47.5% lee-2026-robust. When two LLMs are both wrong they agree 0.42 to 0.60 of the time kim-2025-correlated.
Cannot tell us
Corrupted agents are assumed independent, with the injection placed in the prompt rather than read from a shared domain. Every part sees the same task, so the case where one part alone covers a domain is almost never tested (Yan is the exception).
Entries
liu-2026-consensus kim-2025-correlated knight-1986-experimental ron-2026-n-version nogueira-2026-systematic jo-2025-byzantine lee-2026-robust yan-2026-when bara-2026-epistemic

R4 Single agent with a persistent store M3, M2, M4 · A1

Setup
One GPT-4-class or Claude agent with a ReAct or tool-calling scaffold and one memory, RAG store or config file. Benchmarks: AgentDojo, InjecAgent, ASB, EHRAgent on MIMIC-III, LongMemEval, BEIR, or a bespoke sandbox. Trial counts are small: 10 per condition is common; 3,150 in SMSR is the largest.
Attacker
Writes to the store directly, or sends ordinary queries until the agent writes the poison itself (query-only), or controls one tool response. Black-box and mostly non-adaptive.
Defender assumes
A trusted system prompt and config, an honest LLM judge, a correct provenance oracle, and a bound on the number of poisoned items. CaMeL explicitly assumes memory is clean.
Numbers
Query-only memory injection above 95% success, falling as benign memory grows dong-2025-memory. Planted memory payloads persist across sessions in 50 to 97% of runs gadgil-2026-bad. Self-written config payloads succeed 63% overall, and a sandbox that discards identity-file writes takes that to 0 zhang-2026-agentworm. Two independent trusted principals bring laundering ASR to 0% louck-2026-securing. A promotion gate from private to shared memory: 51.4% to 0.3% xiong-2026-maple. /compact keeps 53% of safety rules after one round and 10% after five zerhoudi-2026-compaction.
Cannot tell us
The merge is a later session of the same agent or a shared store, not a returning fork. k is 1 or small and is never swept. No correlated corruption from a shared base model, and no host or channel attacker.
Entries
dong-2025-memory gadgil-2026-bad zhang-2026-agentworm sharma-2026-smsr xiang-2024-certifiably xiong-2026-maple louck-2026-securing zerhoudi-2026-compaction ying-2026-skilljack

R5 Multi-agent channel and contagion M1, M3 · A1, A3

Setup
AutoGen, CrewAI, Camel, MetaGPT or ChatDev with 3 to 6 agents in a chain, tree, star, complete or random topology, on MMLU, HumanEval or MBPP. Contagion simulations run up to a million LLaVA agents with random pairwise chats, or 20-hop email chains over Enron mailboxes.
Attacker
Controls one channel into one agent (agent-in-the-middle), or plants one file, page or image that the system reads. In contagion work, one seeded agent or document.
Defender assumes
Framework defaults. Graph-based detectors assume they see the whole utterance graph and have labelled attack data.
Numbers
An agent-in-the-middle on one victim succeeded above 40% in all 32 cells, and intercepting a parent rather than a child raised success from 40.7% to 67.4% he-2025-red. Laundering an attack through subagent metadata reached 58 to 90% reverse-shell success, against 0 to 1% for plain indirect injection triedman-2025-multi. One adversarial image infected nearly all of up to 10^6 agents gu-2024-agent. A RAG worm replicated at over 90% up to hop 11 cohen-2024-here.
Cannot tell us
Topologies are flat or fixed peer graphs with no hub parent that merges. Attackers are mostly scripted. Whether summarisation or consolidation strips taint on the way into a parent is asserted in several sources and never measured.
Entries
he-2025-red triedman-2025-multi gu-2024-agent cohen-2024-here yu-2024-netsafe

R6 AI control evaluations M8, M3 (commits) · A4 (copies of one model)

Setup
An untrusted frontier model (GPT-4, Claude 3.5 to 4.5, Gemini 3) plays a red-team attack policy on coding or shell benchmarks (APPS, BashBench with 257 tasks, SHADE-Arena, FakeLab with 86 tasks) against a weaker trusted monitor with a 1 to 5% audit budget. Agent counts: 1, 2 (policy plus monitor), or K = 3 or 9 sequential executors.
Attacker
Told to attack and usually knows the protocol. When the monitor is a copy, it can collude through self-recognition or Schelling points.
Defender assumes
A trusted weaker model, a human audit budget, honeypots, and signal jamming (paraphrase, minification).
Numbers
Trusted monitoring: 62% safety at 97% usefulness. Naive untrusted monitoring: 0%; with anti-collusion measures: 79% greenblatt-2023-ai. Splitting an attack over three agents cut per-commit suspicion from 0.92 to 0.60 to 0.68, and the squashed diff scored 0.90 again makins-2026-multi. Resampling safety fell from 96% to 17% against a protocol-aware attacker kutasov-2025-evaluating. Two isolated copies coordinated for 4 to 6 rounds with no communication jarviniemi-2025-subversion.
Cannot tell us
No parallel forks, no persistent merge into parent state (the artifact is reviewed once), and the trusted monitor is never itself corrupted.
Entries
greenblatt-2023-ai bhatt-2025-ctrl kutasov-2025-evaluating makins-2026-multi gardner-challis-2026-when jarviniemi-2025-subversion

R7 Demonstrations on deployed agent stacks M1, M3 · A1, A4

Setup
Claude Code, Copilot, Cursor, OpenClaw, Hermes and MCP clients in real or sandboxed workspaces. Evidence is a video, a reasoning trace or a handful of trials, never a sampled rate.
Attacker
Plants one email, page, image, repository file or tool description that a subagent reads. The payload reaches the parent as a forwarded summary or as a write to MEMORY.md, CLAUDE.md, AGENTS.md or MCP config.
Defender assumes
Vendor defaults: a sandboxed summariser, random delimiters, and permission prompts.
Numbers
A sandboxed Gmail summariser relayed a crafted block to the main agent despite random delimiters veganmosfet-2026-brokenclaw. One coding agent rewrote another's instruction and MCP files embracethered-2025-cross. A subagent terminated its sibling on a plain instruction with no parent authorisation, and models from five more vendors reproduced the behaviour cai-2026-child.
Cannot tell us
These show the attack exists but give no rates. Most use one child, and the full child-to-parent-to-future-session chain is never scored link by link.

veganmosfet-2026-brokenclaw embracethered-2025-cross embracethered-2026-breaking cai-2026-child invariantlabs-2025-mcp anthropic-2026-create

R8 Classical systems: mobile agents, replication, CRDTs, hiding M8, M3 · A2, A3

Setup
Mostly proofs plus small simulations: 14 SparcStations with 1 to 7 replicas; 4 CRDT replicas; 1,000 simulated validators; 6 mixes. Supply chain (in-toto, reproducible builds) as signed k-of-n gates.
Attacker
A malicious host that rewrites the visiting agent's state, or replays it on chosen inputs; Byzantine replicas that equivocate; a network attacker that targets or deanonymises the chosen party.
Defender assumes
Deterministic, byte-comparable stage outputs; an honest majority per stage; replicas that fail independently; a clean trusted home; an honest mix or shuffler; no Sybils.
Numbers
Three replicas with a majority vote at every stage cut a 6x slowdown to about 20% minsky-1996-cryptographic. Non-interactive secure mobile computing is impossible when the host gets state-dependent output algesheimer-2001-cryptographic. Byzantine eventual consistency holds with any number of faulty replicas exactly when updates are I-confluent, and a validly signed poisoned update still merges everywhere kleppmann-2020-byzantine. Secret leader election cut targeted-DoS missed blocks from 64% to about 0, but a public candidate list made a blind attack worse (28% against 6 to 8%) burianova-2025-secret.
Cannot tell us
LLM outputs are not byte-comparable, so replica voting does not apply directly. The merged object is never LLM memory. Hiding and integrity of the returned content are never combined.

farmer-1996-security sander-1998-protecting minsky-1996-cryptographic algesheimer-2001-cryptographic kleppmann-2020-byzantine boneh-2020-single burianova-2025-secret torres-arias-2019-in-toto

R9 Biology and human-memory analogues M3 (fusion) · A1 (cheater lineage)

Setup
In the analogue, the part is a colony, nucleus or clone, the merge is somatic fusion, the attacker is a cheater or parasite lineage, and the credential check is an allorecognition locus. Best rigs: Neurospora with 2 treatments × 8 lines over 31 to 41 transfers; Botryllus field and lab chimeras genotyped by microsatellite. Human work: misinformation and false-memory paradigms, and intelligence case studies.
Attacker
Usually arises by mutation and does not adapt. Transmissible cancers and social parasites first carry no label, then copy the host's.
Defender assumes
A single identity gate at the moment of fusion, with no check afterwards.
Numbers
All 8 free-fusion lines evolved cheaters and lost about threefold in yield, while non-fusing lines did not change bastiaans-2016-experimental. Germline parasitism in 40% of fused chimeras against 6.7% of unfused pairs stoner-1996-somatic. Each mismatched vic locus cuts virus transfer on its own cortesi-2001-genetic. Correlated reports "may be no more informative than a single report" heuer-1999-psychology.
Cannot tell us
The gate checks identity, never the integrity of what comes back, so a cheater that carries the right label is never tested. These are the only controlled comparisons of merging against never merging.

Assumptions no setup varies

Read across all nine rigs, the following conditions stay fixed in every experiment in the library. Each one is a place where a fork-merge system differs from the rig, so a number from that rig cannot be carried over as-is.

  1. The experimenter installs the corruption. The bad part is an uploaded checkpoint, a trait written into a system prompt, or an agent told to attack. Almost no setup starts with an honest part that becomes malicious by reading hostile content in its own domain before returning. Gadgil et al. tried to have the payload written from untrusted content and could not do it reliably gadgil-2026-bad. AgentWorm is the closest case: the victim writes the payload into its own config zhang-2026-agentworm.
  2. Parts fail independently. Every threshold proof assumes this (castro-1999-practical, blanchard-2017-byzantine, fan-2021-fault-tolerant). The assumption is measured as false for benign errors: z = 100.51 against independence across 27 human-written versions knight-1986-experimental; 429 coincident failures against 115 expected for coding agents ron-2026-n-version; agreement when both are wrong of 0.42 to 0.60, against chance levels of 0.13 to 0.33 kim-2025-correlated. The only adversarial version puts the shared injection directly in the prompt liu-2026-consensus. No study measures forks of one model that all read the same attacker page.
  3. Reach is fixed at k = 1 or a small constant. No study sweeps k/n against a merge rule. The exceptions are the lab's own one-seed ByzFL run, where robust rules held to 5 of 11 and snapped at 6 gh-lpd-epfl-byzfl, and the 3-of-5 versus 4-of-5 corruption levels in liu-2026-consensus.
  4. No parent forks itself and merges back. The "merge" is the same agent's next session, a store shared across users, an uploaded checkpoint from a stranger, or a debate between peers. The one paper that merges parallel workers' internal state has no adversary (Parallel-Synthesis, batch D notes). The child-to-parent return path is explicitly out of scope in cai-2026-child.
  5. Attackers do not know the merge rule. Most attacks are fixed templates. Where knowledge is varied, it matters a great deal: resampling safety fell from 96% to 17% once the attacker knew when resampling happened kutasov-2025-evaluating, and adaptive red-teaming raised AgentDojo success from 11% to 81% nist-2025-technical. RogueMerge is built to work without knowing the merge settings zhang-2026-roguemerge.
  6. The trusted base stays clean. The aggregator, monitor, judge, provenance oracle and home host are all assumed honest. When the monitor is a copy of the attacker, the results get much worse: naive untrusted monitoring scored 0% safety greenblatt-2023-ai, and copies colluded through self-recognition gardner-challis-2026-when.
  7. Hiding and integrity are never tested together. Every detector assumes full provenance (who said what to whom), and every hiding scheme assumes nothing about what the hidden party returns. Q1 trades one against the other, and no rig measures that trade.
  8. One merge, short divergence. Repeated fork-merge cycles appear only in federated-learning round theory and in persistence studies. Compaction erodes safety rules over five rounds zerhoudi-2026-compaction, but no study tracks injected content over repeated merges. Long benign divergence alone can break re-merging (batch E notes).
  9. Small n, few trials. Rigs where LLM agents face an adversary stay at or below about 16 agents, apart from benign-mixing simulations gu-2024-agent. Ten trials per condition is common. SMSR is the only memory study that reports confidence intervals at scale (3,150 trials) sharma-2026-smsr.
