## Research question

Can a false local map change where a swarm gathers evidence, preventing it from discovering its mistake? Use a synthetic archipelago for causal work. Earth coordinate demonstrations are separate and do not establish visual perception; Jev consumes text.

## Strongest version and starting point

The flagship is a loop through judgments, inspection choices and subsequent evidence. Start with **Two Swarms, One World**: counterbalanced early histories, equal final factual evidence, social versus independent decisions, reset versus retained state. A common-input serialization check separates factual equivalence from prompt-order artifacts. Only add adaptive exploration after fixed-evidence comparisons work.

The viewer should show evaluator truth, each swarm's map, the directly exposed region, unique observation ancestry and actual scout paths. An independent inspection may correct a missing island. It is also a valid outcome if swarms quickly recover or exact mapping rules explain the effect.

## Critical design constraints

- Never hard-code avoidance of predicted water and call the resulting trap emergent.
- Keep the whole world inspectable and score mapping accuracy across it.
- Match initial information and available observation process. In adaptive arms, realized observations are a mediator; do not force them equal and erase the question.
- Distinguish explicit retraction, deletion, reset and new evidence.
- Pair by world; do not count neighboring tiles as independent replicates.
- Compare a pooled-evidence single solver and a deterministic mapper.
- Treat persistent finite-window error as external state behavior, not changed model weights or permanent delusion.
