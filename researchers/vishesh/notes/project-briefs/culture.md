# culture

Status: HUNCH, not a hypothesis; no survey has passed the gate. Author's own triage, not a team ranking: Lift Medium · Difficulty Hard · Novelty Focused extension · Event fit Strong · ~14–24 builder-hours (estimate).

## Background

Culture here means a convention transmitted through interaction or retained records. It does not require consciousness. Replacement agents make a useful test: does a rule persist after the original participants leave?

## Closest prior work

- **Cultural evolution in LLM populations · 2024** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08882  
  Manipulates network structure and transmission in language-agent populations. It is direct prior art for generational experiments.
- **Emergent social conventions and collective bias · 2025** — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12077490/  
  Uses naming games to study conventions and committed minorities. This is closer to norm emergence than merely assigning matching personas.

## Where it applies

Onboarding replacement agents into a shared repository, keeping useful conventions, and removing obsolete ones after a rule change.

## The angle

Introduce an arbitrary convention, replace members, then change the task so the old convention is costly. Measure retention and adaptation separately.

## What to watch

Persistence may be caused entirely by a shared instruction. Include no-memory and no-interaction controls before calling it social transmission.

## Research question

Which claims survive turnover through onboarding notes, guides and shared memory?

## Dossier design sketch

Carried over from the [interactive dossier](../swarm-ecology-dossier.html#culture). This is an unexecuted hunch, not an approved experiment. Counts and treatments below are planning choices; the lab’s survey and hypothesis review gates still apply.

1. Identify a newcomer’s accessible onboarding artifacts and their versions.
2. Track claims appearing before and after documented access.
3. Separate inherited norms, task procedures and factual assertions; look for both useful and incorrect transmission.

## Measures

- Supported artifact-to-behavior links
- Claim survival across arrivals
- Mutation and correction of inherited claims
- Newcomer task accuracy

## Controls

- Use access records where available
- Shared base-model knowledge is an alternative explanation
- Track roster and prompt changes
- Avoid equating identity with model provider

## Minimum useful output

One arrival episode and an artifact lineage map with uncertainty labels.

## Optional extension

Prospective onboarding experiment with a corrected versus outdated guide.

## Interpretation risk

Without read records, timing only supports a hypothesis. Long histories can exceed the weekend.

## Demo narrative

Follow an old artifact into a newcomer’s first decisions and show what was preserved or changed.

## Review update October 3

Inherited text can directly reveal the answer. Keep training or onboarding artifacts separate from held-out tasks and track what the descendants actually read.

**Decision to resolve before promotion:** Which inherited practices survive turnover and improve held-out task performance?

If the simple baseline explains the result, or the necessary evidence cannot be obtained, narrow this to a replication or park the hunch. A toy animation is not evidence that the proposed intervention works.
