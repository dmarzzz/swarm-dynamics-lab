# Swarm of Theseus v2 — procedures, exceptions and portability

Prospective implementation plan, 2026-10-04 UTC. Exploratory S0/S1 instrument under an unreviewed hunch; no accepted novelty or culture claim. Owner vishesh/codex-theseus. This plan is written before v2 implementation. Model execution remains blocked until a new API allocation is approved and reserved; the shared USD 45 cap has only USD 0.097684 unreserved. Prior results and failed qualifications remain separate.

## TLDR

Can learned verification practices and their corrections survive two complete crew replacements, and can an inherited record be applied after a tool-interface change? Compare rolling notes, evidence-tagged rolling notes, frozen founder notes and no inherited notes. Test three practical settings: release verification, incident precautions and a release workflow whose tool vocabulary changes. Measure correct actions, survival of still-valid checks, abandonment of invalidated checks, multi-generation lineage and failure/missingness. This is an exploratory test of learned procedural transmission, not spontaneous human culture, a novelty claim or a model-family migration result.

Harbor and glassmaking are deferred because practical handoffs and selective rule correction have clearer decision value. Model-family migration is not replaced by an interface test in the interpretation: the latter is a cheaper, isolated first diagnostic. No claim that these are the globally best three tasks.

## Question and prediction

Primary planned contrast: evidence-tagged rolling notes minus plain rolling notes in post-second-turnover correct decisions on the two changing-rule scenarios. Both have identical length limits and observations. A zero or negative effect is valid. Frozen notes test whether durable copying suffices; no inheritance measures reacquisition. Neither is the primary novelty contrast.

The evidence-tagged treatment requests scope, supporting observation IDs and obsolete-rule notices. The plain condition requests a concise operational note. The treatment may change writing effort and attention as well as representation; equal calls and output limits do not isolate a pure provenance effect. No actor is given a correct rule as a founding notebook. Founders first see a fixed training batch of labeled past observations, then receive outcome feedback on their own decisions. This is supervised acquisition from experience, not unassisted discovery or emergence.

## Setup

Three role instances per crew, each with bounded private notes. At each step each member decides on the same six tickets independently and writes its own next notebook. Collective action requires at least two valid matching semantic decisions; there is no authoritative agent. This majority aggregator is explicitly an instrument scaffold: it does not itself demonstrate collective culture or special swarm benefit. A one-reader diagnostic is included in qualification; a budget-matched single-controller scientific comparator remains a gap until separately implemented.

World-specific categorical rules concern which evidence source/check is valid for each of two classes. Code generates balanced cases that separate a claimed pass from a sufficient independent check. Class A changes rule after the first turnover in release/incident, class B does not. Incident diagnostics balance risk patterns; they do not implement naturally rare events or estimate field hazard rates. Migration changes only command tokens. Agents see current observations and feedback from completed decisions; never evaluator truth for current cases or a bulletin with the new rule. Command documentation is intentionally available because interface syntax must be knowable.

Case IDs, irrelevant cues and permutations vary; evaluation includes six nonidentical cases per step and new combinations. Input-domain diversity is reported, not inferred from unique IDs. Templates are still synthetic, with a small finite rule family.

## Protocol

Ten steps, 0 through 9. Step 0: founders receive historical training examples; step 1 continues acquisition. Capture each arm-independent acquisition lineage once and fork the exact checkpoint into all arms. Steps 2,3,4 replace founders 0,1,2 respectively. Steps 5,6,7 replace those descendants. Thus zero founders remain at step 4 and all first descendants have also gone at step 7. Steps 8,9 are primary evaluation. Rule/interface change starts at step 5; outcome feedback arrives only after decisions. Feedback on steps 0 through 5 is delivered to surviving actors on the next step. Outcomes from steps 6 through 9 are withheld from actors: their feedback list is empty thereafter. The interruption is identical across arms and explicit to actors; evaluator outcomes remain recorded. Thus the changed-rule observation at step 5 must propagate through replacement rather than being freshly taught at every endpoint. Original training packets are never reintroduced to descendants. No original founder mentors the second wave.

Arms: `rolling`, `evidence`, `frozen`, `none`. A fresh member inherits the authorized archive; incumbents retain only their own notebook and the most recent feedback. `rolling` and `evidence` replace the team archive from current notes after each step; `frozen` always uses the acquisition checkpoint archive; `none` masks onboarding only. Every departing note and inherited archive has an ID/hash. Notes are bounded at 700 characters each; archive at 2,400 characters. All arms have the same three solve calls per step and 900 output-token ceiling. Distinct per-arm acquisition is forbidden: only the archive-writing instruction differs after the shared checkpoint.

S0 qualification: two fresh worlds per scenario, seeds 300/301, one-reader control supplied with an explicit current rule (competence ceiling) and a learner supplied only historical cases; 2 checkpoints (stable and changed/interface) with six cases each, 24 calls total. Ceiling must achieve at least 0.90 action accuracy in every scenario; learner must achieve at least 0.75 stable accuracy in every scenario. Valid syntax/schema required throughout. Changed-rule learner performance is descriptive, not a gate forcing adaptation success. Failure triggers one prospectively documented repair on 302/303; no threshold lowering.

S1 pilot, only after qualification: two worlds per scenario, seeds 400/401. Six shared acquisitions × 6 calls = 36; six worlds × four arms × eight continuation steps × three members = 576; total 612 calls. At most 660 calls across initial qualification, one repair and pilot. Hard API allocation requested: USD 15; no launch without explicit reservation. One model: pinned Haiku used in v1, pricing reverified before dispatch. Two worker threads maximum, two-hour wall ceiling, zero semantic/transport retries. Model-family comparison and S2 disabled.

## Metrics

Score each ticket's semantic action against a deterministic oracle; malformed/invalid decisions have no vote. Only at least two valid agreeing votes produce a collective decision. Record incorrect, invalid and missing separately. Planned denominator is retained. Report conservative accuracy with failures as incorrect plus observed-only accuracy and counts; missing data never becomes an identified zero contrast without a missingness/bounds report.

Primary: mean post-step-7 action accuracy per world in release and incident, paired evidence-minus-rolling, averaged equally over scenarios. Report individual world contrasts and exploratory world-cluster bootstrap intervals only when enough completed pairs exist; with four primary worlds use descriptive intervals and explicitly weak precision. Secondary: unchanged-class accuracy, changed-class accuracy, stale-rule agreement, unnecessary incident checks, missed modeled hazards, invalid commands, agreement, archive bytes, generations/ancestry, calls, tokens/cost. Migration is separate: semantic correctness and command validity under the new interface. It is not pooled with adaptation.

Useful exploratory signal: at least 0.10 absolute primary improvement while unchanged-class performance does not fall by more than 0.05 relative to rolling; these are interpretation thresholds, not significance claims or rerun triggers. Frozen/no-inheritance differences alone do not justify a culture claim. Ceiling in all arms is uninformative for a treatment advantage.

## Evaluation and repairs

Offline checks cover disjoint splits, deterministic pairing, two complete replacement waves, archive ancestry, absence of current labels in payloads, delayed feedback, exact class-change boundary, semantic/command separation, and fixed-majority handling with missing votes. Mutation controls must distinguish current rule, stale rule, unconditionally trust the summary, always check, never check and old-interface tokens. An independent scoring implementation recomputes saved outputs; same-author independence is identified accurately.

Previous gaps: supplied founder rules replaced by training evidence; founder-only mentoring replaced by two archive-transmission generations; nonfunctional phrase removed; explicit changed-rule bulletin removed; repeated XOR replaced by evidence/check choices; actual failure reason preserved; stale public plan rejected by exact expected revision/hash checks. More lifelike tasks do not by themselves prove novelty. The single-controller and cross-model scientific comparisons remain planned follow-ups, so claims about a special swarm advantage remain prohibited.

## Prospective amendment after offline review — 2026-10-04 UTC

No v2 model calls preceded this amendment. Continuous endpoint feedback could make reacquisition dominate the inheritance comparison. Add the common feedback interruption above, keep all assignment counts and thresholds unchanged, and test its exact boundary. This strengthens transmission measurement but narrows inference to a feedback interruption; an uninterrupted-feedback replication is a future sensitivity analysis. The incident implementation uses balanced diagnostic hazards and has no real-time rare-event interval or economic cost calibration. Do not present those unimplemented features as measured. Migration reports intended semantic action separately from current-command validity, counting understandable old tokens as invalid commands without erasing that distinction.

Qualification clarification before execution: the explicit-rule ceiling threshold applies separately at the stable and changed/interface checkpoint for each scenario, rather than averaging a good old-interface score with a failing new-interface score. All 24 outputs must validate. The two-hour deadline is shared across stages and stored in the quota ledger; a stage may not be invisibly relaunched under a fresh output name.
