## Cases and practical choices

Three authored task mechanisms share local two-source authority but differ in evidence validity:

- **Release:** authorize the named artifact to the named environment at the named policy revision. A correctly signed approval for another artifact/environment/revision is irrelevant and cannot authorize the release.
- **Failover:** authorize a service move in the named region/generation using measurements in the inclusive freshness interval `[now−2,now]`. Old or future telemetry and another service/region/generation cannot stand in for current witness readiness.
- **Delegation:** authorize a named action on a resource under intervals `[starts,ends)`, with revocation effective at `revoked_at <= now`. An expired, not-yet-valid, revoked or other-action/resource delegation is not usable authorization.

Each family has independently specified labels for clean allow, required dissent (hold), missing required evidence (defer), family-specific invalid evidence (defer), conflicting current required evidence (defer), and irrelevant-source dissent (allow). Add matched clean/invalid-boundary contrasts, case/receipt permutations and irrelevant-source insertion tests. Cryptographic source authenticity is assumed from transport; we are not evaluating signature verification. Structured cases are operational analogues, not real incidents. Two-source consistency is common; the families must not be sold as three independent theories of culture.

Founder histories contain all five possible peers in each of six outcome-labeled episodes; no authority-pair answer field. Exact enumeration must uniquely identify the pair. Scope metadata is factored losslessly. At decision time, actors receive full family-specific records.
