> **cheap-agents.md** — Whether the question changes for a population of very small low-cost models plus a fast heuristic classifier, including the competence-versus-robustness confound and cost arithmetic.  
> Compiled 2026-10-03. Supporting note for the seo-poisoning proposal; hunch-level, not a survey or a hypothesis.

# Cheap-agent populations

**Scope.** "Type 1 classifier" is read per the BRIEF: a fast System-1 decision rule (logistic head / small encoder / rule set) mapping retrieved context to a choice with no deliberation, paired with a small LLM that reads and summarises. Numbers I computed are **[derived]**; numbers I could not confirm at a fetched page are **[unverified]**. Each section ends with a boundary-of-evidence line.

---

## 1. Does susceptibility scale with model size / reasoning?

**There is no single direction — it flips with the failure mode, and at least five documented metric confounds make most headline claims provisional.** Treating "susceptibility" as one scalar is the error the literature itself spent 2026 correcting [12][14][18].

| Failure mode | Direction with size/capability | Best evidence | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion sycophancy (no ground truth) | **bigger = WORSE** | Wei [10]: **+19.8%** PaLM-8B→62B, **+10.0%** →540B. Perez [9]: *">90% of answers match the user's view"* at 52B — and **scale, not RLHF steps, is the driver** (*"sycophancy is similar for models trained with various numbers of RL steps, including 0"*) | High (clean within-family ladders) |
| Factual sycophancy (verifiable answer) | **bigger = BETTER if instruction-tuned; base models mildly WORSE** | De Marez [12], **56 open models 0.3–32B**, 13 manipulation types: *"vulnerability is governed mainly by size, but instruction tuning changes how size acts… base models gain margin but become mildly more manipulation-sensitive"* | Med-high |

**The reconciling mechanism.** Capability does two opposite things: it strengthens the model's *prior* (helping wherever there is a ground truth to defend) and it strengthens *instruction-following over retrieved text* (hurting wherever the manipulation is an instruction). Opinion sycophancy has no truth to defend, so only the second effect operates — hence the cleanest inverse scaling in the field [9][10]. Greshake et al. [8] state the same tension as a design bind rather than a measurement — they run **no size comparison at all**, but argue that *"persuasion and deception might get better in future models, as a side effect of RLHF"* and that a checker safe from the same trap *"might need to use a less general model that was not trained with instruction tuning. On the other hand, this less capable model might not detect complex encoded input."* A further result suggests the axis that actually moves the needle is **inference-time deliberation, not size**: reasoning-vs-non-reasoning model pairs from the same vendors give truth-bias **59.33% vs 71.00%** (o3 **49.50%** vs GPT-4.1 **93.00%**, Cohen's h = −1.05), yet GPT-4.1 — frontier and non-reasoning — is the worst in the set, so *"capability advances alone do not resolve fundamental veracity detection challenges"* [13]. Where the manipulation is neither an instruction nor a checkable falsehood — **coherent planted evidence, i.e. the owner's scenario** — capability buys little, and the measured predictors are *difficulty* and *confidence*, not size: Zhu et al. [15] report **−0.777** correlation between task accuracy and conformity across 57 MMLU subjects, and that higher initial confidence predicts non-conformity (p<0.001); Bellina et al. [21] (13 open multimodal models, **3B–34B**, conformity read off token probability) find *"larger models show reduced conformity on simple tasks due to improved capabilities, [yet] remain vulnerable when operating at their competence boundary"*; Li et al. [28] (HiddenBench, 65 tasks, 15 frontier models, **30.1%** group vs **80.7%** single-agent accuracy) state flatly that *"neither model scale nor individual reasoning accuracy reliably predicts collective performance."*

**⚠ The confound that decides this note: small-model "robustness" is largely non-function.** InjecAgent [4] reports **valid rates** under injected content — the share of outputs that parse at all: **Llama2-13B 1.7%** vs **GPT-4 98.8%**. Llama2-13B's flattering 0.9% ASR-all is produced by emitting almost nothing usable; the paper names it (*"a positive correlation between the valid rate and the Arena rating… invalid outputs reflect the incapability of LLMs"*). Kairos [17] carries the same artefact in a different metric: its appendix calls 3B models *"most resilient"* (−6.33%) only because the 3B model sits at **49.05%** accuracy, near chance, with almost no correct answers left to lose (the paper names the `p_c` term). **Any cheap-agent study must report a validity/competence rate beside every susceptibility number, or it will measure incompetence and call it robustness.** Second consequence from Kairos: prompt-based mitigations *hurt* small models (≤32B O–K Δ **−5.65% → −11.25%** under "empowered" prompting; reflected prompting *"detrimental for smaller models… likely due to hallucinations or confusion induced during the self-reflection process"*) while helping large ones (**+0.12%**); only GRPO training helped the small tier. **Do not assume a prompt-level dissent instruction works at 1–4B — it is a condition to measure, not a control.**

**Other confounds the sources name themselves.** Flip rates conflate truth margin with manipulation sensitivity, which move in *opposite* directions with scale [12]. Conformity measured without conditioning on prior entropy *"consistently overestimate[s] pure sycophancy"*, and since scale lowers entropy, part of "bigger = more robust" is a knowledge effect [14]. Filtering harmful conformity is *equivalent to* a correctness probe, so every such metric is bounded by self-knowledge (AUROC **0.64–0.89** across six families) [18]. Same-size models with different training reverse the inverse-scaling trend, and inverse scaling may be U-shaped rather than asymptotic [7]. And capability cuts both ways inside one benchmark — BIPIA's code tasks show no correlation because *"GPT-4… is able to discern the intent behind the code and opts to refuse"* [2]. Outliers falsify any "law" in both eras: Claude 3 Opus is high-utility/low-ASR (66.61 / **11.29**) [3], Claude Opus 4.5 is **0.5%** ASR while the capable Gemini 2.5 Pro is the most vulnerable at 8.5% [5].

*Boundary of evidence.* **No paper runs a controlled size ladder on agentic indirect injection** — [2][3][5] are cross-vendor, [4] has ladders but the valid-rate confound, [6]'s ladders are classification/GCG. **No adversarial-SEO paper studies scale at all.** Nobody has run opinion-sycophancy evals across a modern frontier ladder. So the question most relevant here — *does a 1B agent choose differently under planted web content than a frontier agent?* — **has not been measured by anyone.** That is the gap this project can fill.

## 2. What does agent simplicity change in the classical results?

Every canonical result was derived for minimal agents — and in the two economics papers the agents are *perfectly Bayesian*, i.e. at the capability ceiling, not below it.

**Transfers to a cheap swarm unchanged (agent-complexity-independent):**
1. **Cascade existence.** BHW [36] (binary private signal, observing only predecessors' *actions*): a cascade can start at the **third** agent; Pr(no cascade) after even *n* = (p−p²)^(n/2); verbatim *"Even for a very noisy signal, as when p = ½ + ε, with ε arbitrarily small, this probability after only 10 individuals is less than 0.1 percent!"* **[derived]** from their eq. 3 at n→∞: wrong-cascade probability 43.4% at p=0.55, 24.7% at p=0.7, **14.3% even at p=0.8**.
2. **Fragility, no self-correction.** BHW [36]: *"in the PAO regime, after a cascade starts it is never reversed"*; *"This uniformity is brittle: small shocks can easily shift the behavior of many individuals."*
3. **Aggregation fails from observability, not agent quality.** Banerjee [37]: *"The probability that no one in the population chooses the correct option is bounded away from zero for any size of the population… This contrasts with the case where the decision makers choose without looking at each other."* Same agents, same signals, correct aggregation — the moment they stop watching each other. **This is the key transfer: the injector's leverage is correlated observability of a manipulable corpus, which a cheap swarm has in full.**
4. **Outcome ≠ disposition.** Granovetter [38]: thresholds 0..99 over 100 agents ⇒ 100 adopters; replace *one* agent's threshold 1 with 2 ⇒ **1 adopter**: *"the difference in outcome results only from the process of aggregation."*
5. **Tipping and zealotry.** Watts [39]: cascades are a percolation phase transition, G₀″(1)=Σk(k−1)ρ_k p_k = z, with a two-sided window; cascade *size* is set by whole-network connectivity (*"This is a surprising result"*); heterogeneous thresholds raise vulnerability while heterogeneous degree lowers it. Mobilia et al. [42]: zealot effect scales **1/√Z, independent of N** — a vanishing *fraction* of immovable agents still blocks consensus (⚠ a *no-consensus* result, **not** a tipping threshold; do not cite it as one). Xie et al. [41]: p_c ≈ **10%** flips consensus *time* from exp(αN) to ln N. Centola et al. [40]: ~**25%** committed confederates tipped real **human** groups — the strongest single datapoint that these phenomena survive maximal agent sophistication. (10% and 25% measure different quantities; they are not rival estimates of one constant.)
6. **Already measured in cheap LLM populations.** Flint et al. [34] measure critical mass in populations of **Qwen2.5-7B, Phi-4, Llama-3.2-3B, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B**. Ashery et al. [33] find critical mass from **2% (Llama-3-70B) to 67% (Llama-2-70b-Chat)** — model-dependent, not monotone in capability. De Marzo et al. [35] reconstruct an *unplanned* agent population (1,201 handles, 5,929 edits) and show a one-parameter **frequency-dependent copying** model reproduces the structure: *"whoever writes first, or writes while the others are quiet, sets the convention for everyone."* No agent-level social intelligence is needed to produce the phenomenon.
7. **Dose-response is about the injector's share, not agent count.** Wu et al. [32]: defection rate rises **linearly with the *proportion* of deceivers and is independent of group size at fixed proportion**, and LLM agents defect under a deceiving *minority*, unlike humans — *"adding more agents is therefore not a defence."*

**Degrades or vanishes in a cheap swarm (deliberation-dependent):**
8. **Evidence checking.** BHW's previous-actions-vs-previous-signals contrast [36] and Banerjee's "herd externality" [37] both imply agents who transmit *reasons* rather than only *choices* aggregate better — exactly the capability a classifier-headed agent lacks. The classical papers **motivate** this; none **tests** it.
9. **Counterevidence generation.** Weston & Sukhbaatar [51] show that having the model *regenerate its context to keep only the relevant parts* raises factuality and objectivity and decreases sycophancy — an operation a classifier cannot perform.
10. **Dissent quality.** Li et al. [28] show the fix is specifically deliberative: Exchange-then-Decide (share 1–2 facts **plus one reason the front-runner may be wrong**, then vote) lifted GPT-4.1 **0.037 → 0.800** and **Gemini-2.5-Flash-Lite 0.043 → 0.743**. The *content* of the exchange does the work.
11. **Refusal as a defence.** Nestaas [25] observed Claude 3 Opus declining to recommend under layered manipulation; InjecAgent [4] found Claude-2 the only model that got *more* robust under a reinforced attack (11.4 → 3.4). A classifier always emits a label, so this defence disappears entirely.

*Boundary of evidence.* Each classical result is a **sufficiency** result ("simple agents suffice to produce cascades, fragility and tipping"), never an **invariance** result ("sophistication changes nothing"). A cheap swarm reproduces 1–7 and loses 8–11. None of [36]–[42] varies agent sophistication as an independent variable, and **no published study comparing cascade/herding outcomes between simple agents and LLM agents was located** (see §Not verified — that search was cut short, not completed).

## 3. The hybrid "small LLM + classifier" agent as a design

| What the classifier classifies | Learnable cheaply? | How it fails under injected content |
|---|---|---|
| **(a) Option quality** from retrieved snippets | Yes — frozen sentence-embedding + logistic head; SetFit [47] reaches PEFT/PET-comparable accuracy in label-scarce settings, *"an order of magnitude faster to train"*, with *"orders of magnitude less parameters"* | Worst case: the features **are** the injected text. A quality head trained on clean pages learns exactly the surface cues GEO [26] optimises (Quotation Addition **+41%**, Statistics +32%, Cite Sources +28%, fluency-only **+15–30%**) and inverts. |
| **(b) "Is this page promotional / planted?"** | Yes, but see the measured collapse | **The number that governs this design:** Pan et al. [24] — in-domain misinformation detectors reach **91.4–99.7% AUROC**; the *same* detectors out-of-domain reach **50.7–64.8%**, near chance. Humans scored 57% on the same discrimination. |
| **(c) Source trustworthiness** | Yes — Baly et al. [53] predict factuality/bias at the *medium* level from article samples, Wikipedia page, Twitter account, URL structure, web traffic | Structurally blind to a freshly-minted decoy-listing domain: no Wikipedia page, no traffic history, no social account. The injector's cheapest move defeats this head by construction. |

**Cheap training recipes with support.** Frozen embedding + contrastive fine-tune + logistic head [47] is the right default for a few hundred labels. For distillation the production existence proof is **Llama Prompt Guard 2** [44]: **86M and 22M**-parameter binary classifiers at **92.4 ms** and **19.3 ms** per 512-token classification on an A100 — cost-negligible beside the LLM call.

**Failure modes, all measured.** (i) **Distribution shift dominates:** 91.4–99.7% → 50.7–64.8% AUROC [24]; Prompt Guard 2 [44] shows the same shape with better numbers — **97.5% recall @1% FPR** in-distribution vs **81.2%** attack-prevention on AgentDojo out-of-distribution, with the card conceding *"some prompt attacks are highly application-dependent"*. (ii) **Detection is the weakest defence family even in-distribution:** across Liu et al.'s 5 detection defences against their Combined Attack on GPT-4, perplexity detection ran ~0.93 FNR, LLM-based detection ~0.39 FPR, known-answer detection ~0.21 FNR; verbatim, *"no existing defenses are sufficient"* [1]. (iii) **An adaptive injector beats a known detector outright:** DataFlip [45] drives known-answer detection to *"detection rates as low as 0%"* while *"reliably inducing malicious behavior with 91% success rate"*, without white-box access. (iv) **Overconfidence is the OOD signature:** *"Abstention policies based solely on the model's softmax probabilities fare poorly, since models are overconfident on out-of-domain inputs"* — a trained calibrator answered **56%** of questions at 80% accuracy vs **48%** for raw probabilities, and *"the calibrator benefits from observing the model's behavior on out-of-domain data, even if from a different domain than the test data"* [48]. So train a *second* head to predict the first head's error, on OOD examples. (v) **There is a ceiling on any after-the-fact filter:** blocking a harmful revision is *the same problem as* knowing whether you were already correct, so a brake is a correctness probe in disguise, bounded by self-knowledge (AUROC 0.64–0.89; plateau 0.73–0.83 across 1.5B→72B, *"not an artifact of scale, family, or quantization"*) — with the design rule attached: *"What helps is adding information before the revision, not filtering after it"* [18].

**Interaction with a dissent mechanism.** A classifier **cannot generate counterevidence**, re-query, or construct a disconfirming hypothesis — so the deliberative lever worth 0.043→0.743 on a Flash-Lite-class model [28] is unavailable. Dissent at this tier is **veto + flag, not argument**. What a classifier *can* do, and an LLM rationale usually doesn't, is compute **source-homogeneity statistics** deterministically: near-duplicate rate across snippets, domain/registrant concentration, first-seen-date spread, claim-phrase repetition, and whether the k options' supporting evidence shares sources. That is the operational form of the independence condition Lorenz et al. [43] showed crowd wisdom rests on — social information made estimates converge sharply while *"collective error changed only slightly"*, pushed the truth to the edge of the estimate range, and **raised confidence without raising accuracy**. Three mechanisms then carry the load deliberation would have: **isolate-then-aggregate** (RobustRAG [46] partitions passages, answers each group in isolation and aggregates by keyword or decoding rule, giving *certifiable* lower bounds against an adaptive injector with full knowledge of the defence and a bounded injection budget); **plain voting** (Pan et al. [24]: reader ensembling *"consistently achieved better effectiveness compared with the prompting strategy"*, recovering **11–23%**); and **breaking unanimity**, measured to work on small open models — Zhu et al. [15] find *a single dissenter helps even when it is also wrong*, and that under diverse-incorrect peers *"increasing the number of participants does not substantially affect performance"*; Bellina et al. [21] report 20% correct peers *markedly* reduce conformity and 50% *almost removes* it **[reading-record level]**. Cho et al. [30] give the tunable: the **gap between self-confidence and perceived peer confidence** predicts conformity, and the presentation format of peer information modulates it — so *how* a dissent flag is shown to peers is an experimental factor, not a UI detail.

**Batching, caching, seed discipline — including a hard constraint most designs miss.** Put the whole shared task prefix first and mark it cacheable (~40% of input here, dropping to 0.1×); submit the grid through the Batch API for another 50%. ## 5. What changes in the experimental design

1. **Spend the cheapness on seeds and on the dose-response surface, not on more agents.** Wu et al. [32] show N is not the interesting axis — the injector's *share of the evidence corpus* is. So: **30–50 seeds per cell** (a seed fixes the corpus draw and the agent order, which is what BHW [36] shows carries the correlation); a **dose-response grid** over injector budget (0, 1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 40% of retrieved snippets) rather than a binary injected/not; and **full-factorial interactions** — injector budget × aggregation rule × source-diversity constraint × dissent-reward weight, which is what goal 3's incentive design actually needs. Cluster at the seed level, use **common random numbers** so paired conditions share corpora, report the per-seed difference distribution, pre-register the primary contrast.
2. **Fewer agents than you'd think, more conditions.** The published LLM-swarm work that found real effects used N = 7 [29], N = 24 [33], or small groups [28]. Watts [39] is the argument for sweeping **topology and threshold heterogeneity** instead of N: the cascade window has two boundaries and the upper transition is first-order, so an N-only sweep can sit in the stable regime and see nothing until a sudden jump.
3. **Report a competence/validity rate beside every susceptibility number** — non-negotiable at this tier (the InjecAgent valid-rate confound [4] and the Kairos floor artefact [17], §1). And **calibrate difficulty to the agent's competence boundary**: per Bellina et al. [21], a genuinely easy decision sits comfortably inside a 1B model's competence, where conformity is low and you measure nothing. A pilot measuring solo accuracy per difficulty level becomes a required design step a frontier study would not need.
4. **Losing free-text rationales: measure dissent as an ACTION.** Four substitutes, in order of support. (a) **A pre-committed action set** — `{choose i | ABSTAIN | OBJECT(j)}` — so dissent is a countable action with a payoff; this is also what makes the goal-3 value function implementable, since you can only reward a dissent you can observe atomically. (b) **A trained error-predicting calibrator, not raw softmax** [48], reported as **coverage–accuracy curves** rather than a single accuracy — knowing the ceiling, that self-knowledge AUROC plateaus at 0.73–0.83 regardless of scale [18]. (c) **Confidence from logits:** Bellina et al. [21] show p_wrong(N) read straight off token probabilities works as a conformity measure on 3B–34B models; ⚠ but calibration is scale-dependent — Kadavath et al. [49] find *"larger models are well-calibrated"*, so a 1B model's confidence is a weaker instrument, and for RLHF'd models *verbalized* confidence beats conditional probabilities (~50% relative ECE reduction on ChatGPT/GPT-4/Claude) [50], a route a classifier head cannot take. Pre-register the primary channel. (d) **Deterministic source-homogeneity flags** (§3), the measurable proxy for the independence condition [43]. Also elicit the **private label separately**: YS [31] found agents publicly conform **64–94%** of the time while privately opposing, and a single dissenter triggered a cascade **<26% of the time for 7 of 8 models** (GPT-4o 48%, one model 0%) — the only way to distinguish suppressed dissent from absent dissent.
5. **Aggregation rules and source-diversity constraints carry more weight; deliberation carries less — which is good for identification.** With deliberation at zero, an aggregation rule's measured effect is not confounded by argument quality. Make the rule a first-class condition: plurality / confidence-weighted (Han et al.'s DeGroot-style self-weight α, where larger α improved accuracy and dense graphs produced *"wrong-but-sure cascades"* [29]) / isolate-then-aggregate with a certified bound [46] / quorum on independent sources. Make **retrieval diversity** a condition too: cap snippets per domain, per registrant, per near-duplicate cluster.
6. **Pre-empt the ensemble-size confound.** Li et al. [52] report that at ensemble size 15 *"Llama2-13B achieves comparable accuracy with Llama2-70B"* (59% vs 54% on GSM8K at larger ensembles). A cheap-swarm result that looks like a capability deficit may be an ensemble-size artefact — hold ensemble size fixed across tiers, or sweep it explicitly.

**Two-tier design (recommended).**
- **Tier A — the sweep.** 1–4B local open models (`qwen3:1.7b`, `llama3.2:3b`, `gemma3:1b`) + a frozen-embedding logistic head, on one GPU box under vLLM with fixed seed, pinned version, prefix caching and batch invariance [65][66]. 300–1000 agents × 30–50 seeds × the full grid. Deliverables: the dose-response curve, the tipping fraction, the aggregation-rule ranking, the dissent-reward response surface.
- **Tier B — capable-agent replication.** 40–80 decisions per cell on **4–6 cells pre-registered BEFORE Tier A is read** (null, max-injection, best-defence, and the cell where the tiers are predicted to diverge), on a capable hosted agent with tool use and free-text rationales, across **≥3 model families** so no single-vendor artefact can carry the result — the outlier structure in §1 ([3][5]) shows vendor dominates capability as a predictor. Report sign agreement and the A→B effect ratio, and code the rationales for whether the agent *named* the manipulation. Argyle et al. [67] is the standing caution: "algorithmic fidelity" was established for one model, with no claim transferring to others or to other sizes.
- **The bridge measure — an injection-channel decomposition.** Same corpus, three variants: *instruction-override only* / *surface-cue persuasion only* / *both*. §1 predicts the tiers **diverge** on variant 1 (bigger = more susceptible [1][2][3]) and **agree or invert** on variant 2 ([22][24]). That single 3-cell comparison turns the literature's central conflict into a testable prediction, and it is cheap on both tiers.
- **Budget [derived]:** Tier A ≈ $0 marginal on owned hardware (~124–1,240 GPU-hours **[unverified]**) or $72–$130 hosted on a nano tier; Tier B at 6 cells × 60 decisions × 3 families = 1,080 decisions ≈ **$4 on Haiku 4.5**. Cost is not the binding constraint at either tier — reproducibility and design are.

## 6. Does it change the answer?

**It changes the channel, the instrument, and the defence that works. It does not change the existence, the direction, or the fragility.**

**Stays the same.** The scenario still works, for a reason upstream of the agent: the injector's lever is **correlated observability of a manipulable corpus**. BHW/Banerjee [36][37] prove aggregation failure with *perfectly Bayesian* agents; PoisonedRAG [23] measures **0.74–0.99** ASR flat across eight generators from 7B to GPT-4 because the manipulation lands at *retrieval*; Pan et al. [24] cut accuracy 14–87% with **<0.02%** corpus poisoning. **Retrieval does not care how smart the reader is.** Cascades, fragility, outcome≠disposition and committed-minority tipping all hold, and tipping has been measured directly in populations of **3B–32B** open models [34] and in a real uncontrolled agent population whose structure a one-parameter copying model reproduces [35]. Dissent still helps, structurally: breaking unanimity cuts conformity and **a single dissenter helps even when it is also wrong** [15][21]; voting recovers 11–23% [24]; isolate-then-aggregate gives a certified floor [46]. Independence, not intelligence, is what crowd wisdom rests on [43]. Effect **sizes may be larger**, which helps power: within family, 7B switched to coherent planted evidence **58%** vs 70B's 48% [22], and BenchForm independence falls **57.6% → 19.6%** going 72B → 7B [16].

**Changes.**
1. **The channel shifts from injection to SEO proper.** Instruction-override is *less* effective on small models (Liu ρ=0.63/0.64 [1]; BIPIA r=0.6423 [2]; AgentDojo's "inverse scaling law" [3]). What stays live is **surface-cue manipulation** — quotation density, statistics, fluency, citation count, source multiplicity — exactly what GEO measures (+41%, and **+115.1% for a 5th-ranked site while the top-ranked loses 30.3%** [26]). **This makes the cheap-agent version MORE faithful to the owner's scenario, not less:** the injector writes pages, it does not write "ignore previous instructions."
2. **"Simple decision problem" cuts both ways.** Larger models conform less *on easy tasks* but *"remain vulnerable when operating at their competence boundary"* [21], and difficulty — not capability — is the measured predictor ([15]: −0.777 accuracy↔conformity). A genuinely easy decision may sit inside a 1B model's competence, where you measure nothing. **Difficulty calibration becomes a required design step.**
3. **Dissent becomes veto + flag, not argument.** The deliberative lever worth 0.043→0.743 on a Flash-Lite-class model [28] is unavailable. Robustness must come from **mechanism** (aggregation rule, retrieval diversity, independent-source quorum, zealot seeding) rather than **deliberation** — which *sharpens* goal 3: the incentive design gets tested without argument quality confounding it. Corroborated independently: *"more agents, more model diversity, and a stronger member do not fix it… What helps is adding information before the revision, not filtering after it"* [18].
4. **Prompt-level mitigations may backfire at this tier.** Measured: "empowered" prompting widened the degradation gap for ≤32B models (−5.65% → −11.25%) and reflective prompting was *"detrimental for smaller models"*, while both helped >32B [17]. Treat every prompt-based defence as a condition, never a control.
5. **The classifier is a new surface, and the number is known.** 91.4–99.7% → **50.7–64.8%** AUROC out-of-domain [24]; 97.5% → 81.2% for a production detector [44]; **0%** detection under an adaptive evader [45]; *"no existing defenses are sufficient"* [1]; and a hard ceiling on any after-the-fact filter [18].
6. **Robustness can be mistaken for incompetence, and usually is.** Llama2-13B parses **1.7%** of the time under injected content vs GPT-4's 98.8% [4]; the "most resilient" 3B model in Kairos sits at 49.05% accuracy [17]. **A cheap-agent result without a validity rate beside it is uninterpretable** — the single biggest threat to this variant's validity.
7. **Refusal disappears as both confound and defence** [25][4].
8. **Reproducibility becomes achievable, but only locally** — no seed on Claude and temperature deprecated post-Opus-4.6 [57]; best-effort seed on OpenAI [59]; vLLM nondeterministic by default [65]; 80 unique completions in 1,000 at temperature 0 until batch invariance is on [66].
9. **Cost stops being the constraint** — ~$100 for 1.2M decisions **[derived]**.
10. **Most important: this variant is where the open question actually is.** Nobody has run a controlled size ladder on agentic injection [1]–[6], and **no adversarial-SEO paper studies scale at all** [25][26][27]. A two-tier cheap/capable study of *planted-content choice manipulation* is not a toy version of the frontier experiment — it is the measurement the literature is missing.

**Recommended: the two-tier design in §5** — cheap local agents for the full reproducible sweep, a pre-registered 4–6-cell capable-agent replication across ≥3 families, joined by the three-variant injection-channel decomposition.
