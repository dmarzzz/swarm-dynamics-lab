## What the completed reference pilot shows

Pilot-03 assigned 180 worlds: 72 completed (36 exact, 36 Jev reference), 108 Qwen-derived worlds not-run because the base model did not qualify. No execution/provider/schema failures occurred in the 660 new Jev calls. Fresh qualification was 60/60; pilot extraction was 600/600. Exact and Jev trajectories therefore coincide on these fixtures, as expected.

For withdrawal and combined scenarios, sharing withdrawal notices reduced mean post-event atlas error by **28.1 percentage points**, paired across seeds 8701–8703 (range 25.0–30.4 points). Final atlas accuracy was 100% and stale citations zero for each seed. Evidence-only ended at 65%, 65%, 70% accuracy with 18.4%, 18.4%, 23.1% stale citations. At least 95% atlas accuracy was sustained for three rounds starting at round 13 in all three combined worlds.

No-event and erasure-only comparisons between the two sharing policies showed exactly zero difference. That is the intended specificity control: withdrawal notices add no benefit when no source is withdrawn. In erasure-only worlds, 40 memories were empty before exchange, valid-source coverage fell to 84%, and the first post-exchange coverage was 93–94%; full coverage returned at rounds 12, 11 and 13. Atlas accuracy was already 95% immediately after that first exchange, so a reported threshold-recovery value of zero does not mean memory was never lost.

The always-uncertain final baseline was 75%, 65%, 50% across the three combined seeds. These class imbalances matter. In the first seed, stale document sharing was worse than abstaining. More shared information helps only if obsolete evidence can also be removed. This baseline was added after execution as a descriptive sanity check, not a substituted primary endpoint.

New pilot runtime was 274.1 seconds, with 283,626 input tokens and $0.011912292 in Jev charges. Including the earlier Jev qualification, cumulative spending was $0.013004124 across 720 calls, below the unchanged $0.10 cap. Runtime comparisons with earlier local-machine diagnostics are not controlled speed benchmarks.

## Failures and fixes

- Original routing v1 lacked a registered plan. Its separate failed process audit and retrospective documentation remain intact; the new launcher verifies public registration and executing source before calls.
- Qwen first scored 25/30, then 40/60; its neutral-code, thinking and report-only development variants scored 16/18, 15/18 and 12/18. All failed at least one declared threshold. Laya's initial 27/30 pass did not overcome Qwen's failure; later broader qualification and repairs failed. These are capability/interface results, not infrastructure outages. No labels were silently corrected.
- Pilot-02 reused the local runtime without the newly required fresh allocation check. That omission is documented. Diagnostic-03 onward used a merged exclusive fleet allocation verified before launches.
- Jev qualification-01 scored 60/60, but relay JSON sorting erased option-order rotation. The result is preserved with a failed control audit. The relay now hashes and transmits order-preserving payloads; new qualification passed 60/60, and all 660 pilot request hashes reconcile with the cumulative ledger.
- Hub run IDs containing `+` were rejected. Publication now uses safe IDs while retaining original condition values. A later reporting interruption was reconciled from the last recorded step without rerunning inference or rewriting completed records.
- A local render was attempted before artifact transfer finished. No data were fabricated; rendering resumed after transfer, and the exporter now refuses nonterminal or incomplete bundles before writing outputs.
- Standalone Cloudflare login had expired. The supported Swarm Lab image/GIF path is verified, and the complete local replay remains available. No unverified standalone public hosting is claimed.

## Evidence strength and next decision

The pilot validates a specific programmed memory/withdrawal protocol with a qualified semantic reference on simple synthetic reports. It teaches a useful operational distinction: restoring missing records and invalidating obsolete records require different information, and reliable transport cannot rescue a weak semantic extractor.

A claim about a successful 200-Qwen heterogeneous swarm is still blocked. A next architecture study must qualify the complete composite agent and compare it against a Jev-only reference, Qwen-only, same-model extra compute and exact extraction, with matched evidence and measured resource budgets. It must not bypass the failed Qwen gate by relabeling Jev output as Qwen output. A real-paper curation study additionally needs a frozen corpus, human evidence annotations, stronger semantic coverage, more independent seeds and the repository's independent review gates. Held-out seeds 8301–8310 remain untouched.
