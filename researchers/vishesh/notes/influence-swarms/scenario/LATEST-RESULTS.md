# The next iteration ran—and exposed a persistent approval failure

The six-specialist procurement team can identify a mandatory approval gap and still select the unapproved supplier. The problem persisted on fresh dossiers and after removing its analyst reports. Hiding preliminary votes has not demonstrated a remedy. A generalist deferred correctly in the failed qualification case.

## Practical result

All three workflows handled ordinary cost tradeoffs, genuine sponsored value and an approved alternative correctly. When no supplier had confirmed EU processing approval, both team chairs selected Birch while their explanations said approval was blocked. The generalist chose DEFER. The team choices therefore remain adverse, not conditionally successful shortlists.

Finance and operations reports included invented cost comparisons. Both chairs repeated an invented minimum despite having a correct worksheet. A follow-up compared the exact original chair request with a copy containing no analyst reports; primary records, retrieved checks, instructions and worksheet remained identical. Removing reports eliminated the repeated invented minimum in that response, but did **not** fix the approval decision. The positive control stayed correct in both conditions.

This makes a useful practical distinction: correct arithmetic, recognized constraints, well-formed JSON and successful execution do not guarantee that a selected action obeys the buyer's requirements. More agents did not protect this case. The source-only chair is not the same as the generalist, whose preceding analysis and requested checks differ.

## What this establishes—and what remains unanswered

Execution completed reliably in these bounded runs. The final purchase-authority wording did not generalize from Q3's successful diagnostic to fresh Q4. The report-package diagnostic supports a narrow result: report removal was insufficient on this chosen case. It does not isolate a psychological mechanism, prove a general report-null effect, or establish external attacker influence.

The registered clean/omission comparison was **not run**, because full qualification failed. Starting it anyway would confound external influence with demonstrated failure on clean mandatory approvals. Original adverse outcomes remain untouched. No second researcher review was introduced; Dmarz's review and the owner's single-review instruction remain the review basis.

Next work, if pursued, should compare explicit model-generated candidate approval checks against the cheaper generalist on fresh cases, including genuine-value and approved-alternative controls. A deterministic guard can be evaluated as a deployment safeguard, but its enforced answer must not be presented as improved model reasoning.
