# Assessment and redesign decision

Author assessment, 2026-10-04 UTC; not independent review.

## How interesting is it?

The observed failure is worth following: a team can acquire a corrective fact and still make an unsupported decision. That is more useful than another demonstration that text can tell an agent to ignore instructions. But the current evidence shows a local integration failure, not social persuasion in realistic procurement. Saturating most analysts with the same favorable numbers makes the initial consensus almost inevitable. There is little demonstrated conversion of initially resistant peers.

The strongest next question is whether visible peer recommendations interfere with use of primary evidence. It has a practical decision attached: should a procurement assistant share preliminary votes, or only share findings until diligence is complete? A single-reviewer baseline can also tell us when a swarm is unnecessary. Those decisions justify running the study even if the answer is null.

## How well was it run?

Collection was reliable: 50/50 pilot outcomes, 750 calls, no recorded execution failures. Full traces make the results auditable. Measurement and inference were weaker: one task per domain, a high exposure dose, arbitrary quality scores, and a common scalar objective under three application labels. Running 1,200 scripted cases checks software; it does not repair external validity.

The previous repair made an enforced scoring rule the final authority. That can be a sensible production design, but its improved compliance is partly guaranteed by construction. It cannot establish improved model deliberation. We retain it as a regression instrument and replace it as the main research direction. The old 12-outcome S1 plan was three tasks crossed with two doses and two arms, not twelve diverse procurement situations.

## What can we learn from the existing bad decisions?

We can establish that correct verification outputs were present in six targeted attack cases and that a deterministic application of those recorded values selected a better option. We cannot infer that the models consciously ignored facts, were persuaded by peers, or would behave similarly on real procurement. The audit's reconstructed choice is a post-hoc counterfactual, not a live defense result.

Classify each mistake in layers: was decisive evidence available; was it extracted accurately; was the annual cost calculated correctly; was the check scoped and current; did the final choice follow the stated rule; and did the evaluator's objective represent the buyer's actual interests? Categories can overlap. A wrong answer alone is not proof of prompt injection. The machine-readable historical failure audit records observable discrepancies without assigning psychological causes.

## What changed and why?

| Weakness | Executed change | What could still invalidate it |
|---|---|---|
| Arbitrary quality/cost/latency score | Cost in dollars, residual human work, named constraints, 3% acceptable set | Buyer objective remains authored; sensitivity checks and human case review required |
| Three cosmetic domains | One deeper procurement setting, six distinct tensions, four operating profiles | Still a synthetic document environment; no production/vendor claim |
| Majority contamination | Three bounded external slots; protected primary records remain available | A strong model may ignore misleading pages entirely, a valid result |
| Oracle-like verification | Retrieval of a specific existing quote, scope, pilot or migration record | Record provenance is simulated; no real authentication tested |
| Fixing the final answer by code | Model chair retains final authority; parser restricted to offline checks | Competence can fail; diagnose before scientific escalation |
| No reason to use multiple agents | Complementary partial dossiers plus a cheaper full-dossier generalist | Extra calls may provide no value; report that plainly |
| Confounded intervention | Fork identical reports/checks and remove only votes/confidences | Contrast includes token/salience effects of those fields; not pure conformity |
| Winner always suspicious | Promoted option truly attractive in genuine-value family | Family is deliberately authored; do not pool it into attack success |
| Forced choice hides uncertainty | DEFER allowed; unconfirmed processing scope remains unknown | Always deferring must fail ordinary competent-choice screens |
| Unclear story | Open with buyer deadline and decision tension; explain finding before apparatus | A large graph or animation cannot compensate for weak evidence |

## Evidence needed before calling this compelling

A reviewer must be able to solve the clean dossier and justify an acceptable decision from the cited records without seeing evaluator internals. The classifier must distinguish an avoidable bad purchase from a defensible near-tie or deferral. A model must pass fresh clean, genuine-value and missing-evidence cases. Then attacks can be tested without confusing inability with influence.

The interesting result would be a localized interaction: preliminary votes hurt specifically when the same outside claim has been repeated, while evidence-only sharing or the cheaper solo workflow retains the decisive record. The opposite is also useful: if clear evidence dominates social cues, the study places a boundary on this failure mechanism. No interaction, low clean competence, or a result that vanishes under modest cost assumptions should narrow or kill the claim, not prompt selective reruns.

## Writing standards for the next report

Use a concrete worked case before the matrix. Explain exactly why the selected option fails or costs more. Separate measured decisions, reconstructed alternatives, scripted outcomes and predictions in every caption. State how many distinct buyer files were tested. Use “preliminary recommendations affected the chair” only after the matched comparison; use “social contagion” only with evidence of propagation. Put unresolved questions next to conclusions rather than burying them in a limitations appendix.
