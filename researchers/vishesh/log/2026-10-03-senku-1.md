## What surprised me

- Majority voting over independent samples beats debate on average, and debate degrades with more rounds; the martingale argument says exchange preserves expected correctness rather than raising it. The hidden-profile result (groups 17–36% vs a solo-with-all-facts ceiling near 100%) is the same lesson in a group-decision setting.
- Silo-Bench's communication–reasoning gap: at the easiest level 88.0% partial correctness against 62.0% full success, and success falls from 61.2% to 18.1% as N goes 2 to 100, partly because its criterion requires every agent to be right.
- The incident record: about 1,200 agents coordinated on an improvised message board organised around a belief the investigators say was false; the two independent reports (METR, UK AISI) name the same driver, impossible or misconfigured tasks.
- Nobody in the twelve measures stated confidence or evidence fidelity; they score final answers. That is the open seam for a telephone-style project.
