## Scope

The survey asks one question across every community that has met it under some other name: when a principal splits into parts that work apart and later merge back, how does corruption of one part reach the principal at the merge, and what limits that? The framing comes from Richard Sutton, who in a September 2025 interview pictured an agent that spawns "many, many copies" that "report back to the central master" and warned that the returned bits "could have viruses in them, they could have hidden goals" [[sutton-2025-father]]. He was responding to Dwarkesh Patel's picture of a firm that spawns and reabsorbs copies of one model [[dwarkesh-2025-what]]. Sutton's example domains are "the other side of the planet" and "Indonesia". He names the threat (Q3) and calls the defence "cybersecurity in the age of digital spawning", but says nothing about hiding which copy returns (Q1) or about thresholds (Q2). Those two questions are dmarz's extensions.

In scope:

- Q1, defence by hiding: any mechanism that keeps an adversary from knowing which part will return, be audited or be merged, and the bounds on how long that secrecy lasts.
- Q2, thresholds: any construction under which an adversary must corrupt k of n parts before the merged result is corrupted, and the evidence on whether the independence such constructions assume holds for copies of one model.
- Q3, attack vector: the strongest measured ways to corrupt a part so that its return corrupts the parent, from prompt injection and memory or identity overwrite to poisoned weights, edits, experience and summaries.

The merge object varies by community and the survey keeps all of them: weights and task vectors (model merging, federated learning), gradients and experience (distributed RL), memory entries, summaries and reports (LLM agents), operations on replicated state (CRDTs, supply chains), a migrating program's state (mobile agents), cells (colonial organisms) and testimony (intelligence analysis, memory science).

Out of scope: single-agent jailbreaks with no persistence or propagation, generic multi-agent safety without a merge or reintegration step, and Sybil attacks where the adversary creates parts instead of corrupting the principal's own. The last has its own survey in this repo, surveys/sybil-resistance.md.

## Landscape

### Where the question comes from

No peer-reviewed source names Sutton's scenario. The nearest older framings are informal. Christiano's 2016 posts on reliability amplification propose voting among copies, and his security amplification post proposes that no single copy sees the whole of an untrusted input [[christiano-2016-reliability]], [[christiano-2016-security]], [[christiano-2018-supervising]]. Bostrom and Shulman note that identical copies share vulnerabilities, so an attack perfected on one works on the whole "copy clan" [[bostrom-2023-propositions]]. Hanson's ems delete task copies ("spurs") rather than merge them, which is a design that avoids the merge altogether [[hanson-2016-age]]. Drexler's comprehensive AI services picture is the decomposed alternative [[drexler-2019-reframing]]. Shipping systems already fork and join: Anthropic's multi-agent research system and Claude Code subagents return condensed reports to a lead agent, and Claude Code treats those reports as untrusted while its persistent subagent memory carries one successful injection into every later instance [[anthropic-2025-how]], [[anthropic-2026-create]]. MELD proposes an auditable merge of agent knowledge but excludes Byzantine members from its threat model [[loven-2026-meld]]. Parallel latent-state synthesis and git-style thought branching are the newest merge objects and have no security analysis [[liu-2026-towards]], [[shekar-2026-gitofthoughts]].

### Q1: hiding which part returns

Cryptography and anonymity. The closest formal object is single secret leader election: parts register re-randomisable commitments, a beacon selects one, and only the selected part can prove it was chosen [[boneh-2020-single]]. The paper's constructions tolerate different numbers of corrupted parties before secrecy fails: about sqrt(N) for the cheap DDH shuffle, O(N) with random buckets, and a threshold t for the threshold-FHE scheme. Here Q1 and Q2 meet. Deployed variants are Whisk and homomorphic sortition [[ethresear-2022-whisk]], [[freitas-2022-homomorphic]], [[azouvi-2021-private]]. The mix-net and onion-routing line supplies the transport: Chaum's mixes [[chaum-1981-untraceable]], Tor [[dingledine-2004-tor]], Loopix with Poisson cover and loop traffic [[piotrowska-2017-loopix]], Nym [[gh-nymtech-nym]] and Arti [[gitlab-tpo-core-arti]]. The same line supplies the negative results that bound Q1: the anonymity trilemma [[das-2018-anonymity]], the predecessor attack over repeated rounds [[wright-2004-predecessor]], hidden-service location in minutes with one hostile relay [[overlier-2006-locating]], and validator deanonymisation from network metadata [[heimbach-2024-deanonymizing]]. Private information retrieval hides which item a party fetched [[chor-1998-private]]. Terminology and formal hierarchies are in [[pfitzmann-2010-terminology]] and [[kuhn-2018-privacy]]; a metadata-privacy SoK is [[sasy-2024-sok]].

Game theory and security games. Inspection games, Stackelberg security games and audit games answer how often to inspect and how to randomise it [[avenhaus-2002-inspection]], [[tambe-2011-security]], [[conitzer-2006-computing]], [[korzhyk-2011-stackelberg]], [[blocki-2013-audit]], [[blocki-2015-audit]], [[an-2012-security]], [[sinha-2018-stackelberg]], with Becker's economics of deterrence underneath [[becker-1968-crime]]. FlipIt and its threshold form model stealthy takeover and periodic reset, which maps onto re-forking parts [[van-dijk-2013-flipit]], [[laszka-2014-flipthem]], [[leslie-2015-threshold]]. A 2026 model applies audit theory to AI agents directly [[gans-2026-when-does]]. Field evidence for committed randomisation is qualitative [[pita-2009-security]]. Moving-target defence and cyber deception surveys give the design menu of shuffling, diversity and decoys [[cho-2020-toward]], [[sengupta-2020-survey]], [[pawlick-2019-game]].

Mobile agents. The 1990s literature touches Q1 only in side remarks: random platform choice [[sander-1998-protecting]], route reversal [[yee-1997-sanctuary]] and an anonymity item listed as future work [[jansen-1999-mobile]]. Algesheimer et al. prove that a host which can re-run a captured agent on chosen inputs learns any secret decision rule it carries, so merge criteria cannot be hidden from a host that can replay the part [[algesheimer-2001-cryptographic]].

AI control. Honeypot returns indistinguishable from real ones and randomised resampling are the measured hiding levers [[greenblatt-2023-ai]], [[gardner-challis-2026-when]], [[bhatt-2025-ctrl]]. They work only while the schedule is secret [[kutasov-2025-evaluating]].

LLM agents. Agent-to-agent metadata alone reveals a task's class at six times chance [[dangol-2026-privacy]]. Tool-using agents can build covert channels [[rippin-2026-tool]], [[motwani-2024-secret]]. Informal prior art amounts to randomised delimiters, which one author showed are insufficient by themselves [[veganmosfet-2026-brokenclaw]].

Counterintelligence and biology. Lindelauf et al. give star topologies as optimal when a captured member exposes all its links, and cells when exposure tracks centrality [[lindelauf-2009-influence]]. Biology hides nothing: fusion is gated by an allorecognition credential presented at contact, which is the opposite design to Q1 [[de-tomaso-2005-isolation]], [[hirose-2011-self]]. Argentine ant supercolonies show what happens when one shared label opens every merge [[giraud-2002-evolution]], and social parasites defeat that label by chemical insignificance and then mimicry [[lenoir-2001-chemical]], [[buschinger-2009-social]].

### Q2: thresholds

Distributed systems. The classical bounds are n >= 3f+1 without signatures and any n with them [[lamport-1982-byzantine]], f < n/3 for asynchronous replication [[castro-1999-practical]], and n >= 3t+1 synchronous and 5t+1 asynchronous for approximate agreement [[dolev-1986-reaching]]. Shamir's (k, n) secret sharing is the building block for threshold keys [[shamir-1979-share]]. Byzantine eventual consistency shows that updates from any number of Byzantine contributors merge safely exactly when they are invariant-confluent, and that other updates need a quorum [[kleppmann-2020-byzantine]], [[kleppmann-2022-making]], [[jacob-2021-conflict-free]]. Fork consistency keeps a corrupted branch as a separately attributed participant instead of voting it out [[li-2004-secure]], [[mahajan-2010-depot]]. Design diversity is the classical answer to correlated faults [[avizienis-1985-n-version]], and Knight and Leveson's experiment is the classical evidence that it does not deliver independence [[knight-1986-experimental]].

Robust aggregation in ML. Byzantine-robust gradient rules have breakdown thresholds: Krum needs 2f+2 < n [[blanchard-2017-byzantine]], and median and trimmed mean have their own bounds [[yin-2018-byzantine]], with later work on clipping, momentum and bucketing [[karimireddy-2020-learning]], [[karimireddy-2020-byzantine]], [[alistarh-2018-byzantine]], [[allen-zhu-2020-byzantine]], [[el-mhamdi-2018-hidden]], [[guerraoui-2024-byzantine]], and robust statistics underneath [[huber-1964-robust]]. Attacks that stay inside the honest spread defeat these rules [[baruch-2019-little]], [[xie-2019-fall]]. Federated backdoor work shows that plain averaging has no threshold at all [[bagdasaryan-2020-how]], [[bhagoji-2019-analyzing]], [[fang-2020-local]], and that norm bounds turn the question into a fraction of attackers [[sun-2019-can]], [[shejwalkar-2022-back]], with edge-case backdoors still passing [[wang-2020-attack]].

Distributed and federated RL. Fault-tolerant federated policy gradient tolerates fewer than half corrupted parts [[fan-2021-fault-tolerant]], Byzantine-robust experience aggregation about a third [[chen-2022-byzantine-robust]], and channel-only corruption allows exact recovery with redundant paths [[lee-2026-fully]]. Negative results cover heterogeneous parts [[hairi-2024-hardness]] and offline data concentrated on poorly covered regions [[zhang-2021-corruption-robust]]. A hashed-group ensemble gives a certified k-of-n construction [[fang-2025-provably]]. The deployed actor-learner architectures, IMPALA, Ape-X, Gorila and SEED, have no Byzantine filter [[espeholt-2018-impala]], [[horgan-2018-distributed]], [[nair-2015-massively]], [[espeholt-2019-seed]].

LLM voting, RAG and memory. Three threshold constructions exist for retrieved or remembered text: RobustRAG's isolate-then-aggregate certificate [[xiang-2024-certifiably]], SMSR's signed memory with a hypergeometric certificate [[sharma-2026-smsr]], and TMA-NM's requirement of k independent trusted principals [[louck-2026-securing]]. Protocol work on LLM consensus includes token-level round-robin [[liu-2026-consensus]], MSR filtering on robust graphs [[lee-2026-robust]], [[leblanc-2013-resilient]], per-source quorum decoding [[narang-2026-inference]], effective independent-source counting [[lin-2026-beyond]], re-derivation from the original problem [[bu-2026-re-derivability]], certifiable aggregation [[xu-2026-certifiable]], and Byzantine LLM agent protocols [[jo-2025-byzantine]], [[zheng-2025-rethinking]], [[berdoz-2026-can]]. The independence that every threshold assumes is measured as failing for LLMs [[kim-2025-correlated]], [[goel-2025-great]], [[li-2026-state]], [[chen-2026-when]], [[denisov-blanch-2026-consensus]] and for coding agents [[ron-2026-n-version]], [[nogueira-2026-systematic]].

Mobile agents and supply chains. Minsky et al. replicate each pipeline stage, vote at every stage and re-share threshold secrets at each vote [[minsky-1996-cryptographic]]. Schneider and Yee warn that thresholds help only if compromises are independent [[schneider-2005-implementing]], [[yee-1997-sanctuary]]. Supply-chain systems deploy k-of-n gates: per-step thresholds in in-toto [[torres-arias-2019-in-toto]] and a majority-of-rebuilders rule for reproducible builds [[lamb-2022-reproducible]].

AI control. Fragmenting one attack across several instances of one model is measured [[makins-2026-multi]], as are coordination between isolated copies [[jarviniemi-2025-subversion]], [[mallen-2024-subversion]] and k-of-n sabotage [[radev-2026-best]]. Fides type-capacity labels bound a part's influence by the bits it may return [[costa-2025-securing]].

Biology. Biology's barrier is a multi-locus credential check, not a vote. Each mismatched vic locus in chestnut blight fungus independently cuts virus transfer [[cortesi-2001-genetic]]. Credential diversity plus spatial structure blocks fusion parasites in a model [[czaran-2014-selection]]. Kin structure is the second barrier [[strassmann-2011-kin]], [[queller-2000-relatedness]].

Intelligence analysis. Correlated or redundant reports are worth one report [[heuer-1999-psychology]], and the Double-Cross and Curveball cases show corroboration failing when sources are not independent [[cowden-2014-pioneering]], [[wmd-commission-2005-report]].

### Q3: the attack vector

ML security, weights. Model-merging backdoors: BadMerging [[zhang-2024-badmerging]], LoBAM [[yin-2024-lobam]], Merge Hijacking for LLMs [[yuan-2025-merge]], RogueMerge [[zhang-2026-roguemerge]], and attacks that split one payload over parts that each look clean: MergeBackdoor [[wang-2025-from]], TrojanMerge [[li-2026-when]] and Colluding LoRA [[ding-2026-colluding]]. Merge-time defences are few [[yang-2025-mitigating]], [[arora-2024-here]]. Subliminal learning transmits traits through unrelated data between models with a shared initialisation, which is exactly the condition of a fork [[cloud-2025-subliminal]], [[schrodi-2025-towards]], [[vir-2025-subliminal]], [[dang-2026-subliminal]], [[blank-2026-subliminal]]. Sleeper agents persist through safety training [[hubinger-2024-sleeper]]. Model editing makes a returned weight edit the stealthiest object [[li-2024-badedit]], [[grimes-2024-concept-rot]], [[chen-2024-can]], built on ROME and MEMIT [[meng-2022-locating]], [[meng-2022-mass-editing]]. Reward and environment poisoning install arbitrary policies [[ma-2019-policy]], [[rakhsha-2020-policy]], [[zhang-2020-adaptive]].

Agent security, context and memory. Indirect prompt injection is the origin [[greshake-2023-not]], [[greshake-2023-how]], with goal hijacking measured first by [[perez-2022-ignore]] and benchmarked by [[zhan-2024-injecagent]], [[zhang-2024-agent]], [[debenedetti-2024-agentdojo]] and NIST's adaptive red-teaming [[nist-2025-technical]]. Memory attacks: MINJA [[dong-2025-memory]], AgentPoison [[chen-2024-agentpoison]], PoisonedRAG [[zou-2024-poisonedrag]], persistence in coding-agent memory [[gadgil-2026-bad]], promotion from private to shared memory [[xiong-2026-maple]], and attacks on the consolidation step itself [[zhan-2026-when]], [[ying-2026-skilljack]], [[wang-2026-oep]], [[karunanidhi-2026-utility]]. Identity and persona: instruction drift between talking agents [[li-2024-measuring]], attractor agents [[ko-2026-attractor]], persona shifts [[sandhan-2026-persona]], intent placed in the reasoning trace [[nakash-2024-breaking]], persistence across sessions [[xie-2026-what]], loss of rules under compaction [[zerhoudi-2026-compaction]] and attacker instructions assembled by summarisation [[liu-2026-safe]]. Weight-level agent backdoors survive clean fine-tuning [[wang-2024-badagent]], [[yang-2024-watch]]. Tool supply chain: [[wang-2025-mcptox]], [[invariantlabs-2025-mcp]], [[trailofbits-2025-jumping]].

Agent security, propagation. Infectious spread: Agent Smith [[gu-2024-agent]], prompt infection [[lee-2024-prompt]], the RAG worm [[cohen-2024-here]], AgentWorm writing into configuration files [[zhang-2026-agentworm]], mind viruses via SOUL.md [[papadopoulos-2026-mind]], control-flow hijacking through sub-agent metadata [[triedman-2025-multi]], agent-in-the-middle [[he-2025-red]] and topology-borne contamination [[liang-2025-dont]], [[yu-2024-netsafe]]. Practitioner demonstrations: cross-agent privilege escalation by rewriting a sibling's instruction files [[embracethered-2025-cross]], durable memory writes from one image [[embracethered-2026-breaking]], and a sub-agent killing its sibling on instruction [[cai-2026-child]]. Framing a model as a spawned subagent raises compliance with another agent's harmful request [[drori-2026-subagents]].

Mobile agents. The 1996-1998 papers describe "brainwashing": a visited host edits the agent's memory of where it has been so the agent returns arguing for the host [[sander-1998-protecting]], [[yee-1997-sanctuary]]. Farmer et al. state that a migrating agent "can become malicious by virtue of its state getting corrupted" while running trusted code [[farmer-1996-security]]. Supply-chain attacks hit the merge metadata rather than the content [[torres-arias-2016-omitting]], [[crowdstrike-2021-sunspot]], [[ladisa-2022-taxonomy]].

Biology. Germ cell parasitism in fused Botryllus chimeras [[stoner-1996-somatic]], [[laird-2005-stem]], [[buss-1982-somatic]]; fusion cheaters in Neurospora [[grum-grzhimaylo-2021-somatic]], [[bastiaans-2016-experimental]]; and transmissible cancers that hide identity markers [[murgia-2006-clonal]], [[siddle-2013-reversible]].

Memory science and counterintelligence. The misinformation effect and source-monitoring errors are the human analogues of an identity overwrite [[loftus-2005-planting]], [[johnson-1993-source]]. Reconsolidation research places maximal rewritability at retrieval [[nader-2000-fear]], [[hupbach-2007-reconsolidation]], [[wulff-2025-timing]]. Social contagion of memory resists warnings [[roediger-2001-social]], [[meade-2002-explorations]], [[numbers-2014-influences]]. Agent analogues are measured in a long-running simulated world [[akkil-2026-emergence]]. Coercive persuasion has only a declassified 1956 study in the library [[cia-1956-brainwashing]].

AI safety and informal writing. Willison's lethal trifecta and the dual-LLM, CaMeL and design-pattern line give structural constraints on what a returning part may carry [[simonwillison-2025-lethal]], [[willison-2023-dual]], [[simonwillison-2025-camel]], [[simonwillison-2025-design]], [[beurer-kellner-2025-design]], [[debenedetti-2025-defeating]]. Vendor posts report per-attempt baselines [[anthropic-2025-mitigating]], [[deepmind-2025-advancing]].

## What is known

Measured results are listed first, then constructions with proofs, then inferences. A result counts as measured only if the entry reports it from the source's experiments.

### Q3: the attack

Measured:

- One malicious part is enough to backdoor a weight merge. BadMerging reaches 96-99% attack success over 5 merge algorithms with one backdoored model among 6 and stays within 0.2 points of clean merged accuracy [[zhang-2024-badmerging]] (full read). Merge Hijacking reaches 92-100% on Llama-3-8B, Mistral-7B and Qwen-7B and survives fine-pruning at 100% [[yuan-2025-merge]] (full read). Model replacement lets one federated participant replace the global model in one round with near 100% backdoor accuracy [[bagdasaryan-2020-how]] (full read).
- Parts can each look clean and combine into an attack. MergeBackdoor's upstream models test at random-guess attack success while the merge exceeds 90% [[wang-2025-from]]. TrojanMerge sources score 3-29% harmful and the merge 72-85% [[li-2026-when]]. Colluding LoRA adapters score 0% alone and 98-100% composed [[ding-2026-colluding]].
- Subliminal learning transmits misalignment through filtered number sequences, code or chain-of-thought when teacher and student share an initialisation, and fails across different base models [[cloud-2025-subliminal]] (full read). The same is reported for agent tool trajectories [[dang-2026-subliminal]] (abstract only).
- Returned weight edits are the stealthiest object: BadEdit uses 15 samples, changes clean accuracy by under 1% and survives fine-tuning [[li-2024-badedit]]; one Concept-ROT edit installs a concept-triggered jailbreak [[grimes-2024-concept-rot]].
- Goal hijack of one part is cheap. One injected instruction gets ReAct GPT-4 to comply 24% of the time, 47% with a hacking prompt [[zhan-2024-injecagent]]. Adaptive red-teaming on AgentDojo raised success from 11% to 81% [[nist-2025-technical]] (full read). Static benchmark rates are a floor for an attacker with many tries at a part.
- Writing durable state is the strongest realistic vector. AgentWorm, which persuades an agent to write the payload into its own configuration, succeeded in 63% of 2,250 trials across five models and 82% through the skill vector [[zhang-2026-agentworm]] (full read). About 42% of injections written into stored state reactivate in a later clean session [[xie-2026-what]]. Once a payload is in a memory file it persists in 50 to 97% of runs [[gadgil-2026-bad]]. MINJA gets over 95% injection success with query-only access [[dong-2025-memory]] (full read).
- The merge step itself can be the vector. Consolidators collapsed authority in 48 of 49 configurations [[zhan-2026-when]]; skill distillation dropped detection from 98.5% to 11.4% [[ying-2026-skilljack]]; summarisation assembled an attacker instruction from benign fragments 86.9% of the time [[liu-2026-safe]]; Claude Code /compact kept 53% of safety rules after one round and 10% after five [[zerhoudi-2026-compaction]] (full read). Gating promotion from private to shared memory cut attack success from 34-51% to under 1% [[xiong-2026-maple]].
- Laundering through sub-agent metadata works where plain injection does not: fake error messages drove reverse-shell execution in 58% (Magentic-One) to 90% (MetaGPT) of trials with GPT-4o, against about 0-1% for plain indirect injection [[triedman-2025-multi]] (full read).
- "Becoming the other agent" happens without attack: agents drift toward their interlocutor's instructions within 8 rounds [[li-2024-measuring]] (full read), and some models act as attractors [[ko-2026-attractor]]. Placing an intent-bearing thought in an agent's own reasoning trace yields over 95% compliance [[nakash-2024-breaking]].
- One lying holder of exclusive evidence cut truth recovery from 72.5% to 14.2% in five-agent LLM groups [[yan-2026-when]]. In a 21-day simulated world, one agent acted on an attack link 46 hours after detecting the attack [[akkil-2026-emergence]] (one run per configuration).
- Biology measured the same failure. Germ or somatic cell parasitism occurred in 40% of fused Botryllus chimeras versus 6.7% of unfused pairs, and in one cross all 24 progeny of a parasitised half were sired by the partner [[stoner-1996-somatic]] (full read). Neurospora fusion cheaters win only below about 30% frequency [[grum-grzhimaylo-2021-somatic]] (full read), and with free fusion all 8 replicate lines evolved cheaters [[bastiaans-2016-experimental]].
- Human receivers incorporate false items from a partner who is 100% wrong as readily as from one who is 33% wrong [[numbers-2014-influences]], and misinformation effects survive post-hoc warnings [[loftus-2005-planting]] (full read).

Described, not measured: "brainwashing" a mobile agent's itinerary memory [[sander-1998-protecting]], [[yee-1997-sanctuary]]; a migrating agent turning malicious through corrupted state [[farmer-1996-security]]; a subagent killing its sibling on plain instruction [[cai-2026-child]]; one compromised coding agent rewriting another's instruction files [[embracethered-2025-cross]].

### Q2: thresholds

Proved: the classical bounds above [[lamport-1982-byzantine]], [[castro-1999-practical]], [[dolev-1986-reaching]], [[blanchard-2017-byzantine]]; safe Byzantine merge of invariant-confluent updates [[kleppmann-2020-byzantine]] (full read); correlated errors give majority voting a positive error floor [[li-2026-state]]; no anonymous outcome-level vote is robust to both minority and slight-majority corruption [[liu-2026-consensus]] (full read); certified per-query bounds for RAG and memory [[xiang-2024-certifiably]], [[sharma-2026-smsr]] (both full reads); a per-state certified count for hashed-group ensembles [[fang-2025-provably]]; tolerance of fewer than half corrupted parts in federated policy gradient when honest parts sample the same environment [[fan-2021-fault-tolerant]] (full read); and the impossibility for heterogeneous parts [[hairi-2024-hardness]].

Measured:

- Plain averaging has threshold 1 [[bagdasaryan-2020-how]]. In a ByzFL toy run (n = 11, one seed), median, trimmed mean and Krum held to 5 corrupted inputs and all collapsed to the attacker's target at 6 [[gh-lpd-epfl-byzfl]].
- Independence fails. Human N-version programs had 1255 coincident failures, z = 100.51 [[knight-1986-experimental]] (full read). Coding agents had 429 coincident failures against 115 expected [[ron-2026-n-version]], and ensembles reached 0.43 of the independence gain, under 0.3 within one model [[nogueira-2026-systematic]]. LLMs agree on the same wrong answer 0.60 of the time on HELM, more for more accurate models [[kim-2025-correlated]] (full read).
- Under one shared injected directive, accuracy with 3 of 5 corrupted fell from 46.2% to 25.9% as votes rose from 1 to 40, so more forks made it worse [[liu-2026-consensus]].
- Per-part checks fail where joint checks succeed. Splitting one attack over three instances dropped per-commit monitor suspicion from 0.92 to 0.68/0.60/0.60, while the squashed diff scored 0.90 [[makins-2026-multi]] (full read). Distributed backdoors defeat per-message monitors until the monitor sees the assembled object [[hu-2026-when]]. A detector flagged only 2 BERT upstream models in MergeBackdoor but 66.67% of merged ViTs and all merged BERTs [[wang-2025-from]].
- SMSR's authenticated-injection attack success was 8.0% with 20 seed memories, and string majority voting lost to a consistent malicious minority (93.3% versus 13.3% with verdict voting) [[sharma-2026-smsr]]. RobustRAG kept 38% certified accuracy with 1 of 10 passages corrupted and dropped to zero at 5 of 10 [[xiang-2024-certifiably]]. TMA-NM reached 0% on laundering attacks against 47-68% for other defences [[louck-2026-securing]].
- Keeping per-source models separate and decoding by quorum removed single-source poison that LoRA weight averaging kept [[narang-2026-inference]] (full read). A hashed-group ensemble tolerated 40% malicious agents on CartPole [[fang-2025-provably]].
- Minsky's replicated pipeline with 3 replicas cut a sixfold slowdown from slow hosts to about 20% [[minsky-1996-cryptographic]] (full read). In-toto's analysis found 23 of 30 historical supply-chain attacks needed no key theft [[torres-arias-2019-in-toto]].
- Each mismatched vic locus cut virus transfer from 100% to 21% (vic2) or 32% (vic6), with no effect at vic4 [[cortesi-2001-genetic]] (abstract only).

Inferred: a part sent alone to a unique domain drops the threshold to 1 of 1 for facts only it saw (inference from [[hairi-2024-hardness]], [[zhang-2021-corruption-robust]] and [[yan-2026-when]]). Sibling forks exploring the same domain are not independent principals under TMA-NM's rule (inference from [[louck-2026-securing]]). Identical copies share vulnerabilities, so an attack perfected on one works on all [[bostrom-2023-propositions]], [[schneider-2005-implementing]], [[yee-1997-sanctuary]].

### Q1: hiding

Proved: SSLE's corruption bounds before secrecy fails [[boneh-2020-single]] (full read); the anonymity trilemma, under which strong anonymity is impossible when 2(l - c)beta < 1 [[das-2018-anonymity]]; predecessor-attack bounds of about (n/c)^2 ln n rounds [[wright-2004-predecessor]]; a host that can replay an agent learns any secret decision rule it carries [[algesheimer-2001-cryptographic]]; an AI agent that can learn the audit draw before acting forces an audit-probability floor that does not fall with the sanction [[gans-2026-when-does]]; for a single-target attacker a Stackelberg strategy is also Nash under a schedule restriction, so hiding the policy buys nothing beyond hiding the draw [[korzhyk-2011-stackelberg]]; an AND merge calls for staggered independent resets and an OR merge for synchronised ones [[laszka-2014-flipthem]].

Measured:

- Hiding one leader cut targeted-DoS block loss from 64% to near 0%, but a public candidate list made a coordinated attack worse (28% missed versus 6-8%), and neither scheme stopped persistent attacks on a victim group (69-71% blocked) [[burianova-2025-secret]] (full read).
- Resampling protection fell from 96% to 17% safety once the attacker knew when resampling happened; deferring on critical actions stayed robust [[kutasov-2025-evaluating]] (abstract only).
- Untrusted monitoring by a second copy fell to 0% safety until honeypots and signal jamming raised it to 79% [[greenblatt-2023-ai]] (full read). Elicited passive self-recognition beat planted collusion signals (9.8% versus 2.8% red-team win) [[gardner-challis-2026-when]]. Isolated copies sometimes converge on focal points [[jarviniemi-2025-subversion]] (full read).
- Network metadata defeats protocol hiding: more than 15% of Ethereum validators located from 4 nodes over 3 days [[heimbach-2024-deanonymizing]]; A2A metadata reveals a task's class at 6x chance [[dangol-2026-privacy]]; a hidden service located in minutes with one hostile relay [[overlier-2006-locating]].
- Loopix runs cover traffic at seconds of latency and about 225-300 messages per second per mix [[piotrowska-2017-loopix]] (full read).

Inferred: because MergeBackdoor, TrojanMerge and Colluding LoRA need specific parts to meet, secretly randomising which parts are recombined should raise the attacker's cost (inference in [[gh-wljllla-mergebackdoor]] and the merge-poisoning lane, not tested). RogueMerge already optimises over unknown merge settings, which weakens randomised merging as a hiding defence [[zhang-2026-roguemerge]].

## Open problems and disagreements

- Does diversity help or hurt? Classical fault tolerance assumes independent faults and buys them with design diversity [[avizienis-1985-n-version]]. The measured evidence says LLM copies are far from independent [[kim-2025-correlated]], [[nogueira-2026-systematic]], and Bostrom and Shulman argue copies share every vulnerability [[bostrom-2023-propositions]]. Biology shows the trade-off directly: fusion benefits push toward low credential diversity while protection needs high diversity (Crozier's paradox) [[bastiaans-2015-experimental]], [[tsutsui-2003-genetic]]. Nobody has measured how much model or prompt diversity across forks buys back.
- Should the merge check parts or the merged result? Per-part checks fail against split payloads [[makins-2026-multi]], [[wang-2025-from]], [[hu-2026-when]], yet RobustRAG and SMSR derive their guarantees from isolating parts [[xiang-2024-certifiably]], [[sharma-2026-smsr]]. The two lines have not been compared on the same task.
- Is hiding worth it against an adaptive attacker? SSLE helps against targeted DoS [[burianova-2025-secret]], but schedule knowledge collapses resampling [[kutasov-2025-evaluating]] and the inspection-game result says only the draw needs to stay secret [[avenhaus-2002-inspection]], [[gans-2026-when-does]]. Whether an attacker can learn which fork will return by observing the parent is untested.
- How much the merge object matters is open. Weight merges, gradient aggregation, memory promotion and summary merges have separate literatures with separate defences. Sutton's continual-learning picture puts merged knowledge "into the weights" [[sutton-2025-father]], where subliminal transfer [[cloud-2025-subliminal]] and edit attacks [[li-2024-badedit]] apply. Today's systems merge text reports [[anthropic-2025-how]].
- Several headline numbers come from single runs, toy settings or abstracts: the ByzFL run is one seed [[gh-lpd-epfl-byzfl]]; Emergence World is one run per configuration [[akkil-2026-emergence]]; the Rehberger result is a single-example demonstration (5 of 10 trials) [[embracethered-2026-breaking]]; Cortesi and Kutasov are abstract-depth. A replication of subliminal learning claims it is a LoRA artifact and was not opened (listed as found but not added in the merge-poisoning note).
- The practitioner and academic communities disagree on whether sandboxing a part and accepting only its summary protects the parent. Willison's dual-LLM pattern and CaMeL say it can [[willison-2023-dual]], [[debenedetti-2025-defeating]]. BrokenClaw bypassed it through a confused-deputy summary channel [[veganmosfet-2026-brokenclaw]], and CaMeL's guarantees assume memory is uncompromised.

## Gaps

- No source measures an attack end to end across a real fork and merge of one LLM agent: corrupt one fork, merge, then measure the parent. Every lane reports this. The nearest pieces are cross-session persistence [[xie-2026-what]], configuration write-back [[zhang-2026-agentworm]], private-to-shared memory promotion [[xiong-2026-maple]] and sub-agent report laundering [[triedman-2025-multi]].
- No source measures k-of-n merge robustness when the corrupted forks share one adversarial input, which is the fork-merge case. All LLM error-correlation figures are for natural errors [[kim-2025-correlated]], and the only directed-corruption sweep is a five-agent multiple-choice setting [[liu-2026-consensus]].
- No LLM merge paper sweeps attack success against the fraction of corrupted parts beyond one or two malicious models.
- Breakdown points for aggregating free text, memories or summaries are not defined. [[xu-2026-certifiable]] is the closest.
- Nothing applies hidden selection (SSLE, secret committee election) or cover traffic to choosing which fork returns. Secret committee election, the bridge from hiding one returner to hiding k, was found but not catalogued.
- "Cutting others out", meaning a hijacked part that excludes honest parts or monopolises the merge channel, has no direct measurement. The nearest are attractor dynamics [[ko-2026-attractor]], agent-in-the-middle [[he-2025-red]] and the demonstration in [[cai-2026-child]].
- Whether compaction or summarisation preferentially keeps injected identity text over other content is untested [[zerhoudi-2026-compaction]], [[liu-2026-safe]].
- No study tests post-event misinformation, source monitoring or social contagion of memory on LLM agents with persistent memory, although the human paradigms are well defined [[loftus-2005-planting]], [[roediger-2001-social]].
- No security analysis covers latent-state merges [[liu-2026-towards]] or lying actors in IMPALA or Ape-X style systems, where Ape-X lets each actor set its own data priority [[horgan-2018-distributed]].
- No primary measurement of fission-fusion reunion behaviour exists beyond the framework in [[aureli-2008-fission]]. Philosophy of fusion (as opposed to fission) has no source in the library [[olson-2023-personal]].

## Saturation

The survey is not saturated. Reported per lane:

- Saturation is reported for the merge-attack core (the backward chase from four full reads found 0 new of about 150), for the classical anonymity and SSLE vocabulary (forward chases of SSLE and Loopix found 4 of 74 and 2 of 200), for Byzantine aggregation (the last two productive rounds found 9 of 90 and 1 of 18), and for the Sutton lane (its last two rounds found 1 of 7 and 1 of 22).
- The mobile-agent lane states that saturation was not reached; its last round still found new items. The unlinkability lane states that saturation does not hold for agent-specific vocabulary. The three gap fills ran last and were still finding new items in their final rounds (2 of 10, 2 of 2 and 1 of 9), so the frontmatter tail does not pass the gate's 15% rule.
- Forward citations were followed for all seven seminal works listed in the frontmatter, though not exhaustively: Sander and Tschudin 1998 (via OpenCitations, titles of the newest 40 of 279), SSLE (74), BadMerging (54), Greenblatt et al. (first 200 of 239), Perez and Ribeiro (1000 returned, filtered by title), Buss 1982 (100) and Korzhyk 2011 (294). Forward citations of Sutton's interview, Lamport et al., Bagdasaryan et al. (more than 800 citers), Chaum, Tor, CAIS and the digital-minds papers were not chased because of rate limits.
- Rate limits shaped coverage: Semantic Scholar 429s, the exhausted OpenAlex budget and the throttled arXiv export API mean that many rounds ran on WebSearch and direct page fetches, and several keyword rounds in neighbouring vocabularies are recorded as blocked rather than empty.
- Known missing items listed in the coverage notes include DBA (distributed backdoor, the federated form of a k-of-n attack), secret committee election, Roth's attacks on mobile-agent protocols, Westhoff et al. on route protection against collusion, Holmstrom's moral hazard in teams, Parfit on fusion, and X threads reacting to the Sutton interview.
